The number that matters moved from 84% to 97% in one night, and every point of it came from data: 4,963 CSXA cervical patients and 7,200 BUU lumbar films entered training. Nothing else that was tried — architecture, size in millimetres, learning rate, epochs, masking — moved a single vertebra by more than a few points. The lumbar number is lower for one reason: about 380 lumbar films with annotated levels exist in training, against ~5,000 cervical.

Why: single-vertebra accuracy against real films of that region in training

The lumbar side of that chart is the whole argument. Zero lumbar films: 7%. About 380 NHANES lumbar films with annotated levels: 81%. Add 7,200 BUU lumbar films whose levels were assigned by counting: 83%. Raise the crop resolution: 85%. The cervical side, with 5,000 annotated patients, is at 98%. The next lumbar step is more lumbar films with levels, which is what the VinDr pseudo-labelling below is for.

What moved the lumbar number, and what did not

  • Resolution: 48 → 96 px targets, lumbar one-vertebra 80.7% → 85.4%. Same lever as on CT (73.7% → 82.8%).
  • BUU's counting-inferred labels: +2.0 points at one vertebra with the films never scored. The counting prior is in training now, deliberately, as a train-only source; the test set is annotated levels only.
  • Absolute size in millimetres: −1.7 points. Third time this has been tried (CT 22 px, CT full merge, films); it has never helped. The ±1 confusions are not a magnification problem.
  • Architecture: ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-tiny within two points of ResNet-18 on CT; ViT-B/16 47 points worse. Not repeated on films.

Where the last 8% goes with the whole scan visible: rendering the 188 errors showed 80% are whole-case shifts — every vertebra in a case one level cranial or caudal together, 151 errors in 24 of 234 validation cases. Only 4 of the 17 VerSe cases that shift carry an atypical count in the raw label; the rest are spines whose field of view cuts the column where the topmost level's identity is under-determined. At wide context the ceiling is enumeration, not local morphology, which is why within-one-level is the honest primary metric for a level-confirmation tool.

3. Outline alone

A vertebra reduced to 128 points on its silhouette, centred and unit-scaled, reaches 72.5% exact, 95.0% within one, 94.9% region on held-out synthetic cases. That equals the image arm at one vertebra at the same resolution: the identity of a single vertebra lives in its outline. The same contour model scores 0.1% on NHANES, which is a shape-definition mismatch (NHANES annotates the body alone; CT labels the whole vertebra), not a generalisation result.

4. CT alone does not transfer

Models trained on projected CT, scored on NHANES films cropped the same way: 7.4% exact at one vertebra, 21.1% at three, and region accuracy of 21–64%, below chance for three regions — a network collapsing to its majority class on input unlike anything it has seen. Tripling the CT data did not change it. Integrating soft tissue into the projection (−200 HU rather than 150 HU) doubles the transfer (27.5% at five vertebrae, region 73.5%) and is still a third of what real films in training reach. Every real-film arm above therefore trains on films.

5. Data: what was used, what was excluded, what is being added

  • NHANES II (543 films; 364 patients after de-duplicating cervical/lumbar pairs), levels annotated: train and test.
  • CSXA (4,963 patients, cervical C3–C7), levels annotated: train and test. This is what took the cervical number to 98%.
  • BUU-LSPINE (7,203 films, AP + lateral; laterals used), levels inferred_from_position: train only, never scored. Excluded from the first report on principle; admitted as a train-only source once the test set was annotated-only, and it helped.
  • VinDr-SpineXR (5,136 lumbar films, lesion boxes, no view or level labels) pipeline running: a view classifier routes the laterals to the corner detector, and the three-vertebra film model (99.1%) assigns levels, to be admitted as a second train-only lumbar source. The first run labelled zero films lateral: the view classifier's training loop read r.view, which in pandas is the Series.view method, so every training film was labelled AP. Fixed (r["view"]) and relaunched 11:05, job 40215525.
  • AASCE (481, AP scoliosis) and Mendeley (591): AP or no levels; not used.
  • The Sequence Loss of the closest prior work (Multi-View, MICCAI 2023) re-injects the counting prior and is left out of every arm. SpineCLUE states the question but never publishes the curve.

6. What comes next, in order

  1. Three- and five-vertebra rungs of the +BUU arm (running).
  2. VinDr laterals pseudo-labelled and admitted as train-only; the lumbar single-vertebra bar above is the number to watch.
  3. Dual-view fusion on BUU's paired AP + lateral films — the one route with an untested information source rather than more of the same.
  4. Supervised-contrastive pre-training of the encoder on the crop bank, then fine-tune: cheap, one run, plausible few points.

7. What these numbers can and cannot support

  • Can: how exact-level accuracy falls with visible context on held-out patients; that region survives when ordinal position is gone; that the outline alone carries single-vertebra identity; that CT alone does not transfer to film; that annotated real films are the binding constraint and roughly how many buy what.
  • Cannot: clinical validation. No number here is a claim about intra-operative safety. NHANES is 1970s film, CSXA and BUU are standing radiographs, none is fluoroscopy, and NHANES annotation is body-only.
