Spinal Border Variants
CTSpinoPelvic1K · 802 densified cases

Both ends of the spine vary together

Measuring the thoracolumbar and lumbosacral borders separately across the whole cohort, then asking whether a variant at one end predicts a variant at the other. It does — strongly.

19.6  odds ratio

Cases with a thoracolumbar variant carry a lumbosacral variant 44 times, where independence predicts 9. Of 796 cases with complete measurements: 667 typical at both ends, 60 thoracolumbar only, 25 lumbosacral only, 44 both.

This is the “one population, not two” claim, made countable. It is an association in segmentation-derived measures, not a causal or clinical result.

Three findings worth keeping

The 12th rib is bimodal

Rib 12 length as a fraction of rib 11 splits into two clear modes: a main population near 0.68 and a distinct hypoplastic population near 0.32. 67 cases fall below 0.30. This is not a tail of one distribution — it is two, which is what a discrete developmental variant should look like.

LSTV cases avoid five free lumbar vertebrae

The cohort piles at five (734 cases). Labelled LSTV cases sit at four (35) and six (26) instead — sacralization pulling one way, lumbarization the other. The 13 cases carrying a rib on a lumbar body cluster at four, as they must.

A thoracolumbar measure separates a lumbosacral label

Ranked by standardised difference against the LSTV label, rib12_len_left_mm clears d = 0.5 — a measurement taken at the other end of the spine from the thing being labelled. That is the co-occurrence showing up a second way, in a measure that knew nothing about the sacrum.

Read these with the caveats

Sacral foramina counts are unusable as anatomy

Intended as the measure that settles sacralization without trusting any vertebra label — four pairs normal, five means the sacrum absorbed L5. The detector does not deliver it. On one symmetric sacrum it returned 3 left and 7 right. Small canals close in a bone mask and morphological closing invents others. Kept in the data as a relative signal, with the reference box set to the cohort mode rather than a textbook 4, and deliberately excluded from the co-occurrence test.

Castellvi axes are proxies, not grades

Lateral span and gap to the ala carry much of what Castellvi grades on, but they are not a graded read, and the flat effect sizes may reflect the proxy rather than the anatomy.

The LSTV labels are the reference, and they are imperfect

33 of 802 carry a non-normal label. One case in the lumbar-rib set (0008) is labelled SACRALIZATION with the two readers in disagreement. Effect sizes are measured against this reference, so they inherit its errors.
