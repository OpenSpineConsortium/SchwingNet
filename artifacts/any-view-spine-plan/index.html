Any-View Spine Plan
Confidential: patent material, before the provisional SchwingNet · From 3D to X-ray · 21 October 2026

Any view, any position, and a 3D gold standard

This plan covers three goals. The first is a network that turns an X-ray taken in one position into another, such as AP to lateral. The second is a detector and classifier that works at any position. The third is the paired X-ray and CT sets serving as gold ground truth for the 3D prior. For each goal it gives what has been published, how well you can expect it to work, and what the census has to count before training starts.

The short answer
StrongPaired X-ray and CT as gold truth Where a CT and the X-ray geometry are both known, level labels need no reader, and registration-based labelling is near perfect in the published work. This is the best-founded part of the plan.
Feasible, measured gapDetect and classify at any position Your own record shows lateral naming already transfers from renders, at 96.4% zero-shot. AP naming collapses to 43.7%, a counting error the 3D prior targets. Detection needs real frames.
Hard as statedAP image to lateral image No published work turns a spine AP film into a lateral one. The closest work moves the view only 9 degrees. What will work is predicting the lateral's levels, corners and alignment from the AP. A synthesised lateral picture is a display aid and not a film.

Should it wait for the census? Partly. Nothing below needs the census to start: the first steps are renders and data plumbing on sets you already hold. The decision to train each model does wait for it, because the per-region counts (cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral) decide where each model has enough real pairs to be tested at all. The census job is built (PR #76) and goes to the grid next. This page's last table fills in once it returns.

The limit no data removes: what an AP film does not show, a model cannot recover. A listhesis or a wedged body seen only on the lateral is invisible on the AP, and a synthesised lateral would paint the typical spine in its place. So a generated lateral must never be read as if it were a film.

What will work, and how well

  • Structured transfer first. From the AP, predict the lateral's levels, corners and sagittal alignment, and score them against the real lateral of the same person. This answers whether the AP holds the information at all. Expect levels and global alignment to transfer about as well as LatXGen's angles did. Expect focal sagittal findings not to transfer. Inferred
  • Pixels second. Train image synthesis as an extra task, so the encoder learns 3D shape from the pairing. The picture is a teaching signal, not a product. Published idea
  • You have more real pairs than the report assumed. BUU-LSPINE is held: 3,600 people with an AP and a lateral lumbar film each, with end-plate lines. That is on the scale of LatXGen's 2,611 training pairs. C-ArmLSpine adds 149 cases with AP and lateral frames and the CBCT of the same patient. COBRA adds full rotations, and renders add unlimited exact pairs. Registry
  • Where it is thin. There are no real paired films above the lumbar spine among the held sets. VinDr-SpineXR's two-view studies are lumbar too, and the census counts how many it has. Cervical and thoracic transfer would rest on renders alone until pairs are found. To count

What the published work adds

  • SyntheX (hip, 20 CTs, real test on 366 cadaver X-rays): trained on realistic simulation with strong randomisation alone, it matched real-data training, with landmark error 6.44 vs 6.46 mm and Dice 0.80 vs 0.79. Scaled up to 10k images it beat it, at 5.95 mm and Dice 0.86. On another imaging system it scored 6.16 mm. For chest lesions it fell short (85% vs 94%). So physics renders can carry detection, but not always. Your 0.3% may be the renderer's fault, which is what the physics renderer ablation (#69) tests. Published
  • Any angle means a per-angle curve: accuracy at each C-arm angle from AP through the obliques to lateral. COBRA's 692 real rotations give that curve on real projections for the low lumbar spine and the sacrum (#60). Renders give it everywhere else.

Expected

Lateral naming at any device is already at the ceiling. AP should rise substantially once counting is fixed, because its error is one systematic shift and not noise. Obliques are unmeasured. Detection at a new device needs some real frames of it, or a renderer good enough that the ablation says otherwise. Inferred from the record

3. Paired X-ray and CT as the gold standard for the 3D prior

On a frame whose patient also has a CT, and whose geometry is stored or can be registered, every vertebra's level and corners come from projecting the CT's segmentation. No reader is involved, so the 3D prior is checked against truth and not against another opinion.

  • How well registration labels levels: LevelCheck (Lo et al., Spine 2015, 20 thoracolumbar surgery patients, 10,000 perturbed views) labelled 100% of views correctly at about 26 s, and 99.3% at 7 s. Earlier work reported 0.2 ± 0.2 mm projection error in simulation and under 1 mm in cadavers. So frames labelled by registration are gold within a fraction of a vertebra. Published
  • What gold truth can and cannot cover: every paired spine set, held or not, is lumbar or lumbosacral, except the digital twin's cadaver, one specimen reaching T2; the four non-spine pairs tune the registration code only. The prior's thoracic and cervical parts can be checked only on renders until a paired set there is found; the plan for reaching C2 from the lumbosacral gold by renders, physics noise, denoising and generative translation is SchwingNet workstream P. Registry
  • The goal stated on 2026-10-22: every paired set is segmented, in ITK-SNAP with AI assistance where no annotation exists and verified by a second reader, so every real X-ray with a CT carries gold per-pixel labels; the annotated X-rays without a CT then either train an independent pseudolabeller or test the network, one role per person. Owner
  • The rule that keeps it gold: a person used to check the prior is never used to fit it. The split is by person, as the catalog already does.

5. The order

  1. Split NHANES II, VinDr-SpineXR and FluoroCap by region in the next census run (the JSON manifest holds the DICOM body parts; a second table in tools/sample_index.py prints them).
  2. The counting prior's grid job (#57, built) and the AP and oblique renders with anchors (#59). These lift AP naming before the talk.
  3. COBRA projected labels and the per-angle curve (#60), the gold set for the lumbosacral spine.
  4. Structured AP-to-lateral transfer on BUU-LSPINE and C-ArmLSpine pairs (#68), scored on held-out persons. Pixel synthesis only as its auxiliary task.
  5. Thoracic and cervical transfer only once the census or new letters find paired films there.
