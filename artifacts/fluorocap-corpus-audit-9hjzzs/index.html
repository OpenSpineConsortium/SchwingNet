01What is in the corpus

The corpus is eleven thousand panel images mined from the literature and from surgical video. Three thousand of them carry machine-assigned vertebra labels. The rest either are not spine radiographs or defeated the detector, and the audit cannot yet tell those two apart.

Coverage gap

Only 29% of known panels produced a vertebra box. The modality classifier scores 93% on a held-out set of 2,143 panels, so a large share of the missing 71% is detector miss rather than a panel that is not a radiograph. A self-trained detector is running and has not yet been folded into a decode.

02Image quality is the binding constraint

Half the corpus is smaller than the window the classifier crops. The level model scales each target vertebra to a fixed height and reads a 320-pixel window around it; on a panel whose short side is 231 pixels, most of that window is interpolation, not evidence.

03Level coverage, and two levels that have never appeared

Blocking finding

T13 and L6 have never been assigned to a single real panel. Both are in the model's 27-class output, so this is not a missing class — the model never predicts them and no caption ever forced one. Only 2 of 2,815 captioned panels mention any transitional term at all. The transitional-anatomy claim, which is the project's headline, currently has no real-image evidence behind it.

The CT render banks show the mechanism. Across 1,175 rendered cases the training distribution holds roughly 1,100 examples of each lumbar level against 68 L6 and 6 T13 vertebrae. No re-weighting will rescue a 180-to-1 ratio; the render banks have to change.

Which evidence term is carrying the corpus

The level model alone is right 38.3% of the time on panels where the caption was held back. The vision-language read, the caption-derived hardware constraint and the figure-mate vote are all substantially better. That is the correct signal that constraint decoding is doing real work — and it is also the reason this corpus must not be used to retrain the level model without re-weighting, because the model would be learning largely from its own errors.

Per level, with the caption held out

Diagnosis

This is a lumbar model. It works from L1 to L4 at 63–70%, weakens at L5 and T12, and collapses through the thoracic spine, at C1–C2 and at the sacrum. An independent check agrees: the four-way region head scores 76.6% on lumbar panels but 44.8% thoracic and 12.6% sacral, calling 90 of 135 sacral panels lumbar. Section 5 finds out why, and the answer is not the one this table suggests.

Root cause

The training bank is built from cervical films and lumbar films. There are no thoracic films in it at all. T2 through T11, T13 and L6 have exactly zero training crops; T12, the sacrum, C1, C2 and T1 have between 22 and 190. The model was never shown the rest of the spine, so logit adjustment and class-balanced sampling — the standard answer to a skewed corpus, and what this audit first recommended — would do nothing. You cannot re-weight a class with no examples.

What is missing already exists elsewhere in the project. The CT render banks carry roughly a thousand crops each of T10, T11 and T12 and eight hundred of the sacrum; VerSeFusion covers C1 through L6; and 55 VerSeFusion cases carry an L6 or a T13. An all-spine bank merging the films with every CT render source is building now.

One gap survives that fix and is separate from it. At L1, where the bank holds 3,843 crops, the model scores 85.4% on its own films and 43.0% on renders. That difference is domain, not coverage, and it is what the cone-beam renderer and a resolution-degradation augmentation are for.

06The decoder works without any caption

Panels from the same figure almost always show the same region. That gives a check the caption cannot contaminate, because no caption text enters it.

Strongest result

Constraint decoding lifts caption-free region agreement from 52.1% to 91.5% and the rate of substantially overlapping level sets from 30.8% to 68.2%. The decoder is not simply copying captions; it recovers structure the classifier alone does not have.

The answer is genuinely encouraging above the transition and genuinely bad at it. Every level from L1 to L4 is named as well or better on a six-lumbar spine as on a normal one, which is what local morphology predicts and what a counting method could not do. But the transitional vertebra itself is never recognised, and the level immediately above it loses two thirds of its accuracy.

Safety consequence

On a six-lumbar spine this classifier would confidently call the sixth lumbar vertebra an L5. That is exactly the error that sends a surgeon one level off, so the rare classes are not a completeness nicety — they are the safety case.

Castellvi strata were requested but never populated: a case-identifier parse in the evaluation failed to join the renders to the manifest, so only normal, L6 and T13 strata exist. Fixing that join is a prerequisite for claiming anything about sacralisation.

10What is not known

No panel in this corpus has been read by a human. Every accuracy figure above is agreement between two automatic sources, which bounds error from one side only: two sources can agree and both be wrong.

11What to do, in order

  1. Read 400 panels. Nothing else converts an agreement number into an accuracy number. The reader pages already exist; stratify by deciding evidence and by region so the weak arms get read.
  2. Finish the all-spine bank and retrain. The thoracic spine and the sacrum are absent from training, not under-weighted, so re-weighting cannot reach them. Merge the films with the CTSpinoPelvic1K, VerSeFusion and cone-beam render banks, split by patient so poses of one scan cannot straddle the split, and retrain.
  3. Oversample T13 and L6 by two orders of magnitude in every render arm. Until a real panel carries one of these labels, the transitional claim is unsupported.
  4. Raise resolution. Refetch publisher-original figures and finish the 1080-pixel video refetch. Half the corpus sits below the model's own crop window.
  5. Fold the self-trained detector into a decode and re-measure the 29% box rate, which is the largest single source of lost data.
