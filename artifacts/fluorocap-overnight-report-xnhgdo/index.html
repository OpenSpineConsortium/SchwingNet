02The level model

08:13The v2 model landed and is in the registry (alias allspine_v2_r1, mirrored). Own validation 78.8% exact, 95.9% within one, 97.2% region; cone-beam renders 62.5% against v1’s 50.0% on their own validation; rare-anatomy renders 52.3%. Its diagnostic now excludes renders of training cases (a filter added this morning): over 800 held-out renders it scores 64.2%, with 4,505 training renders skipped. That is the honest render number. The 92.7% quoted for v1 in the table below was measured on renders whose CT cases were in the training bank and should be read as a training-set score. Held-out per level (v2): L4 80.4, L5 72.3, L3 68.2, T12 66.2, L2 61.6, T11 60.3, L1 56.5, T10 52.4, T9 37.5, T7 39.4, sacrum 100.0 (n 120); L6 50.0 on 10, T13 0.0 on 1.

The all-spine v1 model, trained overnight on films plus every CT render source, fixes the failure the audit found: T2 to T11, which had zero training crops, and T12 and the sacrum, which had about 130 each, are now learned. The lower thoracic spine scores like the lumbar spine; the mid-thoracic levels, which only VerSeFusion renders supply, still sit at 43 to 58%.

Bank v2: clipped vertebrae excluded, rare anatomy added

The v2 bank drops any vertebra cut by the collimator as a target (8,949 of 28,270 cone-beam boxes, 31.7%) and adds the 1,482 rare-anatomy renders. Its model trained to 78.5% exact, 95.9% within one level, 97.3% region on its own validation; on the cone-beam renders 61.9% against v1's 50.0% on its own validation, a comparison to read loosely because v2's validation set excludes the clipped targets; on the rare-anatomy renders 52.7% exact and 93.2% within one level.

Incident

The chain's link step ran ln -sf on a file whose name was already the link's name, which replaces the file with a link to itself. The weights were written at 05:22 and lost about 05:24. The step is guarded now, the bank was untouched, and the retrain was resubmitted at 05:47 with the diagnostic and transitional test queued behind it; the model should land about 08:15 (the first run took 2 h 25 min) and its two tests after that.

03Transitional anatomy

08:14v2, held out (376 renders scored, 2,281 renders of training cases skipped): normal spines T12 90.8, L4 84.3, L5 82.5, sacrum 100.0. Six-lumbar spines: T12 58.1, L4 47.2, L5 38.9, L6 63.0% single-crop (68 of 108 called L6, 29 called L5); the caption-free decoder still calls only 39.8% of them L6, so the rare-column penalty is set too high and is the next thing to tune. T13: 0 of 16 (13 called T12), on four spines; nothing has yet taught the model a thirteenth thoracic vertebra. The v1 numbers in the table below were not held out.

Single-crop accuracy, all-spine v1. On six-lumbar spines the model shifts every lumbar name down by one, that is, it reads the column as five lumbar bodies above the sacrum.

Two changes address this. The v2 bank oversamples the 56 VerSeFusion (50 with an L6, 6 with a T13) and 34 CTSpinoPelvic1K cases carrying an L6 or a Castellvi variant: up to twelve poses in two views each, 1,482 frames kept after dropping frames with fewer than two unclipped vertebrae. And both decoders now try the level column with a T13 or an L6 inserted, against a prior penalty, so a caption-free decode can output either. That change post-dates the test above, whose decoder column reads 0.0% at L6 and T13 by construction; the new decoder has so far passed only a unit check (a synthetic T12 to L6 run decodes as such), and the corpus re-run is its real test. The corpus itself still has no real panel labelled T13 or L6; the pathology search (158,955 articles, 1,961 level-bearing figures matched so far, still reading) is the route to them.

04Synthetic frames

07:25Two verdicts. The CUT translator finished 30 epochs, but its preview shows what it was trained toward: the panel directories as they are, which include MRI slices, a photographed lightbox and annotated screenshots (the modality classifier calls only 2,991 of 18,119 panels spine radiographs at p ≥ 0.7). v1 is not a test of translation; v2, restricted to those 2,991 panels and to renders with at least two unclipped vertebrae, is queued on the H200 partition (jobs 40285316/7; the first attempt ran out of memory on a 16 GB card). The diffusion img2img sweep at low strength answered the other question: at 0.2 it leaves the render essentially unchanged, at 0.35 it already smears end-plates and pedicles. There is no usable setting; img2img is dropped.

Two generators were tried overnight. One produced 12,896 images and failed the only test that matters; the other is queued behind it.

Stopped

At that strength the diffusion prior overwrites the anatomy the labels describe. The array was cancelled at 05:50 after 12,896 images, all kept for inspection, none usable for level training. A low-strength run (0.20 to 0.35, control scale 0.9 to 1.0, 300 cases) is queued to find out whether a setting exists that keeps the bones.

The unpaired render-to-frame translator (CUT) is queued on the H200 partition, which the FluoroGen array had been occupying. It carries every label over exactly by construction, and its evaluation is already scripted: a films-only model scored on raw versus translated renders, which measures the domain gap it closes.
