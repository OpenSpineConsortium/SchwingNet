Your task: read one artifact into its record and then, as its finder, set it against the artifacts it sits with.

Your input is the JSON file ${file}. Read it whole first. It holds:
- artifact: the artifact to read, with its id, title, account, link, readme_summary (the line the artifacts index gives it), text and folder.
- before: its record as the synthesis held it, or null for an artifact new since then.
- changed: for an artifact read before, the lines of its text removed and added since its record was read, each hunk at its line; null where the artifact is new, or where its old text could not be read, so that you read it whole.
- cluster and cluster_file: the topic cluster the artifact belongs to and the file of that cluster's members, each with its id, title, date, text, folder, summary, key claims and numbers; both null for a new artifact.
- clusters: every cluster, with its name, description and file.
- numbers: the file of the numbers table, every figure of every record, one to a line, each with its artifact's id, value, unit, n and context. It is long: search it with the Grep tool for this artifact's figures and quantities rather than reading it whole.
- listed: the rows the contradictions page already holds on this artifact, cluster_rows and number_rows, each with its kind, both artifacts and its topic or quantity.

First, the record. Read the artifact whole: its text, and the files of its folder where the text is cut or leaves something out (a page's data, a table, a script's figures); an image you cannot read is described from its caption and alt text. Write every field from what you read, quoting the artifact's own words as evidence. Where before is given, start from it and check each of its entries against the page as it is now: keep what still holds, correct or remove what the change altered, and add what the change brings. The fields, as the records of the first reading have them:
- kind: page for an HTML page (index.html), docs for a document kept as Markdown (index.md), or the word that fits better where neither does, as design, bench and figures are used for a template, a verification bench and a page of figures.
- genre: one word, or a hyphenated phrase, for what kind of writing it is: report, runbook, plan, analysis, audit, spec, protocol, grant, handoff, checklist and the like.
- date: the artifact's own date as precisely as its text gives it, YYYY-MM-DD, YYYY-MM or YYYY; date_evidence: the words that date it, quoted, and what else the text says about when it was written.
- question: the question the artifact answers, in one sentence; summary: what it says, in three to six sentences.
- key_claims: its main claims, each with claim (in your words), evidence_in_artifact (its own words, copied exactly, at most 300 characters) and grade_hint: measured (a result it reports measuring), reported (a figure or finding it takes from another source), inferred (its own reasoning or design) or unverified (a claim it gives as unconfirmed, or with no support at all).
- numbers: the figures that carry its claims, at most 80, the strongest first, each with value and unit as written, n (the sample it is over) and interval where the page gives them, else null, context (what it measures, over what) and quote (the page's own words, copied exactly, at most 300 characters).
- datasets, tools and models: each named as the page names it.
- decisions: each with date (or null), decision, owner by role (or null) and reason (or null).
- open_questions; unsourced_assertions (claims it gives with no source); stale_markers (statements that hold only at a date, a pending result, a placeholder still to be filled); external_sources, each with title and url_or_citation (a citation, a DOI or the address of a published page); cross_references (the issues, pull requests, repositories, documents and pages it names); people_by_role (each person it names, by role and never by name).
- confidentiality: schwingnet-method where it says what a stage of SchwingNet's method does; schwingnet-other where it gives SchwingNet's data, results, plans or operations and no method; symphony-beethoven where it is about Symphony or Beethoven; personal-outreach for outreach, events and personal pages; mixed where it carries more than one of these, saying which in notes.
- related_titles: other artifacts it bears on, by the titles the cluster files give them, each with relation (same-topic, builds-on, contradicts, supersedes or superseded-by) and why.
- coverage_notes: what you read and how: the files and their sizes as the folder shows them, what the text left out and where you read it instead, what you could not read, and what you withheld under the rules. notes: anything else a planner needs to read the record right.

Then changed: for an artifact read before, two to four sentences on what the change altered in what the record says, naming the claims and figures it moved; null for a new one. And cluster: for a new artifact, the name of the one cluster in clusters whose description it fits best, copied exactly, with cluster_why saying why in one or two sentences; for one read before, its cluster as the input names it, with cluster_why saying whether it still fits.

Last, as the finder. Read the file of the artifact's cluster (cluster_file, or for a new artifact the file of the cluster you chose), search the numbers table, and look for where this artifact and another one say different things. Read each other artifact you name at its own text, and quote it from there. Give nothing the listed rows already give; for an artifact read before, look first at what the change brought. A skeptic will read each candidate, so give one only where both quotes are in their pages, and an empty list where you find nothing.
- cluster_candidates, at most ${max_cluster}, the ones that matter most to a planner first: kind contradiction (two artifacts state the same thing differently), stale (a statement a later source shows no longer holds), unsourced-shared-claim (a claim two artifacts make with no source on either page), superseded-number (a figure a later page revises) or duplicate-text (one text or figure carried by both, which is one claim and not two); each with topic, artifact_a and artifact_b (ids as the files give them, one of them this artifact; artifact_b, statement_b and quote_b may be empty for a stale statement whose later source is the artifact's own later section), statement_a and statement_b (what each says, in your words), quote_a and quote_b (each artifact's own words, copied exactly, at most 300 characters) and why_it_matters (what a planner would get wrong).
- number_candidates, at most ${max_number}: the same quantity over the same scope with different values in this artifact and another, kind contradiction, or updated-later where the later page, by its date or because it plainly follows the other, gives a new figure; each with quantity (what is measured, over what, as exactly as both pages allow), artifact_a and artifact_b, value_a and value_b as each page writes them, quote_a and quote_b (each page's own words, copied exactly, at most 300 characters) and why.
- number_other, at most ${max_other}: figures alike in name but over another unit, denominator, subset or interval (kind unit-or-scope-mismatch), or probably not the same thing (kind probably-different-quantities), with the same fields.
