FluoroCap · validation · 15 September 2026

Is this going to work? What the files and the literature actually say

A measured account of the corpus and the level model as they exist this morning, the blockers to a 99% claim ranked by the strength of the evidence, what six sweeps of the prior art settle, and how the problem would be set up from scratch to cover transitional anatomy, hardware, corpectomy and pathology.

01Verdict

The thesis is not crazy. Shape alone identifies the spinal region in 99.3% and the individual vertebra in 85.5% of isolated CT vertebrae (Mohammed 2020, two-stage model), a shape-based rule labelled the thoracolumbar junction correctly in 100% of 1,242 CTs where rib counting managed 97.2% (Schinz 2025), and physics-rendered DRRs have carried dense geometric tasks onto real C-arm frames with a 5-7 point drop (SyntheX, Spine-DART). What your own files prove is much narrower than the morning reports read: one number, 98.7% single-crop on 8,422 held-out cervical and lumbar radiograph crops at 0.17 mm/px with human labels, and nothing else. Every fluoroscopy figure is agreement with a caption on panels no human has read (0 of 17,486), every transitional figure is on CT renders whose cases sit in the training bank, no real image anywhere carries an L6 or T13 label, the level model alone is the weakest term in the decoder on real panels (37.9% on 169), and the v2 model exists only as a validation JSON from weights that were destroyed. The literature contains no synthetic-trained classifier that reaches 95-99% on real fluoroscopy; the classification-shaped precedents drop 9-28 points at the domain boundary, and the one LSTV classifier trained on 3,116 real lateral films with MRI-confirmed truth reaches 76.4%. So the approach is sound as a research direction and unmeasured as a claim. Data is the problem, but the binding constraint is labelled real intraoperative images, not pixels, and the mined figures cannot supply them; they are a test set that has not yet been read.

03The blockers, ranked by evidence

The ordering is by how directly the evidence is measured, strongest first.

Label blockers

1. No real image carries a human-verified level. Every one of the 17,486 manifest rows reads "caption-derived (author-stated level); reader labels pending"; qc_report.json records adjudicated_gold_panels 0; the 120-panel reading set and 65 reading pages are built and unread; the reader sheet has 4,218 empty rows. This blocks the 99% target before anything else does, because 99% is a number and there is currently no way to compute it: the 57.1% is caption agreement on a subset selected by detector failure, and the render figures overlap training. The QC's own arithmetic says 545 reads for ±3% around 85%; bounding 99% within ±0.5% takes about 1,500 verified images per stratum you report on (Wilson), and the primary strata are at least four (lumbosacral normal, lumbosacral LSTV, thoracolumbar, hardware). What removes it is a two-reader read with adjudication, and for the transitional stratum a whole-spine or CT reference, because radiograph readers agree on LSTV type at κ 0.16-0.32 (Hanhivaara 2024). Cost: 545 panels is two readers for a working day or two at a minute a panel; 6,000 reads is weeks of reader time, and reads of 320-px panels inherit the radiograph LSTV error.

2. Caption labels carry 5-10% counting-convention noise before anyone misreads a pixel. Numerical variants (23 or 25 presacral segments) occur in 7.7% of whole-spine MRIs and are invisible without whole-spine imaging (Tins 2016); 8.8% of 3,147 full-length radiographs had a non-standard count; outpatient level counts were wrong in 54% of the 71 LSTV patients among 550 lumbar surgeries; radiologists number by the iliolumbar ligament, which marks the lowest lumbar-type vertebra and "does not always represent L5" (Carrino 2011); AP radiographs mistype 35.2% of Castellvi cases against CT. The caption's "L4" was, in most figures, assigned by the author counting on a limited field. A 99% target measured against labels wrong in one case in twelve is uninterpretable, and the error concentrates in exactly the transitional stratum the project exists for. What removes it is labels from a source that does not count on the image: 3D-2D registration to the patient's own CT (100% on 398 intraoperative radiographs, De Silva 2016) or a whole-spine reference; and a written label convention (count from C2 with the 25th segment as S1 for training truth, with the morphological "last mobile" name reported alongside at inference). Cost in images: none; it is a decision and a spec.

3. Transitional anatomy has no real positive and thin synthetic support. L6 and T13 are 0 in every real ledger on disk; the caption search found 22 and 20 unique figures of which about half are flagged animal and several more are animal on reading (a cat, two Beagles, a calf, a heifer, a hamster), leaving at most seven possibly-human figures nobody has opened. The render side has 802 L6 crops from at most 90 distinct cases and 44 T13 crops, giving 46.4% on L6 (40.1% called L5) and 10.2% on T13 (59.2% called T12); the memory that 12× duplication did not move L6 says distinct cases, not crops, are the limit, and CTSpine1K's own baseline scored L6 Dice 0 on 28 transitional cases. VerSe20 holds 85 L6 scans, 6 T13 and 90 stump-rib scans, but the local VerSeFusion copy holds about 50 L6 scans and the fhofmann relabel of 1,460 TotalSegmentator and VerSe CTs with explicit T13/L6 classes has not been touched. This blocks 99% arithmetically: LSTV prevalence averages 15.3% across 83 studies, count-changing types (Castellvi III/IV) are about 16% of LSTV and numerical variants another 7.7%, so roughly one patient in ten has a count the caption or the counter gets wrong; at 46% on those the ceiling is about 94% before any other error. What removes it: raw VerSe plus fhofmann plus the CTSP1K 18 L6 and 33 Castellvi cases rendered at up to 12 poses each gives about 150 distinct L6 sources within a week of conversion; real positives then come only from an intraoperative pull enriched for LSTV, which is 12.9% of lumbar surgeries, so roughly 100-130 LSTV patients per 1,000 lumbar cases.

4. Hardware supervision is entirely synthetic while the test set is 57% instrumented. The CT sources contain one spinal implant (CTSP1K's L5-L6 cage); CTSpine1K excluded metal, RSNA-CSF excluded prior surgery, TotalSegmentator does not count implants, VinDr's 536 implant boxes carry no level, and no public dataset labels corpectomy, cages or prior fusion at the vertebra level (a Europe PMC query for corpectomy plus deep learning returned 179 hits, none a dataset). In FluoroCap the hardware panels fill the largest failure groups ("no detection | hardware" 171, "limited FOV | hardware" 118), the implant detector's only hand-check gave 33% recall and 28% false positives on a strip of unrecorded size, and the caption-free split is 55.1% with hardware against 62.9% without. The literature's measured gaps are 93% to 83% for screw segmentation (Esfandiari 2018), 8 of 70 registration failures from implant mismatch (Lo 2015), and hardware accumulation named as a failure cause when a synthetic-only frame classifier fell 26-28 points on a real instrumented cadaver (Pelphix). Hardware occludes the pedicle silhouette and end-plate the identifier reads, and instrumented cases are the level-confirmation cases. What removes it is real instrumented frames with registration labels: every fusion patient has a post-operative CT, so post-instrumentation captures can be labelled by the same route; a cadaver instrumented progressively adds 60-360 registration-labelled frames per specimen at $1,000-3,500 per specimen (1998-2015 prices; no current figure found). Synthetic corpectomy, cage and plate templates remain the interim.

Distribution blockers

5. The thoracic spine has zero real training images and is the highest-risk region. The v2 bank holds no real-film crops at T2-T11 (T1 56, T12 382); v1 scores 43-58% on normal renders at T3-T9; shape alone in 3D is most confused at T5-T9 (Mohammed 2020, thoracic 60.9% direct, 76.9% two-stage); caption-free recall on real panels is 8.5% at T2 and 0.0% at T4 (n 15); wrong-level surgery runs 0.42% thoracic against 0.13% lumbar in 34,544 cases, and every wrong-level review names the thoracic spine as the hardest region. An overall 99% averaged across regions will hide a thoracic figure below 60. What removes it is real thoracic frames from the registration route (thoracic was LevelCheck's most-helpful region, 23 of 30) and a decision on whether the thoracic claim is a separate, weaker one. Cost: hundreds of thoracic cases with CT.

6. The mined figures are a test set, not a training set, and the evidence supports saying so plainly. Their labels are captions; two-thirds carry burned-in text and 3.6% a printed level label, which is a leak a network will learn; half of PMC figures are compound and the best published sub-figure detector reaches 36.9% mAP on real compound figures, so panel boundaries are themselves noisy; 44% of manifest rows have unknown modality and expert readers agree on the projection of a PMC X-ray figure only at κ 0.557 (ROCOv2); 11,446 images sit in near-duplicate groups; and the chest-radiograph literature shows low-resolution training pushes models onto spurious features. The YouTube route turned 541,621 frame records into 1,462 panels capped at 720p, and Instagram's 1,633 images have no labels at all. Training a level head on this teaches arrows and captions; evaluating on it requires reading. What removes it is stopping the use of these panels for level training, reading a stratified sample, and keeping the corpus as an external test. Cost: reader time (blocker 1) and nothing else.

7. Every render evaluation overlaps training and no learning curve exists. The 92.7% diag is on training cases; L6 100% is 13 training crops; sacrum is 100% on renders and 11.9% caption-free on real panels; the model scores 50-62% on its own cone-beam renders; the films-only model drops to 35.7% on renders, which is the direction of the gap you should expect in reverse. Outside the project, no paper tests a DRR-trained vertebra-level classifier on real fluoroscopy; the nearest discrete-label precedents lose 26-28 points (Pelphix) and 9 points (SyntheX's lesion task, which is segmentation accuracy, not classification: SyntheX has no classification task), and Jecklin 2024 needed 3,317 pose-paired real frames from 10 cadavers to lift lumbar reconstruction from a surface score of 0.08 back to 0.63-0.73, still below its 0.79 DRR ceiling, with no intermediate sizes tested. Nothing in the current files allows an estimate of real-domain accuracy. What removes it is a split filter in diag_level_model.py and eval_transitional.py (a day) and the real held-out set (blocker 1).

8. Field of view and anchors. Boxes were found on 17.3% of known panels; 766 of 3,294 decoded panels have none; "no anchor" is in the top failure groups; the Castellvi and rib cues are out of the field on a collimated lumbar shot, and magnified 13-17 cm intensifier modes put one to three vertebrae in frame at a scale the renderer never draws (det_mm 230-430). What removes it is a renderer that samples collimation and magnification modes and a detector retrained on the resulting crops; cost is renderer work and a re-render.

Resolution blockers

9. Pixels per vertebra. The median panel has a 320-px short side and a 55-px lumbar vertebra, about 0.55 mm/px: end-plate ~85 px, disc ~19 px, pedicle 13-22 px, a 2-3 mm facet joint 4-5 px; at the p10 panel the pedicle is 5-9 px and the disc 7 px. The model upsamples 1.75× at the median and 4.6× at p10 to reach its 96-px target, which adds no edges, and the DRRs it trains on are 2.6× sharper per vertebra than the median panel, a sharpness gap the scale normalisation does not remove. The 98.7% was obtained on NHANES films at 0.174 mm/px and 12 bits, 1.8× the linear resolution of the median panel. The nearest musculoskeletal analogue lost 20 points when the source radiograph was 400 px wide even though the network input was 240 px, and blur cost more than pixel count (scaphoid, JMIR 2026); the chest literature shows the internal-to-external gap widening from 5-11% at 1,024 px to 24-26% at 64 px; the one LSTV classifier at 224 px reached 62% specificity on 3,116 films. Native C-arm frames are 1.5k-2k px on a flat panel and 1k on an intensifier, five to six times the panel. Fine morphology (pedicle silhouette, facet orientation, transverse-process-to-sacrum relation, a 38-mm stump rib) is at or below the floor in the lower half of the panel distribution. What removes it, in order of completeness: native frames (the Tier 0 route below), which is the only full fix; finishing the publisher refetch, which is the only measured lever on the existing corpus and already gives 2.5× linear on 53.7% of figures, moving the median vertebra to about 140 px, the DRR regime, with the 1,343 misses concentrated in Medknow (10.4103) and Wolters Kluwer (10.1097) needing proxy access; and rendering the DRRs down to the measured 21/55/138 px-per-vertebra distribution with multi-scale and blur augmentation (calcaneus precedent, AUC 0.938 held across 128-900 px). Super-resolution is not a fix: no controlled radiograph study shows it restoring task-relevant detail, and the one positive chest report is 400 images with an unclear baseline. Cost: refetch, days plus proxy credentials; renderer changes, days; native frames, blocker 1's route.

10. Bit depth, windowing and intensifier physics. Panel bit depth was not measured; every panel is an author-chosen 8-bit window of a 12-16-bit frame, and the resolution sweep records the modality classifier rejecting most real C-arm figures until dark-frame positives were added. Image-intensifier chains, still in service in 2023-2024 series, carry pincushion and S-distortion and vignetting the renderer does not model. DICOM exports arrive with Bits Stored 8-16 and LIN, LOG or DISP intensity. What removes it: window randomisation and an intensifier distortion model in the renderer, and a loader that normalises on Bits Stored and Pixel Intensity Relationship. Cost: days.

04What the prior art says

Datasets. There is no public real intraoperative spine fluoroscopy set with vertebra-level labels. The only open real C-arm bone set, DeepFluoro (github.com), is 366 registration-labelled images of six cadaver pelves with "vertebrae" as a single class under CC BY-NC; the corpora closest to your recipe, 1,017 paired Cios Spin images from 13 cadavers in "Domain adaptation strategies for 3D reconstruction of the lumbar spine using real fluoroscopy data" (arxiv.org) and 50 AP plus 50 lateral cadaver Long-Film images in "Multi-perspective region-based CNNs for vertebrae labeling in intraoperative long-length images" (doi.org), were never released; Veriserum (arxiv.org) is ~110,000 real dual-plane frames of knee-implant phantoms with no anatomy; SpineDepth is RGB-D. Real films with native level labels are NHANES II (17,075 lateral films, 9,667 cervical and 7,408 lumbar, 12-bit at 146 dpi; CDC codebook wwwn.cdc.gov; the dpi and scanner come from the LHNCBC paper, not the codebook), of which about 544 carry NLM landmarks for C3-C7 and L1-L5 only and the grid holds 364 annotated films with 3,053 contours; CSXA (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), 4,963 lateral cervical films with C2-C7 corners and no C1 or T1; and BUU-LSPINE (mdpi.com), 3,600 patients whose levels are counting-derived edge lines, with LSTV codes 20/21 in the full set (15 lumbarization, 129 sacralization by the paper's Table 6) but not in the 2,000-pair extended set on the grid, which is labelled for flexion, extension, rotation and side-bending (correction to the earlier reading of its report workbook). VinDr-SpineXR (physionet.org) has 536 implant, 337 collapse and 12 fracture boxes on 10,466 films, no levels, under PhysioNet restricted access (a signed DUA, not credentialing). On CT, VerSe20 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) is the only public set with an L6 class: 85 six-lumbar scans, 6 thirteen-thoracic, 18 cervical ribs, 90 stump ribs, and Castellvi rows that sum to 255 series rather than the paper's quoted 161; its 95.6% identification and 91.7% Dice belong to the 2020 challenge, not 2019; CTSpine1K (arxiv.org) has 28 transitional cases, excluded metal and scored L6 Dice 0; TotalSegmentator (zenodo.org) has no L6 or T13 class, so any variant spine in it is mislabelled by construction and its v3.0.0 "fixed vertebrae label mixups" may have changed masks you already hold; the fhofmann relabel (huggingface.co) gives T1-T13, L1-L6 and sacrum bodies on 1,460 of those scans. The largest LSTV-labelled radiograph cohort, 3,116 films, is private. What this implies here: validation on real C-arm images has to be built in-house, the film sets on hand are the only real labelled sources and stop at C3-C7 and L1-L5, and the public L6 pool is VerSe plus fhofmann plus your own 18, not the 50 in the local conversion.

Methods. The best accuracy on real intraoperative images belongs to CT-to-image registration: LevelCheck placed labels within PDE < 30 mm on all 398 intraoperative mobile radiographs from 178 patients with a preoperative CT ("Utility of the LevelCheck algorithm for decision support in vertebral localization", pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov; the paper's own exclusion arithmetic and per-region percentages do not reconcile, and 1 of 398 and 24 of 398 labels were flagged questionable by two readers), 100% of 10,000 trials on 20 patients with 8 of the 70 failures at fewer multistarts due to implant mismatch (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), 99.993% on 36 cadaver C-arm frames (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and in its translation study surgeons' unaided median error on 62 hard radiographs was 30.4%, falling to 2.4% with the overlay (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Its documented failure is the one you care about: the single miss in 50,000 simulated trials was a one-vertebra displacement from the spine's periodicity (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). For learned, CT-free labelling on a real intraoperative modality the best found is 88.3% on 100 cadaver Long-Film images from a network trained only on images synthesised from 297 CTs (Huang 2022, above; 88.9% with and 91.2% without instrumentation per the abstract; the Faster R-CNN delta is not verifiable). Direct evidence for the local-identity thesis: a 3D CNN on isolated VerSe vertebrae ("Morphology-Based Individual Vertebrae Classification", inria.hal.science) identified the region at 99.3% and the level at 71.07% in one stage (thoracic 60.91%) or 85.47% in two stages (cervical 84.68, thoracic 76.92, lumbar ~86), with confusions confined to neighbours and worst at T5-T9; a vertebral-shape rule labelled the thoracolumbar junction in agreement with nerve morphology in 100% of 1,242 CTs against 97.2% for rib counting (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Counting-based labellers fail as a block: Windsor's whole-spine MRI method (ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org) errs mostly by taking S2 for S1 so every label shifts by one; VERIDAH (arxiv.org) reaches 99.18% all-correct on CT with anomaly detection at 96-97% by combining per-vertebra heads with weighted sequence prediction. On radiographs the field is thin and excludes variants: whole-spine landmarking on 2,210 and 1,707 films dropped every spine without 25 vertebrae and reported T7 as hard; the only L5 detector used 150 AP films; the one LSTV classifier on 3,116 lateral films reached 76.4% accuracy and AUC 0.84 against a specialist's 83.9% (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and radiography classifies Castellvi type at 32% sensitivity with κ 0.16-0.32 against CT's 76% and 0.63-0.71 (journals.sagepub.com). The anatomy sets the stakes: one in five of 750 whole-spine MRI outpatients lacked five lumbar-type vertebrae (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), every hypoplastic 12th rib in 551 whole-spine CTs fell in the sacralization group and every lumbar rib in the lumbarization group (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and 54% of outpatient counts were wrong in LSTV surgical patients (jstage.jst.go.jp). What this implies here: the bar for a CT-free method on a real intraoperative modality is 88% on uninstrumented cadavers, shape alone tops out in the mid-80s in 3D at native resolution with the thoracic spine worst, and the human baseline you replace errs 30% of the time on hard images; 99% has no precedent from any direction.

Synthetic-to-real. Physics matters more than volume at the start: the same landmark network went from 78.2 mm to 24.7 mm mean error on 78 real cadaver X-rays when naive DRRs were replaced by DeepDRRs, most of the gain arriving before scatter was added ("Enabling machine learning in X-ray-based procedures via realistic simulation of image formation", pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Dense geometric tasks transfer: SyntheX's hip landmarks and segmentation trained only on DeepDRRs from 20 CTs matched or beat the same network trained on 366 real cadaver frames (5.95 ± 3.52 vs 6.46 ± 8.21 mm; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), with the corrections that the scaled-up set is reported as both 10,000 and 18,000 images, that it beat the real-trained model under regular augmentation too so scale rather than the strong-randomisation list is what carried the win, and that the scaling curve is not monotone; Spine-DART's lumbar Mask R-CNN trained on >150,000 DRRs from 377 CTs held Dice 0.82 on 35 real fluoroscopy images of one cadaver against 0.89 on DRRs, a 7-point drop, not the 5 first reported (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); FluoroSAM's 2.95 million synthetic images from 1,621 CTs gave Dice 0.60-0.77 on 1,741 real masks, the same as on synthetic, so volume alone does not raise the ceiling (arxiv.org). Discrete labels do not transfer: SyntheX's only non-geometric task, COVID lesion segmentation scored as pixel accuracy, fell from 94.05% to 85.22% (F1 73.5 to 54.7), and it has no classification task at all; Pelphix's frame classifier trained on 229,488-279,709 simulated images fell from 93.8-99.2% on simulation to 67.6-71.7% on 257 real cadaver frames depending on the version read (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), hardware accumulation named as a cause; pedicle-screw segmentation fell 93% to 83% (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); a DeepDRR-trained pelvis landmark detector went from 5.6 mm synthetic to 12-25 mm real with tools in the field an explicit failure (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Real data closed gaps only when task-matched and in the thousands: 140 pose-paired real spine X-rays cut registration failure from 16.07% to 11.20% (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov; the paper reports no synthetic-domain number, so the size of the gap is unmeasured); 240 real frames lifted catheter Dice from ~0.1 to ~0.55 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); 3,317 pose-paired frames from 10 cadavers took lumbar reconstruction from 0.08 to 0.63-0.73 against a 0.79 DRR ceiling (Jecklin, above); 565k synthetic chest images let fewer than 10k real match a 66k-real baseline at AUROC 0.80 (arxiv.org); synthetic pretraining halved the real cervical films a landmark model needed across n = 100-700 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Translation helped only when a downstream number was measured: CycleGAN real-to-DRR lifted knee-bone Dice from 0.742 to 0.906 (arxiv.org), TD-GAN took a DRR-trained segmenter from failure to 85% against 88% supervised (arxiv.org), and 2D diffusion X-rays matched real-trained landmark error where plain DRRs from the same CTs did not (4.40 vs 4.89 vs 7.83 mm; arxiv.org); papers reporting PSNR or SSIM alone never showed a gain. What this implies here: keep the physics renderer and strong randomisation, expect the identifier (a discrete label) to lose 10-28 points at the real boundary, plan on low thousands of device-matched, label-exact real frames to recover part of that, and note that no paper has ever tested caption-labelled figure panels as the real data that closes a gap.

Edge cases. LSTV prevalence averages 15.3% across 83 studies (range 4.5-35.6%; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) and is 28.6% in 3,855 abdominal CTs when Castellvi type I is counted, of which 68% are type I with no fusion and an unchanged count and only 16% are types III/IV, with the authors numbering from the last rib and calling that a limitation (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); on 1,340 whole-spine CTs a thoracolumbar transitional vertebra was present in 11.2%, an abnormal rib count in 8.4%, and the two were associated at OR 117 (anesth-pain-med.org); numerical variants outnumber morphological LSTV 2.3 to 1 and are invisible on limited fields (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); in-house, rib length does not separate the 16 lumbar ribs (median 45 mm) from the 98 stump 12th ribs (38 mm), and a 648-subject CT cohort found a stump rib in 20.5% (arxiv.org). Wrong-level surgery in 34,544 consecutive cases was 0.12%, thoracic 0.42%, and every event occurred at L4/5 or L5/S1 with 18.8% attributed to image misinterpretation (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); a 2026 systematic review found nine conventional-method studies at 100% level accuracy and no transitional-vertebra study reaching it (link.springer.com). Hardware has no level-labelled public dataset in any modality; corpectomy has no dataset and no detection paper, its appearance documented only in clinical series with 24-54% cage subsidence (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); fracture at the vertebra level exists only on CT (RSNA-CSF, 3,112 studies, 87 segmented, non-commercial, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov; VerSe19 Genant grades on 1,725 vertebrae, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); metastases on 55 CTs with 183 involved vertebrae (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); infection has no public set; scoliosis films (AASCE, 609 AP) use a fixed 17-vertebra schema that cannot express a variant. What this implies here: state which Castellvi grades the LSTV label includes, expect thoracolumbar and lumbosacral variants in the same patient, allow the identifier to output "rib-bearing border, ambiguous" rather than force T12 versus L1, make the L4/5-L5/S1 field the primary endpoint, and declare corpectomy and infection untested unless the real pull supplies them.

Acquisition. Single-institution intraoperative C-arm studies are small (43 scoliosis patients over six years, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov; 62 intraoperative radiographs from 2,314 participants across five hospitals, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and at a university centre 90 of 190 consecutive cases had intraoperative images archived only on film and absent from PACS (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). The two routes that scale real labelled images are registration and CBCT projections: LevelCheck's 398 radiographs from 178 CTs (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and Miao et al., who built 26,950 real X-ray/DRR pairs from 77 intraoperative CBCT datasets at about 350 projections each and tested on 28 clinical spine cases with opaque metal (cdn.aaai.org), noting the projections' low SNR and faint spine; the Cios Spin acquires up to 400 projections per 30-s scan into a 512³ volume, with every DICOM service listed as an option (brochure; Siemens' K181550 summary gives 1952×1952 pixels, 16-bit, 152 µm pitch, DQE 72%). Native frames vary 1.5k-3k across Ziehm detector generations and the OEC Elite CFD figures rest on a search summary only (reported, not verified). De-identification cannot rely on headers: Burned In Annotation (0028,0301) is Type 3 and may be absent (dicom.innolitics.com), the shipped CTP pixel script has no XA/RF or C-arm signatures, MIDI-B contained no XA/RF, and the only validated pipeline covering RF (1,658 images) and XA (1,899) is the JIIM 2024 method with 10 false negatives among 12,578 burned-in-text images over 415,182 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), 1-2 s per image on one GPU; TCIA still inspects every image by eye. Cadavers give 35-366 registration-labelled frames each (DeepFluoro, Pelphix, Spine-DART) at $175-3,534 per specimen in 1998-2015 dollars (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). What this implies here: the cheapest 10,000 real labelled intraoperative images are raw projections from 25-30 3D C-arm scans if they can be exported (not verified), and the next cheapest is a retrospective PACS pull for patients with a CT, labelled by registration, with pixel OCR de-identification; both produce labels that are exact by construction rather than counted by an author.

Resolution. Flat-panel mobile C-arms carry 0.151 mm (CMOS) or 0.194 mm (a-Si) pitch over ~30 cm, i.e. 1,536-1,954 px (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and the intensifier installed base is 1k×1k over 23-31 cm with 1.6-3.5 lp/mm minimum central resolution (OEC 9900 sheet, chesapeakemedicalsystems.com); the median FluoroCap panel is 3-6× coarser linearly. Chest-radiograph classification saturates at 256-448 px for coarse findings but nodule AUC at 64 px is 80.7% of its 320-px value (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), fracture peaks at 512-1,024 and pneumothorax at 2,048 on MIMIC (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), and the internal-to-external gap shrinks from 24-26% at 64 px to 5-11% at 1,024 with 512 recommended (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); the scaphoid model lost 20 points at a 400-px source and 5-16 under compression with blur worst (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); detection mAP is flat down to JPEG q40 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), so the q92-95 PMC figures are not compression-limited. PMC figures are pretraining-grade: median 709×476 px across 24 million pairs (arxiv.org), half compound, best sub-figure splitting 36.9% mAP on real figures (arxiv.org), BiomedCLIP found 224-px input best because larger input hurt (arxiv.org), and expert view labels on PMC X-rays agree at κ 0.557 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). AP radiographs at native resolution mistype 35.2% of LSTV against CT (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Super-resolution helped segmentation boundaries on CT and not classification (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). What this implies here: the median panel resolves body shape, end-plate contour, disc height and pedicle silhouette; facet orientation, the transverse-process-to-sacrum relationship and a stump 12th rib sit at or below the floor in the lower half, so local morphology is plausible for the upper half of the corpus and transitional typing must come from anchors or a better image, and the publisher originals are the only measured lever short of native frames.

05If I started from scratch

Target. One C-arm frame, AP or lateral, any collimation, intensifier or flat panel, 1-30 pulses per second, no preoperative CT. For each visible vertebra the system returns a level under a written convention, a calibrated confidence, and an explicit abstain state for "rib-bearing border, ambiguous" and "count-changing variant suspected". Training truth counts from C2 with the 8th segment as T1, the 20th as L1 and the 25th as S1, the convention of the whole-spine CT literature; at inference the morphological name (last mobile vertebra) is reported beside the enumerated one and disagreement is flagged, because surgeons act on the former and MRI reports carry the latter. The primary endpoint is per-vertebra exact accuracy in the L3-S1 and T10-L2 fields, stratified LSTV versus normal and instrumented versus not, each with a 95% interval; thoracic T2-T9 and cervical are secondary claims with their own numbers. A 99% claim requires the lower interval bound above 98% in every primary stratum, which means about 1,500 verified vertebrae per stratum.

Data plan.

Tier 0, the core: real intraoperative frames with labels from the patient's own CT. Two sources. First, a retrospective PACS pull of intraoperative C-arm captures (XA, RF or SC objects; the vendor's object type is not yet determined) for spine patients who also have a pre- or post-operative CT, labelled by 3D-2D registration on the LevelCheck pattern (398 radiographs from 178 CTs, all within PDE < 30 mm; cadaver C-arm 99.993%; patient C-arm n = 4 at 0.36 mm). Target 3,000-5,000 frames from 800-1,500 patients in twelve months at one centre, planning for about half the cases to be missing from PACS as at Greifswald; enrich for LSTV (12.9% of lumbar surgeries gives 100-190 LSTV patients per 1,000), take post-instrumentation captures from every fusion case so hardware arrives labelled for free, and register corpectomy cases against their post-operative CT so the cage is labelled too. Second, raw projections from intraoperative 3D C-arm scans: 25-30 scans at 350-400 projections each give about 10,000 real frames whose geometry to the reconstruction is exact, labelled by segmenting the volume (TotalSegmentator plus manual check, as Spine-DART did) and projecting, on the Miao precedent of 26,950 real pairs from 77 CBCTs. This route is gated on whether the machine exports projections, which is not verified, and its frames are low-SNR orbital views. De-identify with pixel OCR on every frame (the JIIM 2024 pipeline: SOP-class filtering, keras-ocr, 0.08% false negatives), because the header flag is optional and CTP ships no C-arm signatures, and audit every released frame by eye.

Tier 1, physics renders with variants and synthetic hardware. Sources with the counts they actually hold: CTSP1K 802 (18 L6, 16 lumbar ribs, 33 Castellvi-graded, S1 carved); raw VerSe in VerSe ids (85 L6, 6 T13, 90 stump ribs, 18 cervical ribs); the fhofmann relabel (T13 and L6 bodies on 1,460 scans, also an independent check of your labels on shared volumes); VerSe19 Genant grades (1,725 vertebrae, 91 fractured patients); RSNA-CSF's 87 segmented cervical studies for fracture, under its non-commercial terms; Spine-Mets-CT-SEG (55 CTs, 183 involved vertebrae) for lytic and blastic bodies; CTPelvic1K CLINIC-metal for metal streak in the lumbopelvic field; TotalSegmentator scans only after counting labels per scan; NMDID by application if population-rate variants and in-situ hardware are wanted at scale. Render at DeepDRR fidelity (the 78.2 to 24.7 mm result, most of it before scatter), with the SyntheX strong-randomisation list plus intensifier distortion and vignetting, magnification modes down to 130 mm, collimation, and then downsample and blur to the measured 21/55/138 px-per-vertebra distribution so the network trains at the sharpness it will see. Synthetic hardware: a screw and rod library on the Doerr pattern (30 types), cages, plates, PMMA, and a corpectomy template that removes the body and inserts a cage and plate; budget the 10-28 point gap the literature reports for these until Tier 0 supplies real ones. Volume of 150,000-300,000 frames is routine; distinct cases bound what is learned, as the L6 duplication showed.

Tier 2, real radiographs with native labels, for the film domain and for the resolution ablation: NHANES II (364 annotated films locally, 544 at NLM, lateral only, 0.174 mm/px, 12-bit; 17,075 unlabelled films for pretraining), CSXA (4,963, C3-C7), the full BUU-LSPINE 3,600 by request for its LSTV codes (train-only, levels counting-inferred), the Mendeley 716-film S1-corner set after de-duplicating its 508 BUU films, VinDr's 536 implant boxes for a hardware head only, and AASCE's 609 AP scoliosis films as a coronal-deformity robustness test with its 17-vertebra schema treated as counting-derived.

Tier 3, cadaver: two to four specimens with CT before and after progressive instrumentation (screws, rods, a cage, a corpectomy cage at known levels), imaged on both an intensifier and a flat-panel C-arm at 1, 3, 7.5 and 15 pulses per second in low and high dose and in magnification modes, labelled by registration on the DeepFluoro pattern (about 60 frames per specimen there, 257 in Pelphix). That is 250-1,500 frames: too few to train on, exactly enough to measure the sim-to-real gap per hardware type and per detector.

Tier 4, the mined figures and video: an external test set only, after a two-reader read of a stratified sample, and a source for the modality and hardware heads; never for the level head.

Evaluation protocol. The primary held-out set is Tier 0 patients never used for fine-tuning, split patient-grouped and LSTV-stratified with the existing splitter, labels from registration, plus every cadaver frame and a two-reader, adjudicated read of at least 545 FluoroCap panels as the literature-domain check. Report per-stratum exact accuracy with Wilson intervals, the within-one-level rate, the abstain rate, and the full confusion matrix with the four shifts named (L5-L6, T12-T13, C7-T1, S1-S2). Pre-register the label convention, the strata and the sample sizes before the first Tier 0 frame is labelled. State the comparators: unaided surgeons at 30.4% error on hard radiographs, LevelCheck at 100% with a CT, Huang at 88.3% on uninstrumented cadavers. Run the resolution ablation once, early: downsample NHANES and CSXA crops to 21, 55 and 138 px per vertebra before the 96-px scaling and plot exact accuracy against pixels per vertebra; that curve tells you, before any acquisition, whether local morphology survives 320 px.

90 days. Weeks 1-2: two readers read the 545-panel set; add a split filter to diag_level_model.py and eval_transitional.py and rerun v1 and the landed v2 with training cases excluded; that yields the first real-image number with an interval and the first honest render number. Weeks 2-4: match render sharpness to the panel distribution, add multi-scale and blur augmentation, and run the resolution ablation. Weeks 3-6: convert raw VerSe and the fhofmann relabel, re-render L6, T13 and stump-rib cases, retrain. Weeks 4-8: audit one year of the institution's spine cases for digital C-arm captures and a CT (the 100/190 precedent says count before promising), submit the retrospective protocol, and test raw-projection export on one 3D C-arm. Weeks 6-12: stand up the OCR de-identification pipeline and validate it on 500 frames with a visual audit; run the refetch misses through the proxy for 10.4103 and 10.1097. Day 90 deliverable: a real-image accuracy with a CI, a pixels-per-vertebra curve, a yes or no on projection export, and a count of pullable cases.

12 months. Months 3-6: Tier 0 collection begins; the first 1,000 frames are labelled by registration; one cadaver session with progressive instrumentation on both detector types. Months 6-9: v3 trained on Tiers 1 and 2, fine-tuned on the Tier 0 training split; the cadaver frames give the per-hardware, per-detector gap. Months 9-12: held-out evaluation at 1,500 vertebrae per primary stratum, the FluoroCap external check, the decision on whether thoracic becomes a separate weaker claim, and the write-up with the abstain rate in the abstract.

Stop doing. Training or fine-tuning any level head on caption-derived labels. Quoting render accuracy without a split filter. Scaling the YouTube and Instagram harvests, which turned 541,621 frame records into 1,462 panels capped at 720p and 1,633 unlabelled images. Counting label tokens as images. Carrying NHANES twice in the bank as nhanes_drr. Duplicating L6 crops. Treating figure-mate agreement or caption-free containment as accuracy. Expecting the caption search to yield transitional positives from 22 figures half of which are dogs, cats and cattle. Budgeting super-resolution as a resolution fix.

06What to keep from the current pipeline

The context-ladder crop design with the constant 96-px target and target-marker channel: it is the right way to give a local classifier a fixed scale, and it is what produced the one held-out number that exists. The physics DRR renderer with cone-beam geometry, the det_mm and SOD draws and the implant scheme: it is at DeepDRR fidelity already and needs extension (intensifier distortion, magnification, downsampling), not replacement. The augmentation tiers, which already overlap most of the SyntheX strong list. The CT label infrastructure: labels_for(volume) scheme detection, the S1 carve, the lumbar-rib labels 60/61 with the T13-versus-lumbar-rib distinction, the Castellvi consensus with its recorded disagreements, transition_morphometrics.csv and the level atlas whose 30 mm body height converts panel pixels to millimetres. The patient-grouped, LSTV-stratified splitter. The rare-anatomy render pool and rare_cases_summary.json. The caption and hardware-constraint parser, kept as triage and QC rather than as a label source. The FluoroCap manifest with its honest ground_truth_status field, the built reading pages, the dedup groups and the refetch pipeline, which together make the corpus a usable external test set once read. The modality and view heads (93.1% and 99.5% on held-out films). The transitional evaluation strata script, once it filters by split. The QC's discipline of writing "adjudicated_gold_panels 0" and "545 reads for ±3%" into the report rather than around it. The model registry, so the next destroyed weights are recoverable.

07Open questions the evidence does not settle

Whether raw 3D projections can be exported from any C-arm you have access to; Miao's 26,950 pairs came through a vendor collaboration and the Cios Spin's DICOM services are options. Whether local morphology in 2D carries L6 identity at all: the only real-film LSTV classifier reached 62% specificity at 224 px with 3,116 MRI-confirmed films, and no paper has separated resolution from task difficulty for that question; the pixels-per-vertebra ablation is the first thing that would tell you. How the sim-to-real gap for a discrete level label scales with real frames, since no learning curve exists for any spine task and the only spine curve is for landmarks with diffusion-generated synthetic data. Which "L5" the labels encode; both conventions appear in the source captions and the answer changes the transitional stratum's accuracy by construction. Whether shape alone can exceed the mid-80s in the thoracic spine when the whole vertebra is visible at native resolution, given 76.9% in 3D at T5-T9. How many distinct L6 cases are enough, since 90 gave 46% and 12× duplication gave nothing. Whether the v2 retrain landed and where it sits. Whether the sacrum's 0 tokens in the caption constraints and 11.9% caption-free recall reflect an S1-versus-sacrum token convention or a real failure. What the panels' bit-depth history is and how many came from intensifier chains. Whether the 51 panel files absent from panels.jsonl are duplicates or losses. Whether TotalSegmentator v3's re-labelling changed the vertebra masks already rendered. And the question none of the sources answer because none of them tried: what a reader sees when shown a 320-px lateral fluoroscopy panel of L4-S1 and asked to name the level without a caption; until that is measured, the human baseline for this exact input is unknown.

08How this page was produced

Fifteen agents in one run on 15 September 2026, 06:55 to 07:35 grid time. Two measured the corpus and the models read-only from the named files on the grid and in WSL. Six swept the literature, one dimension each: datasets, methods, synthetic-to-real transfer, edge cases, acquisition routes, resolution. Every load-bearing claim from those sweeps (48 in all) was handed to a separate agent told to refute it against the cited source: 36 were confirmed, 12 held in substance with a number or attribution corrected, none were refuted; the corrections are written into the text above, and claims whose source could not be opened are marked "reported, not verified". A final agent wrote the synthesis from the measurements, the findings and the verdicts, with measurements outranking the morning report. Total: 2.24 million tokens, 1,090 tool calls, 41 minutes. Nothing on this page was projected; where a number was not measured, the text says so.

Sources are named inline: grid and WSL ledgers for every measurement (panels.jsonl, manifest.csv, panel_preds.jsonl, decode_v2_v2.jsonl, refetch_hires.jsonl, level_model_diag_allspine.json, transitional_eval_allspine_r1.md, qc_report.json, allspine_v2_chain.log, the training-bank meta files), and the linked papers for every claim from the literature. Counts are snapshots from 06:55 to 07:35 grid time on 15 September 2026; several ledgers were still being appended. The word "measured" on this page means read from a file by a command that was run.
