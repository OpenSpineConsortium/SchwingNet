The 128 low pelvic incidences were the plate leaning backwards solved

The tilt gate measures the angle from vertical and is blind to the direction of tilt. _endplate orients its normal by the superior component alone, so a plane fitted to the wrong surface returns a posteriorly leaning normal at a perfectly normal angle — and sails through. Rendering it showed what it lands on: where the S1 carve fails, the plane is fitted to the ventral surface of the sacrum. On one record "S1" spanned most of the sacrum and PI read 2.8°.

Both are measured on a body isolated by cutting at the canal wall, and the L5 transverse processes arise in front of that wall. Contamination is confined to L5 and large there: end-plate +2.2 mm with 95 records over 60 mm, pedicle +4.5 mm. Three replacement rules were built, run over all 802, and none beat the one that ships — so the row is dropped and the text says why.

Recumbency raises sacral slope — the manuscript said it lowers it corrected

Stated twice, and backwards. Verified against Hasegawa's own table: supine 34.1° against standing 27.0°. The gap to Veilleux is cohort, not method — his 200 were asymptomatic, and the one matched supine-CT series reports 53.4 / 34.1 / 19.2 against your 52.7 / 33.2 / 19.7.

Figure 5 back to one published CT line reverted

Drawing Hasegawa beside Veilleux gave 24 deformity patients the same visual weight as 200 asymptomatic subjects, with no n on the figure to weigh them by. Table II carries both, where the n is visible.

Rib bases were hardcoded and silently wrong on one label set fixed

Two directories that both look like "the labels" have voxel-identical vertebrae and rib blocks that differ by one. The constant was right for one and wrong for the other — it moved rib12_to_crest_mm in 237 of 802 records, worst 36 mm. Now read from the scheme shipped beside the labels.

BUU ships LSTV ground truth nobody was reading unused

It is in the S1a row's class field on lateral films. 3,461 normal / 15 lumbarization / 130 sacralization — 4.0% prevalence, which is the population rate rather than a referral-enriched one. Published SOTA on radiographs is AUC 0.84, binary, single centre, at 62.3% prevalence. You have subtypes at true prevalence, and a CT cohort stratified on the same thing.

architecture

One backbone, four heads — not one flat class list

Level identity goes on a head, never on the detector decided

A VinDr film has lesion boxes and no corners; a CSXA film has corners and no lesion boxes. In one flat class list every CSXA image teaches "no osteophytes here" — which is false, nobody looked. And because C3 appears only in CSXA and T7 only in AASCE, a detector with level classes learns dataset signature wearing level identity's clothes. A second head reading pooled per-instance features only ever sees the vertebra itself.

Adding CSXA breaks the cervical confound for the first time enabling

Cervical levels are now covered by two independent sources — CSXA and NHANES. That makes the experiment possible at all: train the level head on one, test on the other. Survives the source swap, it is morphology. Collapses, it was signature.

The DRRs are the version of that experiment with no confound left next

6,416 synthetic radiographs where level is known with certainty from the 3D labels — unlimited level supervision, zero annotation, one rendering style. No published work uses DRRs to supply level identity to a 2D model; everyone uses them for landmarks, registration or 3D reconstruction.

A competing group is already on CSXA
