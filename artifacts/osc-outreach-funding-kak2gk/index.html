OSC Outreach Funding

Where to ask for money to run OpenSpineConsortium outreach, ordered by how fast it can actually arrive — not by how much it is. The first seven are local and small; the radiology societies at the end are the opposite, and their window opens next month. Each entry says what it will and won't pay for, and who has to be the one signing.

Check this before you write anything

Are you enrolled this year? Nearly every award below requires a currently enrolled medical student. A research or gap year usually keeps you enrolled — but "usually" is not "verified." Confirm your registration status with the Registrar before you spend an afternoon on an application, because it decides which half of this list you can use.

Two structural facts that shape the whole list. Greg is a resident, so he cannot be the applicant on any student award — that is why you are applying and he is not. And OpenSpineConsortium is not a Registered Student Organization, which several of these require. The fastest way around that is to have an existing registered organization host the event with OSC as the program, rather than registering OSC itself.

Why this one first: the amount is almost exactly what is needed, and it is the only source on this list with no committee calendar between the ask and the answer — you send a completed Funding Request Form to a named person.

Why this one: it is the only source here built for events rather than travel. SAFB exists to fund registered organizations running speakers, workshops and seminars on campus — which is what an OSC annotation workshop actually is.

The catch, and it is a real one: the event must be on campus and free and open to the public for its whole duration. A Zoom-based annotation session for enrolled trainees may not qualify at all. If the outreach can be staged as an open on-campus workshop, this is your best fit; if it can't, skip it rather than reshaping the program to chase the money.

Why this one: the largest per-student amount available quickly, and it carries a genuine fast path most people miss — if an abstract is accepted fewer than 60 days before the event, you may submit within three days of the acceptance instead of 60 days ahead. WSU GME Seed Grant

up to $2,500
ApplicantGreg, not you
Cycle2026–27 open, rolling
FundsSupplies, software, printing
BlocksTravel, registration, page fees

Why it's on the list even though you can't apply: it is the one source that will pay for materials — printing, supplies, software, even contracted help. That is a large share of what an outreach program actually spends money on, and nothing else here covers it. Greg applies as the resident; you would be named in the mentoring plan.

The catch: it explicitly prohibits travel, conference registration and publication fees — so it cannot cover the journal's excess-page charge, which was the other reason this search started. It also requires IRB documentation, a faculty mentor's written approval and a mentoring plan, so it is a real application, not a form.

The radiology societies bigger money, slower clock — but the cycle opens next month

Two things gate this whole group

The October window. RSNA's 2026–27 cycles are closed and the next ones open in October 2026, with medical student deadlines around 1 February. That is the single most useful date on this page: it is far enough out to prepare properly and close enough that preparation should start now.

These fund the programme, not the party. Nothing in this group pays for an outreach event the way SAFB does. They fund research experience, or an education project, over a year. That suits OSC — which is a training programme with a published framework and a released dataset — but the application has to be written as that, not as a request for event costs.

RSNA Research Medical Student Grant

$3,000 + match
With dept match$6,000
ApplicantMedical student
OpensOctober 2026
Deadline1 February

Why this is the one to aim at: it is the largest award on this entire page that you can hold in your own name, and it is designed for exactly your situation — a medical student getting a supervised research experience in medical imaging. The $3,000 is matched by the sponsoring department, so the project is funded at $6,000. RSNA also awards up to ten additional grants to students who identify as underrepresented in medicine.

The catch: the departmental match is not automatic — someone has to commit it, so that conversation happens before the application, not after. And this funds research, so OSC goes in as the research programme it is, with the dataset and the published framework as evidence, rather than as outreach.

What to put in every one of them

Most student funding requests describe an intention. This one can describe a record, which is the whole advantage — use these numbers in each application rather than writing a new case each time.

802CT records annotated and openly released
13medical students trained as annotators
1peer-reviewed paper on the education model, in Cureus
2permanent archives: a Zenodo DOI and a public dataset mirror

The published framework paper is Schehr A, Kim J, Schwing G, OpenSpineConsortium: an open-source framework for medical student engagement in computational spine imaging research, Cureus 2026 — doi:10.7759/cureus.112661. Cite it. A reviewer deciding between applications will take a peer-reviewed description of the program over any amount of description you write yourself.
