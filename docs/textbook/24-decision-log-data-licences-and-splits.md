# 24 Decision log: data, licences and splits

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it logs decisions about data, licences and splits, names methods only by what they are for, and leaves how each stage works to Part III.

This chapter is the log of every recorded decision about what data enters training, what stays out, what is held out as a test and what is gated, together with the licence and split decisions that govern them. For each it gives the date, who made it as the record names them, the reason the record gives, its status on 24 October 2026 and what would reverse it, so that a planner can tell a standing rule from one whose reason has lapsed. It answers which sources are in, out, held out or gated and by whose decision; which licence decisions are settled and which wait on a reading; which split and leak decisions hold and which audit would reopen them; and what would reverse each. The sets themselves are [chapter 4](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md) to [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md); the rules for splits, leaks and licences, and what each check found, are [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md); the decisions about evaluation, reporting and operations are [chapter 25](25-decision-log-evaluation-reporting-programme-and-operations.md); and who may act on what is [chapter 23](23-who-may-do-what.md).

## How the log was kept

The 69 records of the artifact inventory carry about 512 decision entries. Some seventy of them concern data, licences or splits, spread over about twenty records from the X-ray handoff of 3 September 2026 to the grid pipeline of 21 October. They consolidate into thirty-four decisions, numbered DL1 to DL34, merged where two records made the same choice for the same reason and kept apart where a later record changed the reason. An undated entry is dated by its artifact's date or, where the repository records when the rule was first made, by that; the date column says which.

Who decided is named by role and only as the record names them. "The owner" means the record quotes or attributes the owner's own words or call; "a session" means a Claude session working for the owner, whose choice stands until the owner overrules it; "the record" means the artifact states the decision with no owner at all, which is common in the registry of 20 October, whose entries are rendered from older files. The plan asked that each decision be confirmed with the owner as his own or a session's recommendation; that confirmation was not available to this chapter, so the column gives the record's attribution and no more.

One settlement sits beside every decision whose recorded reason was a permission, an approval or an agreement. On 14 October 2026 the owner said that he holds permission and has filled out the data use agreements for all of the project's data, VinDr-SpineXR and FluoroCap included, and that neither the IRB scope nor directive D1 is a reason to drop a result ([docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md](../../docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md), [docs/checkpoints/2026-10-14e/memory.md](../../docs/checkpoints/2026-10-14e/memory.md)). Where an older record gives such a reason, this log gives the decision and its date as recorded and sets that settlement beside it. A licence that two records read differently is logged as a question about the record, never as a question about whether the project may use the set.

Status is one of five words. In effect: the decision holds and nothing recorded since has moved it. Done: carried out once and finished. Superseded: a later decision replaced it, named in the row. Lapsed: the decision stands in the record but its stated reason no longer holds, so it stands only if a new reason is written. Overtaken: the record shows practice departing from the decision before a later decision caught up.

## What enters training, and what stays out

### FluoroCap (DL1)

FluoroCap is the clearest case of a decision whose reason is quality, not permission. The [Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) of 20 October carries it as "out of training by directive D1; a test set at most; still inside the old fold banks", and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) of 17 October gives the reason in a number: 72.8 percent of its panels are reproductions of published figures with no pixel residual. The directive is the owner's of 23 September, as the session record of 14 October dates it, and the same record says in so many words that D1 took FluoroCap out of training for quality, not for permission. On 14 October the owner kept it out of future training in his own words ("I don't think fluorocap will help"), while the existing models that still carry FluoroCap crops keep their recorded results and nothing is retrained to remove them. That last clause matters for a planner: the [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) found 2,100 FluoroCap crops per fold bank in the C-ArmLSpine fold models and called it a defect against D1; the owner's word of 14 October settles that those results stand, and the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) (row 7) carry the registry's wording with the fold-bank fact beside it.

The older records reach the same place by other routes. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) of 15 September recommended that no level head train on caption-derived labels and that the mined figures serve as an external test set only, after a two-reader read of a stratified sample, because about two thirds of panels carry printed text and 3.6 percent a printed level label. The superseded [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) of the day before is the only record of the first form of this choice: it said the corpus must not retrain the level model without re-weighting, and dropped re-weighting the same day, a method decision that chapter 16 carries as M16. The [FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/) adds two rules of its own for the corpus as a dataset: its Dataset B takes only licence tiers A and B, so no panel with an unresolved licence enters it, and reader labels replace the detector counts once the reference standard is complete. The two-reader read has not been done, so D1's escape clause has never been exercised.

### VinDr-SpineXR (DL2)

The registry of 20 October says VinDr-SpineXR "is dropped from training; only its implant boxes are used", and gives as its reason that its PhysioNet agreement sat outside the IRB determination HPR 2026-269 with its own determination pending. That reason is stale. The notice of HPR 2026-330, the supplement to 2026-269, came on 29 September 2026 and covers VinDr-SpineXR under its PhysioNet agreement; the repository's memory says so and names the older lines that still call it pending ([docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md](../../docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md)), the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) (row 12) confirm the registry's line should be corrected from that notice, and the owner's word of 14 October settles permission and agreements for all the data. The repository's own record for the set, [data/sources/vindr_spinexr.json](../../data/sources/vindr_spinexr.json), now gives a different account of the exclusion: the v8 and v9 level banks left VinDr out for comparability, with no written criterion, its implant boxes trained the hardware detector, and the C-ArmLSpine fold models carry 2,632 of its crops.

So the decision stands as practice with no live reason. Two records show it was not always the plan: the [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) of 13 September wanted VinDr laterals labelled and admitted as a second train-only lumbar source, and [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) listed all 10,466 VinDr images among its open "use all" items. What forecloses VinDr as a held-out benchmark is a different fact, which the atlas states: its release test split was consumed in building labels, so no test number on it is held out. A planner who wants VinDr in a version's training needs only to write the criterion the record lacks; the permission question is closed.

### BUU-LSPINE (DL3, DL4)

Three decisions govern BUU. The earliest, of 13 September, admitted its counting-inferred level labels as a train-only source, never scored, once the test set held only annotated levels; the ladder reports that admission helped by 2.0 points. The second, of 17 September in [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/), barred all 144 anomalous BUU studies from training and moved them to the test partition, because 124 of them had sat in the training partition of every bank before and BUU's counted names are wrong on those studies by the dataset's own reading. The third is the licence rule, which Research Directions of 8 October words as "no weight trained with BUU laterals leaves until the owner has read the licence" and the registry of 20 October restates. Its reason as recorded is the EULA, which forbids redistribution and requires acknowledgement by name, and the fact that the owner had not read the licence himself. The owner's settlement of 14 October stands beside it. The rule bites only on weights leaving the grid, not on training, and the licence file [data/licences.json](../../data/licences.json) still carries the class the classifier's rule proposed, unknown, with no owner's review entered; an owner's reading entered there is what lifts it.

One BUU item has no decision at all: the 3,600 AP films sit in no bank with no written criterion, which the handoff's "use all of a public dataset" rule (below) counts as an unjustified truncation.

### NHANES II unannotated films (DL5)

The registry says the 16,531 unannotated NHANES films stay unused "by a written criterion", the criterion being that training on machine labels cut anomalous accuracy by about twenty points, which chapter 16 carries as M19. The [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) (row 23) note that the atlas pins a refutation of about nineteen points to the IEEE lateral set's machine labels while the registry pins a twenty-point cut to these films, and rule that the record cannot tell which source and which magnitude go with which experiment. The decision stands either way, since both readings say machine-labelled films hurt. Against it stands Research Directions' list of open "use all" items, which names these films for the detector's own training, and the any-view plan of 21 October, which splits NHANES II by region in the next census run. The record of 21 October adds a reversal route: the VertXNet labels of the same films, found that day and not yet fetched, carry radiologist landmarks with vertebral identity.

### The YouTube re-encodes (DL6)

The registry keeps the YouTube re-encodes for private tracker screening only, never training or a slide, until a request is answered, because the copies are unpermitted. This is the one exclusion in the log whose reason is the absence of a permission from a third party, an uploader, and it is not what the owner's 14 October settlement covers: the letters to uploaders, as drafted on 26 September, say the copies were saved for screening and will be deleted on request. The FluoroCap overnight report of 15 September records the harvest's end: 428,606 raw YouTube frames without level evidence deleted when the disk filled, and the harvest not restarted.

### Spinal-AI2024 (DL12)

Spinal-AI2024 is excluded only from the commercial lineage. The [OpenSpine Board](../../artifacts/openspine-board-t3gfds/) of 11 September advised against downloading it, since a set with no licence file is all rights reserved by default and inherits the terms of its upstream corpus. [Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/) records that the owner made the call to pull it anyway, tagged research-track so the licensing gate keeps it from any commercial lineage, with the status revisit. The atlas adds the split rule, that its 44 synthetic identities, one of them 1,270 times, must sit wholly on the training side, and the registry confirms all of it is on training in G3. For Cobb angles the decisions page adds that synthetic films train and real films validate, never the other way.

## What is excluded, and why

Four sources are out of the holding's use altogether, each for its own reason. SpineSAC (DL10) has no truth: its masks are empty on the sawbone CT, so the [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 22 September ruled its results a sheet to look at, not a number to quote, until a threshold segmentation of the phantom CT gives projected truth; it also states no data licence. Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 (DL11) was excluded by directive D6, which the reproduction manifest cites to a resume note of 22 September as a BUU redistribution; the atlas counts about 500 BUU persons and 12 VinDr studies in it and notes that 71 films that duplicate neither were left out with no written reason. Glocker's spine CT (DL13) is excluded by the acquisition plan's rule 3 for its no-derivatives licence, in [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) of 8 October, which also holds tier 2 of the acquisition back by stopping rule 4 until tier 1's priors are measured and defers LUMOS for want of a fetch route. And the X-ray handoff ruled out three web sets on 3 September as scraped or miscited, one of them an MRI set routinely cited as X-ray.

Two exclusions are of a route, not of a set. Radiopaedia (DL7) is never fetched by script: a session declined to scrape it on 16 September because its content signals forbid AI training and its licence is non-commercial, the atlas session did not fetch its site on 17 October because its terms bar automated access, the owner's instruction in the atlas is not to save frames until approval arrives, and the project's standing rule is the owner's. The [grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/) of 21 October records the licence signed and paid and waiting on Radiopaedia's countersignature, which the datasets contradictions (row 35) carry as the newer state. CT-RATE (DL8, DL9) is held off by size, not terms: access was granted on 21 October, the owner turned the full 21.3 TB fetch off the same day, and his standing rule is that it is never submitted without his number for the space it may take; the ungated re-uploads were deliberately not used, so any fetch is of the gated record.

## What is held out as a test

### The two external film sets of 3 September (DL14, DL15)

The [Spine X-ray Data Handoff](../../artifacts/spine-x-ray-data-handoff-bddbdd/) made two hold-out decisions on 3 September, and the record shows neither held. It named the IEEE DataPort lateral 236 the evaluation hold-out, "annotate it; never train on it", because until it existed every test image came from a source the model had trained on. Two weeks later the v5 bank of Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor carried 212 of those films with machine labels, and the atlas records that the arm using them was measured and refuted. The handoff of 8 October lists the set in no level bank, so the current lineage has not trained on it, but any model descending from the v5 bank has, and under the hold-out rule (DL16) those films are not held out for it. The set still has no annotation, so it is not yet a test set for anything. Reversing the overtaking is simple to state: annotate the 236 and score them only on a lineage that never held the v5 bank, which version 1, rebuilt from its sources, is.

The same handoff kept the 208 Honduran Mendeley films out of training as the only cross-site lateral lumbar test set, and the board of 11 September called them clean for commercial use, to be deduplicated before use as a test set. D6 then excluded the whole Mendeley set on 22 September, and the v5 bank of 17 September held 584 Mendeley films, more than the 508 BUU-derived ones the September pages counted. The record never says the 208 were kept apart, and the datasets contradictions (row 22) rule that neither the registry's "adds no new person" nor the 208 holds without a recount; the film count itself, 714 or 716, is open in the concept ledger. The decision is superseded by D6; the recount that DL11 needs would also say whether a cross-site test set remains in it.

### The test persons of the catalog and the sealed set of the study (DL18, DL19)

For SchwingNet's own versions the held-out persons are named in a file, [data/splits/test-persons.json](../../data/splits/test-persons.json): the three real L6 cases of C-ArmLSpine, the transitional stratum, and the two demo sweeps, which are never trained on. The list grows only by an explicit pull request and, as the [retraining plan](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) of 20 October says, only by a person no training shard has ever held. The [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) of 21 October widens the rule to every model of that plan: C-ArmLSpine's test persons stay out of every training, and a person used to check the counting prior is never used to fit it, because C-ArmLSpine is the test device and its truth must stay gold. For the planned in-house study the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/) sets the same principle for the study team: cases split by patient, never by frame, and a fixed held-out set drawn and sealed before any training, with analysis code frozen before fine-tuning; it includes all three spinal regions and transitional anatomy as subgroups and excludes children and identifiable prisoners. The superseded [Protocol 1 eProtocol draft](../../artifacts/protocol-1-eprotocol-draft-mkmh7p/) of 15 September alone records that the Children's Hospital of Michigan was excluded for now, with a separate protocol to follow if warranted.

## Splits and the hold-out rule

The rules that make a number quotable are the owner's of 23 September, recorded in the handoff of 8 October. The hold-out rule (DL16) holds an item out only if its patient key is in neither the training nor the validation set of the checkpoint or any ancestor, exempts only declared public ImageNet and COCO weights, and counts the unknown as a failure. G3 (DL17), the person-disjoint split applied the same day with a passing leak audit, is frozen and never rerun in place; Research Directions adds that if the second reads of transitional cases move a record, the split changes and the leak audit runs again before anyone is scored. Both rest on the two historical leaks, NHANES II and CTSpinoPelvic1K each entering the banks twice, and the decision that every number of the leaked families is record only (DL21) follows from them; the owner revised that rule on 23 October for this book, so such a number may be quoted as a record, never as a final value. The ninth of the handoff's rules adds that a leaked initialisation is fixed by retraining, never disclosed as a caveat, which is why version 1 rebuilds every shard. The registry's line that it "ends the double counting of NHANES II annotated films in the old fold banks" is that rule applied to the new store.

Patient-disjoint splitting itself (DL20) is older: the X-ray handoff required it on 3 September, splitting by patient id and never by image, because BUU pairs an AP and a lateral per patient, Mendeley contains BUU patients and NHANES participants have two films. The catalog's split (DL18) carries it into SchwingNet as a salted hash by person, so a person added later falls to the same side whoever ingests them. G3 and the catalog's split are different decisions and nothing maps one onto the other, which [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) sets out. The audits that would reopen a split are named in the record: the CTSpinoPelvic1K audit of whether one release id is one person, running on 22 September according to [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) with no outcome recorded; the content-hash checks CHK-6 and CHK-7, never yet run on a real shard; and the person keys still unverified on 8 October for AASCE, CSXA and VinDr. Chapter 16's M18 (the clean encoder on G3 training persons) and M20 (version 1 as a rebase) are the method decisions these split decisions serve.

## Truth pools

Four decisions say which labels may count as truth. The seven CTSpinoPelvic1K records whose L6 call disagrees between the release label, the readers' own count and the registry (DL22) are out of every truth pool until re-read; State of the literature gives the counts as 18, 16 and 29 persons. The one-labeller screen of COBRA (DL23) is not trusted, since it matched reader truth on 10 of 24 L6 and 0 of 52 T13, and the registry keeps only three of its calls. A film with no anomaly ground truth is dropped from a split rather than assumed ordinary (DL24), since reading absence of evidence as absence of anomaly manufactures specificity; the [Rib Anchor Audit](../../artifacts/rib-anchor-audit-t7wr2h/) excludes truncated ribs by rib number on both sides, because excluding them one at a time left 112 asymmetric cases, and excludes its 20 indeterminate cases rather than undercount them; and [Spinal Border Variants](../../artifacts/spinal-border-variants-fye5ef/) keeps its sacral foramina counts as a relative signal, deliberately out of the co-occurrence test. The X-ray handoff's rule against inventing levels belongs here too: where a source gives positions rather than names, as BUU and AASCE do, the record says the level was inferred from position.

## Licences

The licence decisions form a line from a README to code. On 11 September the board and the decisions page made licensing a control in code rather than a sentence (DL25), because nothing in a checkpoint records what it saw; on 23 October SchwingNet's classifier took that over, with one entry per source in [data/licences.json](../../data/licences.json), the strictest class the licence's words support, a recipe that cuts a catalog to the classes a network may use, and a provenance record written beside every run ([docs/memory/training-data-licences.md](../../docs/memory/training-data-licences.md)). Only the owner's reading may loosen a class, and the owner reviewed four entries on 23 October.

The holding report of 8 October decided how a conflict is kept (DL26): six sources carry two readings or none, CTPelvic1K, VerSe, DeepFluoro, the Geneva hip set, Ljubljana and SpineSAC, and the index records both readings and marks redistribution unknown until each is settled against its release page. These are questions about which record is right, and they decide what a derived bank may be redistributed as, not whether the project may use the set. The Lab Recipe of 27 September fixed the two-track rule (DL27), the private-forever list (DL28), CTSpinoPelvic1K's own licences with their inherited share-alike clause, and the five criteria a dataset must meet to sit under the umbrella determination HPR 2026-269, which VinDr's supplement of 29 September answered for the one set that did not; the commercial readings the records leave to counsel, NHANES II's agreement and COBRA's non-commercial terms, belong to the commercial track alone and are [chapter 23](23-who-may-do-what.md)'s. The FluoroCap card's tier rule and the access setup's limit on the closed-journal refetch of 14 September, about 600 figures with matched captions, one request every three seconds per host and never full text in bulk, are the corpus's own licence decisions.

## Where the data lives and who reads it

Three decisions place the data (DL29). The X-ray handoff of 3 September put everything on the grid, never on a laptop or a personal drive, with VinDr in particular never leaving it, never on a public model hub, through an API or in a shared drive. The Lab Recipe's fifth rule put large outputs on the lab's Ceph volume, not home, after home reached 97 percent and a 260 GB deletion left about ninety scripts reading an empty held-out set. The holding report of 8 October made it the layout: code stays on home, data goes to the Ceph volume one folder per source with a README each, and the folders already there keep their old names because running chains name them. SchwingNet's catalog keeps the images on the grid at their content addresses and lets only aggregate counts leave ([docs/memory/the-data-catalog.md](../../docs/memory/the-data-catalog.md)).

The holding report also made VinDr-SpineXR, BUU-LSPINE and every bank holding their pixels owner-only from the first byte (DL30), giving the named-person agreement and the EULA as the reasons; the owner's settlement of 14 October stands beside it. The report listed this as a choice only the owner makes, owner-only or readable by the two members of the lab group, and copied owner-only meanwhile; no later choice is recorded. Approved students read everything else, and a student who needs those two applies for their own access, as the X-ray handoff had put it, each person signing their own agreement. Two more of the report's choices default the same way: the Hugging Face routes stay unpinned, so a re-acquisition may not be bit-identical, and TotalSegmentator is keyed by release id, accepting up to 24 merged or split people until an alias map is built. The newest storage decision is the owner's of 23 October (DL31): every fetch reads its exact size from its source before a byte lands and is refused whole if it would pass the volume's quota after what running fetches still owe ([docs/memory/the-size-rule.md](../../docs/memory/the-size-rule.md)).

## The letters, the requests and the future direction

The request letters carry two layers of decision (DL32). The sheet of 26 September, as the atlas presents it with the owner as drafter, has every letter say non-commercial academic evaluation, letters 1 to 3 promise not to train on the data, letters 1 to 4 offer involvement or co-authorship, and the letters to video uploaders promise deletion on request; each sentence comes with the instruction to change it before sending if the frames might validate the product or be used for fine-tuning. On 21 October the owner set the wording every later text follows: a short letter with a fixed opening line about applying computer science to spinal health, sent from his university mailbox on his word, with no text that says or implies that a model, a neural network, machine learning, AI or training is built or used, for patent confidentiality ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), [docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md](../../docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md)). The owner signs every agreement himself, since an agreement binds the person who accepts it ([docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md)). Which requests stand where on 21 and 22 October is [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md)'s table.

The one forward-looking data decision is the owner's of 20 October (DL33): expand the workflow into veterinary medicine. The registry names its starting point as "the LevelCheck pig set", and the repository corrected that on 22 October: the set is the pig lower-limb phantom of Goswami 2020 on IEEE DataPort, seven projections with a CT and two cone-beam CTs, not the LevelCheck pig study, and it holds no spine, so it tunes registration and names no level ([data/sources/veterinary_pig_levelcheck.json](../../data/sources/veterinary_pig_levelcheck.json)); a canine thoracic set stands beside it since 21 October. Finally, the owner's plan of 20 October and the grid pipeline of 21 October decide the order data enters (DL34): version 1 trains on the registry as it stands and waits on no long fetch, and when time runs short some evaluation rows, the CPU latency and the export step drop first while the registry, version 1 and the evaluation page never drop; chapter 16's M23 carries the order itself.

## A rule that runs under all of them

The handoff's ninth rule, in the owner's words, is to use all of a public dataset and to fix or justify every truncation in writing. Read against this log it is the test most decisions fail in one place: the BUU AP films, Mendeley's 71 non-duplicate films, VinDr's level exclusion, the COBRA Varian cases and DeepFluoro's 2,293 unposed frames each sit outside training with no written criterion, and the datasets contradictions (row 30) note that no criterion is written for the last. None of these is a permission question; each is a missing sentence, and the log's "what would reverse it" for each is that sentence or the data's return.

## What we know

FluoroCap is out of training by the owner's directive D1 of 23 September, for quality, and the owner kept it out of future training on 14 October while existing results stand. VinDr-SpineXR's exclusion from the level banks no longer has a live permission reason: HPR 2026-330 of 29 September covers it and the owner settled permission and agreements for all the data on 14 October. No BUU-trained weight leaves the grid until the owner's licence reading is entered; the 144 anomalous BUU studies are barred from training. SpineSAC, Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 and Glocker's CT are excluded, each for a stated reason; Spinal-AI2024 is out of the commercial lineage and wholly on training. Radiopaedia is never fetched by script, CT-RATE in full is off until the owner gives a number, and the YouTube re-encodes serve screening only. The hold-out rule and G3 of 23 September, the five frozen test persons and the rule that C-ArmLSpine's test persons stay out of every training govern every quotable number. Licences are enforced in code, the strictest reading proposed and only the owner loosening it; images stay on the grid, owner-only where an agreement names a person; and the letters speak of non-commercial evaluation and, since 21 October, of no model at all.

## What we do not know

The record does not say which of these decisions the owner made in his own words and which a session made and he let stand, beyond the attributions the table gives; the plan's wish to confirm each with him was not met here. Whether the IEEE 236 can become a test set depends on an annotation not yet made. Whether any Honduran films survive as an independent set waits on a recount, and the Mendeley count itself, 714 or 716, is open in the ledger. Which pseudo-label experiment produced which accuracy cut, NHANES's or the IEEE set's, the record cannot tell. The CTSpinoPelvic1K id audit has no recorded outcome, CHK-6 and CHK-7 have not run on a real shard, and the seven L6 records await their third read. No written criterion covers the BUU AP films, Mendeley's 71 non-duplicate films, VinDr's level exclusion or the COBRA Varian cases. The six licence conflicts have not been read against their release pages.

## What would change our mind

A two-reader read of a stratified FluoroCap sample would let D1's test-set clause work; only the owner's word moves the training exclusion. A written criterion for VinDr, or a version that brings it in, would end DL2's practice without a reason. The owner's licence reading entered in the licence file would lift DL3. Independent labels for the NHANES films, such as the VertXNet landmarks, would test DL5. An uploader's permission would free a YouTube clip; Radiopaedia's countersignature would open its cases under its terms; the owner's terabyte number would start CT-RATE. A recount of Mendeley would settle DL11 and DL15 together, and an annotation of the IEEE 236 scored on a clean lineage would revive DL14. A CTSpinoPelvic1K audit that pairs ids, a moved L6 record or a failing CHK-6 or CHK-7 row would reopen G3 and every split keyed on it. A release page read for each conflicting source would settle DL26 for that source.
