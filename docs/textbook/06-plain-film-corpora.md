# 06 Plain-film corpora and their level truth

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's own planners and stays in this private repository with the rest of the book; it cards data and quotes results by what they show, and leaves how any stage works to Part III (chapters 10 to 16).

Every vertebral level a SchwingNet model has learned from a real radiograph came from a handful of public plain-film corpora, and they do not mean the same thing by a level. This chapter cards those corpora (NHANES II, BUU-LSPINE, CSXA, VinDr-SpineXR, AASCE, Mendeley 5jdfdgp762, the IEEE DataPort lateral 236 and Spinal-AI2024) with what each says about the level, what its terms say, which films a rule keeps out of training and why, and what stopped each download in September. It reconciles the three totals that circulate (4,327, 50,887 and 12,709) and states the AP gap, so that a planner can tell which films can be scored against a named level, which only against a convention, and which figures the record has not settled.

## Four kinds of level truth

The distinction this chapter rests on was written down before most of the data arrived. The [Spine X-ray Data Handoff](../../artifacts/spine-x-ray-data-handoff-bddbdd/) of 3 September 2026 set the rules: split by patient and never by image, acknowledge every source, and "Do not invent levels. Where a source gives positions rather than names (BUU, AASCE), the manifest records `level_source = inferred_from_position`. That distinction is the project." The [Vertebra Detector Field Report](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/) made the rule a test, and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) shows it still in force in the corner corpus, whose `level_source` reads annotated for NHANES II, `inferred_from_position` for BUU-LSPINE and AASCE, and unknown for Mendeley.

Four kinds of truth follow. An annotated level is one a person named under a protocol; among the held plain-film corpora only NHANES II's landmarked subset and CSXA are of this kind. A level inferred from position is a name assigned by the order of rows or points, right exactly when the spine has the count the annotators assumed; BUU-LSPINE and AASCE are of this kind, and Spinal-AI2024's levels are assumed outright. A pseudo-label is a name the project's own machinery wrote on a film that came without one, as on the unannotated NHANES II films, VinDr-SpineXR and the IEEE lateral 236; how it was made belongs to [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md). And some films carry no level at all.

The [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/), canonical on level naming, keeps the line in its evaluation: its test set is annotated levels only, and BUU's counted names entered training without ever being scored. A number scored against an inferred level or a pseudo-label is a number about a labelling convention, not about anatomy, and should say so.

## The corpora, one card each

### NHANES II

NHANES II is two corpora. The landmarked subset is 543 lateral films of 364 persons (atlas and registry), 275 cervical and 268 lumbar (handoff and atlas), digitised from film of 1976 to 1980, with a mask per vertebra keyed by its name, on Zenodo (record 10223910) under CC BY 4.0. The handoff calls it "the only source with named levels under one protocol" and warns that its corners were fitted to the masks, not drawn by a radiologist. One ingest gave 3,217 vertebra instances and a second holds 3,053 on the same films, a gap unexplained until the masks are counted again (collision 30 of [the contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md)).

Which levels it names is not settled. The handoff lists C2 to C7 and T1, and L1 to L5 and S1; the atlas adds T11, T12 and the lower end plate of C2; the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) and the VertXNet source record say C3 to C7 and L1 to L5 only. That narrow reading does not fit the test set of the [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/), which scores 46 of its 982 NHANES crops at the sacrum and counts 66 thoracic crops, a count the reality check's "no thoracic" leaves unexplained ([the contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md), on the 8,422 crops). A Zenodo record by the same authors (Scientific Reports 14:3341) adds radiologist landmarks with vertebral identity and the reference bodies C2 and S1; the sweep of 21 October found it ([docs/research/xray-datasets-missing.md](../../docs/research/xray-datasets-missing.md)) and it is not fetched ([data/sources/vertxnet_nhanes2_labels.json](../../data/sources/vertxnet_nhanes2_labels.json)); reading it would settle which end bodies the films carry.

The subset is also where the worst leak began: it entered banks v2, v6 and v7 twice under two names, so that 48 of 54 validation patients had their copy in training (atlas). The [Handoff: SchwingNet, CTSpinoPelvic1K and the imaging programme](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) of 8 October calls every number from those families record only, the [SchwingNet Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) calls it "a leak the registry ends", and [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) holds it in full.

The survey set is 17,075 index entries at the CDC (9,667 cervical, 7,408 lumbar), of which 17,073 TIFFs from 9,698 subjects are on disk and two were empty at the source (atlas); the handoff notes that its cervical films were taken in maximum flexion. The census of 22 October, [docs/research/data-manifest/MANIFEST.md](../../docs/research/data-manifest/MANIFEST.md), counts the same 17,073 TIFFs. How many carry no landmark is not settled: the atlas and the registry say 16,531, but 17,073 less 543 is 16,530, and no page says whether all 543 are among the CDC films (row 9 of [the datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), cannot-tell). The project wrote pseudo-labels on 16,234 of them (atlas), a figure the [level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md) say still needs its source file (row 26); the census counts exactly 16,234 NPY files beside the TIFFs, which [its note](../../docs/research/data-manifest/README.md) reads as pipeline output, not truth. None of the films is in the level bank now, and the owner named them on 22 October as the unannotated set the screener of issue #64 will run over ([docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md)).

What pseudo-labels on such films cost the record has not settled. [The registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) gives 20 points of anomalous accuracy, and its basis in [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) is exact naming on anomalous real films of 87.2 percent (84.3 to 89.7, n = 572) with the leaked bank v2 against 66.8 percent (62.9 to 70.4) with the pseudo-labelled bank v5, each a record, not a final value. [The atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) gives "about 19" for the refuted IEEE arm; the collisions table reads it as about 20 (collision 182), but the ledger lists both values as open ([row 182 of the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-182-accuracy-cost-of-training-on-pseudo-labels)), so neither is a current value. Which films the pseudo-labelled bank drew on, and so whether the registry's NHANES line and the atlas's IEEE line describe one experiment, only the results catalog can say (row 23).

### BUU-LSPINE

BUU-LSPINE (Klinwichit 2023) is the largest held corpus of lumbar films with a position on every vertebra. The release is 7,200 films, an AP and a lateral for each patient, and 7,204 are on disk: 3,600 AP films and 3,604 lateral JPEGs, three of them exact copies, 18.5 GB in all (atlas; row 14). The patient count carries a doubt. The atlas, the registry and the [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) give 3,600 people, but the [State of the literature: datasets held, missing, size and power](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) page also offers "3,600 films from 1,200 patients", the collisions table cannot tell which holds (collision 23) and the ledger leaves the count open (its row 23), so this chapter counts BUU in films.

What BUU gives is a position: the upper and lower end-plate lines of L1 to L5 and S1, with names "inferred from row order (assigned by position, not annotated)" (atlas). Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor found why that matters: the authors worked "under the assumption that every person's spine consists of five lumbar vertebrae", so on a sacralised spine the fifth set of corners lands on S1. The exceptions are recorded as a per-patient class: 129 four-lumbar and 15 six-lumbar cases on the L4L6 sheet (atlas), and 130 sacralizations and 15 lumbarizations from the class field of the lateral films' S1a row ([OpenSpine Board](../../artifacts/openspine-board-t3gfds/), repeated by the decisions page). Each count is credited to its page with its field, and the two are not reconciled (row 15; collision 24; the ledger's row 24 leaves both open). The anchor-free report adds that nine of the 15 six-lumbar reads are of patients aged 7 to 17.

The 144 are 4.0 percent of the release, which the board read as the population prevalence. The datasets contradictions page rejects that reading (row 41): 4.0 percent is BUU's flag rate on lateral films, against a pooled prevalence of 15.3 percent across 83 studies, a record, not a final value, carried only by the superseded [Protocol 1 eProtocol draft](../../artifacts/protocol-1-eprotocol-draft-mkmh7p/), and planning priors of 9 to 18 percent for any transitional vertebra, 3.2 percent for an L6 and 1.6 percent for four lumbar bodies on the literature-state page. The flags are the authors' reads, not a reference standard, and radiography finds a transitional vertebra with 32 percent sensitivity and a kappa of 0.16 to 0.32 (Hanhivaara 2024, as the atlas and the reality check give it). Since the flags record a count, four or six lumbar bodies, a transitional vertebra on a five-lumbar spine has no flag to carry; this chapter therefore reads every specificity or AUC measured against the unflagged films as a floor.

The board and the decisions page record 465 grade-3 and 194 grade-4 slips across 36,060 annotated end plates. Beside the release sit the 2000 relabel, a pixel subset from which the corner corpus drew 19,991 vertebra instances, and a 400-patient sample that no code references. The 3,603 laterals are in the v9 level bank; the 3,600 AP films are in no level bank and no written reason covers that (atlas; the 8 October handoff), though that handoff counts them among the 12,709 films behind the body detector.

The terms are a bespoke end-user licence agreement reached through the release site's request form, "EULA, 'No License Provided'; acknowledgement required, no re-identification; never redistribute" (atlas), with no non-commercial clause and no commercial grant (board). The registry records the licence as unread by the owner, with its rule that no weight trained on the films leaves the grid until the owner has read it, and the datasets contradictions carry both lines with the terms (row 16).

### CSXA

The Cervical Spine X-ray Atlas (Ran 2024) is the cervical corpus: 4,963 lateral films with 23 keypoints each whose labels name the level, four corners on each of C3 to C7 and the lower end plate of C2 by the 3 September handoff, cross-checked in three tiers by four spine surgeons (atlas). The board alone records the converter's two silent traps, 715 films facing right and a label that is a point in some files and a polygon in others, a record, not a final value; the 24,815 named vertebrae it parsed the decisions page repeats. The census counts 4,963 images, 4,962 with a sidecar.

CSXA has no C1, though the registry's level field reads "C1 to C7" (level-naming row 74). All 4,963 films are in the v9 level bank (4,214 training, 749 validation), so CSXA is "not held out until re-keyed" (registry), and one image per patient is the release's untested claim. Another group (Barr 2026) has released 20,063 synthetic radiographs generated from the set (board, atlas). The record does not carry the September pages' "largest annotated lateral set in existence", which cites no survey (row 53).

The board and the decisions page record that CSXA was added so that cervical levels had two independent sources, CSXA and NHANES, and a result could be checked across them. On held-out patients the [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) named a single cervical vertebra correctly 97.7 percent of the time on 7,900 CSXA and NHANES crops, and any single vertebra 96.7 to 96.9 percent on all 8,422 crops, each a record, not a final value. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) gives 98.7 percent on the same 8,422 crops from a different model, scored one crop at a time (CSXA 99.4, NHANES 93.6), also a record, not a final value. The contradictions carry each figure with its own model, and no page says the 98.7 replaces the ladder's (level-naming row 27; the main page's ruling on the 8,422 crops). All were measured before the NHANES double entry was found on 22 September, and the level-naming page's notes put the ladder under the 8 October handoff's ruling on leaked banks.

### VinDr-SpineXR

VinDr-SpineXR (Nguyen 2021) is the largest held real corpus with radiologist labels, and none of them is a level: 10,466 DICOM images in 5,000 studies, 8,389 training and 2,077 test, with radiologist lesion boxes in 13, 8 or 7 classes depending on the page (row 13). The board and the atlas give its terms as the PhysioNet Restricted Health Data License 1.5.0, the registry as a credentialed licence. The 3 September handoff quotes PhysioNet as restricted, a registered user who signs the agreement, and corrects the field report's "credentialed", which the registry, the holding report and the atlas's access line still say; the datasets contradictions carry the handoff's quoted wording and flag the later pages' word until the record page is read again (row 11). The ethics determination is settled: [docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md](../../docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md) records that WSU IRB HPR 2026-330 of 29 September 2026, the supplement to HPR 2026-269, covers it, as the atlas gives, and the registry artifact's "pending" is stale (row 12).

Its use has narrowed. The project wrote pseudo levels on 7,625 of its films and consumed the release's test split in doing so, which the atlas says forecloses VinDr as a held-out benchmark. It is 0 of 10,466 in the v9 level bank, and the registry says only its implant boxes are used. But the 8 October handoff found 2,632 VinDr crops in each fold bank behind the C-ArmLSpine naming results, and no page says whether those banks were rebuilt; the record carries VinDr as out of the level bank and in the old fold banks as of 8 October (row 13, cannot-tell).

### AASCE and BoostNet

The AASCE 2019 challenge set is the only held real source of mid-thoracic level positions ("T7 only in AASCE", the board and the decisions page say) and the only held real AP whole-spine set with corners. The ledger marks its counts current ([row 26 of the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-26-aasceboostnet-labelled-film-count)): 609 labelled films, 481 BoostNet training and 128 test, plus 98 challenge test films whose landmarks were never published; the grid's older request-form copy holds 579, the copy the decisions page counts (row 19). Each film carries four corners on 17 vertebrae and three Cobb angles, and its levels are a position: T1 to L5 is assumed from the top annotated vertebra, and the handoff warned that a film not starting at T1, or a spine with 13 ribs or an L6, shifts every label below it, "precisely the failure the whole project is about".

The atlas and the source record use it as a thoracic test set only (608 films, one dropped for a degenerate box), and the atlas records that its training and test uses conflict and are undecided; 457 of its 481 training films once fed deleted banks ([data/sources/aasce.json](../../data/sources/aasce.json)). No licence has been found and the images' reuse terms are not recorded (row 20). The anchor-free report adds, from Wang 2021, that the held-out films are 70 percent mild curves and that C2 is not in frame.

### Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 and its Honduran images

The September pages give 716 lateral lumbar films, 208 from Honduras and 508 re-filtered BUU films, with boxes and four corners from L3 to S1 and no level names, which the atlas says can come only by counting up from S1. The atlas counts 714 JPEGs, 698 annotated, and judges the set a redistribution of about 500 BUU persons and 12 VinDr studies, excluded by directive D6, with 71 films that are not duplicates left out without a written reason; the registry excludes it as adding no new person; the census counts 714 JPEGs and 698 annotated images. The record has settled neither the count, the atlas's and the registry's 714 and the September pages' 716 each credited to its page and not reconciled, since the atlas never says it recounted (row 21 and its follow-up; the ledger's row 20 leaves both open), nor what the set adds, since the registry's "no new person" conflicts with both the 208 and the 71 and neither holds without a recount (row 22). It is in no level bank (8 October handoff), though 2,666 of its vertebra instances are in the corner corpus behind the body detector (atlas).

### The IEEE DataPort lateral 236

The IEEE set is 236 standing lateral films from head to mid-thigh with no annotation (doi 10.21227/pacw-ws92), a 909.55 MB zip by the handoff and a 1.3 GB tree by the atlas, two sizes that may measure different things (row 23). Its licence is not stated (atlas) and not recorded (registry); access was through an IEEE DataPort subscription. It is the handoff's evaluation hold-out (below), and the pseudo-label arm that would have used it was refuted, by "about 19 points" in the atlas's words, a figure the ledger leaves open beside the registry's 20 (the NHANES card).

### Spinal-AI2024

Spinal-AI2024 is 20,000 machine-generated AP films with 19,999 Cobb-angle triples and COCO annotations (decisions page). The atlas found only 44 synthetic identities among its 8,358 usable films, one appearing 1,270 times, and the registry gives the 20,000 as films of 44 identities, so no patient was imaged and its levels are assumed. The board advised against downloading it because it has no licence file, which means all rights reserved by default; the decisions page records that it was pulled anyway, on the owner's call, and tagged research-track; the registry holds it with every film on the training side of the G3 split and the licence not recorded (row 24). The census counts 20,001 images. With so few identities, a split by film would put one identity on both sides, which is why the atlas says the whole set must sit on the training side.

### Sets named but not held

[Chapter 4](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md) cards every set not held; three would add plain-film level truth. TVGH-SpineXR is described as carrying T1 to L5 per-vertebra labels, but its page answered 403 at a login and access was never requested; MrOS and SOF hold lateral thoracic and lumbar films with fracture grades on named levels, by a data request (their source records). CM Spine, 208 intraoperative G-arm images, is not held either; the September claim that it is the only public intraoperative source is not carried (row 54), and C-ArmLSpine is the held open intraoperative set ([chapter 5](05-real-fluoroscopy-and-c-arm-sets.md)).

## Held out by rule, and why

Three groups of films are kept out of training by a written rule, and each rule has its reason in what the films are.

The IEEE lateral 236 were held out to be the one external test: "This is the evaluation hold-out ... Annotate it; never train on it." The femoral heads are in frame, which makes it the only public lateral source in the record on which pelvic incidence can be measured, and without it every test image comes from a source the model also trained on, as the field report says of its own held-out results. A superseded bank broke the rule with 212 pseudo-labelled films, no live bank holds the set, and no reader has annotated it, so the test itself does not yet exist.

The 208 Honduran films were held out because they were the only films in the Mendeley set that were not BUU's. The handoff called them "the only genuine cross-site lateral lumbar test set in the project" and told the next session to keep them out of training entirely, since a BUU-trained model tested on the other 508 would be tested on its own training patients. The atlas has since excluded the whole set and found the overlap wider than BUU alone, so the cross-site test the rule protected has not been built: whether 208, 71 or some other number of independent films remains is a recount the record has not made.

The 144 BUU anomalous studies are barred from training because their counted names are wrong by the release's own reading. The anchor-free report found 124 of them in the training partition of every bank to that date, labelled L1 to L5, so a model trained there learned the ordinary answer on the siblings of its test films. It barred all 144, and its v6 bank, a leaked family whose figures are records only, moved them to test, where anomalous interior naming scored 49.9 percent on 128 films (interval 41.5 to 58.9) against 89.9 percent on ordinary films (87.1 to 92.7), a record, not a final value. [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) of 8 October repeats the pair, on 128 films against 453, with the 144 held to validation; the level-naming contradictions carry it as record only, the partition being test on one page and validation on the other, until a clean-bank re-run (row 25). [Chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) takes them up.

Other films are kept to one side without being kept out of training: splits are by patient, since BUU and NHANES give two films per person and Mendeley holds BUU's patients. [Chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) carries the split rules and the leaks.

## The corpus totals and how they reconcile

The plan asks for 4,327, 50,887 and 12,709 to be reconciled. They reconcile, because each counts a different thing.

The field report's 6,073 instances on 958 images (727 patients) from NHANES II, Mendeley and a partial BUU is carried once, as the early three-source manifest (row 50); its 0.957 AP50 on 558 held-out instances is an early in-distribution result, with BUU on only 15 test instances. By 7 September the corner corpus held 33,943 instances on 5,401 films: 19,991 from the BUU 2000 relabel, 8,069 from AASCE, 3,217 from NHANES II and 2,666 from Mendeley (atlas; collision 34).

4,327 is the September detector's training set, behind mAP50 0.991 and PCK at 0.1 of 0.812 on the [Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/) page, which says that model had never seen a named level, a lesion, a transitional label or a Cobb angle. ## The AP gap

On 3 September, by the superseded handoff (a record, not a final value), all 125 AP vertebra instances the detector had seen came from 50 BUU files; the handoff called that "the whole reason AP is the gap" and expected the 400 and 2,000 subsets to take AP to roughly 12,000 instances. By 7 September the corner corpus held 18,061 AP instances of its 33,943, from the BUU 2000 relabel and AASCE, so for corners the gap closed in four days.

For level names it moved rather than closed. The v9 level bank holds no real AP film: BUU's AP films are in no level bank, AASCE's are a thoracic test set and Spinal-AI2024's are synthetic. The Any-View Spine Plan reports zero-shot naming on C-ArmLSpine frames at 96.4 percent lateral (595 of 617), the later lateral figure (collision 106), against 43.7 percent AP, which the ledger marks current, 276 of 631, replacing an earlier 37.88 percent ([row 107 of the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)). The plan reads the AP failure as a one-level cranial shift, "a counting error, not an appearance error", and does not tie it to the missing AP films; how the project means to close the gap is method, in [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md). The data side is this chapter's: every real AP and lateral pair held is lumbar (BUU's 3,600, C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases and, by the plan, VinDr's two-view studies), by the plan and the census note, and the only real AP films with level positions, BUU's and AASCE's, are in no level bank.

## What a planner should take from this

Score only against annotated levels, and say which kind of truth stands behind every number. On real films that leaves NHANES II's 543 and CSXA's 4,963, both lateral, one of them film from the late 1970s and the other cervical only. BUU's names are a convention wrong on at least 144 studies, AASCE's assume a T1 start that the handoff said to spot-check on 50 films before trusting, and Mendeley's export names nothing.

Lumbar annotated truth on real film is the binding constraint. The ladder counts about 380 lumbar films with annotated levels in training against about 5,000 cervical ones, a figure the detectors contradictions carry with that scope (row 40), while the handoff and the atlas give 268 lumbar films in the whole landmarked subset; no page reconciles the two, and the ladder ran before the double entry was found. Its regional figures, each a record, not a final value, for the reason the CSXA card gives, show the cost: in the [ladder's](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) arm that the level-naming contradictions carry (row 56), a single vertebra was named correctly 97.6 percent of the time in the cervical spine and 85.4 percent in the lumbar spine on 410 crops, with 95.5 percent in the thoracic spine on 66 crops and 93.5 percent at the sacrum on 46; in another arm, adding BUU's counted names raised the lumbar figure by 2.0 points, from 80.7 to 82.7 percent. A lumbar number on real film should carry its n, and a thoracic or sacral one is a band, not a ranking, as the ladder says.

No external real-film test with level truth exists yet. The IEEE 236 have no annotation, the Honduran films have not been recounted, VinDr's test split was consumed and holds no levels, and the 144 anomalous BUU studies have sat in two partitions. Until one of those changes, a real-film level result is a result on a source the model also trained on, and the field report's warning that such a result "is not evidence of cross-site generalization" still holds. Quote a total by what it counts, check the ledger before quoting a holding, and see [chapter 24](24-decision-log-data-licences-and-splits.md) for the decisions on data, licences and splits.

## What we know

The held plain-film corpora give four kinds of level truth. Only NHANES II's 543 landmarked films and CSXA's 4,963 carry annotated names; BUU-LSPINE and AASCE carry positions whose names are inferred; Mendeley's export carries no names; VinDr-SpineXR and the IEEE 236 carry none; Spinal-AI2024's levels are assumed on synthetic films. Pseudo-labels were written on NHANES II, VinDr and IEEE films; none of them is in the v9 level bank, though VinDr crops were in the older fold banks on 8 October.

The v9 level bank's real films are CSXA, BUU's 3,603 laterals and the 543 NHANES films. BUU's own reading marks at least 144 studies as four- or six-lumbar, and those 144 are barred from training. The NHANES landmark set once entered three banks twice, and those families are records only. VinDr is covered by HPR 2026-330, AASCE's counts are current in the ledger, and the retraining target is the decisions page's 34,000. The circulating totals reconcile once each is read as what it counts; every September download block but CM Spine's and TVGH-SpineXR's was cleared by 23 September; the held real AP and lateral pairs are lumbar, by the Any-View Spine Plan and the census; and zero-shot AP naming on the C-arm stands at 43.7 percent, current in the ledger, against the later lateral figure of 96.4 percent.

## What we do not know

The record does not settle which end bodies the NHANES landmarks name, why the two ingests hold 3,217 and 3,053 instances, whether 16,531 or 16,530 survey films carry no landmark, or where the source file of the 16,234 pseudo-labelled films is, though the census counts 16,234 NPY files beside the films. It does not reconcile the ladder's about 380 annotated lumbar films in training with the 268 lumbar films of the landmarked subset. It does not settle BUU's patient count, whether its flags are 129 or 130 four-lumbar, how many transitional spines the flags miss, Mendeley's count, or how many Mendeley films are independent of BUU and VinDr.

For VinDr it leaves open the lesion class count and whether the fold banks still hold its crops. It does not settle what pseudo-labels cost, 20 points by the registry or about 19 by the atlas, nor which films the pseudo-labelled bank drew on, nor which target a retraining used, and nobody has annotated the IEEE 236. Whether CSXA holds one image per patient is untested.

## What would change our mind

A recount of Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 against BUU-LSPINE and VinDr-SpineXR by person key would settle whether a cross-site lumbar test of real films exists: if 208, or even 71, independent films survive it, the project has one. Reading the VertXNet record would settle the NHANES end bodies, and if it confirms C2 and S1 on these films NHANES becomes an anchored source of named levels. A human annotation of the IEEE 236 would give the project its first external real-film level truth and retire the in-distribution caveat above.

A by-patient count of both BUU fields, the L4L6 sheet and the S1a row's class field, with the three duplicate laterals removed would settle 129 against 130; a count of the CDC films with no landmark entry would settle 16,531 against 16,530, and a file count of the Mendeley archive and of the September export 714 against 716. A reading of BUU's flagged and unflagged films against a reference standard would show whether the flags are a floor, as this chapter takes them to be, or close to complete. A listing of the current C-ArmLSpine fold banks would show whether VinDr is out of training but for its implant boxes. The results catalog's row for each pseudo-label arm would say which films the cost was measured on and whether it is 19 or 20 points, and the retraining job's manifest which target it used. MrOS, SOF or TVGH-SpineXR, if obtained, would end the dependence of lumbar and thoracic level truth on a few hundred NHANES films and AASCE's assumed T1.
