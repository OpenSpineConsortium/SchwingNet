# 23 Who may do what

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it gives roles, rules and the incidents behind them, and leaves how each stage of the method works to Part III.

This chapter maps every action an experiment takes to the role that may take it. A planner reads it before writing a plan that spends grid hours, opens a dataset request, hands a task to a student or lets a session run unattended, because each of those steps has an owner, and a step taken by the wrong role is either refused by a tool or, worse, quietly done. It answers four questions the plan sets: who may start, stop or spend, and what words count as the owner's permission; which data each role may read, copy or move, and what never leaves the grid; what a session may do alone, what needs the owner and how a handoff is written; and which rules exist because of a named incident. It closes with what a new student or a fresh Claude project reads first. People appear here by role: the owner, who is the principal investigator; the senior scientist, who is co-principal investigator; the senior clinical principal investigator; a clinical co-investigator; a project lead and co-lead; a student or member; and a session, meaning any Claude worker.

## The roles and where each is written down

Four artifacts carry the role map, and they were written for different readers at different times, so a planner needs to know which one governs which question.

[The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) of 27 September is the canonical statement of the lab's chain rules and the cluster's canonical artifact for programme direction ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), closing notes). It sets three roles inside each project and three above them. Inside a project, the lead owns the question, the frozen pre-registration and the manuscript, answers project mail within two working days, holds a fifteen-minute weekly check-in and merges the project's pull requests; the co-lead can do everything the lead can, takes over when the lead leaves and is the second reader on every freeze; members take tasks, each one a line on the board with a name on it. A project without a co-lead is unstaffed and takes no members. Above the projects, the senior scientist screens, the senior clinical principal investigator confirms clinical reads, and the owner signs off every freeze and every submission. The recipe's projects live in a separate student-projects tree with its own board; its lead-merges rule is that tree's rule.

[spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/) of 13 October is the canonical statement of how a student works in the private spine-program repository through Claude. There the owner merges every pull request; a student never edits the default branch and never merges. Folders are divided into student folders and folders the owner takes first: a student may read every folder, but an issue in an owner-first folder, or labelled for the owner first, becomes the student's only when the owner hands it over by a comment naming the student or by removing the label. The contradictions page marks this artifact canonical for the programme rules but not for the grid steps, which [Connect the grid to GitHub](../../artifacts/connect-the-grid-to-github/) replaced the next day (row 49, superseded-by-later; closing notes).

[Handoff: SchwingNet, CTSpinoPelvic1K and the imaging programme](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) of 8 October is the canonical handoff. It states ten rules that bind all work, says which handoff files are for the owner and the Claude project only, and lists the questions only the owner can answer in a student's first week. Its companion of the same day, [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/), names the decisions that wait on the owner before an experiment proceeds.

In this repository the roles are the protocol's. [docs/agents/OWNERSHIP.md](../../docs/agents/OWNERSHIP.md) gives one area, the whole repository, to any worker, and says the repository owner merges everything except the pull requests of a worker whose file says it lands by auto-merge; CODEOWNERS asks the owner to review every pull request. No worker is registered yet ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md), Owners), so today every pull request here is merged by the owner. [docs/agents/WORKERS.md](../../docs/agents/WORKERS.md) says what a worker is: a person's sessions under the person's GitHub login, a routine, or a worker in GitHub Actions, each claiming issues under the one protocol in [docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md](../../docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md). The coordinator that releases idle claims and tells overlapping work is code in the board's run and is nobody's job.

The two statements of who merges therefore differ by place, not by contradiction: a project lead merges in the student-projects tree, and the owner merges in spine-program and here.

## Who may start, stop or spend

### The owner's word and number

The one rule that governs spending in this project is written in the repository's standing decisions: every grid job and every training waits for the owner's word and the number of jobs, and usage credits are never used ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), durable decisions; [docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md), Out of scope). A planner should read "word and number" literally. A plan that says a training is ready to run is not permission; a message from the owner that says to run it and how many jobs is. The grid section of [chapter 21](21-the-grid-how-a-run-starts-waits-and-finishes.md) says how a run is then started, and [chapter 22](22-quotas-cost-and-the-budget-of-a-run.md) how its size is bounded.

The rule has one standing exception, granted in the owner's own words and written into the project file: the synthesis reads that keep the artifact record current start on their own, ten runs a day unless a repository variable raises it, fifty at most, and a variable set to off stops them; the owner chose this on 23 October ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md), Scope and Out of scope). Those runs spend the one Claude credential the project keeps, the repository secret the owner adds; nothing else spends Claude usage on the repository's account without a further word.

The dataset records carry the same rule at the level of a single fetch. [SchwingNet Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) says on its first screen that sample-level counts stay empty until a grid job runs on the owner's word, and every record's "measured" field repeats it. [docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md) sorts every step of getting a dataset not held into four kinds by who may take it, and a plain download is one of them: a Slurm job that runs only on the owner's word and number.

### Stopping and cancelling

Stopping is cheaper than starting but not free. The lab recipe's grid rule 12, dated to the owner on 27 September, says that before anything is cancelled every job is recorded with its script header as its resume instruction, and the session rules repeat that a job is written down before it is cancelled ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), the grid and working with Claude). The point is that a cancelled job whose resume instruction lived only in a terminal is lost work, and the person who cancels it owes the record.

### What a student spends

A student pays for their own Claude plan, Pro or Max, on their own account, and uses their own grid account requested from the university with the owner as sponsor ([spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), accounts). A student's Claude usage is therefore the student's, and a student's jobs run under the student's grid account, never the owner's; the onboarding's first task says so in as many words. The one-account rule of the [Claude Code on a Slurm + Apptainer Cluster playbook](https://claude.ai/artifact/7dBJwcSiDjUFrAKSJfFhEF) binds each student the same way: one person's subscription authenticates only that person's sessions, no one else's work is routed through it, and no tokens are pooled to widen a window. The contradictions page reads the playbook and the REFORMS checklist as one rule stated twice by the same author, not two independent sources (row 71); how the owner's own workers run on the grid is [chapter 31](31-claude-workers-on-the-grid-and-the-labs-machines.md)'s subject, not this chapter's.

## Who may read, copy or move data

### The volume

The holding sits on the lab's Ceph volume, and who may read what there is the owner's decision, recorded in [Imaging data holding: manifest, migration and index](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/), the canonical artifact for the holding. Approved students read every source folder under the data root that is not restricted: the CT releases, the anomaly-harvest CTs, the real X-ray and fluoroscopy sets, the plain-film corpora, the render banks, the index database and each folder's README. Access rides on the lab's existing gate: an approved student's grid identity gets a named entry on the volume root, kept in step with approvals every fifteen minutes, and below it the data folders are readable by others. The owner alone reads VinDr-SpineXR, held under a named-person PhysioNet data use agreement, and BUU-LSPINE, whose licence forbids redistribution, together with every bank that holds their pixels; those are copied with owner-only permissions from the first byte. The same page lists that choice among the decisions only the owner can make, with group-readable access for the two members of the volume's group as the other option, and keeps the sets owner-only meanwhile, so the restriction is the page's standing state rather than a decision recorded as final. The first pass over the folders already on the volume ran on 8 October and opened 53 folders, kept VinDr closed and read back a sampled file from every opened folder as readable by others.

This supersedes the lab recipe on one point. On 27 September the recipe listed among what was not yet true that no grid data path existed that students could read, because the volume's group had two members and the owner's home was not group-readable, to be closed by a group-readable export or an access list. The holding page reports that route built eleven days later, and the contradictions page rules the recipe's line superseded by the later artifact (row 48). The onboarding of 13 October describes the student's side of the same gate: without the owner's note that a student's grid identity is on the volume's access list, every lab path answers permission denied ([spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), grid setup).

A student who needs one of the two restricted sets applies for their own credentialed access; the index still lists them so the holding reads as one ([Imaging data holding](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/), student access). The onboarding says the same from the other side: the data credentials for VinDr-SpineXR, BUU-LSPINE and PhysioNet are needed only by the tasks that say so, and the issue says where to ask. The determinations that cover the data are stated in the records: the university's IRB determination HPR 2026-269 of 2 September covers public de-identified data that downloads without an agreement, and its supplement HPR 2026-330 of 29 September covers VinDr-SpineXR under its PhysioNet agreement ([docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md](../../docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md)); the registry's line calling that determination pending is stale (the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), row 12).

### What never leaves

The lab recipe's private-forever list is the binding statement: the level-identification code, FluoroCap, the model weights, the private CT segmentation repository and the private plain-film render dataset never go into a public repository and never onto the website ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), data rules). The handoff restates it as rule 8 and widens it to all level-identification text and figures, adds that a disclosure audit runs before any talk or submission, and that only the public version-12 deck is disclosure-safe for the CNS talk ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), operating the work). This repository's own scope says the same for the present: nothing public before the provisional is filed, no preprint and no slide of the method, and the talk shows what SchwingNet does, not how ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md), Out of scope).

One older page reads otherwise. The FluoroCap dataset card of 14 September defines two releases from one manifest, and the contradictions page cannot tell from the records whether they were ever meant to be public; it carries FluoroCap as private and never redistributed, with the two releases as frozen internal subsets (the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), row 8). A planner treats FluoroCap as private; [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md) holds the card.

Below the private-forever list sit three narrower rules. Large outputs and every image stay on the grid; the onboarding's rule for a task that touches data is that only small results, a table, a count, a log excerpt, come back to the branch, each number with the path of the file it came from, and that a student ships only the paths the issue needs ([spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), tasks that need the grid). CTSpinoPelvic1K's volumes are never redistributed, and the repository ignores their file type ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), licences). And no weight trained on BUU-LSPINE leaves the grid until the owner has read its licence: the registry carries the licence as "unread by the owner" with that rule attached, the directions note names the reading as the owner's to make before any weight leaves, and the contradictions page carries the terms from the atlas together with that rule (the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), row 16; [Research directions](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/), what waits on the owner). The owner reads every licence before a weight leaves; [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) holds the licences themselves.

### Moving and deleting

Moving data is a batch of jobs, every step on a compute node, and deleting a home copy is the last step, taken only after a second comparison of the two copies ([Imaging data holding](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/), where it goes). The holding page lists the choices it will not make on its own: deleting the regenerable or superseded copies, where the batch of nnU-Net results goes, renaming the folders already on the volume and what becomes of the identified home entries each wait as a row of decisions only the owner can make, and meanwhile the plan deletes nothing beyond verified home copies.

### The study that would hold identifiable data

The one planned study that would bring identifiable images into the work is the retrospective fluoroscopy protocol, a draft for the university IRB ([Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), canonical for the OR study cluster; [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md) holds its place in the readiness plan). Its role map is stricter than the lab's. The owner and the clinical co-investigator alone retrieve and de-identify images on the hospital's systems, and a second team member checks every frame of a random share of the cases (10 percent proposed, itself to confirm); the master list linking study IDs to medical record numbers is held separately by the owner on the hospital's server and destroyed once abstraction is verified; annotators and computing staff see only coded, de-identified frames; and nothing is shared outside the study team, with any future release of images going to the IRB as an amendment first. The principal investigator's line, the annotators' names, the storage location and whether the grid is approved for coded research data are all marked "to confirm" in the draft, so none of this binds until the protocol is approved.

## Requests, agreements and letters

Getting a dataset not held is where the roles are drawn most finely, because an agreement binds the person who accepts it and sent mail cannot be recalled. [docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md) of 20 October sorts every step into four kinds. A session may draft a letter and send it only once the owner approves that one letter. A web form with no signature may be filled by a session only with a browser, so from a cloud session its text goes to the owner to paste. An account, a click-through or signed agreement, a credentialing attestation or a fee is the owner's own hand, always. A plain download is a grid job on the owner's word. Every one of the 44 rows in that note names who acts under those four kinds, and the owner appears in nearly all of them.

The standing decision on letters is narrower than the note's: dataset request letters go from the owner's university mailbox on the owner's word, short, under a fixed subject tag and opening line, with addresses verified from institutional pages or papers, and the owner signs any data use agreement ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), durable decisions). The note of 20 October left open whether a letter went from a personal or the university address; the standing decision settles it. Unheld datasets are obtained with collaborators in a separate private repository worked through the same protocol (the same decisions). Two cautions in the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) bind whoever sends a drafted letter: every letter says non-commercial academic evaluation, so a frame that might ever validate the product needs a commercial licence instead, and four letters offer co-authorship, which the sender checks against the project's authorship plans first. One source is never fetched by script at all, by the owner's decision, since its terms bar automated access ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md); [docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md)).

## What a session may do alone

A session is a worker like any other. It claims an issue by pushing the branch `claude/N` with a claim file, labels the issue, posts its plan, opens a draft pull request at once, runs the checks, and finishes by deleting the claim file, marking the pull request ready and labelling the issue in-review ([docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md](../../docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md), Claim to Finish). It does not merge here; the owner does ([docs/agents/OWNERSHIP.md](../../docs/agents/OWNERSHIP.md)). Since 22 October every pull request marked ready, a person's or a session's, carries a section headed "Adversarial review" in its body, and the required check holds it there; the rule began as the owner's own instruction to sessions and became part of the required check so that it binds collaborators too ([docs/memory/the-review-record.md](../../docs/memory/the-review-record.md)).

What a session must never do is written in several places and agrees across them. It never handles a credential: the [owner's checklist](../../docs/setup/owner-checklist.md) opens by saying sessions cannot take its steps, which need the owner's own accounts, and that no token is pasted into a chat or committed; the grid runbook tells a student that Claude on the grid must not see the token and that a session was right to refuse handling it ([Connect the grid to GitHub](../../artifacts/connect-the-grid-to-github/), what this sets up). It never pushes to a public place, by the private-forever rule above. It never pushes to the default branch or to another worker's branch, which the push guard refuses before the push runs ([docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md](../../docs/agents/PROTOCOL.md), rules that hold everywhere). And it stops where only the owner can act, saying exactly what is missing.

Some of those stops are not policy but the environment. A cloud session cannot create a repository, approve a deployment or publish a release, each answered 403, and its proxy refuses most dataset and publisher hosts, so no real fetch runs in a container ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), constraints). The onboarding gives the student's version: a Project thread has the clone and GitHub and nothing else, no grid, no ssh and no job submission, so a task that touches data is planned in the thread and run from the student's grid session ([spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), tasks that need the grid). The architectural analysis of the two Claude surfaces explains why the chat sandbox cannot stand in for that session: it resets between tasks and cannot see a working tree, keys, a toolchain or an internal network ([Claude Chat vs. Claude Code](https://claude.ai/artifact/SnuzFDHCC9kM3GgYziMApb)). [Symphony in brief](https://claude.ai/artifact/9CDCiZqYfbrobDk6rRrMho) gives the protocol's own account of what runs without any person: the board, the release of idle claims and the overlap notices are code in the board's run, while planning epics, settling blocked tasks and integrating finished epics are Claude's work in the coordinator workflow; [chapter 29](29-symphony-the-protocol-its-guarantees-its-comparison-and-its-cost.md) holds the protocol.

A session that submits grid work follows the recipe's discipline: it returns the job ids, an estimated finish and the files to collect, writes them into the checkpoint, and ends the turn; it does not poll the queue for more than about ten minutes ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), working with Claude). Status is computed from what exists on disk and in the queue, never written by hand, because a hand-written table once listed a job as pending three hours after the scheduler showed it cancelled.

## Credentials, by role

The credential rules bind the shipped product and every student, and they are the same in every artifact that states them. A credential is read from a file only its owner can read, mode 600, never echoed, never on a command line, never in a job script or its exported environment, never in a log and never in a file of any repository ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), data rules; [spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), rules that matter); the grid runbook adds that the student types it into that file with an editor and never into a chat with Claude ([Connect the grid to GitHub](../../artifacts/connect-the-grid-to-github/), what this sets up). Students use their own read tokens for datasets that need one. For the grid's access to GitHub, a student makes one token on their own GitHub account and stores it in a git credential file in their own grid home that git reads by itself; this replaced the onboarding's earlier one-line token file the next day (row 49, superseded-by-later), and the runbook's troubleshooting table tells a student to copy an error into the project chat with the token and their grid identity left out ([Connect the grid to GitHub](../../artifacts/connect-the-grid-to-github/)). The [FluoroCap Access Setup](../../artifacts/fluorocap-access-setup-jlvkft/) page of 14 September states the same contract for the harvester's service keys: one environment file readable only by its owner, outside every repository, filled with an editor, and a check that reports which keys are set and whether each service answers, never a value.

The product follows the same rule. The playbook for Claude Code on the grid puts no credential in the container image; each user mints their own token on their own computer and stores it in a file only they can read ([Claude Code on a Slurm + Apptainer Cluster](https://claude.ai/artifact/7dBJwcSiDjUFrAKSJfFhEF)). The handoff keeps the names and locations of credentials in a folder for the owner alone and never their contents ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), operating the work). And the Microsoft app this project uses to read the owner's drive is delegated: it acts only as the person signed in, on that person's own files, with no administrative power ([docs/setup/folders-admin-consent.md](../../docs/setup/folders-admin-consent.md)).

Personal data is held to the same standard as credentials. Survey files, contact spreadsheets and staff lists never go into a repository; no student name, username or personal e-mail goes into any file, while a GitHub login on a worker label and in a claim file is working metadata and is allowed; and a student's grid identity is withheld in everything written back ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/); [spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/)).

## The research track and the commercial track

The lab recipe's last data rule is that students, the grid and non-commercial data are the research track, and nothing moves from it to a commercial track; it gives the reason only as written policy ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), data rules). The handoff adds what follows for anything that leaves: never state the commercial goal or name the product, never re-add level-identification text to a public repository, and hold the manuscript that discloses invention aspects private ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), manuscripts). The handoff's manuscripts file is itself for the owner and the Claude project only, because it carries the disclosure and technology-transfer record.

The boundary on the other side is the LLC note, which the owner allowed this chapter to cite. As it stands, it is a plan on defaults the owner has not confirmed: a one-member LLC that would hold Symphony's copyright and nothing of the level-identification invention disclosure, a draft operating agreement for a lawyer, and nothing filed or paid ([docs/research/llc/README.md](../../docs/research/llc/README.md)). The plan beside it ([docs/research/llc/PLAN.md](../../docs/research/llc/PLAN.md)) says the university very likely holds the first right to the level-identification disclosure, that the LLC cannot simply receive disclosed material, that no SchwingNet code goes into any LLC repository until a waiver or an exclusive licence from the university is signed, and lists asking for one as a later step. Read together, the two say that the level-identification work stays on the university's technology-transfer route and the research track, and that any outside entity would reach it only through a licence or waiver from the university, which no record shows sought or granted. A planner who wants a student's work to feed a product needs the owner's decision first, because the recipe forbids the move and no record shows it made.

## Authorship and credit

The lab recipe gives one authorship rule: authorship follows the ICMJE criteria; among those who qualify, the middle of the byline is ordered by recorded volume, from annotation ledgers, git history, the journal and the board's task lines; first and senior authorship are set at the pre-registration and changed only in the deviations file; and every author's fields live in a front-matter file with a source and a confirmed flag, so nothing unconfirmed prints ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/), leaving, handover and credit). The same page lists, among what was not yet true on 27 September, that three statements of authorship had existed and that this rule closes the gap once the owner confirms it. No later record says the owner confirmed it, so the rule is the recipe's and its confirmation is open.

## How a handoff is written

The state of the work lives in files, never in a conversation, because a session ends when its limit is hit. The lab recipe gives the shapes. A checkpoint block opens with the time, a one-line state and the owner's directive verbatim, then lists what is done with paths, job ids and hashes, the pending jobs with their collect steps, what to do when the jobs finish, what must not be redone, what is committed, what waits on the owner, and the limits every write-up must carry. A member who leaves a project opens one pull request, merged by the lead within a week, that appends a handover block to the journal, moves the name to alumni with each open task reassigned, and, if the lead leaves, makes the co-lead lead and names a new co-lead, so a project is never left without both ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/)).

The handoff of 8 October shows the same discipline applied to a whole programme: a catalog of every result with a status word that wins over any other file, a recipe for every deliverable, the rules, and an exported memory with redactions applied by the builder script so that the owner-only notes stay in the owner's folder ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), context exported). In this repository the research rule adds one more constraint: what a session finds out goes under docs/research/ in the same pull request as the work, and a handoff under docs/checkpoints/ points to it and never carries it ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), conventions).

## Rules that came from incidents

The plan asks which rules exist because of a named incident. The lab recipe states each rule beside the event that paid for it, and the following are the ones that bear on who may do what.

The private-forever list exists because the website shipped 121 MB of model weights until 14 September. The rule against personal data in a repository exists because a staff list with e-mail addresses sat in a public folder for 171 commits. The rule that a credential never goes on a command line exists because a token on a command line is in the shell history and the job log. The rule that the login node runs only submission and inspection commands exists because dozens of smoke tests ran on a node shared by about 95 users on 21 September. The rule that large outputs go on the volume and not in home exists because home reached 97 percent, 260 GB were deleted, and about 90 scripts then read an empty held-out set. The rule that a session submits, estimates, checkpoints and stops exists because on 24 September polling agents burned a session limit four times in one day while the grid did the work. And the habit that an empty, missing or defaulted input stops the run would by the recipe's own tally have prevented 16 of the 29 incidents counted on 27 September ([The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/)).

## What a new student or a fresh project reads first

A new student starts with the onboarding: request the grid account first, since it takes longest; accept the GitHub invitation; set up a Claude Project whose instructions, description and memory stay blank, so nothing typed there can drift from what the owner's threads read; install the grid extension once the owner has added the student to the testers group and the volume's access list; and take issue 19 as the first task, because it needs no data and exercises every piece of the setup ([spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/)). The handoff gives the first week in days: the handoff's own README, both repository instruction files and the lab recipe on day one; the data platform's manifest and the usage and leakage notes on day two, until the student can say which bank trained the encoder; the laptop self-tests and the power numbers on day three; one figure rebuilt from committed files on day four; and on day five, with the owner, one open item chosen and its pre-registration written before anything runs ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), a student's first week).

A fresh Claude project loads the handoff's README, its infrastructure file and its context index as standing context, and not the archived memory index, whose one-line summaries carry leaked numbers. In this repository the equivalent is automatic: CLAUDE.md imports the project file, the protocol and the memory index into every session, and the board prints at the start ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)). The owner's checklist is the one list of the steps a new account needs that only a person can take ([docs/setup/owner-checklist.md](../../docs/setup/owner-checklist.md)).

## Older and duplicate versions

No artifact this chapter reads is marked superseded by the clustering. Two are superseded on single points by later ones, and a planner should know which line went stale. ## What we know

The owner signs every freeze and submission, gives the word and the number for every grid job and training, signs every agreement, has every letter sent from the owner's university mailbox, reads a licence before any weight trained on that set leaves the grid, and merges every pull request in this repository and in spine-program. The senior scientist screens and the senior clinical principal investigator confirms clinical reads. A project lead and co-lead run a project in the student-projects tree, and the lead merges there. A student works only issues in student folders or handed over by the owner, on the student's own Claude and grid accounts, never touches a token on a command line and never merges. A session claims, works in the open, checks and stops where the owner must act, and never handles a credential or pushes anything public. Approved students read the whole holding except VinDr-SpineXR and BUU-LSPINE and their banks, which stay the owner's alone while the owner's choice on them is open. Nothing of the level-identification work goes public before the provisional is filed, and nothing moves from the research track to a commercial one.

## What we do not know

Whether the owner has confirmed the lab recipe's single authorship rule is not recorded after 27 September. Who is on the volume's access list today, and the list as applied, are not in any record this chapter read; the holding page gives the policy and the first pass, not the roster, and leaves open whether the two restricted sets stay owner-only or become readable by the volume's group. The retrospective protocol's principal investigator, its annotators, its storage and whether the grid is approved for coded data are all marked to be confirmed. The LLC plan is on defaults the owner has not confirmed.

## What would change our mind

A registered worker whose file says it lands by auto-merge would change who merges its pull requests here. A new data use agreement or a change to one of the two restricted sets' terms would change who may read them, and only the owner would make it. An approved IRB protocol for the retrospective study would add roles, a clinical co-investigator and annotators on coded data, that the lab's map does not yet hold. A confirmed LLC or a technology-transfer decision would redraw the line between the research and commercial tracks. A filed provisional would open what may be shown.
