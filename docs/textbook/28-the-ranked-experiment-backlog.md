# 28 The ranked experiment backlog

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it ranks experiments by what they settle and what they cost, names each method only by what it is for, and leaves how any stage works to Part III.

This chapter turns the open questions of the record into one list a planner can take from the top. Each entry says what question it settles, what data it needs and whether that data is held, the gate it must pass and the rule that stops it, what it costs in effort and in grid time where the record gives a figure, which word of the owner it waits on, and which chapter of this book its answer would change. The list is ranked by the evidence's own order, measurement first, human reads second and models third, and then by the calendar to the talk of 2 November. No entry below is permission to start. Every grid job, every fetch and every training waits for the owner's word and the number of jobs, and the record gives a stated job count for none of the entries that are still to run; the count is to be asked for, entry by entry, when the step is ready.

## Why this order

[Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/), the canonical roadmap of 8 October, states the order that governs the list: "measurement first, human reads second, models third". Its reason is a single fact. On ordinary spines the 2-D system is near its ceiling on the one real C-arm, but on transitional spines no number exists on a real frame with a human read, and "the verified real-frame transitional test count is zero today". Every model built before that count moves is scored against automatic truth or against renders, so the measurements and reads that create a test set are worth more than any further arm. The same page expects the first clean model to fail its validation screen and calls that failure a result to be stated plainly.

The roadmap was written from six reader notes and three independent proposals that agreed on the facts and disagreed on the axis of ranking. One ranked by how soon a number comes back that changes a decision, one by the parity idea and its feature inventory, one by whether a pre-stated claim can be decided at the n on disk. The page took the second as the programme and grafted the first's "smallest experiment that changes a decision" onto every project. This chapter keeps that graft as its rule for ties: where two entries settle questions of equal weight, the cheaper one runs first.

Three later records reshape the roadmap's sixteen-project list without overturning its order. The version 1 plan of 20 October ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)) makes the workflow the trunk every experiment hangs on. The report of 21 October ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)) turned the owner's question of how to use his segmented CTs into sixteen workstreams, A to P, with issues #57 to #70, #127 and #135 under the parent #56. And the [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) of the same day says the decision to train each model waits on a per-region census, since the counts per region decide where each model can be tested at all. So the census rises to the top, and the roadmap's one-hour single-vertebra 3-D target (PAR-1) belongs to the parity programme that [chapter 27](27-open-questions.md) holds, not to the line ranked here.

The calendar is the other constraint. The provisional is to be filed by 30 October and the talk is on 2 November; the version 1 schedule put the registry and first shards on 23 October, version 1 trained by 28 October and latency with the recorded sweep by 29 October, and a synthesis of the notes of 23 October records that the first ingest of a real frame had not yet happened, a slip that moves every later date ([docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md), section 3).

## Measurement first

### 1. The per-region census

The question is how many real X-rays, annotated or not, real AP and lateral pairs, X-rays with a CT of the same person, and segmented CTs the holding has in each region of the spine: cervical, thoracic, lumbar and sacral. The Any-View Spine Plan makes every later training decision wait on it, because the per-region counts decide where each model has enough real material to be tested. The first census ran on 22 October as the sample index, 140 groups, 7,008,826 files and 7.3 TB ([docs/memory/sample-index.md](../../docs/memory/sample-index.md); [docs/research/data-manifest/MANIFEST.md](../../docs/research/data-manifest/MANIFEST.md)), and the plan's table was filled from it. Three large sets, NHANES II, VinDr-SpineXR and FluoroCap, still wait for the next run's split by body part, so the entry is that second run.

What the first run already shows decides several entries below. No real AP and lateral pair is held above the lumbar spine; the only real X-ray with a CT of the same subject above L1 is a single cadaver reaching T2, not yet fetched; lumbar pairs number 3,600 people in BUU-LSPINE and 149 cases in C-ArmLSpine; and the sacral region holds COBRA's 692 cases and DeepFluoro's six specimens ([Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/); [docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md), section 4). The plan's thresholds of what makes an approach testable, such as about 100 persons per region for a gold check of the 3-D prior and about 25 real frames of a new device for detection, are Inferred from the papers it read.

The gate is those thresholds: a region below them gets no test of that approach, and its claim rests on renders until pairs are found, which the plan states for the thoracic and cervical spine. The cost the record measured is the first run's: a scan array of 64 tasks of 8 to 97 minutes each, a merge of about five hours and a report step of about five more. The data is held. It waits on the owner's word for the census word to be sent again and on the count of its jobs. Its answer changes [chapter 4](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md) and the any-view section of [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md).

### 2. The three direct physics tests

[What Is Left After Physics](../../artifacts/what-is-left-after-physics/), canonical for renders and the sim-to-real gap, decomposes four real DeepFluoro frames against renders of their own CTs and withdraws an earlier board's claim that a learned CT-to-fluoroscopy model "would have nothing local to learn". Its own stated limit is that nothing in it connects to the detector's misses. It names three tests with no number anywhere in the record, each asking whether the appearance gap that remains after the physics renderer is what holds real-frame detection down; [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) holds what each test does. The one closest relative that was measured, a training-free swap from real frames on 21 September, gave 74 percent against a 64 percent baseline, a record, not a final value, and inside the seed spread.

The data is held: DeepFluoro's 366 real frames with exact pose, and the earlier arms' folds. The gate is the seed spread. The page warns that every effect size in the literature it verified is smaller than the 26-point spread this detector showed across seeds at one seed, so each test is read with seeds averaged and specimens as folds, and an arm inside the spread shows no effect however steady its sign. The September template of six folds by three seeds implies eighteen trainings an arm, Inferred, not stated for these tests. One of the three, the correlation of missed frames with the fitted appearance terms and the view angle, needs no training and is the cheapest; it could run before the talk on the owner's word. The other two wait on his word and the count. A positive answer would change the renders chapter's account of what holds recall down; a null one would leave population and real frames as the levers.

## Human reads second

### 3. The human reads on the three benches and the CT queue

[Chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md) holds the three benches: the level verification bench, a random seeded sample of 179 panels and 865 vertebrae meant to give the first unbiased estimate of real-panel accuracy; the LSTV verification bench, 585 figures harvested for transitional terms, for finding examples rather than measuring accuracy; and the FluoroCap adjudication bench, 120 panels across 31 strata, for seeing which evidence fails. None has a stored verdict that any export shows, each keeps one record per item with no reader named, and two of the three show the machine's answer. The first step is therefore to read the three stores and record what they hold, then key every read by reader before a second reader starts.

The reads the record values most are not on the benches. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/), canonical for FluoroCap, set a two-reader read of 545 panels, about plus or minus 3 points around 85 percent, for the first two weeks of its plan, at a day or two for two readers; no record says it happened, and the 200-panel FluoroCap test set does not exist ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 35). On the transitional side, Research directions names "the single most decisive afternoon in the holding": a reader on the 11 field-supported COBRA L6 calls, because they can retire the real-frame transitional claim before any model is built. Behind them stand a one-hour coverage render, to see whether COBRA's field holds the sacral anchor at all, and the 63-volume queue (57 field-supported COBRA calls, 3 re-read candidates, 3 C-ArmLSpine CBCTs) read on CT under a frozen protocol.

The gates are the roadmap's. Exact level on transitional real frames at a Wilson lower bound of 90 percent needs 35 persons with no error; variant sensitivity at 85 percent needs 22 carriers with no miss; today the verified count is zero and seven at-risk persons have a frame, none verified. The expected yield of the 63-volume queue is about 5 L6 and at most 19 verifiable, so 22 carriers cannot come from COBRA alone. The stop rule is stated in advance: if the read confirms fewer than 22 carriers with an exported frame, no fluoroscopy transitional sensitivity is claimed and the render number is reported alone. The data is held; the readers are not, and the owner named a senior neuroradiologist as the second transitional reader ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), section 8). The reads spend no grid time; the coverage render is one job and waits on the owner's word for it. A completed read changes [chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md), [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) and, for the panels, [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md).

## Models third, cheapest first

### 4. The counting prior from 3-D at decode

The largest error in the record is AP naming: zero-shot on C-ArmLSpine it names 276 of 631 vertebrae, 43.7 percent, a one-level cranial shift of the whole column, against 96.4 percent (595 of 617, [92.3, 98.4]) on lateral frames ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)). The concept ledger marks 43.7 percent current over the September page's 37.88 ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)). A whole-column shift is a counting error, not an appearance error, and workstream A (issue #57) targets it with a prior about the column learned from the segmented CTs and applied when the levels are read out, with no training and no new frames. What the prior is and how it is applied belong to [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md). Its claim to fix the shift is Inferred; the closest published analogue is in CT, and none was found on radiographs.

It is built and waits on the grid ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md), section 5); its only run so far was a dry run of the plumbing on a synthetic catalog with an untrained model, 14 of 68 against 11 of 68, which the report itself calls a test of the plumbing and not a result. The data is held, with one gap: the grid paths of the CT segmentations are recorded in another repository the sessions could not read, so the first job lists them before it reads them. Workstream F (issue #62) feeds it readers' calls made on CT. The gate is the validation witness frames, AP accuracy before and after, with the transitional stratum reported apart; the record states no numeric bar, and the stop rule is the risk it names, that the prior forces a confident wrong answer on a transitional spine, answered by abstaining where the hypotheses lie close. Cost: 1 to 2 days of work, one CPU job of about 1 to 2 hours on 32 cores to fit the prior, and a decode over the reported stack's dumped AP posteriors in CPU minutes. It waits on the owner's word and the count for that one job.

### 5. AP and oblique renders with projected anchors

Workstream C (issue #59) renders new frames from CTSpinoPelvic1K and the TotalSegmentator whole-spine subjects along the arc where naming collapses, AP and the obliques toward it, matched to C-ArmLSpine's angle bins but never to its persons, carrying the anchors down from 3-D, and fine-tunes version 1 from its checkpoint on them. Workstream B (issue #58) rides with it: the rare-class renders already made, banked as train-side shards after the person-key chain shows no source CT is a test person's, since the only real L6 persons held are the three frozen test persons. What the renders carry and how the fine-tune uses them is [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md) and [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md).

The reason it ranks here is the record's clearest transfer finding, Measured on the reported stack: renders carry naming but not detection. A renders-only detector found 1 of 287 vertebrae on C-ArmLSpine at its working threshold, while the encoder whose only intraoperative appearance came from renders named lateral frames at 96.4 percent. The data is held. The gate is the validation witness frames; B's renders are dropped if they hurt. The cost the record estimates is 2 to 3 days, 1 to 3 GPU-hours to render about 100,000 frames on one H200, and the bank and leak jobs for B at about an hour of CPU. The fine-tune's bound is not settled: three notes cite a 6 GPU-hour bound for the version 1 run that the version 1 plan does not state, so the figure is the owner's to set ([docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md), section 3, item 1). It waits on his word for the render job, the bank and leak jobs and the fine-tune.

### 7. The leave-one-device-out designs

The ledger's route to "one network for any C-arm" was a synthetic corpus styled per device, trained with each device left out in turn and tested on that device's real frames, two designs of six trainings in all, queued on 22 September. It stopped short of a claim: the styled frames were "not yet statistically inside the real domain", and until that check passes "the claim that physics-styled synthetic frames match real ones is not made". No outcome of the six trainings is recorded (renders contradictions, row 53). Workstream E (issue #61) carries the same measure into October: every layer of device harmonisation is scored by leave-one-device-out accuracy, the held-out-device design the record already uses ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)). What styling and harmonisation do is [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md).

The design matters more than any single arm because of what the held-out-device evidence already shows. A detector with no frame of the test device finds 7.0 percent of bodies; with real frames of other devices it finds 80.5 percent (231 of 287, [74.1, 86.8]), Measured on the reported stack; 24 frames of the device add three to four points and flatten ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)). The September re-score of the same 287 vertebrae under a matched box convention, 90.6 percent, is a record, not a final value, beside 80.5 percent until the run behind the latter names its convention ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 13). Research directions adds that six devices are needed for any two-sided P at or under 0.05. The data is held for four devices (C-ArmLSpine, COBRA's Elekta cases, DeepFluoro and the Geneva phantom). The gate is the styled-frame check first, then held-out accuracy per device. E's estimate is 3 to 7 days and 10 to 20 GPU-hours. It waits on the owner's word and the count, and its result changes the device-transfer sections of [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md).

### 8. The two-anchor arms as a matched pair

[Counting Against Two Anchors](../../artifacts/counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu/), canonical for transitional vertebrae, designs a 3-D model that counts from two anchors so that a transitional column shows up as a disagreement between them; [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) holds the heads, the arms and the patch. Its first two arms run as a matched pair on the same folds, and their difference is the result. Research directions carries it as CT-7 with a gate already written: a receiver operating curve on the count disagreement separating the 26 transitional-labelled records at counts four and six from the 757 normal-labelled ones at four and five. None of it has run, and whether the arms ever ran in the earlier programme's repository is in no source read.

The data is held: the 802 CTSpinoPelvic1K records. The roadmap ranks it fourteenth of sixteen because it waits on three things before it: the canal-closing fix of CT-1, the pre-registration freeze, and readers for the disputed records, since a re-read that moves a record changes the split and the leak audit is run again before anything is scored. The record gives no GPU-hour figure and no fold count for it. It waits on the owner's word and the count once those three are done, and it is after the talk. Its answer is the 3-D side of the transitional design in [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md).

### 9. The registration route on COBRA

[Lift Before Label?](../../artifacts/lift-before-label-jzjq8t/), canonical with What Is Left on the renders question, answers yes to using CT-to-frame registration as a source of labels: it puts an exact level on a real frame with no reader counting on the image, and the registered real frames are worth more as fine-tuning data than their rendered twins. October turned that into workstream D (issue #60): COBRA's real projections labelled from each case's own planning CT through the stored geometry, which also gives real sweeps for the per-angle curve and, beside the release's simulated projections at the same geometry, the one kind of pair that measures the simulation gap directly. Workstream O (issue #127), the owner's ask of 22 October, extends it to gold masks on every paired CT and X-ray set, verified by a second reader.

The data is held: 692 COBRA cases, of which 145 Elekta cases are banked and 225 Varian cases have never been exported; no vertebra masks ship with it. Its limit is the field: the sacrum is defined on 18.2 percent of 609 test frames, so COBRA teaches the low lumbar levels and the sacral anchor and nothing thoracic or cervical. The gate is a person looking at a sample of projected corners, preceded by entry 3's one-hour coverage render, which answers whether L5 and the sacral ala are in the field at all. If the field does not reach the anchor, COBRA serves the detector's sacrum and not the count. The record's estimate is 3 to 5 days of work, 3 to 6 GPU-hours to segment the 692 planning CTs and 50 to 100 CPU-hours for the projection as one array job, plus the readers' hours for O. It waits on the owner's word and the count for each job, and only the coverage render belongs before the talk. Its result changes [chapter 5](05-real-fluoroscopy-and-c-arm-sets.md), [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) and the anchor sections of [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md).

### 11. The transitional cross-validation

On 18 September Naming Vertebrae Without CT had eight held-out transitional spines, the one six-lumbar among them named wrongly, and quoted no figure because eight "cannot distinguish 60% from 95%". It announced a cross-validation over all 376 transitional patients, each round training without a third and testing on that third, "the only way to get a real number from the data on hand". No source records an outcome (level-naming contradictions, row 40), and how many transitional patients exist across all sources is open in the concept ledger, between 190 and 376 ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-55-transitional-patients-available-across-all-sources)). Most of those persons are CT persons seen through renders, and 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons sit in the earlier encoder's training lineage, so any run on that lineage is not held out.

The roadmap's clean version of the same question is FLU-2: an encoder started from ImageNet on the G3 training persons only, scored on the 7 render-scoreable of the 11 G3 validation L6 persons. Its stop rule is the strongest in the record: zero or one miss of the 7 proceeds to three seeds and two heads; two or more stops the fluoroscopy arm, and the manuscript says "not at parity" with the number. No test person is scored until paper 2's ledger carries an "H1 seen" entry, and whether the version 1 schedule honours that rule is a cannot-tell in the record ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 1). The data is held. Three rounds is the count the September design implies; FLU-2 starts with one seed and then three. It waits on the owner's word and the count, and its result rewrites the measured-numbers section of [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md).

### 13. The retrospective collection

The [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), a draft for the university's eProtocol system, would collect the archived intraoperative stills and cine loops of a clinical collaborator's spine operations from 2021 to 2026, with level truth from the operative note and post-operative imaging. Its primary endpoint is per-body detector recall on held-out cases, its first hypothesis at least 97 percent with a half-width of one point, which the normal approximation sizes at about 1,100 held-out bodies; a pilot query of 20 cases fixes frames per case and the held-out fraction before the test set is sealed. Its stated limitation is that archived stills were not taken under the counting protocol, so the share of countable cases is unknown.

The data is not held: nothing has been pulled. [Chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md) holds the protocol's route and two Inferred cautions, that the interval sizes an estimate rather than a test of "at least 97 percent", and that bodies cluster within patients. The grant puts the submission in the first quarter of 2027. Training on its frames would be grid work and waits on the owner's word and the count; the collection itself waits on the owner's submission. It is the last entry because it is the slowest, not because it is the least valuable: it is the only route in the record to a number on the hospital's own devices and patients.

## Routes refuted and not to be run again

Five routes were tried or weighed and failed on named evidence; [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) holds the detail. They are listed so nobody proposes them again without the finding that would readmit them.

- Adversarial translation (CycleGAN, ADDA). On SyntheX's six DeepFluoro specimens neither beat no adaptation, and under strong randomisation CycleGAN added nothing. Readmitted only if an arm with it beats the same arm without it by more than the seed spread, seeds averaged and persons as folds.
- Diffusion image-to-image restyling. At strengths that looked like fluoroscopy it had lost the vertebrae, and no strength was usable. Readmitted only by a strength or conditioning that keeps the projected labels on their bodies, checked frame by frame. Its output, the synth_rare frames, is a loose thread: whether their labels are exact is to be settled by a count and a look before entry 5 banks them.
- Reconstruction, lifting one frame to 3-D before naming. No paper reports a gain over the 2-D classifier from one view, and the prior completes an L6 or an absent rib toward the typical spine, which is what decides the label. Readmitted only by a lift that beats the direct 2-D route on real held-out frames.
- Super-resolution. Rejected without a run: detail below the CT's resolution is not in the input, so what a network adds there is invented. Readmitted only by a controlled radiograph study showing restored task-relevant detail.
- Synthetic generation of whole CTs. Rendered, 3-D-generated CTs lost to real frames and to 2-D X-ray diffusion on DeepFluoro (Rapuri 2026, as Lift Before Label? reports). Readmitted only if a vertebra-conditioned generator at higher resolution reverses that, the open question Lift itself leaves.

The refutation is narrower than the list sounds. CUT's no-gain verdict rests on a first version trained toward panels most of which were not spine radiographs and a second version reported nowhere. And the October programme reinstates generative translation inside two later workstreams: L (issue #68), view translation, whose structured output comes first and whose synthesised picture is a teaching signal only, and P (issue #135), which carries naming from the lumbosacral gold to C2 and ends in a reverse ablation that removes one ingredient at a time. The two reconcile only on the evidence's own terms: P keeps its translation only if the arm without it loses by more than the seed spread. Editing CTs to make rare anatomy (workstream N, issue #70) is not refuted, only untried.

## Older versions

## What we know

The record's order is measurement, then human reads, then models, and it rests on one fact: no verified transitional test person exists on any real frame. Renders carry naming and not detection: lateral naming on a device never seen is 96.4 percent and a renders-only detector finds almost nothing, while AP naming at 43.7 percent is a whole-column shift. The counting prior is built and needs one CPU job. The clean DeepFluoro retrain, the leave-one-device-out trainings, the real-film sweep arms and the transitional cross-validation were each started or queued in September and none has a recorded outcome. Five routes are refuted on named evidence. Every entry waits on the owner's word and job count.

## What we do not know

Whether the counting prior fixes the AP shift, or forces wrong answers on transitional spines. Whether the appearance gap left after physics explains any detector miss. Whether any of the three benches' stores holds a verdict, and whether the 545-panel read or any CT re-read has been done. How many transitional patients the sources hold, between 190 and 376. Whether COBRA's field reaches L5 and the sacral ala. What the September jobs on DeepFluoro, the device designs, the real-film arms and the 376-patient cross-validation returned, if they finished. What the version 1 fine-tune may spend. Whether the seed grant was submitted, and when the retrospective protocol goes in.

## What would change our mind

A census run showing real pairs or gold CT pairs above L1 at the per-region thresholds would raise the thoracic and cervical entries now left to renders. A counting prior that fixes the AP shift on the validation witness frames would demote entry 5's anchor renders to a robustness study; one that fails would raise them. Eleven COBRA L6 calls read as false positives would retire the real-frame transitional claim from the holding and leave the requests and the retrospective collection as the only routes, raising entry 13. A physics test with an effect beyond the seed spread would reopen appearance as a lever and readmit M ahead of the device designs. A recorded outcome for any September job would replace its entry with a reading of that outcome. And an arm of workstream P whose translation beats the arm without it beyond the spread would readmit generative translation for that use.
