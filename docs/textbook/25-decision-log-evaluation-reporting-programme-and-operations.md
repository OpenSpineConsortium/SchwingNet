# 25 Decision log: evaluation, reporting, programme and operations

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it records decisions and their reasons, names each method only by what it is for, and leaves how any stage works to Part III.

This chapter logs every decision outside data and method: how a number is reported, what happens to the papers, the order of the programme's work, the rules of the grid, the governance the project has settled, and how a new person or Claude Project starts. Each decision carries its date, its owner by role, its status, the reason the record gives and what would reverse it, so a planner can tell a rule that answers a measured incident from a habit nobody has examined. The families are R (reporting), V (papers and venues), P (programme), O (operations), G (governance) and N (onboarding). Method decisions M1 to M23 live only in [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md); data, licence and split decisions are in [chapter 24](24-decision-log-data-licences-and-splits.md). A pull request that changes a decision here should name its number.

## Reporting: how a number may be said

### R1, accuracy with coverage

On 16 September the overnight report [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) changed how the project reports a level model. Its reason was that no published system reaches 99 % on plain radiographs, so a single accuracy at the target would be untrue; the system instead reports a pair, how often it is right on the cases it answers and how many it answers, with the whole risk-coverage curve rather than one flattering operating point. The decision was a working session's, recorded as the agent's own.

The rule outlived the number that prompted it. The page's headline, 99 % accuracy among answered at 35.7 % coverage on held-out renders, came from the allspine v2 family, which the handoff of 8 October marks leaked; that figure is quoted here as a record, not a final value, from [the overnight report](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/), and the handoff names "the 99 % selective-accuracy claim" among the numbers that are record only. A planner should keep the reporting form and discard the figure. How a coverage-accuracy pair is computed and bounded is chapter 18's ([Metrics, intervals and witness frames](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)); what makes the system decline a frame is a method and belongs to [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md). What would reverse R1 is a system that answers every frame at the stated accuracy on held-out real frames, at which point coverage is 100 % and the pair collapses to one number.

### R2, intervals clustered at the case

[Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) of 22 September made every confidence interval clustered at the case, because frames within a case are not independent; the page reports that a naive interval covered the truth 70.8 % of the time. That coverage figure is Reported: this chapter did not read the simulation behind it. The retraining plan of 20 October keeps the rule and moves its unit: every naming number carries a Wilson interval on person-keyed n, the evaluation gate's rule ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md), section 4). The move matters because a case is not always a person. C-ArmLSpine's records give 149 sessions and 120 distinct subjects without settling which counts ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 22, cannot-tell), so an interval clustered at the case may still be too narrow where one person has several cases. R2 would be revised, not reversed, by a person table for each source that shows cases and persons coincide.

### R3, per source and never pooled

Three records give one rule. The [Vertebra Detector Field Report](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/) reports results per source and per acquisition, never pooled, because a pooled number would hide the generalisation behaviour worth measuring. The research directions of 8 October add that a pooled device number is never quoted without the device-held-out arm beside it, and that the published 0.990 for one thoracolumbar shape test is reported as a ceiling, never as the three-dimensional side of parity, because each C-arm is its own domain and the 0.990 is a within-patient number. The owner's plan of 20 October keeps the transitional stratum in its own rows, never pooled, at every version. The overnight review of 16 September applies the same rule to the literature (V5). Chapter 19 ([What a valid comparison needs](19-what-a-valid-comparison-needs.md)) turns R3 into the conditions for setting two numbers side by side. Nothing in the record argues for reversing it; a pooled figure may be printed only beside the per-source ones it summarises.

### R4, within-one-level as primary, and how it narrowed

The [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) of 13 September called within-one-level "the honest primary metric for a level-confirmation tool", because at wide context most remaining errors were whole-case shifts of every vertebra by one level together, a limit of enumeration rather than of each vertebra's appearance. No owner is recorded. Every later record the log reads makes the exact level the headline: the handoff's results, the OR ledger and the retraining plan's per-level naming accuracy all report the exact name. The log therefore records R4 as narrowed: within-one remains the right secondary figure wherever a whole-case shift is the dominant error, and a planner who reports a confirmation tool may lead with it, but the programme's claims are about the exact level. The judgment is Inferred from the later records; no artifact says R4 was withdrawn.

### R5, nothing from eight transitional spines

[Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) of 18 September decided that no figure is quoted from the eight held-out transitional spines, because eight spines cannot tell 60 % from 95 %. Its superseded twin of the same day, [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/), alone adds that no rate from the reader study is quoted either, since its ten misnamed transitional frames were a census, not a sample. The research directions of 8 October generalise the rule into numbers: 22 carriers for an 85 % sensitivity bound, 35 error-free at-risk persons for a 90 % bound, and, if the read confirms fewer than 22 carriers with an exported frame, no fluoroscopy transitional sensitivity is claimed and the render number is reported alone. On 8 October the verified real-frame transitional test count was zero. R5 is reversed only by reaching those counts; chapter 19 gives the power behind them.

### R6, the catalog wins

The handoff's author, a working session, decided that where any other file or memory note differs from the results catalog of 8 October, the catalog wins, because it was one record of every result found on disk that day, each row with its file, model, bank, split, truth, tool, job and a status word. The rule binds the old programme's numbers. SchwingNet's own numbers come from a different record: every row of the new evaluation stamps the version, the shard list's hash, the commit and the clean-tree flag, and a dirty tree is refused ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md), section 4). A planner quoting an old number goes to the catalog; one quoting a version 1 number goes to the run record. Where the two speak of the same claim, the log's reading is that the newer run record wins, since the catalog was a snapshot and the run record is produced by the result itself; the record has no case of the two disagreeing yet.

### R7, manifest keys and the freeze

The owner's rule, stated in the handoff's ten rules and in the Lab Recipe, is that every printed number enters the manuscript through a manifest key and never by typing, and that a study is pre-registered, frozen with its hash and then scored, with every departure in a dated deviations file. The recipe's reason is that pre-stated rules make a negative result reportable: when one corpus came out against the render-ablation rule, that cell went into the manuscript. The recipe also records how far the rule had reached on 27 September, and it had not reached far: the SchwingNet tables carried 167 bare numerals because the check scanned only the prose, and none of 79 manifest keys began with a job id. Those gaps are Reported from the recipe; whether they closed is not in the sources this chapter read. The retraining plan carries the same discipline into the new workflow as run records and a frozen witness set. Nothing would reverse R7; only its gaps close.

### R8 and R9, report the failure, and sample at random

Counting From The Sacrum decided on 22 September that both the naming result and the unsolved detection problem are reported, because reporting both is what makes the first credible. The standing determination of the next day (P1) says the same of parity: report "not at parity" plainly if that is the result. The Lab Recipe's checklist for a submission asks that failed bars be reported as failures. Together these make a negative result a deliverable, not an embarrassment.

The [Level Verification Bench](../../artifacts/level-verification-bench-mkjxsy/) set how a human check of model output is drawn: a random sample within each route with a fixed seed, not a set the model's own uncertainty picked, so the accuracy is an unbiased estimate; can't-tell marks leave the denominator; and the sample is the measurement, never training data. The overnight report gives the reason in a published example where noisy validation labels made the apparently best model look worse than it was. Chapter 17 ([Benches and human reads](17-benches-and-human-reads.md)) holds the benches themselves.

### R10, leaked families as records

The handoff records the owner's decision that numbers from the allspine v2, v6 and v7 families, the anchor_drr split and vindrps are not quoted, because each comes from a leaked family; the clean replacement is named beside each in the catalog. On 23 October the owner revised this for the book: such a number may be quoted as a record, with a direct link to its source and the words that it is not a final value, so that it can be updated later. Allspine v2's 99.02 % plain-film figure is the example the owner named; it is a record, not a final value, from [the handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/). The held-out rule that defines a leak, and the split that fixed it, are data decisions and sit in [chapter 24](24-decision-log-data-licences-and-splits.md). The OR ledger of 22 September made three smaller decisions of the same kind: a 100 % sacrum figure on one cadaver set was ruled not the method's and sent to a clean retrain, the results on a sawbone phantom were declared "a sheet to look at, not a number to quote" until projected truth exists, and a held-out detection cell was re-scored under one truth convention before any comparison ([OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/); chapter 19 gives the convention finding).

## The programme's order

### P1, the standing determination

The research directions quote the standing determination of 23 September: name the level correctly on a transitional spine (L6, T13, sacralised L5, four lumbar) from C-arm exposures, at parity with an ordinary spine, and report "not at parity" plainly if that is the result. The record names no owner for it. Its reason is that it is narrower and stronger than "anomaly-aware": one Castellvi type is unrecoverable from a lateral by construction, so the target is the count-changing types. The owner's goal of 20 October is broader, low-latency, high-accuracy vertebral classification on fluoroscopic video or images ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md), section 1), and it carries P1 as the transitional stratum reported in its own rows at every version (R3). The log reads P1 as still in effect inside the broader goal; only the owner's restatement would end it. How a transitional spine is named is chapter 14's.

### P2, measurement first, human reads second, models third

The research directions of 8 October, written by the owner from six reader notes and three independent proposals, set one order of work: measurement first, human reads second, models third. The reason was that the two-dimensional system was at ceiling on ordinary spines while no number existed on a real transitional frame with a human read, so the first projects should define the three-dimensional target and measure which features survive projection, the reader work that creates power should start in the same week, and models should wait for a frozen truth file and a clean encoder. Week one had no model in it. The page took proposal B as the programme, because the three asks were its structure and its feature-level specification was what a student could act on, and grafted proposal A's rule of the smallest experiment that changes a decision and its ordering of the hour-long ratio-only run first, and proposal C's venue and power discipline. Three rules ran through the order: if the re-reads move a record, the split changes and the leak audit is re-run before the clean encoder scores anyone; no weight trained with one licensed film set leaves until the owner has read its licence; and nothing trains on the flagship recipe before the first gates close. The ranked list that follows from P2 is chapter 28's ([The ranked experiment backlog](28-the-ranked-experiment-backlog.md)); the method decisions on views and order of work are M21 to M23 in chapter 16.

The owner's words of 20 October changed the order in practice. They put the data workflow first, made the first model trained to work version 1, and asked that evaluation on real frames be visible from the first checkpoint; the grid pipeline of 21 October schedules version 1 as trained on 28 October. Whether the 8 October order still binds is one of the record's open disagreements ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 0, cannot-tell), and so is whether a held-out transitional test person may be scored before the paper's ledger records the hypothesis as seen (row 1, cannot-tell). The log's reading, Inferred, is that the two orders answer different claims and can both hold: the October 20 order governs what is built and shown before the talk, which is version 1 on ordinary spines with its transitional rows reported as they come out; the October 8 order still governs any transitional claim, which needs the human reads first and the counts of R5 before it is made. A planner should not read version 1's existence as permission to claim transitional parity. P2 would be re-ordered by verified real-frame transitional persons arriving, which removes the reason models had to wait, or by the owner restating which order binds.

### P3, one miss of 43

The research directions record the owner's choice, as the page's author, that the 85 % bound on the 43 test L6 persons allows one miss, not two, because 42 of 43 gives 87.9 % and 41 of 43 gives 84.5 %. Proposals A and C had allowed two. The figures are the page's own arithmetic and are Reported here. The clean encoder's screen has its own stop rule, zero or one miss of seven render-scoreable validation persons to proceed and two or more to stop with the "not at parity" sentence, which the page justifies by the screen's pass rates at assumed true recalls. Both rules are reversed only by a different pre-registered bound.

### P4 and P5, the owner's word, and what drops first

The repository's goal page and its durable decisions hold that no training job, grid job or Claude run is submitted without the owner's word and the number of jobs, and that usage credits are never used ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md)). The research directions add that each later model variant is built only after the owner's decision. The synthesis reads of the textbook are the one standing exception the owner made, on 23 October, with a daily cap. P4 is the most important operating fact for a planner: a plan that needs a job ends at the job's description and waits.

The grid pipeline of 21 October records that version 1 no longer waits on the multi-day X-ray fetch and starts from the first catalog, which holds C-ArmLSpine, because the X-ray sets reach the next catalog version as they arrive. The retraining plan's section 9 sets what drops first if time runs short, some film rows of the evaluation set, the CPU latency and the export step, and what never drops: the registry, version 1 and the evaluation page. On the same day the owner turned the full CT-RATE fetch, 21.3 TB, off; it is printed, never submitted. Both decisions are tied to the talk and are expected to be revisited after 2 November.

## Operations: the grid's rules and the incidents behind them

Every operating rule here exists because something went wrong, and the Lab Recipe prints each beside its incident. The rules bind every job, including a ten-record test. How a run starts, waits and finishes on the grid is [chapter 21](21-the-grid-how-a-run-starts-waits-and-finishes.md); quotas and cost are [chapter 22](22-quotas-cost-and-the-budget-of-a-run.md); Claude workers on the grid are [chapter 31](31-claude-workers-on-the-grid-and-the-labs-machines.md).

### O1, nothing on the login node

The recipe's first rule allows the login node only submission, queue reads, cancellation, listing, tailing and git, after dozens of smoke tests ran on a node shared by about 95 users on 21 September; downloads are jobs too. The onboarding of 13 October states the owner's stricter form: nothing runs on the login node, every computation is a job, even a one-minute one. A general cluster playbook of 3 October allows a small ceiling of work there; the contradiction page rules the onboarding's rule the one that holds on this grid (row 41, confirmed). Nothing would reverse O1.

### O2, afterany, and where the gate departs from it

The recipe's third rule is that a chain uses afterany, never afterok, and the child reads a file to decide whether its parent succeeded, because on 9 September an eight-hour limit left a whole chain unsatisfiable. The SchwingNet gate of 21 October does not follow it everywhere: its graph links setup, the first catalog and the version 1 run with afterok and uses afterany for the X-ray chain and the leak checks ([SchwingNet grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/)). No record says this departure was decided or why. The log's reading, Inferred, is that an afterok edge in the version 1 path trades the recipe's protection for a simpler failure, a job that never starts rather than one that starts on missing input, and that a planner who extends the gate should either keep the afterok edges short or follow the recipe and check the parent's output file. Either a recorded decision or a repeat of the September incident would settle it.

### O3 and O4, the requeue partition and the resume record

The recipe's student templates were fixed on 27 September to use the requeue partition and its QoS for CPU work, node-local scratch in shared memory with a trap to remove it, explicit checks and afterany. On 21 October the owner ordered that SchwingNet's grid jobs use the lab's existing, tested Slurm knowledge: every job is now queued through hpcworker's job submit with a class, the CPU class meaning the same requeue partition, and no job header of ours names a partition or a QoS ([docs/memory/grid-jobs-go-through-hpcworker-job-submit.md](../../docs/memory/grid-jobs-go-through-hpcworker-job-submit.md), issue 77). O3 is replaced in form and kept in substance.

The owner's rule of 27 September is that before anything is cancelled, every job is recorded with its script header as the resume instruction. The gate keeps the same habit: it records every job it submits, and its stop word cancels only its own jobs. The handoff adds the warning that resume is not reproduce, since chains write to fixed folders and come back unchanged unless the folder is moved aside.

### O5 and O6, no polling, and status computed

The recipe limits an agent's queue polling to about ten minutes in all: an agent submits, estimates, checkpoints and stops, because on 24 September polling agents burned a session limit four times in one day while the grid did the work. A playbook's rule on how often to poll is a different limit, and both hold together (row 42, refuted as a contradiction). The recipe also holds that status is computed from disk and queue by a tool, never hand-written, because a hand-written table listed a job as pending three hours after the scheduler showed it cancelled. The gate's status word follows it, reading the scheduler's accounting and each stage's report.

### O7, O8 and O9, Nextflow, the shipping commands and a second worker

The recipe uses Nextflow only for a pipeline of three or more stages over many cases, mostly on CPU and mostly other people's tools, and keeps the GPU census as the scheduler's arrays it already is; none of the imaging repositories runs Nextflow, and the public tutorial that said otherwise was to be corrected. The contradiction page finds no conflict with the later checklist (row 43, refuted).

The onboarding forbids PowerShell for the commands that ship code to the grid, because it breaks the stream, and forbids saving a job script with a heredoc.
