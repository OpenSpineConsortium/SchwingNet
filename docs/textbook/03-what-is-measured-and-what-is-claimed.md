# 03 What is measured and what is claimed

Label: schwingnet-internal. The chapter sets the programme's own numbers beside the records they rest on, for the project's planners only, and it stays in this private repository with the rest of the book until the provisional is filed on 30 October 2026.

This chapter lays every headline number of the SchwingNet work, from naming on real C-arm laterals to the count of verified real-frame transitional test persons, which is zero, beside what it rests on, so that a planner never quotes a leaked, superseded or render-only figure as a final value. Each is graded from the contradictions pages and the concept ledger, and each disagreement is left where the record leaves it, with what would settle it named. Two rules frame the rest: where files disagree, the earlier programme's results catalog wins, and this repository's version 1 workflow has so far produced numbers only on a synthetic bank.

## How a number is read here

### Four grades and where they come from

A figure is Measured here only where a repository record read for this chapter tables the run with its file. For real frames that record is [docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), which copies the reported stack's rows from `01/STATUS.md` in spine-program with their ids, counts and intervals; STATUS.md itself was not read, so "reported stack" stands beside the grade, and a figure that record gives with neither count nor interval is graded Reported. For the new workflow the records are its evaluation, dry-run and latency pages, each linked where it is used. A figure an artifact gives is Reported even where the artifact says it checked it: [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) says every number on it is verified against a file on disk, and no such file was read for this book. A judgment, or a design claim with no outcome, is Inferred; a figure with no source, or one another record contradicts, is Unverified.

### The catalog wins

The [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) names one record above every other, `RESULTS_CATALOG.md`: one row per result found on disk that day, with its file, a status word from CURRENT to OPEN, RECORD ONLY and SUPERSEDED among them, and the rigor marks of the 22 September audit, such as leaked, selection split, one seed and automatic truth. Its rule: "Where any other file or memory note differs, the catalog wins." The catalog lives with the earlier programme's handoff, outside this repository, and was not read for this book, so where this chapter cannot settle a disagreement it carries both values with their pages and names the catalog row as what would settle it.

### Two bodies of numbers

Every real-frame number below belongs to the reported stack, the earlier programme's models and results in spine-program's `01_carm_level_detection`. The new workflow was run end to end twice on 21 October and scored 556 of 556 and 430 of 431 test crops ([docs/research/evaluation/README.md](../../docs/research/evaluation/README.md)), on a synthetic bank built so that a trainer that reads nothing scores chance ([docs/research/v1-demo/README.md](../../docs/research/v1-demo/README.md)). Those runs measure the workflow, not SchwingNet. The evaluation page holds a trap: its synthetic frames are keyed to the five frozen C-ArmLSpine test persons, and a row reads "source carmlspine" at 430 of 431, yet no frame of any person was read. Version 1 has no detector stage yet, so every row it reports is scored over truth boxes. The [SchwingNet grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/) page of 21 October counted zero grid jobs, "every number so far is from synthetic CPU runs"; the grid jobs run since, a probe for the latency work and a census of the holdings ([docs/research/data-manifest/README.md](../../docs/research/data-manifest/README.md)), score no model.

### The contradictions record has an error rate of its own

The verdicts this chapter leans on were themselves sampled and read again on 23 October: of 30 drawn at random that put a candidate on the confirmed list, 26 held (87 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 70 to 95), and of 24 that kept one off it, 22 held (92 percent, 74 to 98) ([the contradictions of the artifacts](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#how-far-the-verdicts-hold)). A row is evidence, not proof. The pages also disagree with each other, and where they do this chapter names both.

## The one real intraoperative human set

C-ArmLSpine (Surgio-B51) is, in the words of the [State of the Literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/), "exactly one real intraoperative human set with a volume". COBRA2026 is radiotherapy cone-beam projections of the pelvis ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)), and DeepFluoro is six cadaveric pelves on one device. [Chapter 05](05-real-fluoroscopy-and-c-arm-sets.md) describes the set; what matters here is what it can carry.

It holds at most 149 cases, 299 spot frames and 149 cone-beam volumes from one C-arm model ([Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)), and two projection sweeps of about 300 frames are kept for the demo ([docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md](../../docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md)). Its level truth is automatic, two labellers agreeing on 814 of 910 vertebrae; no person has read a level on any frame of it, and its three L6 cases rest on one automatic labeller alone. Its counts disagree, which bears on every interval on it: 149 cases, 148 cases with 296 frames, or 120 distinct subjects. The verification cluster says to carry "149 cases, 120 distinct subjects by the split keying, person count unverified" until the subject keys settle it ([Verification benches](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 2).

On this set the record has measured lateral and AP naming with and without the device in training, detection with the device held out, the count, and detection in cropped fields, and nothing transitional. The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) found no pixel near-duplicate between its test frames and the external corpora. A pixel audit cannot catch a person leak, and the cluster page reads that audit and the person-level leaks below as both holding ([Level naming and counting](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 30).

### What the record decides

The follow-up's group ruling, made twice, settles three rows. For one vertebra in view, zero-shot lateral and zero-shot AP, no October page keeps the September figure, so the three read updated-later: carry 74.8, 96.4 and 43.7 percent ([the contradictions of the artifacts](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#every-confirmed-number-collision-read-again)), and the concept ledger marks 43.7 percent current ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)). The September values of those rows, 72.8, 96.3 and 37.9 percent on the [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) and [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/), are each a record, not a final value. The demo plan gives 96.4 percent with its count and interval, 595 of 617 [92.3, 98.4], and the paired difference from the device-adapted arm, +1.0 point [-1.9, 4.0] (A3.7): on this device the record cannot tell a model that never saw the device from one adapted to it. It gives 43.7 percent as 276 of 631 with no interval, and 97.4 percent with neither count nor interval.

The whole-frame figure is not settled. The October pages give 97.4 percent to whole-frame naming and 98.2 percent to a different measure, the coupled count, 1.2 points above the count's own 97.0 percent, a gain of three cases with P 0.25; the State of the Literature, of the same day, still calls 98.2 percent, 606 of 617, "the best number". The judges therefore ruled it cannot-tell: carry 97.4 and 98.2 percent each with its pages, and leave to the results catalog, or to paper 1's claims file, which defines both (the OR study page, row 31), whether the September figure was superseded or is the coupled count. Its case count is a confirmed contradiction too, 148 on the ledger against 141 with 617 vertebrae on the sacrum page, for the fold record to settle ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-104-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-in-the-five-fold-cross-validation-behind-the-982-exact-level-figure)). The [SchwingNet Figures](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-xyz6kr/) page says its plot of both fold groupings is "where the 98.2 vs 92.5 lives", and no page says in words what 92.5 is ([number collision 108](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#alike-in-name-probably-not-the-same-thing-66)). The plot answers it: its legend names a case-grouped five-fold and a day-grouped three-fold cross-validation, and at the whole lateral frame the first stands at about 98.2 percent and the second at about 92.5, so 92.5 is most likely the day-grouped figure, a reading of the plot and so Inferred. A planner who needs one lateral figure should quote the zero-shot 96.4 percent with its count and interval, and give 97.4 and 98.2 percent only with their pages and the note that the record has not settled them.

### The AP figure and the cost of adapting to one device

43.7 percent, a one-level cranial shift, is the largest error in the record ([docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md)); [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md), [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md) hold its cause and remedies. On COBRA laterals with the boxes given, over 904 scored calls, the model adapted to C-ArmLSpine is right on 43.5 percent [36.4, 50.1] at 28.1 percent coverage and the zero-shot model on 79.9 percent [74.5, 84.6] at 57.4 percent; with automatic detection the zero-shot model reaches 90.3 percent [86.1, 93.9] over 599. The ledger's reading is "Fine-tuning the level model on one device costs it the others." These are Reported, on another population, so the cluster page sets them beside the any-view plan's within-a-point result rather than against it (Level naming and counting, row 83). That adapting to one device bought nothing measurable on it and cost accuracy on another is Inferred.

## Counting when the boxes are right

The counting results travel as one paragraph, on [Counting Levels Under Fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/), the [AIDaS Seed Grant Proposal](../../artifacts/aidas-seed-grant-proposal/), the [Level Counting Visual Abstract](../../artifacts/level-counting-visual-abstract/) and the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), all from one manuscript in preparation, and it is one measurement however many pages carry it, as the cluster page rules for the proposal and the grant ([The case for the OR study](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md), row 52). It claims the count exact on 99.17 to 100 percent of "reached walks" in twelve arms of 16 to 801 spines, among them 834 of 834; 1,205 of 1,209 (99.67 percent, 95 percent interval 99.16 to 99.91) on 262 real lateral radiographs; 6,234 of 6,255 on 801 abdominopelvic spines; and 7,168 of 7,228 (99.17 percent) on 522 spines from a second institution, by region 99.73 percent lumbar and 95.45 percent thoracic on the 801 and 83.84 percent cervical on the 522. All are Reported, since no run record was read.

The arms were scored with the annotation's boxes, so that the count is tested on its own terms; with the boxes of a detector that finds 71.67 percent of bodies on real radiographs, the grant reports exactness falling from 100 to 30.06 percent on 79 held-out spines. The real-radiograph arm needs C2, which a learned detector finds in 0 to 8 percent of 25 labelled instances on real films, so that arm is exact given the boxes and undemonstrated on a real image (the OR study page, row 34). With the stack's own detection on real C-arm frames the figures are lower: the demo plan's 97.0 percent for the count (A4.7, no count or interval), and the ledger's 96.3 percent of frames exact on the same device, not the September zero-shot naming that shares its digits (Level naming and counting, row 45). ## Detection on a device it never saw

Detection, not naming, is the binding constraint, as Counting From The Sacrum itself says, though its claim that naming is solved "to a standard nobody has published" rests on no recorded search (Verification benches, row 74). The measured figure is the demo plan's. With C-ArmLSpine held out of the detector's training, recall is 231 of 287 vertebrae, 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8], at the operating point the record reports (A6.1), and 93.9 percent on a repaired denominator, with no count or interval (A6.5); the pre-stated 97 percent target "is met by no held-out cell" (A6.13). The same record gives a ladder of training conditions on that test set, from 0.3 percent (1 of 287) on its lowest rung through the 80.5 percent to 84.0 percent on its highest, with no interval on any rung but 80.5 percent (A6.9 to A6.12, A8.2). The top rung adds frames of the test device itself, so it and the plateau of about 82 to 85 percent are same-device figures, the handoff's "same-device plateau about 84 %", not held-out ones. The one comparison the record calls decided at its n is held-out-device against same-device detection on one device, 28 cases, 8 to 0, Holm P 0.0078 (State of the Literature). [Chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md) and [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) say what each rung trained on.

The September figure is 90.6 percent. On 22 September the [ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) found an earlier 5.2 percent to be an artefact of the truth-box convention and re-scored the held-out detector at 90.6 percent on 56 whole frames and 287 vertebrae, with the sacrum found on 83.9 percent; Counting From The Sacrum repeats the 90.6 percent as not counting-grade. Both are Reported. Three cluster pages read the October 80.5 percent as superseding it ([Verification benches](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 3; Level naming and counting, row 9; the [detectors page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md), row 5); the OR study page reads the pair cannot-tell and carries 90.6 percent with its convention stated until paper 1's claims file says which device was held out, under which convention and over which denominator ([The case for the OR study](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md), row 33). The two share their 287 vertebrae, and the ledger's 90.6 percent is at a looser operating point than the demo plan's, which suggests one detector read at two points; no page says so, and the reading is Inferred. The record has not settled the pair. The figure this book grades Measured is 80.5 percent; 90.6 percent is quoted only as the ledger's September re-score at its own point and convention, a record, not a final value.

Cropped fields fare far worse. By the ledger's convention-free centre rule the held-out detector finds 35.9 percent of 566 vertebrae in 175 three-vertebra crops and 18.6 percent in 287 one-vertebra crops, against 82 percent for the same-device control. Yet the same page's list of what is not supported calls the crop re-score pending, so these figures are to be run again, not quoted as the stack's recall in a surgeon's field. That list is the planner's line: counting-grade detection, at least 97 percent, on a device held out entirely is not supported, its best clean figures being 18 to 50 percent on COBRA, 32 percent on an image intensifier and, on C-ArmLSpine, 84 percent for the sacrum and 91 percent for vertebrae, the latter the September 90.6 percent and so a record, not a final value. Of the comparators, Counting From The Sacrum's published 0.96 to 0.98 names no source, [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/) says no per-vertebra recall on real fluoroscopy is published at all, and the State of the Literature names Rao 2024's in-domain 0.964, which may be cadaver and phantom only: carry Rao's figure so qualified, and drop the range (Verification benches, rows 4 and 5).

## What the proposals and the grant quote

Three blocks of figures recur across the September proposal, the October grant, the retrospective protocol and the visual abstract, each one measurement however many pages carry it. The comparators are Reported throughout, and none was checked against its paper, since this chapter had no network. Naming Vertebrae Without CT gives a published network's 88.3 percent on cadaveric long films in that role, Huang 2022's by Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor, a figure the [Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/) study says "did NOT survive verification". The concept ledger marks the pair a confirmed contradiction ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-123-best-published-registration-free-vertebra-naming-accuracy)), so carry each with its modality and task, and call neither the best. LevelCheck registers to the patient's own CT, "a ceiling, not a rival": labels within 30 mm on all 398 radiographs of 178 patients (De Silva 2016). With its overlay surgeons named 97.6 percent of 62 hard radiographs against 69.6 percent unaided (Manbachi 2018, five surgeons), with no published denominator; a superiority claim over it has no power below 152 persons and needs 708 for 80 percent power at a true 99 percent, so the handoff calls it a comparator that "cannot be exceeded at any reachable n". [Chapter 19](19-what-a-valid-comparison-needs.md) sets out what a valid comparison needs.

## What is strictly unmeasured today

Transitional accuracy on real frames is unmeasured. The handoff counts "0 verified real-frame transitional test persons against 35 needed", and the demo plan of 20 October still finds "no transitional accuracy on any real image with a human read; 7 at-risk persons with a frame, none verified". Research Directions sizes the gap. All seven named correctly would give a best-case lower bound of 64.6 percent against a 90 percent target; the 85 percent sensitivity claim needs 22 carriers with no miss, none of them real, and at a true recall of 72.4 percent the chance of 22 of 22 is 0.0008; paper 2's first hypothesis needs 19 at-risk persons and has 7 unverified, so it cannot be run. The best rendered L6 recall, 56.1 to 72.4 percent on ten held-out persons, is render-only. As Naming Vertebrae Without CT put it, "Transitional performance is unmeasured, not poor." [Chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) and [chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md) hold the design and the reads that would change this.

Detection on any device is unmeasured in the sense a claim needs: with four devices the smallest two-sided sign-flip P is 0.125 (State of the Literature), no held-out cell meets the pre-stated 97 percent, and paper 2's frozen COBRA sacrum detector found the sacrum on 3.4 percent of frames, its frozen endpoint holding on 57.0 percent of ordinary development frames, before the detectors were replaced.

Latency on a C-arm is unmeasured. The retraining plan finds no machine of a surgeon's room measured, the stack's only CPU figure 3,350 ms per frame, its runtime-cost file absent and its "17 ms" banned, and the demo plan adds that no laptop latency exists. The latency page sets the budget at one frame interval of C-ArmLSpine's sweeps, 97 ms at about 10.3 frames a second, real-time meaning a p95 strictly under it. The only latency measured in this repository is version 1's frame path without a detector, on the synthetic dry-run model in a cloud container: p95 15.1 ms through ONNX Runtime on four threads and 31.3 ms through PyTorch, over 300 frames, which measures the workflow, not SchwingNet or an operating-room machine. The superseded [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/), replaced by Naming Vertebrae Without CT, alone times the older chain, a quarter to half a second a frame on one ordinary CPU core, with no file named: a record, not a final value. [Chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md) holds readiness.

More absences stand on the record. No person has read a level on any real fluoroscopic frame (Research Directions), and no reader study exists (State of the Literature). C2 has not been shown on a real image (the OR Readiness Ledger). The state-of-knowledge note finds no number on a cervical or thoracic C-arm frame and no synthetic-to-real ratio swept, though the handoff lists a plain-film fraction sweep of 17 September that this book has not read; and it finds no number of version 1, the model this repository exists to make, on any real frame.

## What we know

The reported stack names lateral C-arm vertebrae well on the one real intraoperative human set without having seen the device, 96.4 percent of 617 vertebrae with a lower bound of 92.3, and adapting to the device bought no measurable gain. It names AP frames badly, at 43.7 percent, an error the record describes as a one-level shift. Its detector, with the device held out, finds 80.5 percent of bodies, and no held-out cell reaches the 97 percent the count needs, so detection, not naming, is the binding constraint. Counting is near exact when its boxes are right, a claim that rests on annotation boxes and collapses with a weaker detector's boxes. The FluoroCap level model has one held-out number, 98.7 percent on radiograph crops; its render scores are training-set, leaked-bank or out-of-domain figures. The leaked families' numbers are records, and the catalog names their clean replacements. No verified real-frame transitional test person exists. In this repository nothing has yet been measured about SchwingNet itself.

## What we do not know

We do not know whether the September 98.2 percent was superseded by 97.4 percent or measures something else, how many cases sit behind it, what the second fold grouping and its 92.5 percent are, or whether the folds keep each person on one side, since C-ArmLSpine has been recounted as 120 distinct subjects in 149 cases. We do not know whether the September 90.6 percent and the October 80.5 percent are one detector at two operating points, or what the stack's recall is in a surgeon's cropped field, since the ledger calls its own crop re-score pending. We do not know the FluoroCap level model's id and context rung, why its page says no thoracic, or whether the plain-film figures of Counting From The Sacrum hold persons out under the rule of 23 September. The DeepFluoro sacrum and the variance a render explains are each open between the proposal and the grant, and every comparator waits on its paper being read. Nor do we know what version 1 scores on any real frame, how fast any model runs on an operating-room machine, or how any model names a transitional spine on a real frame.

## What would change our mind

The catalog's rows would settle much of this chapter at once: a row marking the September 98.2 percent CURRENT on person-disjoint folds would make it the whole-frame figure, and one marking it SUPERSEDED would leave 97.4 percent alone. The cross-validation fold record would settle the case count and the fold grouping, and a recall curve of the held-out detector with both operating points marked would show whether 90.6 and 80.5 percent are one detector. A held-out device cell at or above 97 percent with its interval would end the claim that detection is the binding constraint, and a held-out cell on a second real device would let a device-level claim be tested at all. A two-reader read of the transitional case series would give the first transitional number on real frames, and a model naming all 35 frozen at-risk persons without error would meet the 90 percent bound. Version 1's first evaluation on the frozen C-ArmLSpine test persons would be the first number this repository holds about SchwingNet; if it fell well below 96.4 percent on lateral frames, the new workflow would not have reproduced the reported stack, and the book would have to say so. A timing of the whole frame path on an operating-room machine with its p95 under 97 ms would change the latency line. A clean-bank re-run of the selective prediction, on at least the 298 answered cases, or 2,673 class-conditional, that the State of the Literature says a 99 percent claim needs, would replace the 99 percent record. And reading VDVM (Zhang 2023), Huang 2022, De Silva 2016, Manbachi 2018 and Rao 2024 in full would settle the comparators.
