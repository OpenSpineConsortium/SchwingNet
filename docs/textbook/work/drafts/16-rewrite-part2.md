
## Two targets, and how they live together (M1 to M3)

The level-naming cluster reads two opposite principles as successive designs, with the handoff's combined model as the current state ([the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 13). The naming note of 18 September ([naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/)) names each vertebra from its morphology, never from a count, because "every failure mode in the published work traces to a count: a fixed cardinality, or a tally from a landmark that a transitional segment corrupts". Re-implementing each paradigm on the same 1,653 films and 8,422 vertebrae at the tightest field, it found counting from a reference vertebra exact on 1.8 percent and abstaining on 97.3, counting from an oracle landmark abstaining on 98.5, a level-as-class detector naming 64.0 percent, and one-frame naming 97.0 percent with no abstention: in a tight field a count has no landmark to start from.

The OR proposal ([counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/)), with runs of 21 September, decided the opposite: count, never name, and let nothing learned decide the level. A sweep from a trusted anchor, the sacrum going up or C2 coming down, links each frame to the last by normalised cross-correlation within 0.6 of a body of the commanded step; the system says "four from the sacrum", never "L4", and declines a walk that fails to link or loses its anchor. With annotation boxes the count is exact on 1,205 of 1,209 real laterals. The report of 22 September ([counting-from-the-sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/)), the cluster's canonical statement of the chain, gives it as expose, detect, link, chain, count, confirm ("Only the shaded boxes are learned; the rest is geometry"), confirms an instrument frame by its correlation peak along the chain (L2, a margin of 0.18 over one body off) and names detection as the binding constraint, since per-body recall compounds over the eight to twelve bodies a count crosses. It still is: at confidence 0.74 the held-out C-arm detector finds 231 of 287 bodies, 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8], and no held-out cell meets the pre-stated 97 percent ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)).

The anomaly rule of that day ([vertebral-count-anomaly-rule](../../artifacts/vertebral-count-anomaly-rule/)) gives the arithmetic for a whole column: with C1 excluded, the counts above and below a vertebra sum to 22, and otherwise the ordinal is reported and the name withheld; a column with an extra and a missing segment passes. That evening the rule's annotations were taken out of the manuscript's figure rows, with no reason recorded ([schwingnet-figures-jshksq](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-jshksq/)), and the final build states the paper's premise: the count from the sacrum is undetermined in a limited field, and the network resolves it. It also calls weak the margin of 0.01 on an L6 counted on styled frames ([schwingnet-figures-xyz6kr](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-xyz6kr/)). A third form, two ordinal counts from the sacrum and from the lowest rib-bearing vertebra, is designed and unrun; on 8 October it is CT-7, ranked fourteenth of sixteen behind the visibility experiment ([counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu](../../artifacts/counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu/); [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md)).

Neither principle survives alone. Naming fails as a whole column: zero-shot AP naming on C-ArmLSpine is 276 of 631, 43.7 percent, a one-level cranial shift and the largest error in the record (the demo plan; the [concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine) marks 43.7 current over September's 37.88), and rendered six-lumbar spines fail by the same whole-run shift on 26 to 28 percent of films ([research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)). Counting fails where the boxes or the anchor fail: a missed or duplicated body moves every name above it, and with no anchor in view there is nothing to count. Each mechanism's failure is the other's strength.

M3, in the programme the owner took on 8 October, joins them: names from detected boxes in a limited field, the count from the detected sacrum as the second mechanism, and disagreement as the stop signal. The handoff ([handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)) lists the count and its coupling as the one part of the model with no learned parameters. Its base is thin. The count alone names 97.0 percent of anchored bodies and the coupling adds 1.2 points, three cases at P .25 carried by L6 calls on five-lumbar frames, while the three real L6 patients score 0 of 5 and 1 of 5; the stop signal caught 5 of 6 erring frames. The two mechanisms' agreement on anomalous spines has never been measured, and no verified real-frame transitional test person exists against the 35 a claim needs.

Version 1 adds the counting prior of 21 October ([docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md), item 1; [src/schwingnet/chain.py](../../src/schwingnet/chain.py)). Sorted along the column, a frame's bodies take the labels of one stretch of one column hypothesis, with 11 to 13 thoracic and 4 to 6 lumbar bodies, chosen by a Viterbi pass over each body's posterior, the CT spacing likelihood of each neighbouring pair and the hypothesis's prior. A missed body is a two-level step paid for by a penalty, and when the best labelling beats the best different one by less than a margin the decoder shows "?", so a close call between the standard and an L6 column is never forced. Weights and margin come from validation frames only. Its one run, a dry run with an untrained model, tests the plumbing, not the method ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)).

In planned version 1, then, the learned stages name each body, the count runs beside them, the prior decodes the column, disagreement stops the frame and a close column abstains, with abstain and stop rates beside every accuracy and the transitional rows never pooled. The prior stays at decode and out of the loss, as two older decisions require: the context ladder of 13 September ([level-context-ladder-xd7eltc](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/)) left the closest prior work's sequence loss out of every arm because it re-injects the counting prior, and the research directions say a feature-supervised variant "must not become a sequence loss". At decode the prior can be switched off and its effect read on the transitional rows. On version 1's witness frames, a stop rate that falls mostly on correct frames, or one mechanism reaching parity on transitional persons alone, would reverse M3.

## The architecture (M4 to M6)

The decisions page ([spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/)) locked one network with four heads on a shared backbone (detection with four keypoints, a level head, a lesion head and later Cobb landmarks), each head's loss masked on images not annotated for it, so that a film with corners but no lesion boxes does not teach that it has no lesions; UniDet (Zhou 2022) and LENS (Yan 2021) were its prior art. It was never built. The FluoroCap pipeline map of 14 September ([fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs](../../artifacts/fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs/)) already runs a corner detector beside a four-model level ensemble, the handoff describes SchwingNet as five separate pieces, and the detector cluster records the shared backbone as a locked design never implemented ([the detector contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md), rows 2 and 3). It would return only by winning the cross-source level test.

Level identity on a head held in principle. A detector with level classes can reach near-perfect training accuracy by reading an image's source, since C3 appears only in CSXA and T7 only in AASCE; the page's supporting figure on identifying a dataset from its images carries no citation, and the cluster drops it (row 72). The detector field report ([vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/)) built the detector to match: one class with four corners, and horizontal flip disabled, since on a lateral a flip swaps the anterior and posterior corner slots and the keypoint head fails to converge. The level became a separate ResNet-18 crop encoder over 27 classes with a BiGRU run head over two to nine bodies ([chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md), [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md)). A level-class detector trained on one cervical source and tested on the other that survives the swap, the experiment the page proposed and nobody ran, would reverse M5.

The sacrum's detector came from the misses: three in 243 held-out bodies, two of them the sacrum, the landmark the chain anchors on. The two naming pages of that day turn the three misses into 97.6 and 99.2 percent, and the verification cluster carries the count alone ([the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 13). The sacrum "is not a four-cornered body and should not be asked of a model trained on four-cornered bodies"; single-class and split by patient, its detector reached the precision and recall in the table. The built system's anchor comes from the real-frame vertebra and sacrum detector, trained on 3,094 real frames. M6 is done.

## The chain from frame to count (M7 to M11)

Box repair fixes pairs much closer than the frame's own spacing, a duplicate, or much further, a miss its neighbours place, with the gains the table gives. The decision inside it is the spacing measured in the frame, never an expected number: the published lumbar baseline decodes exactly five boxes and, by its authors' account, drops a vertebra on a six-lumbar spine. It fills at most two bodies per gap and never extrapolates past a run's ends, since inventing a missing sacrum would fabricate the landmark. It was measured on unstratified held-out films; real six-lumbar or sacralised frames on which it adds or drops the extra body would reverse it. Because the prior also treats a miss as a two-level step, the first witness run should show whether the two count one gap twice.

Majority linking fixed what the naming note calls the single largest error in the project: unanimity let one bad box collapse a shared block, entering every shared vertebra again as new. With human boxes the two rules are identical to the second decimal, which made the change safe. The superseded page alone shows the state before it, a sweep that scored worse than no sweep (66.0 against 72.3 percent at two vertebrae), a record, not a final value ([vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/)). M8 is done for the spot-film chain. Version 1's recorded sweep links by a causal tracker ([src/schwingnet/sweep.py](../../src/schwingnet/sweep.py)) that joins each box to the track it overlaps most, averages level probabilities over frames and shows "?" on a weak best level; nothing the majority rule measured transfers to it, and its constants are unmeasured.

The overlap was set on renders on 17 September and kept after the next day's re-measurement. One body of overlap leaves a single vertebra to agree on, and the lumbar column is periodic enough that one body can align a whole frame one level off; four bodies are worse, "at more than twice the exposures, because every extra frame is another link that can be joined wrongly". The re-measured table survives only on the superseded page (3.0 exposures at two bodies against 6.9 at four; 98.3 against 89.8 percent with two in view), a record, not a final value ([vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/)), and its chain-intact column is flagged (row 15). The anchor page ([naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)) fixes a 9-inch field swept up from the sacrum, matching the one clinical stitching series' four to six exposures at 20 to 30 percent overlap, and found retakes a quarter-vertebra away no better (81.3 against 82.8 percent), so a decline means count or sweep further. Real sweeps on a new device that move the optimum would reverse M9.

The disc rule decides what is sacrum, on which both mechanisms depend: a body still separated by a disc counts, one whose disc is gone is sacrum, and a disc between S1 and S2 makes the top segment separate. Bridging is a poor guide, mixed on a third of cases, and experts disagree on the sacral endplate (kappa 0.32 to 0.38). Locked since the group settled it, it would fall to a two-reader read against CT truth in which it loses to the fusion rule.

The threshold of 0.25 was kept by measuring the obvious lever and finding it wrong: the miss rate is 2.42 percent at 0.25, 0.10 and 0.05 alike, so the misses were not sitting just below the cut, while lower thresholds doubled the spurious boxes. The superseded page adds naming with three in view falling from 85.6 to 81.3 percent, a record, not a final value ([vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/)), and the chain's survival is a figure the ledger marks cannot-tell ([Row 206](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-206-sweep-chain-survival-with-the-detectors-own-boxes)). It is to revisit now. The October evaluation reports detection at 0.74, and the demo plan reads the renders-only detector as 1 of 287 at 0.74 against 31.7 percent at 0.25, a figure the detector cluster marks cannot-tell between conventions (row 6), though it shows threshold and convention alone moving a number a hundredfold. No page says which threshold version 1's chain uses, or how the prior's miss penalty should meet a lower threshold's spurious boxes; the version 1 detector's curve on the validation witness frames, read with the chain, should settle it.

## Renders and the physics (M12 to M15)

The physics page of 21 September ([ninety-three-pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/)) found the residual after exact geometry, physics and nine device terms to be a smooth per-frame field, so the map was calibrated, not learned, and transfers to whole-spine CTs because every fitted term belongs to the device; per-angle terms bought nothing. How much of a frame the physics explains is unsettled: 80.9 percent on that page, a record, not a final value, or 86 percent for physics alone and about 90 for the calibrated renderer once the stored frames are read as log-compressed, which the renders cluster carries ([the renders contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md), row 1), while the ledger marks 80.9 current ([Row 12](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-12-share-of-a-real-deepfluoro-frames-variance-explained-by-the-physics-renderer-alone)).

What tested M12 later is stronger than what made it. On renders of living whole-body CTs, six folds by three seeds, a RealDRR-style refiner, a high-pass version of it and a Fourier low-band swap added about eight to ten points of real-frame sacrum recall, all the same sign and each inside the 26-point seed spread, and the refined render matched the 366 held-out real frames slightly worse than the plain one (68.1 against 69.3 percent of display variance): "It sharpens and it invents" ([counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/)). The population of CTs was the larger lever: renders of DeepFluoro's own CTs found the sacrum on 90 to 97 percent of real frames against 64 or 77 percent, though the seed-grant proposal gives 96 to 98 and the ledger leaves the figure open ([Row 10](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-10-sacrum-recall-on-real-deepfluoro-frames-detector-trained-on-renders-of-deepfluoros-own-cts)). The residual page's verified literature agrees: in SyntheX (Gao 2023) physics with strong randomisation reached parity with real-frame training (6.44 against 6.46 mm), with CycleGAN and ADDA adding nothing. What would reverse M12 is the test that page names and nobody ran: a learned term, or the fitted smooth fields sampled as an augmentation, beating the seed spread. The OR ledger ([or-readiness-ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/)) adds that styled frames are not yet inside the real domain (noise-power distance 0.111 against a real floor of 0.015).

The lift page of 15 September ([lift-before-label-jzjq8t](../../artifacts/lift-before-label-jzjq8t/)) refused a reconstruction stage: no paper reports a lift-then-classify gain, and single-view lifting is a prior-driven completion that completes an L6 or an absent twelfth rib toward the typical spine, the very feature that decides the label. Its advice to use both views was measured two days later and lost: the AP alone scores 40 to 47 percent with a lateral-trained encoder, and averaging a weak opinion into a strong one costs more than it adds ([naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)).

The overnight report of 15 September ([fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/)) found no usable img2img strength (at 0.2 the render barely changes, at 0.35 end-plates and pedicles smear, at 0.45 to 0.65 the vertebrae are lost) and cancelled the array after 12,896 images, none usable for level training; its first unpaired translator was no test of translation, its target being the unfiltered panels. By 8 October the research directions find SDXL, CUT, CycleGAN and ADDA giving no gain in any arm and exclude them. The priors note ranks second a shard of the rare-class styled renders already made (12,929 frames, 1,763 L6 and 86 T13), kept only if the witness frames say it helps, while the FluoroCap cluster carries them as the cancelled array's output, unusable for level training ([the FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md), row 25). A shard that lifts the transitional witness frames without lowering ordinary naming would narrow M14 to "never generated anew"; one that does not confirms it.

No synthetic data generation is the owner's instruction of 16 September ([overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/)), recorded with the session's note that the transitional flag's learning curve agreed (0.652, 0.704 and 0.713 at 10, 40 and 120 positive patients). Renders of real CTs were in use the same night and remain the only labelled L6 and T13, and the verification cluster leaves the scope to the directive's own record (row 42). The lift page judged transitional CT editing worth a week while advising against generating whole CTs; the line between rendering, editing and generating is the owner's to draw.

## Training (M16 to M20)

The corpus audit of 14 September ([fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/)) first said the corpus "must not be used to retrain the level model without re-weighting", then withdrew it the same day: T2 to T11, T13 and L6 had no training crops, and "You cannot re-weight a class with no examples". The all-spine bank, the films merged with every CT render source, replaced it. Oversampling T13 and L6 by two orders of magnitude failed too: the reality check ([fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/)) records that twelvefold duplication did not move L6, since distinct cases, not crops, are the limit (802 L6 crops from at most 90 cases). By 8 October variants make up 25 to 35 percent of rendered CTs, by which CTs are rendered, never by duplication, and logit adjustment had bought three points on ordinary spines and nothing on variants. The shard registry of 20 October keeps the rule, though its incremental runs oversample the newest shards for rehearsal, a duplication by recency whose effect on a rare class the validation witness frames must show.

Mixed training became the default on 8 October because pretrain-then-fine-tune lost 25.6 anomalous points, but the research directions list as open whether that refutation of 17 September was ever re-run on a clean bank, and say the default rests on a leaked-bank arm. The 45-cell ratio array (real share 0.1 to 0.9, three seeds) has not run, and the demo plan says no synthetic-to-real ratio was ever swept. Version 1's rebase is mixed by construction; until a clean-bank re-run decides it, the default is a design choice, not a measured one.

The clean encoder follows from the owner's rule of 23 September: an item is held out only if its person key is in neither the training nor the validation set of the checkpoint or any ancestor, only declared ImageNet and COCO weights are exempt, and unknown is not clean. With 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons in the v8 and v9 training splits, the encoder starts from ImageNet on G3 training persons ([chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md)), and the retraining plan's rebase step starts every stage from declared public weights. One distinction stays: the zero-shot lateral 595 of 617, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4], is held out by device, not by the G3 rule.

Decoder-named pseudolabels have been out of training since 18 September, and the measured cost is weaker than the structural reason. Training on 76,042 pseudolabelled crops, 30 percent of the bank, took anomalous naming from 87.2 to 66.8 percent with ordinary naming unchanged, but the 87.2 is from the allspine v2 family, a record, not a final value ([naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)); the page later found the two figures off by six and fourteen points, and the cost, nineteen or twenty points, is open in the ledger ([Row 182](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-182-accuracy-cost-of-training-on-pseudo-labels)). The structural reason is that the decoder's alphabet is the ordinary one: it cannot emit an L6, so it files every transitional spine as ordinary, and its agreement with human names on ordinary spines (96.3 percent of cervical and 89.9 percent of lumbar bodies, anchored) says nothing about anomalous ones. The owner's ask of 21 October ([docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md), section 6) opens the door M19 leaves: an independent pseudolabeller trained on annotated X-rays, keeping only labels whose column the counting prior accepts, with a read sample for its precision and one role per person. One that raises anomalous naming, verified by a second reader, would reverse M19 for its labels.

Version 1 is locked by the owner's words of 20 October: one detector, one encoder and one head, one seed each, on the registry as it stands, with a card recording the weights' hash, the shards seen, the commit and a clean-tree flag. A dataset is added by one pull request and one incremental run from the latest checkpoint, and no epoch is run again; the validation witness frames choose the epoch kept, and the test frames never do. The trainers stay the record's scripts, called by path at a named commit; whether their starting checkpoint restores the optimizer, and whether a resume re-reads the whole bank, are not recorded, and the first run measures them. The reason is the banks' history: each new source meant a new bank and a training from its first epoch, and the leaks came from banks rebuilt by hand. A resumed run worse than its rebase on the validation witness frames would make the rebase the default after each architecture change.

## Views and the order of work (M21 to M23)

The any-view plan of 21 October ([any-view-spine-plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/)) puts structured transfer first: predict the lateral's levels, corners and sagittal alignment from the AP and score them against the same person's real lateral. No published work turns a spine AP into a lateral (the closest moves the view 9 degrees), and what an AP does not show a model cannot recover: "A synthesised lateral picture is a display aid and not a film." It needs about a thousand real pairs per region and about a hundred test persons, and the held sets have no real paired films above the lumbar spine. The X-ray-from-3-D programme ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)) puts it after the talk, with "a real chance that the structured transfer is only as good as the counting prior already makes the AP", which is also what would reverse it.

The same plan lets renders and data plumbing start at once but holds each any-view training decision for the census, since the per-region counts decide whether a model can be tested at all. The census ran on 22 October (140 groups, 7,008,826 files, 7.3 TB), with three sets waiting for a body-part split. It does not hold version 1 back: the grid pipeline ([schwingnet-grid-pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/)) starts version 1 from the first catalog, which holds C-ArmLSpine, without waiting on the multi-day X-ray fetch.

The order to 2 November is the retraining plan's: the registry, shards and splits, with C-ArmLSpine and the sweeps first; the leak checks as code inside ingestion; the flow with its Slurm adapter, dry-run on one epoch; the evaluate step and the witness frames; version 1, one rebase run per stage; then latency, ONNX parity and the recording. The targets are the registry by 23 October, the witness frames at epoch 0 by 25 October, version 1 by 28 October (about 8, 14 and 10 hours for detector, encoder and head, each after the owner's word and number), latency and the recorded sweep on 29 October and the provisional on 30 October. The witness set's thoracic and cervical film rows, the CPU latency and the ONNX export fall off first. The order differs from the research directions of 8 October, which trained nothing on the flagship recipe before gates G0 to G3 closed and scored no G3 test person before paper 2's "H1 seen" entry; the programme cluster reads both differences as cannot-tell ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), rows 0 and 1). The plan supersedes the earlier order for version 1, but whether the owner holds its test reading, which holds the only real L6 test cases, to the "H1 seen" gate should be asked before 28 October.

## What has already been overturned, and by what

The four reversals the plan names are settled in the record.

The thin-bone deficit was the collimator border. Measuring the residual by region, the OR proposal found the border ring, a fifth of the field, holding about two thirds of it, because the real frame falls off through the penumbra while the render stops at a hard edge; inside, cortical edges agree to within a few grey levels at every bone thickness, and the deficit "has been withdrawn". The lesson: separate the field's edge before reading a residual as anatomy.

"Nothing local" was too strong. The physics page found 0.4 percent of the residual's variance at 2 pixels or finer, "so there is nothing for a regional network to learn", a record, not a final value. On four real frames the residual page showed a twelve-coefficient smooth field lifting the explained share from 83.0 to 91.6 percent, and from 54.6 to 86.9 on the worst specimen, but leaving 0.1 to 0.8 percent per band from 2 to 16 pixels, two to seven times the noise floor, along cortical edges that 1 mm CT voxels cannot render sharp and in bowel gas that moved between scans. The field is "yes to learn, no to predict": the device sets it at each exposure, so no model given only the CT can know it. This narrowed M12 rather than reversing it: cortical sharpness is the one place a learned term could help, and the one place it could move a label.

The D2 claim, that the decoder chose variant templates only on films that missed the junction, was a tautology of the programme's code: the pseudolabelling script cleared the supervision tier at and below the junction whenever it flagged a variant, and the manifest read only that tier. Read from the decoder's full placement, 164 of 191 variant calls contain a sacrum, and the variant rate is 9.0 percent [7.8, 10.4] with the sacrum in the decode against 0.32 percent [0.22, 0.46] without; against a population prevalence of 4 to 9 percent, most are false positives made where the junction was visible. That is why the prior abstains rather than decides on a close column, and why the decoder's labels stay out of training.

Re-weighting was withdrawn because there was nothing to weight, and every later fix without real examples failed alike: the naming note lists re-weighting, synthetic oversampling, pretraining on simulated films and two pseudolabel admission rules as moving nothing on six-lumbar naming before real six-lumbar images entered training. How far those images moved it is unsettled; the verification cluster carries the research directions' rendered L6 recall of 56.1 to 72.4 percent on ten held-out persons (row 21).

Five more were overturned without the plan naming them, and none should be proposed again unchanged: the shared backbone, unanimity linking, two-view fusion, oversampling by two orders of magnitude and pretrain-then-fine-tune, the last on a leaked bank, which keeps M17 open. Two headline numbers fell with their banks rather than with a method: the 99 percent among answered at 35.7 percent coverage ([overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/)) and the 99.02 percent shortcut-free film number ([handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)) come from leaked families and are each a record, not a final value (row 9), to be re-run on the G3 split before any selective-accuracy claim is made.

## What we know

The method is two mechanisms, deliberately: naming each body from its morphology, which answers in a tight field where a count has nothing to start from, and counting from the detected sacrum along a sweep, which has no learned parameters and catches the whole-column shift that is naming's largest error. Version 1 joins them with a stop signal and a CT counting prior applied at decode, which abstains on close calls instead of forcing an ordinary name; the prior is built and has no result yet. Of the twenty-three decisions, three are locked (M5, M10 and M20), thirteen are in effect with a stated condition, four are done (M6, M8, M16 and, for its first run, M22), two are to revisit now (M11 and M17) and one, M4, is overturned in practice. The chain decisions rest on measured gains on held-out films and renders; the physics decisions on arms that failed to beat calibration by more than the seed spread, and on literature that agrees; the training decisions partly on structure (a class with no examples cannot be re-weighted, a decoder with an ordinary alphabet cannot teach L6) and partly on arms from leaked banks.

## What we do not know

Whether the two mechanisms agree or disagree usefully on anomalous spines has never been measured, and no verified real-frame transitional test person exists against the 35 a claim needs, so the purpose of M3 is unmeasured. How much of a real frame the physics explains, the detector's held-out per-body rate, sacrum recall from population-matched renders, the chain's survival with the detector's own boxes and the cost of pseudolabels are each given differently by different pages, and none is quoted here as a current value. Which threshold version 1's chain will run at, and whether repair and the prior's miss step count one gap twice, are written nowhere. Whether pretrain-then-fine-tune loses on a clean bank, whether the resume restores the optimizer and how the recorded sweep's tracker performs are unknown. The scope of the owner's rule against synthetic data generation is not written, and neither is whether version 1's test reading waits for the "H1 seen" entry.

## What would change our mind

On version 1's witness frames: a stop rate that falls mostly on correct frames would weaken M3; the detector's curve would settle M11; a resumed run worse than its rebase would qualify M20; and a styled shard that lifts the transitional frames without lowering ordinary naming would narrow M14. On a clean bank: pretrain-then-fine-tune holding its anomalous points would reverse M17, and a re-run of the pseudolabel arm on the G3 split would put a measured comparison behind M19. On real transitional frames read by two readers against CT truth: repair adding or dropping the extra body would reverse M7, and the disc rule losing to the fusion rule would reverse M10. On the physics: a learned term or a sampled-field augmentation beating the seed spread would reverse M12. On views: structured transfer that does no better on AP than the counting prior would reverse M21. And from the owner: a word on the scope of M15 and on whether version 1's test reading waits for the "H1 seen" entry would close the two decisions only the owner can close.
