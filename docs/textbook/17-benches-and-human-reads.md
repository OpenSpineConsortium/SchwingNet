# 17 Benches and human reads

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter stays in this private repository and gives the instruments for a human read, their designs and their state, while how the machine reaches the labels they check belongs to Part III, in [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md) for FluoroCap and [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) for naming.

Every number the project holds for a real published panel or a real fluoroscopic frame is agreement with something no person has checked, an author's caption or the output of automatic labellers. A human read is what turns agreement into accuracy. This chapter gives the planner the instruments built for that read and the state of each: three benches in the owner's gallery, the FluoroCap dataset card's reference standard, the September reading sites and reader study, the reading rules of October and the protocol for a retrospective collection of the project's own fluoroscopy. It settles what each instrument measures and on which sample, why a measurement sample is random, seeded and never trained on, what the reference standard is and who reads, how many reads each question needs, checked here with Wilson intervals, and which reads have never been done. One fact governs the rest: all three benches were exported with no verdict in them and their stores were not copied, so each is a tool and not a result, and no record reports a completed read on any of them.

## What a human read measures

The record uses the words "human read" for three measurements, and mixing them puts the wrong number against the wrong claim.

A verification read shows the reader the machine's label and asks whether it is right, as the [Level Verification Bench](../../artifacts/level-verification-bench-mkjxsy/) does with the name drawn on each box. It measures one model's accuracy on the population sampled. It creates no truth that another model could be scored against, and the reader is not blind to the answer being judged.

A reference read makes truth. The [FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/) of 14 September specifies it: two readers blind to each other and to the machine's proposal, a third who adjudicates their disagreements, and the dataset, the model and the analysis plan locked before any performance claim. Such a read can score any model, and its two independent reads say how hard the task itself is. It costs at least twice the reads plus the adjudication, and it is the only design the card accepts as ground truth.

An assisted-reading study measures people: how often a surgeon is right without the system and with it, how often a correct suggestion rescues a wrong read, and how often a wrong one overturns a correct read. [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) draws the line: its own figures are the machine judged alone, while the published 97.6 percent it is set against is a human and a machine together.

Two warnings apply to all three. The [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) of 14 September says that agreement between two automatic sources bounds error from one side only, since both can agree and be wrong. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) of 15 September adds that captions carry five to ten percent counting-convention noise before anyone misreads a pixel, with numerical variants in 7.7 percent of whole-spine MRIs (Tins 2016) and a non-standard count in 8.8 percent of 3,147 full-length radiographs, so a 99 percent target measured against captions is uninterpretable. A reader who counts on the same limited field inherits the same error, which is why the transitional stratum needs a reference that does not count on the image.

The second warning is the join between a read and the machine's output. [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) of 17 September set the machine's names against 543 human-labelled NHANES films and found agreement of 96.3 percent on 970 cervical bodies and 89.9 percent on 258 lumbar bodies, in a subset of films that [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) defines; the same 543 films are listed in that page's own training bank, so the comparison is not shown to be held out. Two earlier versions had reported 0.23 and 40 percent, one because it joined the wrong files and the other because its key collided a patient's cervical and lumbar films. The page adds that high agreement on ordinary spines under a shared convention is no evidence about anomalous ones.

## The three benches

### The level verification bench

The [Level Verification Bench](../../artifacts/level-verification-bench-mkjxsy/) is undated. [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/), the report of 16 September, calls it the bench that unblocks everything, waiting for the owner, and says about half an hour of reading would give the first unbiased estimate of real-panel accuracy the project has had. Its page calls it a random sample of decoded panels, 179 panels and 865 vertebrae, "the measurement, not training data", random within each route and drawn with a fixed seed, so that the result is an unbiased estimate, which a set chosen by the model's own uncertainty could not give. Its data file, counted for this chapter, holds 179 panels with 865 boxes: 70 panels from PubMed Central, 70 from video, 28 from Wikimedia Commons and 11 from Reddit, and 401 lumbar, 219 cervical, 213 thoracic and 32 sacral boxes. It holds no verdict field and records neither the seed's value nor the code that drew the sample.

The reader marks each box right, wrong or can't tell and types the correct level where it is known. One key marks a whole panel right, which the page expects to be the common case, though nothing measured supports that (FluoroCap contradictions, row 51). Accuracy is right over right plus wrong, so the can't-tell rate has to be reported beside it, or a reader's caution would read as the machine's success. Verdicts are saved as one record per panel, naming no reader, in the page's store. The repository's [artifact index](../../artifacts/README.md) says the stored rows were not copied, and the exported counter of zero of zero is static text shown before the page loads its rows ([benches contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 79). Only a read of the store can say whether anyone has read a panel.

A completed read would estimate, route by route, the accuracy of the names the decoder gave to the panels it decoded when the sample was drawn: not the panels it left unnamed, not a later decode, and not the corpus as a whole without weights. The routes are unequal, 8,616 PubMed Central figures against 84 from Commons on 16 September, while the bench draws as many panels from video as from PubMed Central, so a pooled accuracy needs route weights that no page records (benches contradictions, row 46). The boxes sit about 4.8 to a panel and share its image and its run of names, so the interval lies between the panel-level and the box-level widths and comes from a bootstrap over panels, whose method [chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md) holds. Near 85 percent, 179 panels give a Wilson interval of 79.0 to 89.5 percent and 865 independent boxes 82.5 to 87.2. The bench claims no precision of its own; it is close to the size the audit gives for +/-5 points (the table below), and the [FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md) (row 32) ask that its accuracy carry an interval clustered by panel, about +/-5 percent.

### The LSTV verification bench

The [LSTV Verification Bench](../../artifacts/lstv-verification-bench-6m2qhr/), pass one of 15 September, holds figures harvested for transitional terms and asks what each figure is and which transitional finding is visible. Its data file, counted for this chapter, holds 585 figures: 330 from OpenAlex and preprint PDFs, 165 from PubMed Central's first pass (untagged in the file, every one linking there), 79 from Wikimedia Commons and 11 from Open-i and MedPix. The harvest's guess of modality is unknown for 382, radiograph for 153 and fluoroscopy for 28, and captions name an L6 on 8 figures and a T13 on 2. There is no verdict field.

The reader types each figure as a human radiograph, a human fluoroscopy image, a CT scout, a CT or MRI image, not an X-ray, or an animal; marks the visible finding from ten choices, from an L6 or a sacralized L5 through the rib variants to a wrong-level montage, none, or can't tell at this size; grades the quality; and notes the numbering where the caption needs it. Only a typed figure counts as reviewed, and a usable one is a human radiograph, fluoroscopy image or scout.

The Honest 99% page calls the bench deliberately enriched, for finding anomalies rather than measuring accuracy. Chosen by caption terms, its reads can give no accuracy and no prevalence, only a count of real, typed transitional figures, the examples the project lacks most. Even the typing is imperfect: expert readers agree on the projection of a PubMed Central X-ray figure only at kappa 0.557 (ROCOv2, cited by the Reality Check). The store keeps one record per figure with no reader named. The zero counter is static (benches contradictions, row 80, and the [transitional contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/transitional-vertebrae-and-border-variants.md), row 29, which warns against calling the bench empty), and no later record mentions a second pass.

### The adjudication bench

The [FluoroCap Adjudication Bench](../../artifacts/fluorocap-adjudication-bench-rnsyir/), undated, holds batch b1: 120 panels from the decode in `decode_v2_v2.jsonl`, stratified, in its words, across deciding evidence and spinal region; what the deciding evidence is belongs to [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md). The manifest has 31 strata, most of four panels, the largest of eleven and some of one. Of the panels 46 are lumbar, 34 thoracic, 33 cervical and 7 sacral, and 93 carry a caption that names a level. The artifact index gives 121 images, which is wrong: manifest, strata and folder agree on 120 (benches contradictions, row 48).

The reader reads the film first and reveals the caption only if it is needed, the reveal being recorded, and chooses correct, off by one, wrong by more than one, cannot tell or not a spine radiograph, with the correct levels and a note. On a caption-free panel the page tells the reader that this read is the only ground truth the panel will ever have. But the page shows what the pipeline decided, so the read is not blind, and it keeps one record per panel, keyed by the image, in a store every reader shares and merges on opening, so a second reader would overwrite the first. The FluoroCap contradictions confirm that the bench is single-read (row 30): it can keep neither two blind reads nor an adjudication, and its reads must be keyed by reader and an adjudication pass added before agreement can be reported. Whether instructions outside the page asked for two readers is not recorded (benches contradictions, row 45).

Batch b1 also predates the refetch's re-split of the corpus, so any verdict on it applies to that decode alone (benches contradictions, row 81). And 120 panels over 31 strata can show which evidence fails, not how often: near 85 percent the bench gives a Wilson interval of 77.5 to 90.3 percent, four of four in a stratum has a lower bound of 51 percent, and three of four spans 30 to 95. Neither a per-stratum accuracy nor a bound near 99 percent is possible (benches contradictions, row 44), and the bench claims neither.

### What the three share

The benches differ in purpose, measuring, finding anomalies and seeing where the evidence fails, and share three properties. No export holds a verdict. Each keeps one record per item with no reader named, so none can hold two independent reads. And two of the three show the machine's answer. Their designs also do not pool without route and stratum weights that no page records (benches contradictions, row 46). The artifact index notes that the adjudication bench had no verdict saved as of 22 October, but the exports show only the static page and not the stored reads (FluoroCap contradictions, row 41), so the note does not settle the store. The record treats each bench as built and not yet scored, and restates the two verification benches' sizes on the Honest 99% page (benches contradictions, row 66); a planner should cite them the same way until their stores are read.

## Random, seeded and never trained on

The level bench draws at random because a set chosen by the model's own uncertainty could not estimate accuracy, and the Honest 99% page calls such a set a biased estimator that cannot be reused as a test set, a live risk since the project was choosing models on noisy labels. The page cites a chest-radiograph study in which noisy validation labels made the apparently best model measure 45.9 percent when its true accuracy was 69.9 percent, and a diabetic-retinopathy study in which adjudicating 0.22 percent of the labels moved an area under the curve from 0.942 to 0.986. It names neither paper, so both figures stay unverified.

The project's other reading queues are rightly not random. The September sites put the likely transitional films first, and the October screening plan in [docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md) sends films to a reader ranked by the machine, so readers' time goes where the anomalies are. A ranked queue is the tool for finding rare anatomy and growing the rare classes, but its accuracy describes the queue and not the population. The annotation rules keep measurement apart: each round a random sample of a few hundred machine-labelled films is read, the precision per level and per finding is reported with its interval, and prevalence comes from confirmed reads only.

A fixed seed makes the sample exist before any verdict. It cannot be redrawn until it looks better, anyone can audit it, and its strata and size are declared first, as the Reality Check asks when it says to pre-register the label convention, the strata and the sample sizes. The level bench records neither the seed nor the draw, and keeping both beside the sample is a cheap repair.

A measurement sample never trains anything, for the reason [chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) gives for every split: a person the model has learned from is no longer held out. In [docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) the test witness frames are drawn at every checkpoint but read only at a version boundary, never to choose one, since selection on test persons counts as seeing them, and a test person's items go only to a test shard, never to a trainer, which is also where the owner's new annotations of the rare classes land. Under the annotation rules test persons are never machine-labelled, and machine labels carry the reader `model:<run id>` in shards of their own. A bench verdict, once given, is therefore test truth, and reads meant to train a model must be made on other persons, so the reading programme has to plan its two jobs on different persons from the start.

## The reference standards and who reads

### FluoroCap

Beyond the protocol above, the card says the working manifest will be frozen as version 1 with the reader labels, which will then replace the detector's counts. It names screening pages for students and anchor-labelling pages for readers, says the study has not started, and calls the level now attached to a panel a proposal for the readers, not ground truth. Nothing has started since. The Reality Check finds no human-verified panel among 17,486 manifest rows, the reader and adjudication fields empty, the 120-panel reading set and 65 reading pages unread and the reader sheet's 4,218 rows blank, and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) of 17 October finds quality control pending on all 21,603 rows and no annotated test set of about 200 panels. The FluoroCap contradictions (row 41) carry it with its date: no FluoroCap panel had a human read as of 17 October, and the stores must be read before anyone claims zero later. [Chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md) describes the corpus these reads would serve.

Two cautions shape a FluoroCap reference read. A reader of a limited field counts as the author did, so the Reality Check asks for a written label convention before any reading. And on transitional anatomy the panel itself is weak: radiograph readers agree on transitional type only at kappa 0.16 to 0.32 (Hanhivaara 2024), which [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) gives as a sensitivity of 32 percent and a kappa of 0.22 on radiographs against 76 percent and 0.66 on CT, so that stratum needs a whole-spine or CT reference. The [Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/) page bounds the argument: its verifiers voted down, one to two, the claim that such kappas make plain-film labels unusable as ground truth, and it notes that the published gold standard was one radiologist's read across modalities. A two-reader read of ordinary panels is a reference; a read of a transitional panel without its CT is evidence. And what a reader can name on a small caption-free lateral panel of L4 to S1, the median panel having a 320-pixel short side, has never been measured, so the human baseline for that input is unknown.

### The retrospective collection

The [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), a draft with many fields still to confirm, takes the operated level from the operative note of a single surgeon of record, chosen for consistent documentation, with post-operative imaging as the reference where the note is ambiguous or records a re-localisation. Trained annotators, medical or graduate students on the study team, box every visible body, the anchors and any radiopaque instrument by a written protocol, see only coded frames, and send disagreements to the investigator, the owner. Cases are split by patient; the held-out set is drawn before any training, stratified by region and prior instrumentation, and sealed, with the analysis code frozen; and per-body recall is reported with a patient-level cluster bootstrap interval.

On 18 September [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/) planned the collection as radiographs enriched for transitional anatomy and read against cross-sectional imaging. The operative note records what the surgeon did, and in a wrong-level case it records the wrong level unless the error was found and written down, so the recourse to post-operative imaging is what keeps the reference from inheriting the error the system exists to catch. For frames whose patient had a preoperative CT, the Reality Check's route depends on nobody's counting: registration to that CT, reported correct on all 398 intraoperative radiographs of the LevelCheck series (De Silva 2016), though even there two readers flagged 1 and 24 of the 398 labels as questionable.

### The reads of October

The October rules govern every read from now on. Every annotation names its reader, with a role and a time, and a second reader writes a second file, never an edit, so two readers' calls on one image are the transitional stratum's two-reader truth and a disagreement is kept as data. The column's count is recorded once per image with a confidence of sure, likely or cannot tell, a body that cannot be named is kept as unknown, and a hidden corner is left empty rather than guessed. The retraining plan reports the transitional stratum as its own rows with Wilson intervals on person-keyed counts, and holds frames waiting for a second read for an outside clinical reader, sending nothing until the owner says so. The [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) records the owner's goal of 22 October: every paired set verified by a second reader; where a CT and the X-ray geometry are both known, level labels need no reader; and annotated X-rays without a CT either train an independent labeller or test the network, one role per person.

Research Directions' rule that a segmenter's call is never promoted without a read rests on evidence in the [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) of 8 October: the labeller used to screen one CT collection for transitional anatomy matched reader truth on 10 of 24 L6 calls and 0 of 52 T13 calls in CTSpinoPelvic1K. Research Directions' CT queues are read under a frozen protocol: the 63-volume queue; nine disputed records for a third reader blind to the first two; and a Castellvi grading of all 802 cases by two readers and a senior adjudicator, held to a second-reader kappa of at least 0.63, at about 40 to 67 reader-hours, with 33 graded against the 71 to 142 expected. Those 33 count graded CTs, a different quantity from the verified real-frame persons below ([programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 18).

### Who reads

The readers appear by role, and the role follows the task. Students screen and trained annotators box; a radiology resident was to settle the September sites' disagreements and surgeons to take the reader study; the owner is to adjudicate the retrospective annotations and adds new annotations of the rare classes; an outside clinical reader is to give the transitional stratum its second read once the owner allows it; and a senior adjudicator is to settle the CT grading. The benches name no reader: the Honest 99% page says they wait for the owner, and the adjudication bench's page does not say.

## Reader-study designs

### The twelve-case quiz and the reading sites

[Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/), the summary of 18 September that [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) supersedes, alone holds the September reading programme, and its figures are quoted here as [a record, not a final value](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/). A twelve-case quiz with answers taken from CT, nine needed and taken once, gated both annotation sites, because most people have never been shown a transitional spine, and two readers who share the same misconception agree with each other, and that agreement is what gets written into the training set. The quiz is a gate and not a measurement: nine of twelve has a Wilson interval of 46.8 to 91.1 percent. The measure of the reading is the agreement between readers, which the sites reported live as Cohen's kappa.

One site held 3,000 radiographs already labelled by the model, where one name fixes the whole film and the reader says whether a junction is transitional; the other held 1,500 published panels with their captions shown for rough location only. Each film was to have two readers, kappa on a progress tab and disagreements sent to a radiology resident, and Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor describes the same two queues. Both showed the reader the model's view of how likely the spine was transitional and a flag on any suspect box, as a prompt to look harder, and the page says neither is an answer. These were reads for training labels, ordered to find anomalies and not blind to the machine. No later record reports what they produced, and their links are not reproduced here.

### The surgeons' reader study

The canonical design, in Naming Vertebrae Without CT, is the measurement the clinical claim actually needs. Surgeons name the level on limited-field films, each film seen twice, once unaided and once with the system's answer shown, separated in time and in randomised order so that the first read cannot be recalled during the second, with transitional spines over-represented. It yields four rates: the unaided accuracy, a local baseline; the assisted accuracy, comparable to the 97.6 percent of the CT-registration overlay; the rate at which a correct suggestion rescues a wrong read; and the rate at which a wrong one overturns a correct read, the harm such aids rarely report. It is a reader study on stored images, not a clinical trial, and measures help to a person and not outcome. The comparator is Manbachi 2018, five surgeons on 62 hard films, 69.6 percent unaided and 97.6 percent with the overlay in cases that had a segmented preoperative CT. Whether a surgeon with this system reaches the second figure without a CT is, the page says, a question nothing yet answers.

The superseded summary alone holds the tool as built, its figures again [a record, not a final value](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/). The reader moved a simulated C-arm, seeing bone only where it pointed, and each move cost a recorded exposure; readers were never told the answer, and each film's travel stopped short of the sacrum. The suggestion was wrong on purpose on 33 of the 77 films, against about 3 percent in ordinary use, to measure how often a confident wrong suggestion talks a correct reader out of a correct answer. The page states the limit itself: only ten held-out frames were both transitional and misnamed by the system, all ten are in the study, and that is a census rather than a sample, so the study can show that a wrong suggestion overturns a correct read but not how often, and no rate should be quoted from it. Nine of the 33 error films were real radiographs and the rest renders.

Two consequences follow that the page does not draw. With wrong suggestions on 43 percent of the recorded films, an assisted accuracy describes this mix, so it must be reported separately for correct and wrong suggestions, or re-weighted to the ordinary error rate, before it is set beside 97.6 percent. And readers who meet wrong suggestions that often may trust them less than they would in practice, which would make the overturn rate an underestimate of the harm in use.

None of it has been run. The State of the literature lists no human reader study and says Manbachi's design has no counterpart here, and the [handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) of 8 October says paper 2, the comparison with registration-assisted surgeons, needs human reads of transitional test persons, its blinded gate having been cancelled on 27 September.

## How many reads each question needs

Each size in the record answers its own question, and the benches contradictions (row 43) ask that each be carried with its target and never compared with the others as one number.

| Question | Target | Reads needed | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy of FluoroCap's labels | +/-5 points near 85 percent | 196 adjudicated panels, [a record, not a final value](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) | Corpus Audit |
| The same, tighter | +/-3 points near 85 percent | 545 panels, two readers and adjudication | Reality Check |
| A 99 percent per-vertebra claim | lower bound above 98 percent in each of at least four primary strata | about 1,500 verified vertebrae per stratum | Reality Check |
| A 99 percent selective-accuracy claim | 99 percent among answered cases | about 298 answered; 2,673 class-conditional over 27 classes | State of the literature |
| Calibration per class on real film | a class-conditional guarantee | about 99 real cases per class | Honest 99% |
| Per-body recall, retrospective collection | 97 percent +/-1 point | about 1,100 held-out bodies (1,118) | Retrospective protocol |
| The same at 99 percent | +/-0.5 points | about 1,520 bodies | Retrospective protocol |
| Exact level on transitional real frames | Wilson lower bound 90 percent | 35 persons with no error | Research Directions |
| Sensitivity to a variant | lower bound 85 percent | 22 carriers with no miss | Research Directions |
| Paper 2's superiority over 97.6 percent | lower bound above 97.6 percent | 152 with no error; 708 for 80 percent power at a true 99 percent | State of the literature; Research Directions |

The arithmetic holds, with three qualifications. The audit's 196 and the Reality Check's 545 are the normal approximation at an assumed 85 percent, 195.9 and 544.2 reads; near 50 percent the same widths would need 384 and 1,067. Wilson intervals are asymmetric near the top of the scale: 196 panels at 85 percent give 79.3 to 89.3 percent, 5.7 points below and 4.3 above, and holding the lower side within 5 or 3 points takes 246 or 630 reads, the figures to use when a claim rests on the lower bound. The protocol's 1,118 is the same approximation at 97 percent, and 1,100 bodies give 95.8 to 97.9 percent before clustering; the protocol calls its 1-point width a planning value, bootstraps by patient, reports the width achieved and checks on a 20-case pilot query whether the held-out set can reach 1,100 bodies at all. The [renders contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) (row 31) find its 1,520 and the Reality Check's 1,500 to be one calculation by two methods, and the [OR-study contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md) (row 22) carry about 1,100 for the primary endpoint.

The 1,500 per stratum is a width, not a power calculation. At an observed 99 percent it gives 98.4 to 99.4 percent, and a stratum reading 99 percent clears 98 from about 753 reads. But at a true 99 percent, 753 reads clear 98 only about half the time, 1,500 reads, allowing at most 19 errors, about 88 percent of the time, and 2,000 about 95 percent, so a stratum that must clear the bar needs nearer 2,000. The small gates check: 35 and 22 with no error give Wilson lower bounds of 90.1 and 85.1 percent, the seven unverified persons Research Directions counts give at best 64.6, 152 is the first count with no error whose exact two-sided bound clears 97.6 percent, and 708 gives about 82 percent power at a true 99 percent. The 298 is the error-free count at which an exact one-sided bound brings the error rate down to 1 percent, and 2,673 is 27 classes of 99.

Against these targets the benches are small: 179 panels sit near the +/-5-point size and far below the 545, and 120 panels are spread over 31 strata. The record prices the reads. The 545-panel read is two readers for a working day or two at a minute a panel, and 6,000 reads are weeks (the Reality Check); grading the 802 CTs is 40 to 67 reader-hours, and each stage of reading 1,052 tier-1 CTs is 50 to 90 (Research Directions).

## Reads never done

On the date of the latest record none of these reads had been completed.

- No FluoroCap panel had a human read: none of 17,486 rows on 15 September, quality control pending on all 21,603 on 17 October, and no annotated test set of about 200 panels.
- The two-reader read of the 545-panel set, which the Reality Check set for the first two weeks of its 90-day plan, has no record of having happened.
- Whether any bench's store holds a verdict is open, since none was read here (benches contradictions, rows 79 and 80).
- No human level read exists on any real fluoroscopic frame: Research Directions says every real two-dimensional number is against automatic truth, and the State of the literature gives C-ArmLSpine's levels as the work of two automatic labellers.
- No verified real-frame transitional test person exists, against the 35 or 22 the gates need; the eight transitional spines of Naming Vertebrae Without CT are of any image type, renders included, with no human read stated ([level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 40).
- The surgeons' reader study has not been run, and the human baseline on a small caption-free panel has never been measured.
- The CT queues stand open, and Research Directions names the most valuable single read in the holding: if a reader is free, the 11 field-supported L6 calls of the COBRA screen are the single most decisive afternoon, because they can retire the real-frame transitional claim before any model is built.
- The retrospective collection is a protocol, its case counts and held-out fraction waiting on a pilot query.

This is the bottleneck the record names, not an oversight: Research Directions orders the work as measurement first, human reads second and models third, and every gate in the table waits on a read.

- The Honest 99% page is a snapshot of 16 September (benches contradictions, row 83), and its headline of 99 percent accuracy at 35.7 percent coverage on held-out renders is [a record, not a final value](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/), from a bank the October record calls leaked and asks to be run again (benches contradictions, row 9).

## What a planner should do first

These steps follow from the record; none is a measured result.

1. Read the three stores and record what they hold, with the date.
2. Key every read by reader, keep adjudication as its own pass, and hide the machine's proposal on a random part of each bench, so a bench can report agreement between readers and how far the proposal moves a read.
3. Keep each sample's seed, draw and decode beside it, and label any verdict on batch b1 as belonging to its decode.
4. Write the counting convention, strata and sizes down before the first read.
5. Do the two-reader read of the 545-panel set, a day or two for two readers.
6. Keep measurement reads and training reads on different persons, and never train on a bench.
7. Read the transitional stratum against CT, starting with the 11 L6 calls.

## What we know

The record holds three benches and the design of a full reading programme, and no completed read. The level verification bench is a random, seeded sample of 179 decoded panels and 865 boxes, drawn within route, for measuring the accuracy of the machine's names; the LSTV bench is an enriched set of 585 transitional-term figures for finding and typing real anomalies; the adjudication bench is a stratified set of 120 panels in 31 strata from a superseded decode. Each was exported with no verdict, keeps one record per item with no reader named, and none can hold a two-reader read. FluoroCap's reference standard is two blind readers and an adjudicator with everything locked first, and the built bench cannot run it. The retrospective collection's reference is one surgeon's operative note, with post-operative imaging where the note is ambiguous. October's rules make every read name its reader and never machine-label a test person. The record's sample sizes are sound for the targets they state, and the [concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md) settles no figure for any bench or read.

## What we do not know

We do not know whether any verdict sits in any bench's store; the seed and draw behind the level bench; who was meant to read the adjudication bench, or whether instructions outside its page asked for two readers. We do not know the accuracy of any FluoroCap label, the human baseline on a small caption-free panel, how far a shown proposal moves a reader, or how often a wrong suggestion overturns a correct surgeon. No record reports what the September sites produced, and the retrospective collection's case counts, held-out fraction and box-matching criterion are still to be confirmed.

## What would change our mind

Verdicts found in the stores would turn the benches into results, and the first to report would be the level bench's accuracy per route, with its can't-tell rate and an interval clustered by panel. A blind subsample read with and without the proposal would show whether a verification bench can stand in for the card's reference read. A two-reader read of the 545-panel set would replace caption agreement with accuracy and measure the captions' convention noise; a kappa near the radiograph values for transitional typing would confirm that the stratum needs CT, and a high one would allow panel reads there. A pilot query short of 1,100 bodies would reopen the retrospective protocol's size, and a reader study on an ordinary mix of suggestions could put a rate on overturned reads.
