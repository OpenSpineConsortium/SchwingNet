# 08 FluoroCap as a dataset: the card, the harvest and what it may be used for

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter stays in this private repository and gives FluoroCap's facts, counts and rules only, while how its evidence decoder and level model work belongs to Part III, in [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md).

FluoroCap is the project's corpus of published spine fluoroscopy and radiograph panels, mined from journal figures, open image collections, video and social media. This chapter settles, for whoever plans an experiment near it, what the corpus is and what one manifest row carries; which count to quote for each date; which routes and credentials fed it, and under what rule for secrets; what its caption-derived labels are worth; and what it may be used for. The last answer is fixed by the record, a test set at most once readers have labelled it, so the chapter says what reading that takes and where the reading stands.

## What FluoroCap is

The canonical definition is the [FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/), generated at 09:43 on 14 September 2026. It defines FluoroCap as a prospectively defined, reproducible collection of published intraoperative spine fluoroscopy and radiograph panels whose captions name the vertebral level, assembled under a written search-and-inclusion protocol for the validation of vertebral-level identification on intraoperative images. The card cuts two releases from one manifest: Dataset A takes every open-access licence tier for research use, and Dataset B keeps only the CC BY, CC0 and public-domain panels.

The card reads like the card of a public release, and the later record overrides it. [The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) of 27 September lists FluoroCap among what is private forever, never in a public repository and never on the website; the October [handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) repeats it as its rule 8; and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) gives the licence as figure reuse only, never redistributed. Both the [FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md) (row 7) and the [datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) (row 8) carry the later rule. Datasets A and B are therefore two frozen internal subsets of one manifest. The atlas gives the frozen v0.1 as 2,531 panels in A and 991 in B, and the [reproduce note](../../docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md) names that release the authoritative copy, with a hash check against its lock record that no record shows run.

### What one manifest row carries

The card's account of a panel is the canonical one. Each source figure is fetched, hashed as downloaded, split into its panels at the gutters, re-encoded from pixel data so that no EXIF or DICOM metadata travels with it, hashed again, and written to one row. The manifest of 14 September had 110 columns. A row carries the source record and the routes that found it; the licence string as read, its URL, when it was read, the fields derived from it (commercial use, derivative use, redistribution) and a tier; the fetch record (HTTP status, client string and time); the de-identification basis; modality and projection; the levels named for the figure or for the panel; the devices, positioning and population sentences quoted from the article; image geometry; the inclusion or exclusion reason; prespecified subgroup flags; and the reference-standard fields that readers fill in, `reader_1`, `reader_2` and `adjudication`, beside `ground_truth_status` and `human_qc_status`. The hashes run in a chain from the original figure to the panel, so every panel traces back to the published figure it was cut from.

The licence is judged per row, not per source. Anything not clearly Creative Commons goes to tier E and waits in a review queue until a person reads the article's licence statement, and no panel with an unresolved licence enters Dataset B; on 14 September that queue held 166, a count the card's sentence calls figures and that matches Dataset A's 166 tier-E panels. The card's legend sets tier A for CC0 and public domain, B for CC BY, C for CC BY-SA, D for non-commercial and E for other or unknown, but its own counts do not follow the legend. Dataset A's tiers read A 1,117, C 901, D 631 and E 166, with no tier B at all, although 1,099 of Dataset B's panels carry a CC BY licence (1,094 of them under the plain string "cc by"); the counted tier A takes in those CC BY panels with 15 CC0 and 3 public-domain ones, which together make Dataset B's 1,117. Whoever filters on the tier column should read the coding from the manifest itself.

De-identification rests on the source: these are figures already published. The card says no patient record, DICOM header or institutional image was accessed; it marks every included panel as re-encoded with no metadata carried, and its scan of the captions for identifiers found none on the 2,815 included panels. The contradictions page limits that sentence to what the card describes, the v0.2 manifest of journal figures, since the corpus on disk also holds video frames, closed-journal copies fetched through the institutional library proxy, and Instagram and Reddit images (rows 4 and 5); the runbook's Instagram route keeps each post's caption, poster and date.

### Inclusion and exclusion

Four inclusion criteria must all hold: an open-access source or an aggregator of the PMC open-access subset (I1); a caption that states fluoroscopy, C-arm or intraoperative imaging, or plain radiography (I2); a caption that names the spine (I3); and a vertebral level named for the figure or for the panel (I4). Exclusions are recorded with their reasons rather than silently dropped: no level named for the panel (E1), a panel segment that names another modality (E2), a non-human subject (E3), a screening classifier's judgement that the panel is not a spine radiograph (E5), a short side below 128 pixels (E6), and a hit in the caption's identifier scan that waits for review (E7). The card calls cadaver, phantom or model subjects (E4) and licence problems (E8) flags for review at that version, not exclusions, and 459 of Dataset A's panels carry the cadaver-or-phantom flag; yet its own exclusion counts list E4 on 89 excluded rows, as the only reason on 24 of them, so its prose and its counts disagree here too. On the [card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/)'s counts, a record, not a final value, the 10,281 rows of 14 September gave 2,815 panels from 2,090 figures in 1,199 articles to Dataset A and 1,117 panels from 826 figures in 529 articles to Dataset B; the 7,466 rows excluded were led by the screening judgement and a missing level, alone or together. The search protocol, with its query strings in eleven languages, is written down.

### What the pipeline map adds

The [FluoroCap Pipeline Map](../../artifacts/fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs/) is canonical for the corpus's structure, and structure is all this chapter takes from it: sources from the open literature to video, Reddit, patent drawings and manufacturers' sites; an ingest lane that ends in near-duplicate groups, so that a split keeps each group on one side ([chapter 09](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md)); and a release frozen only after two readers and an adjudication. Two of its claims have no source in the record: that the manifest regenerates byte-identical from the same inputs, which the card also asserts and no recorded run shows (row 48), while the reproduce note says the harvest itself is not bit-reproducible, since web sources drift; and that PMC throttles the grid's outbound address and not the owner's workstation, which the access setup shares and no recorded test supports (row 49).

## The harvest: routes, credentials and the secrets rule

The [FluoroCap Access Setup](../../artifacts/fluorocap-access-setup-jlvkft/) of 14 September is the runbook for every credential the harvesters use. It is a member of the cluster, not its canonical page, and the night after it was written tested most of its routes.

### How secrets are handled

All credentials live in one file on the owner's workstation, readable only by the owner and outside every repository. Values go in with an editor and never on a command line, so they never enter a shell history or a conversation. A check command reports which keys are set and probes each service, by name and pass or fail only, never a value. Browser sessions travel as exported cookie jars, and no password is ever stored. The page promises that no value is ever printed, written to a log or a commit, or sent anywhere but the service it belongs to; it carries no secret itself and says none should be pasted into a chat. [The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) makes the rule the programme's: credentials are read from files, never echoed, never passed in a job's exported environment and never committed, and students use their own read token.

### The routes, in the order the runbook set

The runbook orders the work in five steps. First, a contact address, which identifies the harvester to the Crossref, Unpaywall and NCBI polite pools and alone carried the credential-free refetch. Second, four keys: NCBI's, which allows ten requests a second instead of three for the 2,900 figures the refetch resolves through it; the YouTube Data API's, at 10,000 units a day; Springer Nature's, for figure lists; and Elsevier's, free, with entitlement to closed articles coming from the institution. Third, closed journals: the campus network first, on the belief that publishers authenticate by address, and only if that failed the library proxy, logged into once in a browser, its session cookie exported, the password never stored. Fourth, Instagram, through a dedicated account. Fifth, when convenient, Flickr, Reddit, Google Programmable Search and PatentsView, the last because Google Patents had cut the harvester off after forty requests.

The runbook set the closed-journal refetch its own limits: only the figures that already carried a matched caption, about 600, at one request every three seconds per host, capped per day, never full text or PDFs in bulk. It counted the publishers' text-and-data-mining interfaces (Elsevier's, Springer's and Wiley's) as covered by the institutional licence and advised asking the library for the terms. Two sites stayed outside the harvest altogether: the [Honest 99 report](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) records that Radiopaedia and Eurorad were not scraped.

The [FluoroCap Overnight Report](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/) shows what the night made of the plan. The owner's credentials worked: the proxy session reached SAGE, Wiley and JNS and was used on 653 figures, of which 217 were kept, and 98 figures came from PDFs, through Wiley's mining interface and open PDFs, matched to their figures by content. Wolters Kluwer, Ovid and Thieme stayed blocked, each needing a proxy session of its own. The campus-network route, which the one probe of that day left inconclusive, was never needed, so the runbook's worries about library access are history (rows 40 and 49); the network stages ran from the owner's workstation, as the reproduce note still prescribes.

### The mining terms against the library's current statement

The plan asks for the terms to be checked against the library's current statement. The record's statement is in [docs/research/README.md](../../docs/research/README.md): Elsevier granted ScienceDirect and Scopus text and data mining on 18 October 2026, and on 19 October the library and Elsevier confirmed that Claude may read Elsevier text for academic use while the token and the text stay private, as the [notebooks memory](../../docs/memory/notebooks.md) repeats. That statement is Elsevier's alone, and it came a month after the harvest: the overnight report counts 129 figures replaced from Elsevier's image server and 94 from Wiley, through the proxy and Wiley's mining interface, on the night of 14 to 15 September. A search of the repository finds no statement of Springer's or Wiley's terms, so for those two interfaces the September runbook, which counted them under the institutional licence, is still the only record, and their terms are unsettled.

## The refetch that raised panel size

On 14 September the defect the [audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) found in the pixels was size, not quality. On its figures, a record, not a final value, the panels' short side ran 128, 181, 231, 328 and 600 pixels at the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 95th percentiles; 45.8 percent were below 224 pixels and 7.9 percent reached 512; and half the corpus was smaller than the window the level model reads ([chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md)). The commonest exposure fault, a blown-out panel, touched 5.1 percent of 900 files opened. The cause was the source, in the runbook's account: the figure PMC serves is the rendition the publisher deposited, while the publisher's own site keeps the larger file, and for the subscription titles PMC holds the author's manuscript at whatever size the author uploaded.

The runbook's test on 21 figures replaced 18 with publisher originals, at gains in pixel count of 16 times at Cureus, 9 to 20 at MDPI, 12 at Elsevier's image server, 6 at Frontiers and 4 at Springer, BMC and Nature; the three it missed were SAGE's. Round one then ran over 2,901 figures. The [overnight report](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/)'s count, 1,489 replaced at a median 6.3 times the pixels, is a record, not a final value; the [reality check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) superseded it the same morning with 1,558 of 2,901 replaced (53.7 percent), a median gain of 6.26 times in area (about 2.5 times linear), and the replaced figures' median short side raised from 474 to 1,254 pixels. The latest count is the Honest 99 report's of 16 September, 2,059 figures replaced at a median 6.25 times, which no page marks final (row 16). Checked against the runbook's test, the run held up: its median gain of about six times sits inside the test's spread of 4 to 20 times, though nearly half of round one's figures were not replaced.

The latest size distribution in the record is the reality check's, read from the panel index on 18,068 figure panels, with 51 files on disk that have no entry. The short side runs 133, 212, 320, 494 and 1,043 pixels at the same percentiles; 28.8 percent are below 224, 23.5 percent at least 512 and 5.2 percent at least 1,024. The ledger marks the [median of 320](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-157-median-fluorocap-panel-short-side) current and the audit's 231 superseded. Video panels run 180, 320, 435, 640 and 720 pixels, none at or above 768, because the sources were 720p; the runbook's premise that video is the one source at 1080 pixels native is not what the harvest delivered (row 29). Native C-arm frames are 1,536 to 1,954 pixels across on a flat panel and about 1,000 on an image intensifier, so the median panel is three to six times coarser linearly; a detected lumbar vertebra is a median 55 pixels tall on the 4,218 included panels; and the panels' bit depth was never measured, while every panel is an 8-bit window the author chose from a 12- to 16-bit frame. The reality check's verdict puts the refetch in its place: the binding constraint is labelled real intraoperative images, not pixels, and the mined figures cannot supply them.

## What the labels are worth

Every level in FluoroCap is caption-derived: the level the author stated, or a machine proposal attached to it ([chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md)), which the card calls a proposal for the readers, not ground truth. Every row says so. The `ground_truth_status` field reads caption-derived (author-stated level), reader labels pending, and `human_qc_status` reads pending, on all 2,815 included panels on 14 September and on all 17,486 manifest rows on 15 September, and on 17 October the atlas finds the same on all 21,603.

### What the 14 September audit alone holds

The [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) is superseded by the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) of the next morning, and its specific figures appear only in it, so each is quoted here as a record, not a final value. Three of them still frame the corpus. The first is agreement with the caption. On the 389 caption-held-out panels, where agreement with the caption is a test (on 1,678 others it is not, for reasons chapter 15 gives), the proposal's level set and the caption's nested one inside the other on 56.8 percent, with a mean Jaccard of 0.34 and no interval. The reality check measured it again at 57.1 percent, Jaccard 0.341, on 380 caption-free panels, also with no interval, and warned that they are not a random hold-out, since they were selected by whether the caption fitted; the contradictions page carries that later pair with its caveat and calls neither pair an accuracy (row 3). The audit's own reading is the one to keep: agreement between two automatic sources bounds error from one side only, because two sources can agree and both be wrong. The second is size, the half of the corpus below the level model's window, which the refetch addressed. The third is that T13 and L6 had never been assigned to a single real panel, taken up below.

### Why nothing may train on them

The reality check gives the reasons in its ranked blockers, and they are reasons about the data, not about any model. The labels are noisy before anyone misreads a pixel: caption labels carry an estimated 5 to 10 percent counting-convention noise. The papers it cites put numerical variants at 7.7 percent of whole-spine MRIs (Tins 2016) and a non-standard count at 8.8 percent of 3,147 full-length radiographs; outpatient level counts were wrong in 54 percent of 71 patients with a transitional vertebra; and AP radiographs mistype 35.2 percent of Castellvi cases against CT. In its words, the caption's "L4" was, in most figures, assigned by the author counting on a limited field, and a 99 percent target "measured against labels wrong in one case in twelve is uninterpretable".

The panels also carry what a network would learn in place of anatomy. Of 2,815 panels scanned for text, 67.5 percent carry printed text and 3.6 percent a printed level label, a leak a network will learn. Half of PMC figures are compound and the best published sub-figure detector reaches 36.9 percent mAP on them, so panel boundaries are noisy too. Modality is unknown for 7,642 of 17,486 manifest rows (44 percent; the count then, since 21,603), and 11,446 images sit in 10,233 near-duplicate groups. In its words, training a level head on this teaches arrows and captions; evaluating on it requires reading.

Much of the corpus is not a spine radiograph at all. The screening classifier scored the 18,119 figure panels of 15 September: 5,665 (31 percent) it called likely spine radiographs, and 8,843 (49 percent) not an x-ray at all; and it calls 732 of the 2,071 decoded panels that carry a level label (35 percent) not a spine radiograph. These are the classifier's calls, not verified counts, since its 93.1 percent was measured on 2,143 held-out film-manifest items, not on FluoroCap panels (row 2), and they replace the audit's inference that a large share of the panels without a vertebra box were detector misses (row 1); how large that share is stays unmeasured until a person reads a sample of them. The 2,991 panels that pass at its stricter setting are classifier-passed, not verified, one measurement that the overnight report and the reality check both carry (row 44). The registry and the atlas also give a share of panels that are reproductions with no pixel residual, but the contradictions page asks for the script, threshold and output file behind it before it is cited (row 46), and its records point only to notes in another repository, so this chapter does not quote it.

### L6, T13 and the transitional stratum

The audit's statement that T13 and L6 had never been assigned to a real panel was true of the decode of that day, and the reality check found the same zero on 15 September; the zero was a property of that day's decoder rather than of the corpus (chapter 15 says why). The decode of 16 September placed L6 on 338 panels, 145 in the report's caption-named column and 193 in its caption-free one, and T13 on 254, 106 and 148. Those are machine placements that no person has checked, and the contradictions page states the one thing the record supports: no real panel carries a human-verified L6 or T13 (row 12). It adds that nothing explains 145 caption-named L6 panels when the caption search found so few transitional captions. The same report describes a transitional bench of 585 candidate figures, deliberately enriched for finding anomalies rather than for measuring accuracy.

The caption search is the second count the record has not settled. The audit counts 2 of 2,815 captioned panels mentioning any transitional term. The reality check counts 22 unique figures in the fluoroscopy arm, at most 7 of them possibly human and none read, and 20 in the pathology arm, 3 of them human, with most of the rest animals in both. The ledger marks the count cannot-tell, so neither is a current value, and the reality check lists expecting transitional positives from those figures among the things to stop doing. A transitional stratum would need a reference beyond the caption, a whole-spine image or a CT, since radiograph readers agree on the type of a lumbosacral transitional vertebra only at kappas of 0.16 to 0.32 (Hanhivaara 2024); [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) takes the stratum up.

## What it may be used for

### The rule, and how it was reached

The record moved in four steps. On 14 September the audit still allowed retraining the level model on the corpus, on a condition of its training recipe. On 15 September the reality check took that back: its Tier 4, the mined figures and video, is an external test set only, after a two-reader read of a stratified sample, and the first thing it says to stop doing is training or fine-tuning any level head on caption-derived labels. Directive D1 of 23 September then dropped FluoroCap from training altogether, as the [reproduce note](../../docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md) records. The October handoff states it as the directive that FluoroCap is out of every training set, and [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) puts FluoroCap in no training list, as a test set of about 200 panels or written off. The registry and its record now read: out of training by directive D1; a test set at most; still inside the old fold banks. The atlas adds that the planned annotated test set of about 200 panels does not exist. The contradictions page carries D1 as the rule (row 8), and the [retraining plan](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) writes it into the new workflow's design, in which a shard that carries a source the directives exclude, FluoroCap and D1 named, is refused and never registered. That is a design, not a run; what can be read today is that the workflow's [registry](../../data/registry/README.md), one file per source that enters the training workflow, holds 24 sources and FluoroCap is not among them.

### The fold banks

The plan asks for confirmation that no FluoroCap crop remains in any training bank, as the handoff's defect note asks, and the record cannot give it. The handoff of 8 October counted 2,100 FluoroCap crops in each C-ArmLSpine fold bank, banks built from `allspine_v7`, to which a page of 17 September had added 2,385 FluoroCap panels (level-naming contradictions, row 37), and its list of open items repeats it; the level bank itself holds none, by its list of what is not in it. The registry's status of 20 October still ends "still inside the old fold banks" (row 9), and this repository's [demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) of the same day reads the model card as listing FluoroCap crops in a mixed bank. No later record lists the banks by source, and the census counts files by tree, not crops by bank, so the question stays open.

## The reference standard and where it stands

The card's reference standard is two readers, blind to each other and to the machine proposal, a third who adjudicates their disagreements, and the dataset, the model and the analysis plan locked before any performance claim; the working manifest was to be frozen as v1 with the reader labels. None of it has started in the record. The card says the study has not begun; the reality check finds 0 of 17,486 rows human-verified, the reader and adjudication fields empty on every row and the reader sheet's 4,218 rows blank; and the atlas finds human QC pending on all 21,603 rows on 17 October (row 41, which asks that the benches' stored reads be read before anyone claims zero on a later date). After 17 October the repository's [artifact index](../../artifacts/README.md) records no verdict saved on the adjudication bench as of 22 October, while the level verification bench's stored rows were not copied, so whether anyone has read it cannot be told from here.

Two benches exist to start it. The [FluoroCap Adjudication Bench](../../artifacts/fluorocap-adjudication-bench-rnsyir/) holds 120 panels, batch b1, drawn from a decode that predates the refetch's re-split, stratified by the kind of evidence behind each proposal and by region into 31 strata of about four panels each (93 with a caption level and 27 without). A reader chooses correct, off by one, wrong, cannot tell or not a spine radiograph. The bench is single-read: it keeps one read per panel in a store every reader shares, so a second read overwrites the first, and it can hold neither two blind reads nor an adjudication (row 30); its b1 verdicts, once made, apply to that decode only (row 81 of the [benches contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md)). The [Level Verification Bench](../../artifacts/level-verification-bench-mkjxsy/) is the other design: 179 panels and 865 vertebrae drawn at random within route with a fixed seed (70 from PMC, 70 from video, 28 from Commons and 11 from Reddit), which it calls the measurement, not training data. Its random draw, once read, gives the first estimate of accuracy that is not caption agreement (row 3).

The size of the read is settled. The audit puts 196 adjudicated panels at +/-5 percent and 545 at +/-3 percent, and the reality check takes the same 545 reads for +/-3 percent around 85 percent. The reality check replaced the audit's plan of 400 with two readers reading the 545-panel set in the first two weeks, a working day or two at a minute a panel, and its evaluation protocol names a two-reader, adjudicated read of at least 545 FluoroCap panels as the literature-domain check. A claim of 99 percent is another matter: bounding it within +/-0.5 percent takes about 1,500 verified images in each stratum reported, by the Wilson interval, over at least four primary strata (lumbosacral normal, lumbosacral transitional, thoracolumbar and hardware). The benches built hold 120 and 179 panels (row 31), and the October pages' 200 panels would test to about +/-5 percent by the audit's arithmetic.

FluoroCap already serves one check. The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 22 September found no near-duplicate pair between the real test frames and 29,484 FluoroCap panels; its further claim that FluoroCap's article list holds none of the test datasets' papers names no query and stays unverified (benches contradictions, row 76). And [docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md](../../docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md) counts FluoroCap's panels among the different hardware a leave-one-device-out test on real frames would draw on.

Six pages define FluoroCap, and a planner should know what each older version alone still holds.

- The [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) is superseded by the reality check, and it alone holds the figures of 14 September: images by route, the short side before the refetch, the 56.8 percent containment, per-level recall, the hardware tables, the day's renderer fixes, its plan to read 400 panels and the first statement that no real panel carries T13 or L6.
- The [FluoroCap Overnight Report](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/) is canonical for the night's log, though the reality check superseded its counts that morning; it alone holds the replacements by publisher, the proxy and PDF routes, the outage and the frame deletion, and the pathology search of 158,955 articles, 26,941 read and 1,961 level-bearing figures matched at the time.

- Three duplicates carry one measurement twice: the 2,991 classifier-passed panels (row 44), the audit's short-side row copied into the overnight report (row 43), and the share of reproductions in the atlas and the registry (row 46).

## What we know

FluoroCap is a private corpus of published spine fluoroscopy and radiograph panels, defined by a written protocol for validation, with one manifest from which two internal subsets were frozen at v0.1 (2,531 panels in A, 991 in B). Each row carries its source, its licence judged per row, its fetch record and its reader fields, and every panel is re-encoded with no metadata. The current counts are 29,500 panels and a 21,603-row manifest, which are not one population, and 20,700 panels decoded on 16 September; the census of 22 October measured 201.9 GB where the records place it, against the 316 GB carried since September. Its credentials live in one owner-only file outside every repository. The refetch had replaced 2,059 figures by 16 September at a median of about six times the pixels, and the median figure-panel short side had risen from 231 to 320 pixels, still three to six times coarser than a native frame. Every label is caption-derived, the one agreement figure that is a test is about 57 percent containment on a selected subset, no panel had a human read by 17 October, no adjudication-bench verdict was saved by 22 October, and no real panel carries a human-verified L6 or T13. By directive D1 FluoroCap is out of every training set and a test set at most, and the new workflow's registry holds no FluoroCap entry.

## What we do not know

We do not know how accurate any FluoroCap label is, because no panel has been read, nor which counting convention each caption's level follows. We do not know whether any real panel shows an L6 or a T13, how much of the corpus without a vertebra box is a detector miss rather than a non-radiograph, or whether the screening classifier's calls hold on FluoroCap panels. We do not know whether any training bank still holds a FluoroCap crop, since no record after 20 October lists the banks by source; which of the census's two copies is the one in use; or which command counted 29,500. We do not know whether anyone has read the level verification bench, whose stored rows were not copied here; the terms of Springer's and Wiley's mining interfaces, for which the repository holds no statement; or how the share of reproductions was computed, since its records name no script, threshold or output file. The dataset version of the 21,603-row manifest, and whether version 0.3 was finished, are unrecorded, and the manifest's byte-identical regeneration has never been shown by a recorded run. The panels' bit-depth history, whether the 51 files without an index entry are duplicates or losses, and how well a reader names a level on a panel of the median size without its caption are unmeasured.

## What would change our mind

A two-reader, adjudicated read of the 545-panel set, or the read of the random level verification bench, would replace caption agreement with an accuracy and an interval, measure the counting-convention noise the captions are estimated to carry, and turn FluoroCap into the external test set the plan describes. A read of the 338 L6 and 254 T13 placements, or of the transitional bench, that found human-verified positives would overturn the statement that no real panel carries one. A fresh decode of the current corpus would give a current coverage figure. A listing of every training bank's sources would settle the fold-bank question either way. A recorded run that regenerates a frozen manifest and reproduces its hash would let the book state the regeneration property; the script behind the share of reproductions would let it quote that share; the migration batch's status, with a recount of the copy in use, would settle which size to quote; and the level verification bench's stored reads would say whether the reference standard has started.
