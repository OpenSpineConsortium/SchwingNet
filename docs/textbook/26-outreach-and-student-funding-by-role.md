# 26 Outreach and student funding, by role

Label: outreach. This chapter records pages that hold no method and no data, the outreach money a student may ask for and one community event, and it names every person by role alone.

This chapter settles three things for a planner. First, where a currently enrolled medical student in the research year, with the resident as mentor, can ask for money to run OpenSpineConsortium outreach, in what order, which record to cite, and which sources require what of whom. Second, what the Celebrate Life soccer night is, for whom, when and where, and how families sign up, given that two flyers disagree. Third, which open questions decide eligibility before anyone spends an afternoon on an application. The pages behind it are [OSC Outreach Funding](../../artifacts/osc-outreach-funding-kak2gk/) of September 2026, the canonical funding page, and [Celebrate Life Scoliosis Night](../../artifacts/celebrate-life-scoliosis-night-julcos/), the canonical flyer, with the earlier [Celebrate Life Flyer](../../artifacts/celebrate-life-flyer-noutom/), which it supersedes ([the clusters](../../artifacts/_synthesis/clusters.md), cluster 14). Two pages from elsewhere in the book are read beside them: the [AIDaS Seed Grant Proposal](../../artifacts/aidas-seed-grant-proposal/), whose home is [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md), and the [spine-program student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/), whose home is [chapter 23](23-who-may-do-what.md).

## The roles

Four roles recur. The student is a currently enrolled medical student spending a research year with the project; the funding page was written for this person. The resident is the owner, a surgical resident who leads the project, mentors the student and appears on both flyers as the physician contact. The club's operations assistant is the second contact on both flyers and speaks for the venue side of the soccer night. A faculty sponsor or faculty principal investigator appears wherever a funder requires a faculty appointment, and the record names none yet. Other offices appear by function: the Registrar, the Office of Student Affairs, the coordinator of student organisation events, the GME research coordinator and the AOA councilor.

The division between the student and the resident decides most of the funding page. Nearly every student award requires a currently enrolled medical student, so the resident cannot be the applicant on any of them, which is why the page tells the student to apply. The reverse holds for the GME seed grant, which is the resident's to apply for, with the student named in its mentoring plan. A separate rule, from a different call, governs the AIDaS seed grant: there the principal investigator must hold a tenure-track or tenured faculty appointment and be an AIDaS member, so the resident is a co-investigator and the faculty slot is still a placeholder. The contradictions page checked whether these two statements conflict and refuted it: they are two rules from two calls, not one claim made twice ([the outreach contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/outreach-events-and-student-funding.md), row 23).

## Where the student can ask for money, in order

The funding page orders its sources by how fast money can arrive, not by how much it is, and it groups them into four clocks. The order is sound for a student who needs a few hundred dollars soon; it is the wrong order for someone building a programme, and the page says so by ending with the slower, larger radiology awards. Every amount, deadline and contact on the page was read off the funders' own pages in September 2026, and the page itself warns that levels and cycles change without notice; none has been re-read since ([the outreach contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/outreach-events-and-student-funding.md), row 14). Every figure below is therefore Reported, a September snapshot, and a planner re-reads the funder's page before putting any of them in a submitted application.

### Days: a request to a person, no committee

The School of Medicine Student Senate funding request is the page's first entry: a request of 250 to 400 dollars against a ceiling of 450, made by an enrolled student who sends a completed form to a named office, with no posted deadline. It is the only source with no committee calendar between the ask and the answer. Its catches are that the Senate calls itself the last resort and expects the applicant to have tried alumni and mentor funds first, that anyone who has already taken alumni or Senate money is ineligible, and that the form is written around conference attendance, so the request must say plainly that this is an outreach event and what the money buys.

The Student Activities Funding Board funds events, capped per event, for vendors, food and supplies, and is the only source on the page built for events rather than travel. Its applicant is a registered student organisation, through a budget request against posted deadlines, and its rule is strict: the event must be on campus and free and open to the public for its whole duration. A remote annotation session for enrolled trainees may not qualify at all. The page advises speaking with the coordinator of student organisation events before writing, and skipping the board rather than reshaping the programme to fit it.

### Weeks: an office reviews it

The Office of Medical Student Research's Scholarly Activity Award pays up to 500 dollars per student, with a group rate of 3,500 dollars for organisation travel, and needs a 60-day lead time. It carries a fast path: when an abstract is accepted fewer than 60 days before the meeting, the student may apply within three days of the acceptance. It funds airfare, lodging and registration for travel to present within the continental United States and Canada, so it serves outreach only when a conference presentation is attached.

The School of Medicine Alumni Association's funds reach student organisations through the Office of Student Affairs for activities, travel and events. Amounts and deadlines are not published, so the page treats this entry as a lead to confirm by telephone, not a specification.

### Months: real applications with sponsors

The WSU GME Seed Grant pays up to 2,500 dollars on a rolling 2026 to 2027 cycle, and it is the resident's application, not the student's. It is the one source on the page that pays for materials, supplies, software, printing and contracted help, and it explicitly refuses travel, conference registration and publication fees, so it cannot pay the journal's excess-page charge that started the search. It needs IRB documentation, a faculty mentor's written approval and a mentoring plan.

The AOA Medical Student Service Leadership Project Grant pays 9,000 dollars over two years, 5,000 then 4,000, to a student team, with a deadline of 16 February 2027. It is the only award on the page that funds outreach as a programme rather than one event. It has four gates: an AOA chapter member should lead the team, the project must be new, the institution may submit only one proposal every three years, and the Dean must endorse it with a commitment of matching funds.

The Community Foundation for Southeast Michigan publishes no process the page could find. Its case rests on a precedent: a student group of the same school won 70,000 dollars over two years from this foundation for STEM outreach to local youth, built in the order outreach event, then poster, then grant. The page draws the lesson that the record came before the money.

The contradictions page notes that the funding page's opening line calls its first seven entries "local and small", while the sixth is a national award of 9,000 dollars and the seventh rests on a 70,000-dollar precedent ([the outreach contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/outreach-events-and-student-funding.md), row 20). The phrase is wrong for those two; the order by speed of arrival still holds.

### The radiology societies: larger money, a later clock

Two facts govern this group. The RSNA cycles for 2026 to 2027 were closed in September and were expected to open in October 2026, with medical student deadlines around 1 February; no later page records whether they opened or what was posted (row 12). And these awards fund research experience or an education project over a year, not an event, so an application goes in as the training programme with its published framework and released dataset, not as a request for event costs.

The RSNA Research Medical Student Grant, 3,000 dollars matched by the sponsoring department to 6,000, is the largest award on the page the student can hold in their own name, and RSNA awards up to ten more to students who identify as underrepresented in medicine. The departmental match is not automatic and has to be committed before the application. The RSNA Education Project Award, 10,000 to 20,000 dollars for one year covering salary, fringe and direct costs, is the closest fit in shape, but it requires RSNA membership and is aimed at educators, so the resident or a faculty sponsor applies and the student is named on the project. The Radiology Education Research Development Grant, up to 10,000 dollars from four radiology education bodies, published neither eligibility nor deadline, and the page calls its amount the only reliable figure. The Society of Interventional Radiology offers a student research grant due 1 February and a summer internship due 13 March, a weaker fit because the project is spine imaging, not interventional radiology; the page advises one application done properly over two done thinly. The RSNA Education Scholar Grant, up to 75,000 dollars for established educators, is listed as a destination, not a target this year.

The page uses three superlatives, the largest award in the student's name, the closest fit and the serious one, and the contradictions page refuted the claim that they conflict: each has its own scope (row 21).

### Which to ask first

The page's own text disagrees with itself on the first step. It heads the Senate entry "why this one first", yet the Senate entry and the alumni entry both say the alumni funds must be asked first for a Senate request to be admissible, and its closing steps put the alumni ask first (row 19). The reading that holds is the procedural one: confirm enrolment, ask the Office of Student Affairs about alumni funds, then send the Senate request naming what alumni funds said. In parallel, settle RSNA membership and the departmental match before the October opening, and aim one radiology application at 1 February. Whether either of those pre-October steps was taken, the record does not say (row 13).

## The record to cite

The funding page asks every application to describe a record rather than an intention, and gives four counts as of September 2026: 802 CT records annotated and openly released, 13 medical students trained as annotators, one peer-reviewed paper on the education model in Cureus in 2026 (doi 10.7759/cureus.112661), and two permanent archives, a Zenodo DOI and a public dataset mirror. The onboarding page of 13 October names that release as CTSpinoPelvic1K v11 on Zenodo and Hugging Face. The 802 agrees with the dataset record the concept ledger settles for the project's own release, 802 CT volumes ([the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md), CTSpinoPelvic1K), and [chapter 7](07-ct-collections-their-labels-and-the-border-variants.md) holds the dataset. A grant's figure of 801 spines in one counting arm is a different count, distinct subjects within one evaluation, not a second size of the release, and the contradictions page refuted the conflict (row 25). The student count of 13 is a September snapshot that no later page updates (row 16); before citing it, count again.

The AIDaS seed grant belongs to the in-domain validation plan of [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md), not to outreach, but it matters here for two reasons. It asked 20,000 dollars for 2027 with a budget line of 4,800 dollars for 200 hours of student annotation support, the one place in the record where student work is paid from a grant. And its submission deadline of 16 October 2026 has passed while the draft still holds placeholders for the faculty investigator, the department and the budget lines; no page records whether it was submitted (row 17). What the grant would validate is the project's level-identification method, which [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md) holds with its results.

One cost the funding page does not cover: the onboarding page has each student pay for a Claude Pro or Max subscription, about 20 US dollars a month at Pro, from their own pocket (row 18). Of the sources above, only the GME seed grant pays for software, and it is the resident's application; no student award on the page names a subscription.

## The Celebrate Life soccer night

Both flyers announce one night at the Detroit City Fieldhouse on Saturday 24 October, with the same two contacts, the club's operations assistant and the resident as physician contact, the same registration form, the same event page and the same promise that professional soccer players from Southeast Michigan coach the children for the whole game. The clusters treat them as one registration and one night, and treat the later flyer as current; the earlier one's own export summary calls it the first Celebrate Life flyer.

The later flyer is a soccer night for every child under 18 living with scoliosis or a spine condition, whether bracing, under observation, or after surgery with the surgeon's agreement, from 5:00 to 7:00 PM, beside the Fieldhouse's Turf or Treat event, held indoors on two side-by-side fields, one for trick-or-treating and one for the game. Teams are picked so that everyone plays, families are welcome on the sideline, and costumes are encouraged, with the note that a brace fits under one. It adds a watch party afterwards for the club's men's professional team in the upstairs restaurant, and a facility rule that outside food and drink are not permitted, so families eat upstairs. Families sign up on the registration form, reached by the flyer's QR code, so that teams can be picked; this chapter does not reproduce the form's link, the contacts or the address.

The earlier flyer alone holds a different night on the same date. It addressed every paediatric haematology and oncology patient under 18, children in treatment for cancer and blood disorders, with a 6:45 PM kickoff and no end time, beside the club's Trunk or Treat, cars with decorated trunks in the Fieldhouse lot. Its sign-up promised to feed families as well as pick teams, and it printed the club's social account where the later flyer prints the Fieldhouse's. The contradictions page confirms each of these differences: the audience, the time, the companion event, the food and the social account (rows 0 to 4). The coaching, sideline and costume lines are copied word for word between the two, which shows one flyer was rewritten from the other and gives the coaching promise one source, not two (rows 5 to 8). Neither flyer names a player or a written commitment behind that promise, nor cites the venue's own listing of the companion event (rows 8 and 9).

Neither flyer states a year. 24 October 2026 is a Saturday, which fits, but the owner has not confirmed the year, and the contradictions page marks both dates cannot-tell (rows 10 and 11). The flyers name no funding source for the night, and the funding page never proposes one; the board's on-campus rule would not fit an off-campus venue, but no record says anyone tried (rows 26 and 27). The flyers print the resident under a general surgery affiliation, while the seed grant names a neurosurgery department marked to confirm; the record has not settled the departmental name (row 22).

## What we know

The canonical funding page orders 12 sources by speed of arrival and says for each what it pays for and who signs. The student applies for the student awards; the resident applies for the GME seed grant; an RSNA member, the resident or a faculty sponsor, applies for the RSNA education award; a faculty member must lead the AIDaS seed grant. The alumni ask comes before the Senate request. The record to cite is 802 released CT records, 13 trained student annotators, one Cureus paper and two archives, as of September 2026, and the 802 agrees with the settled dataset count. The later Celebrate Life flyer is current: a scoliosis and spine night for children under 18, 5:00 to 7:00 PM, beside Turf or Treat, with one registration form shared with the earlier oncology flyer.

## What we do not know

Whether the student's research year keeps them enrolled, which decides half the list; whether the institution's AOA slot is free; whether any outreach can be staged on campus, free and open to the public, as the activities board requires; whether a registered student organisation will host the programme, since OpenSpineConsortium is not one; whether a department will commit the RSNA match and whether RSNA offers students membership; whether any funder's September terms still hold; whether the AIDaS grant was submitted; the event's year, whether the registration page is live and who the coaches are; and the resident's departmental name as it should be printed.

## What would change our mind

A re-read of any funder's page showing a changed amount, deadline or rule replaces the September figure. A Registrar's answer that the research year is not enrolment removes every student award and leaves the resident's GME seed grant and the education awards. An AOA councilor's answer that the slot is used removes that grant until three years after the slot was last used. A newer flyer or the owner's word on the year, the audience or the time replaces the later flyer's details. A new student count, or a new release, changes the record to cite.
