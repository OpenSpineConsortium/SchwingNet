# 18 Metrics, intervals and witness frames

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter gives results and the rules for reporting them to the members of this private repository, names each method only by what it is for and what it achieved, and leaves how any stage works to Part III; nothing of it is copied anywhere before the provisional is filed.

A planner who designs the next experiment needs to know, before it runs, which number it will produce, in which stratum, with which interval, and by what path that number may reach the evaluation page, a manuscript or a slide. This chapter settles them from the September artifacts that set the reporting rules, the October records that re-ruled their numbers, and the repository's code and evaluation page, taking the plan's four questions in order. Where the code and the rules differ, it says so, because each difference is work owed before version 1's numbers are quoted.

## Which number answers which question

### Exact level and within one level

Every naming result in the record is printed twice: exact level, the share of named vertebrae whose name is the true level, and within one level, the share named at most one level off. The gap between them says how much of the error is a near miss. On the one real C-arm set, C-ArmLSpine, [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) gives within one level at 100% on whole lateral and AP frames, and, with one vertebra in the lateral field, 90.23% within one against 72.85% exact, a record, not a final value: the October records carry 74.8% exact with one body in view, and the [programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md) rule that the later figure holds.

The [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) explains why the gap opens at wide context. Rendering the 188 errors of its CT arm with the whole scan in view showed that 80% were whole-case shifts, every vertebra of a case one level cranial or caudal together: 151 errors in 24 of 234 validation cases; of the 17 shifting VerSe cases, 13 were spines whose field of view cut the column where the topmost level's identity is under-determined. Its conclusion: "At wide context the ceiling is enumeration, not local morphology, which is why within-one-level is the honest primary metric for a level-confirmation tool." The same shape recurs on film and on the C-arm. [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) found 83% of its errors to be exactly one level, a record, not a final value, since its banks predate the 22 September leak audit; and the [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) says that errors with three vertebrae or more in view are almost all one level.

So the answer has two parts. Within one level is the primary measure of what the reading can promise at wide context, printed with exact level and the share of errors that are whole-case shifts, since that share says which of the two carries the information. But within one level is not the success criterion of a clinical claim, since a one-level error is the wrong-level error the tool exists to prevent. The endpoint the October records fixed for that claim is "exact level on a transitional spine, person-clustered, with the strict all-sacrum-frames denominator printed beside any anchored number" ([Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)). The frozen paper-2 endpoint is stricter still: it scores a person wrong for a missed body, an unanchored frame or an abstention, not only for a wrong name, and on ordinary development frames it held on 57.0% ([State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/)). A planner therefore reports the endpoint as exact level per person with every way of failing counted, and reads within one level, and the error structure under it, to learn why the endpoint failed. Region is the coarser fallback: the ladder gives region and within one level at 99.7% each with one vertebra in view on 8,422 film vertebrae, records, not final values, since the [FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md) cannot tell whether that set is person-disjoint. The methods measured are [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)'s and [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)'s.

### Accuracy paired with coverage

A method that may decline to answer is reported as a pair, accuracy among the cases it answers and the share it answers, and as the whole risk-coverage curve rather than one operating point. [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) set that form on 16 September: 99% among answered at 35.7% coverage for the exact level (2,956 answered, lower bound 98.7%), 99% at 55.7% for a region, an area under the generalized risk-coverage curve of 0.0466, and conformal sets at a 1% error rate with a mean of 2.56 names and a single name on 33.6% of vertebrae at 99.2%. Every one of those figures is a record, not a final value: the [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) rules that they come from the leaked allspine v2 bank, and Research Directions marks the result "leaked bank; re-run needed". If the record is quoted at all, both granularities travel together, since 35.7% and 55.7% answer different questions.

What survives the leak is the form and the sizes a claim needs. The State of the Literature puts a 99% selective-accuracy claim at about 298 answered cases, the error-free count that puts an exact one-sided 95% bound at 99%, and a class-conditional 99% over 27 classes at 2,673, about 99 per class. The Honest 99% page adds that its curve was measured on renders and a deployed threshold needs real frames, "because conformal coverage has been measured collapsing from 90% to 7% under shift", and records the defect that matters most for reporting coverage: "The abstention rule declined on exactly the patients it protects", four sacralised patients. An overall coverage would not have shown it, so coverage is printed inside the stratum the abstention protects as well as overall. How the method declines is [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md)'s; the bench that measures it is a uniform random draw, since, by the same page, "a set chosen by the model's own uncertainty is a biased estimator that cannot be reused as a test set" ([chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md)).

### Detection: recall, AP, PCK and the convention under them

The [Vertebra Detector Field Report](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/) set the detector's metric conventions, which outlive its numbers. Recall "is not reported globally, by design": on partially labelled sources a missed vertebra may be one the dataset never annotated, so detections are scored only inside the trusted-background region and are neither credited nor penalised outside it. PCK is "normalised by each vertebra's own body height - never image diagonal", which would mean something different on a cervical crop than on a full-spine film. Results go per source and per acquisition, because "a pooled number would hide exactly the generalization behaviour worth measuring". The page's own cautions are rules too: a per-source gap resting on BUU's 15 test instances is "not yet a measurement", and an in-distribution AP@50 "is not evidence of cross-site generalization". Its figures, 0.957 AP@50 on 558 held-out instances among them, are records, not final values: the [detectors contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md) rule them superseded by a later model trained on a larger corpus, which [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md) holds.

A recall is a statement about a truth convention. On 22 September the OR Readiness Ledger re-scored one set of predictions on 287 vertebrae of 56 whole C-ArmLSpine frames three ways: 5.2% against vertebral-body boxes, 90.6% against whole-vertebra outlines, and 90.9% under a mutual-centre rule that needs no box convention at all. The control behaved as predicted, the same-device arm falling from 90.6% under body boxes to 40.8% under outlines and holding at 91.6% under the centre rule. The overnight 5.2% that had read as a failure of device transfer was a box convention. Since then a detection figure names its truth convention and is printed beside a convention-free score.

The held-out-device recall itself is not settled. The ledger's re-score gives 90.6%; the later results catalogue, which the repository's [demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) cites, gives 231 of 287 = 80.5% [74.1, 86.8], and 93.9% on a repaired denominator. [The results catalog](https://github.com/OpenSpineConsortium/spine-program/blob/main/01_carm_level_detection/docs/handoff/RESULTS_CATALOG.md) shows the two are one detector, the arm lodo_carm under outline truth at IoU 0.5, at two thresholds, 0.25 and 0.74, the cross-device operating point the catalog says was adopted as the headline after the 0.25 result was seen and marks threshold-dependent (rows A4, A6.1 [THR], A6.2), where three contradiction pages had ruled that the later figure supersedes and three that the record cannot tell. Neither is quoted here as current, since the catalog re-scores the 231 of 287 on rebuilt truth as 236 of 287 (A6.5). What is settled is the bar. Counting From The Sacrum explains why counting needs near-complete recall: "Recall compounds over eight to twelve bodies." If misses were independent, a recall of 90% per body would find all of eight bodies about 43% of the time and 97% about 78%; the demo plan records that no held-out cell meets the pre-stated 97% target, and the ledger lists counting-grade detection on a device held out entirely as "not supported".

The ledger adds two checks a planner applies to any anchor or detection figure. A fixed box placed where the sacrum usually sits hits the true sacrum on 85.7% of C-ArmLSpine test frames, 58.5% of COBRA's and 13.5% of DeepFluoro's, which the ledger puts down, with no measurement of surgeons behind it ([verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 71), to surgeons centring the field the same way every time; "sacrum found" on C-ArmLSpine is therefore partly a statement about layout, and a sacrum figure is printed beside that baseline. And DeepFluoro's test split carries 83 kept and 229 dropped out-of-field boxes, while older evaluations scored recall against all 312: two denominators were in circulation, so a figure names its denominator.

### Strata that are never pooled

Per source, per device and per view, numbers stay apart. Research Directions puts the device rule plainly: each C-arm is its own domain, "Pooled is never quoted without the device-held-out arm beside it, and six devices are needed for any two-sided P at or under 0.05." The arithmetic is a sign-flip test's: with k devices the smallest two-sided P is 2 x 0.5 to the power k, 0.125 at four, 0.0625 at five and 0.031 at six, which is why the State of the Literature says device-level claims cannot be tested with the four devices held. Within one device a paired comparison can be decided: held-out-device against same-device detection went 8 to 0 over 28 cases, Holm P 0.0078.

View is a stratum for the same reason. With no real frame of the device in training, lateral naming on C-ArmLSpine is 595 of 617 = 96.4% [92.3, 98.4] and AP naming 276 of 631 = 43.7%, a one-level cranial shift (demo plan); the 43.7% is [current in the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine), where the September 37.88% is superseded, a record, not a final value. Pooled over the two views the same runs would print 69.8%, a number that describes neither. Device shows the same: on COBRA laterals with detection given, the model fine-tuned on C-ArmLSpine scores 43.5% [36.4 to 50.1] at 28.1% coverage where the model that never saw C-ArmLSpine scores 79.9% [74.5 to 84.6] at 57.4%, 904 scored in each arm, and the ledger's sentence, "Fine-tuning the level model on one device costs it the others", is visible only in a per-device table.

The transitional stratum is the one the method most needs apart. [Counting Against Two Anchors](../../artifacts/counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu/) says why: "A model 97% accurate overall and 0% on transitional cases is the null result in costume, and only the split reveals it." The [retraining plan](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) makes the transitional stratum its own rows, never pooled, and [tools/evaluate.py](../../tools/evaluate.py) reports it apart; but its "all levels" row also counts the transitional vertebrae, so the overall row is read beside the transitional row, never alone. The same file has no view or device stratum yet, and needs both before it prints a C-arm number from more than one view or device.

Validation and test are strata of another kind: "validation is seen", by the handoff's rule, so a number on the split that chose the checkpoint is never a held-out result, and since the validation set grows with each shard, only test rows are comparable across versions (retraining plan).

## Intervals when frames are not independent

### What the record says

Counting From The Sacrum put the rule in one sentence: "Frames within a case are not independent; a naive interval covers 70.8% of the time, so every interval here is clustered at the case." It gives the case-bootstrap lower bound on the lateral whole-frame figure as 95.71%, and the ledger prints [95.7 to 100]. The figure under that interval, 98.22% over 141 cases on one page against 148 cases on the other, the results catalog marks SUPERSEDED (row A3.11), its current figure being 601 of 617, 97.4% [93.2, 99.0] (A3.1), a ruling the contradiction pages, which left the question to the catalog, have not yet re-ruled on, and the October 98.2% is another quantity, the count with its coupling (A5.1); with the [case count open in the ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-104-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-in-the-five-fold-cross-validation-behind-the-982-exact-level-figure), so the interval too is a record, not a final value. The 70.8% is stated by that one artifact, with no file or method named, and nothing in the record disputes it.

Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor clustered at the film. Its interior-window figures used a film-cluster bootstrap with 2,000 resamples: 91.8% [89.4, 94.1] on 455 ordinary films against 66.0% [39.6, 87.2] on 12 anomalous films, and, in a later bank, 49.9% [41.5, 58.9] on 128 anomalous films against 89.9% [87.1, 92.7], films the page puts in test and Research Directions in validation ([level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 25), all records, not final values. The same page says of its other tables that "Confidence intervals are on the vertebra and ignore clustering by film", and it found its earlier 20-film anomalous 87% "optimistic by six points" once 144 films were tested. Two lessons follow: the cluster is the unit, and twelve clusters give an interval 48 points wide, which is printed as it is.

The State of the Literature measured the clustering: it plans the transitional-against-ordinary comparison at 19 per arm at a design effect of 1 and 74 at a design effect of 4, "3.84 to 4.47 measured". Under a normal approximation a naive 95% interval covers 2 Phi(1.96 / sqrt(deff)) - 1 of the time, so 70.8% corresponds to a design effect of about 3.46, and the measured 3.84 to 4.47 to coverage of about 68% to 65%. The reported stack's own interval, whose method the demo plan does not name, agrees: 595 of 617 printed as [92.3, 98.4] is about twice as wide as a naive Wilson interval on 617 vertebrae, 94.7 to 97.6, which is the widening a design effect near 4.2 gives.

### A check by simulation

The plan owed a check of the 70.8% on the case structure; the fold record is on the grid, so it was run here on two structures that fit the record: 617 vertebrae in 141 cases (53 of five and 88 of four) or in 148 cases (25 of five and 123 of four), the record's 11 errors in 617 as the expected error rate, and a share of the errors arriving as whole-case shifts. Each setting ran 20,000 replicates; the case bootstrap ran on 2,000 of them with 1,000 resamples each.

Three findings follow, all Inferred on assumed structures. The 70.8% is reproduced when about 80% of the errors are whole-case shifts, the share the ladder measured on its CT validation, while scattered errors leave the naive interval at its nominal 95%: the 70.8% describes how that test's errors fell, not a constant. The case bootstrap the record adopted is right in kind but also short, 83% to 92% wherever some errors are whole-case shifts, because eleven errors in two to six cases leave resampling too few error-bearing cases to see the spread. A Wilson interval on an effective sample size, the vertebra count divided by a design effect of 4, covered 95% or more in every setting. So beside every clustered interval goes the number of cases that carry an error, and where that number is small the design-effect interval, or an interval over cases scored right or wrong, is the conservative statement, with the bootstrap beside it rather than instead.

### Small denominators and the gates

Where denominators are small, intervals are printed, not smoothed. Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor: "Transitional denominators are 9 to 45 vertebrae per cell. The intervals are wide, and printed rather than smoothed." The ladder's thoracic and sacral columns rest on 66 and 46 vertebrae, and it asks the reader to "read them as bands, not rankings". The evaluation page prints every fraction as correct over n with its interval, so a level at 15 of 15 reads 0.796 to 1.000, not perfect.

The pre-stated gates are Wilson lower bounds on persons. With no error the bound is n / (n + 3.84), so 35 persons all right give 90% and 22 carriers all right give 85% (Research Directions, State of the Literature); with one, two and five errors the handoff gives 53 and 34, 69 and 45, 113 and 74, rechecked there independently and again here, as are the 64.6% that seven persons all right give, the 0.0008 chance of 22 of 22 at a true recall of 72.4%, and the stop rule of at most one miss in seven, which passes 38% of the time at 72.4% and 72% at 85%. Superiority over the 97.6% comparator needs 152 error-free persons, or 708 for 80% power, which the records call unreachable, and "The comparator's own interval is unknown". Comparisons are pre-stated too: "Parity is a pre-stated non-inferiority claim with a 5-point margin. An interval that merely includes zero is not parity." The October coupled reading's 98.2% against the count's 97.0% is a gain of three cases, P 0.25 by Research Directions, which is what three discordant cases all going one way give (the method is [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)'s). The repository's rule says the same in code: a new measure beats the reported one only when its lower bound exceeds the old point estimate ([src/schwingnet/measure.py](../../src/schwingnet/measure.py)); and Counting Against Two Anchors asks for arms run "as a matched pair on the same folds", so that the difference is paired.

### What the repository's code does today

Version 1's interval is the evaluation gate's Wilson rule in [src/schwingnet/flow.py](../../src/schwingnet/flow.py), a correct score interval with contracts on its bounds, rechecked here against the page's numbers. But [tools/evaluate.py](../../tools/evaluate.py) computes it over vertebrae and only counts the persons beside each row, and no bootstrap, design effect or clustered interval exists anywhere in the repository's tools, src, tests, grid or scripts folders. The retraining plan asks for "Wilson intervals on person-keyed n", and the dry run's REFORMS item 7b (Kapoor 2023) is marked met by "a 95 percent Wilson interval on every row from its correct and n, persons counted per row", while item 7c, statistical tests and their assumptions, is missing and waits on the owner's answer ([the run's report](../../docs/research/v1-demo/runs/cd37124c487d/REPORT.md)). The interval the page prints today is therefore the naive one the September record warned about. The first dry run shows the size of the gap: its test row reads 556/556 = 1.000 (0.993 to 1.000) over five persons, while five persons all right give a Wilson interval of 0.566 to 1.000 on persons. A real run's truth lies between, at the design effect its errors have, so the clustered interval is owed before version 1's test row is quoted.

## From a result to a table or a slide

### The result row and the run record

Every number version 1 reports is a ResultRow ([docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md](../../docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md)): the measure, the stratum, correct and total, the persons behind them, the run id, the commit, the clean-tree flag and the catalog's sha256. A row from a dirty tree is refused, so is one with more correct than total, and since total and persons are each at least one, an empty stratum yields no row at all. That answers the [Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/)'s tally of 27 September, where stopping on an empty or defaulted input would have prevented 16 of 29 incidents, "an empty held-out set that flattered every result" among them.

The run's own record, RUN.json, is written before the first step: the run id (twelve hex digits of a hash of the commit, the catalog's sha256 and the start instant), the commit and clean-tree flag, the catalog's version and sha256, whether the split is disjoint, the stage and its settings, and each job's cost. A record from a dirty tree, on a split that is not disjoint, or whose run id is not that hash, is refused. The ledger row repeats the catalog's version and hash beside the cost and how the run was verified, appended by the record step, never by hand ([docs/research/v1-demo/LEDGER.md](../../docs/research/v1-demo/LEDGER.md)). So the stamp the plan asks for is carried as the catalog version in the record and the ledger and as its hash in every row.

The path from a run to a table is short and closed. The evaluate step reads the test part once, after training, and writes the rows; `tools/flow.py record` copies the run's small files into docs/research/v1-demo/runs/ with its ledger row; and `tools/eval_page.py write` writes the [evaluation page](../../docs/research/evaluation/README.md) from the recorded runs, which the required check holds, failing whenever the page is not what the runs give. The evaluate step logs those same rows to W&B, so the page and W&B carry one set of numbers. Each run also gets a report that lists the items of CLAIM 2024 (Tejani 2024) and REFORMS as met, declared or missing; the second dry run's is incomplete, 15 of 44 CLAIM items and 13 of 32 REFORMS items met and 48 missing, and a missing item is work to do before that run's numbers are published.

### The older chain to a manuscript

The Lab Recipe holds the chain the earlier programme's manuscripts compiled through: "a number enters the manuscript only through that chain", and "Nothing is typed by hand between the results file and the PDF." Each results file carries a `_run` block naming its job, commit, script, inputs and pre-registration, and refuses uncommitted changes unless they are explicitly allowed. `numbers_manifest.json` holds one entry per quoted number: the results file, an RFC 6901 JSON pointer, the format, such as `pct1` for a percentage to one decimal, the kind, a digit-only job id and the pre-registered item it answers. The tex carries `\RESULT{key}` and nothing else, in tables as well as prose; the macro tool's check refuses any pending key, unused key or bare numeral, a missing result prints a red pending marker with its job, and one reproduce target exits non-zero on MISSING or MOVED, since "A reproduction that cannot fail is not a reproduction." These are the number formats a manuscript may carry: a manifest key with its format, never a typed numeral.

The same page audited the chain that day and found it unfinished: 167 bare numerals in the SchwingNet tables, because the check scanned only the prose; 0 of 79 manifest keys carrying a job id; no `_run` block in the files the manifest points at; and no entry naming its pre-registered item. Its advice on checking a number completes the chain: "The recurring failure is a number that is wrong but plausible", so a number is checked outside its own computation, against published values, an independent implementation and the spread rather than the median.

### The catalogue's status words and what a slide may show

The handoff's results catalogue of 8 October holds every result found on disk that day, one row each, with its file, model, bank, split, truth, tool and job, a status word and the figure it feeds, and "Where any other file or memory note differs, the catalog wins." Its status words run from CURRENT and CURRENT-SEL (measured on the split that chose the checkpoint) to RECORD ONLY and OPEN, each row with the rigor marks of the 22 September audit, such as leaked, one seed and automatic truth. Three of its working rules are this chapter's: every number carries its file, and what no file records is written "not recorded"; every quoted score goes through the evaluation gate; and manuscript numbers enter through manifest keys, never typed. Its check of evaluation against selection and lineage, CHK-9, covered 563 rows, and the evaluation-gate tests passed 58 on 8 October. The method it tells every new analysis to copy is the freeze: "write the rule and its success criterion, hash the document, stamp every output row with the hash, and treat any number produced before the freeze as calibration".

A slide is held to the same chain: the project's goal ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md)) requires that every number the talk shows is reproduced by a command from a record in this repository, and the talk shows what SchwingNet does, not how. Latency has rules of its own, [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md)'s, and comparators are [chapter 19](19-what-a-valid-comparison-needs.md)'s, with Counting From The Sacrum's reason for printing the failing stage beside the succeeding one: "Reporting both is what makes the first one credible."

## Witness frames

### What they are

The witness frames are fixed real frames scored and drawn at every checkpoint, from the untrained network at epoch 0 to the last, so that the improvement is "a sequence of pictures a person reads rather than a curve they trust" ([src/schwingnet/witness.py](../../src/schwingnet/witness.py)). They answer the owner's ask, in the retraining plan, that evaluation be exposed "from start all the way to perfection on a real image". There are two sets. The validation set is 24 C-ArmLSpine frames of validation persons across L1 to L5, drawn by the trainer at every epoch. The test set is 24 frames of test persons, plus two frames of each of the three L6 cases and eight evenly spaced frames of each of the two demo sweeps, drawn by the evaluator over every kept epoch's weights at the version boundary ([docs/memory/evaluation-and-wandb.md](../../docs/memory/evaluation-and-wandb.md)). Each frame is drawn whole with every body boxed, green where the name is right and red with the truth and the prediction where it is wrong, as one strip over the epochs and an animation, with a sheet of every frame at the first and last epoch ([tools/witness.py](../../tools/witness.py)).

### How they are chosen and frozen

Each frame is ranked by a salted hash of its bytes' sha256, so the choice depends on no order of reading; the lumbar levels are taken in turn, and within a level the person shown least so far, so the set spans levels and persons rather than repeating the person with the most frames; a sweep is sampled evenly from its first frame to its last; no frame holds two roles; and a missing kind is named, since "Nothing missing is silent". The first run on real frames is to be recorded with `tools/flow.py record --freeze`, which writes `data/witness/witness.json`; every later run draws exactly those frames or is refused, and tools/witness.py refuses a catalog that has lost a frozen frame or holds it in another part, "or the page compares nothing". On 24 October no frozen list exists: data/witness/ is absent, and the dry run's list was chosen from its own catalog, not frozen. One sweep, the retraining plan notes, has no export with level names yet.

### What they choose, and what they must not

The witness frames choose nothing. The checkpoint is chosen on the whole validation part, never on epoch 0, and the test frames are read only at the version boundary, because selection on test persons counts as seeing them. The dry run's record bears this out: its model was taken at the epoch of best validation accuracy, epoch 4, 0.996 (0.985 to 0.999, n=472), with no test row read in training. Here the memory note overrides the older plan. The retraining plan of 20 October had the 24 validation witness frames choose the checkpoint; the memory note and the evaluation page of 21 October replaced that with the whole validation part. What the plan alone still holds is the set's rationale and its later rows: a film row per region, which joins the test set only once the lineage check clears its persons.

One tension is open. Research Directions bars scoring any test person in the transitional work until the paper's "H1 seen" entry exists, while the pipeline draws the test witness frames at every version boundary, and the programme contradictions page cannot tell whether the two conflict. Until the owner rules, a planner treats each test witness read as a look at test persons and records it in the ledger like any other.

| 4 | 0.0149 | 470/472 = 0.996 | 93/93 = 1.000 |

Between epochs 1 and 2 the training loss fell from 0.2775 to 0.0217 while validation fell from 0.879 to 0.394, and the strips show which bodies broke; at epoch 4 every witness body is named, and the test witness frames, read once at the boundary, go from 1 of 91 test bodies, 0 of 28 transitional and 0 of 61 sweep at epoch 0 to all of them at epoch 4. As the run's note puts it, "the curve alone would hide which frames broke, the strips show it."

Two limits are this chapter's judgment. A witness set is a picture, not an estimate: its frames are chosen to span levels and persons, not drawn at random over cases, so its fractions are not the test accuracy and its intervals, 93/93 printed as 0.960 to 1.000, describe the frames and claim nothing about the population. And the test strips are only as independent as the test persons. The dry run had five test persons, two of them the sweep cases, and the page's strips show its 24 general test witness frames all coming from the three L6 persons and the test and transitional strips showed the same three people. The real test list decides how much the strips can show.

### Where they are seen

The frames, the table and the curves go to W&B, project SchwingNet, online where the grid's key is found by name and offline under the run's folder otherwise, pushed by the flow from the login node as soon as each step ends; the sync file and the flow's last line say "W&B NOT PUSHED" with the reason, and logging never stops a run ([tools/wandb_log.py](../../tools/wandb_log.py)). The retraining plan's default, offline on the compute node and synced from the owner's own computer, was superseded by the owner's words of 21 October that the grid holds a key and runs are pushed there ([docs/research/v1-demo/README.md](../../docs/research/v1-demo/README.md)). The dry run was written offline only and not pushed. The page is read on GitHub by the repository's members and never put on Pages, "since Pages on a private repository may be public on this plan".

## Which versions hold

None of the eleven artifacts read here is marked superseded in [the clusters](../../artifacts/_synthesis/clusters.md), but several of their figures are, and two pairs are one source told twice. Counting From The Sacrum and the OR Readiness Ledger carry one C-ArmLSpine results table, one measurement by the [verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md): the first alone holds the two-decimal figures and the 70.8% claim, the ledger alone the re-score, the position prior, the denominators and the device tables. The handoff and Research Directions share their naming and transitional lines, so a figure in both is one source, not a confirmation.

The October records re-ruled the September headlines, which are now records, not final values: 74.8% over 72.85% with one vertebra in view, 96.4% over 96.27% for lateral naming with no real frame of the device in training and 43.7% over 37.88% for AP, while whole-frame naming is 97.4% on the results catalog's word, the September 98.22% superseded and its case count left open by the ledger, and held-out-device recall, 80.5% at 0.74 and 90.6% at 0.25 from one detector, stays a record while a re-score moves it. The selective-prediction figures come from a leaked bank; Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor's figures are records because its banks predate the leak audit and its anomalous films' partition is disputed; the field report's detector figures are superseded, and the retraining plan's checkpoint choice by witness frames and its W&B default are superseded by the memory note and the evaluation page.

## What we know

A naming result is reported as exact level and within one level together, with the share of errors that are whole-case shifts; the clinical endpoint is exact level per person on a transitional spine, every miss, unanchored frame and abstention counted wrong. A method that declines is reported as accuracy with coverage and its whole curve, coverage also inside the stratum the abstention protects. A detection figure names its truth convention, denominator and trusted region, beside a convention-free score and, for an anchor, a position-prior baseline. Source, device, view and the transitional stratum are never pooled, and a device claim needs six devices. Intervals are clustered at the case or the film, small denominators are printed as they are, and the gates are Wilson bounds on persons whose arithmetic holds. Every version 1 number is a ResultRow stamped with its run id, commit, clean tree and catalog hash, on a page the required check holds; manuscripts take numbers only through manifest keys. The witness frames are chosen by a reproducible rule, drawn from the untrained network on, and choose nothing.

## What we do not know

The C-ArmLSpine fold record's case structure is on the grid, so the 70.8% was checked here only on structures that fit the record, and it is not known whether the artifact computed it on the real one. The superseded whole-frame figure's case count, the held-out-device recall on rebuilt truth and the person-disjointness of the 8,422-vertebra film set are unsettled in the record. The selective-prediction figures have not been re-run on the clean split, and no calibration on real frames exists. The interval version 1 will print is unsettled too: the code gives a per-vertebra Wilson interval, the plan asks for person-keyed n, and no clustered interval exists in the repository. No frozen witness list exists yet, one sweep has no export with level names, and whether a test witness read at a version boundary counts as the "H1 seen" entry has not been ruled.

## What would change our mind

If the fold record shows the C-ArmLSpine errors scattered across cases rather than shifted whole, the naive interval was nearly right for that figure and the 70.8% belongs to another test; if it shows them all shifted, the case, not the vertebra, is the unit, and an interval over cases becomes the primary one. If the results catalogue's fold record settles the case count and the whole-frame figure, the ledger's open rows close and the interval of the surviving figure can be quoted. A re-run of selective prediction on the clean split would replace the 35.7% and 55.7% records with figures that can be quoted. If the evaluate step gains a person- or case-clustered interval, the per-vertebra Wilson interval stops being the reported one, and REFORMS 7b and 7c can be answered honestly. A frozen witness list drawn from real test persons who are not all transitional cases would make the test strips an independent picture; and a held-out set with six or more devices would turn the device rule from a caution into a test.
