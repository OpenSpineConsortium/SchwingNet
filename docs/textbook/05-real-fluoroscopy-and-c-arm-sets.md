# 05 Real fluoroscopy and C-arm sets: held, requested and gated

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is written for the project's members, stays in this private repository until the provisional is filed on 30 October 2026, and names a method only by what it is for, leaving how any method works to Part III.

A planner who wants to test on real C-arm images has four questions to settle: which real intraoperative or C-arm sets are already on the grid, and with what level truth, geometry and licence; which other sets can be had only by a letter, a form, a fee or a signed agreement, and where each request stands; which missing sets would add the most; and how much is known about who is in C-ArmLSpine, the one held human intraoperative set with a volume. This chapter answers them from the canonical data records, says where they disagree and which reading holds, if any yet does, and ends with the source the project means to build, the retrospective collection of archived C-arm images at the Detroit Medical Center, whose study is Part IV's [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md). It quotes no result obtained on these sets; results are in chapters [17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md) to [20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md).

## Which record holds what

The cluster "Datasets, holdings and licences" has four canonical artifacts. The [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) of 17 October is the catalog, with the texts of sixteen request letters and a ranked list of ten gaps. The [SchwingNet Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) of 20 October gives each source a reach word (held, get, gated, embargoed, none or excluded) and the part it plays; the generated page [docs/research/datasets-inventory.md](../../docs/research/datasets-inventory.md) renders the same records with changes up to 23 October. [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) of 8 October says what the published work holds and the project lacks. The [imaging data holding report](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/) cards the 212-source manifest and is read in [chapter 04](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md).

None of the four is the record of whether a request went out. [The datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) settle this in rows 33 and 61: request state is read from the records of spine-datasets, the private repository where the unheld sets are sought with collaborators, as they stood at commit 7a74bb6 on 21 October, and the atlas serves only for letter texts and catalog entries. The [SchwingNet grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/) of 21 October counts those records, and two repository notes, [docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md) and [docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md](../../docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md), carry the state request by request.

The catalogs count different things on different dates, so each count is named with its catalog and date (row 32). The atlas holds 217 datasets, 189 from the project's records and 28 found only on the web; on 17 October 52 were held, 16 were marked requested with a letter drafted and 149 were not held. By kind, 34 are fluoroscopy frame sets (2 held), 21 fluoroscopy video (8 held), 17 CBCT projection sets (2 held) and 65 pairs of a CT with a real X-ray (9 held); levels are named on 36 and pose is known on 63. The registry's 60 records of 20 October are a record, not a final value ([the registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/)): the pipeline's 111 of 21 October are the newest count of the same records. The inventory files the fluoroscopy side in three tables: real intraoperative stills (11 sources, 5 held, the FluoroCap literature panels of [chapter 08](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md) among them), video and cine (25, 3 held, 1 gated) and real projections with a registered CT (5 rows, 4 held, three of them COBRA2026's and one the femur set below; 1 embargoed).

The [Spine X-ray Data Handoff](../../artifacts/spine-x-ray-data-handoff-bddbdd/) of 3 September, superseded by the holding report and read for plain films in [chapter 06](06-plain-film-corpora.md), calls CM Spine "the only public intraoperative source", a claim no survey stands behind and this chapter does not carry (row 54): CM Spine is 208 intraoperative G-arm images the project does not hold, and the held open intraoperative set is C-ArmLSpine.

### C-ArmLSpine and its two sweeps

C-ArmLSpine (Zhang 2024) holds 149 acquisition sessions on one mobile C-arm, the FIRST/Yiying Surgio-B51, each with spot frames and a cone-beam CT from the same device and session: 299 spot frames, 149 CBCT volumes of 58,706 slices covering about T12/L1 to S1, and two raw CBCT sweeps. It is released on Zenodo (record 22768651) as one zip of 15,581,614,253 bytes with no registration and no data use agreement, under CC BY 4.0, and unpacks to 59,609 files and 23.3 GB. The registry's 86.834 GB is the whole C-ArmLSpine folder on the grid, which also holds the project's derived banks and level and pose files, and no page breaks it down (row 3; collision 4 of [the contradictions record](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md), read again as a difference of scope); a re-acquisition plan carries the atlas's release sizes.

The release ships no labels and no poses. The project derived level names on each CBCT with two published automatic labellers, TotalSegmentator and SPINEPS/VERIDAH, and kept them only where both agree, which they do on 814 of 910 vertebrae; the atlas records T12 as named 10 percent of the time and T11 never. The registry's "two labellers" were never people (row 5): no person has read a level on C-ArmLSpine. Three cases carry an L6, each called by one automatic labeller alone; none carries a T13. Because the CBCT is lumbar only, no whole-column count can check the names ("no count", in [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)). The names reach the spot frames through the project's own registration ([chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md)). A planner should treat this truth as automatic, lumbar and unreviewed.

The release has reading traps: a series UID that changes with every slice, a placeholder PixelSpacing of 1.0, no FrameOfReferenceUID, case134's frames stored inside its CBCT folder, and case118, a -58 degree oblique. One case's DICOM headers carry a real patient identifier, which is never printed.

The two sweeps, case134 and case39, hold 602 raw projections in all. The registry counts 301 XA frames in each, and the atlas's 301-item bank of case134 agrees (row 4); Research directions gives the same 602 but a 302-frame spin for case134, a disagreement the contradictions record confirms (collision 3). The collision's hint carries 301, which matches the bank and the total, but a confirmed contradiction leaves both values open until a census of the sweep by DICOM modality and rows. The sweeps are the only confirmed consecutive-frame sequences with a paired volume, but their motion is a rotational arc at known gantry angles, not a translation along the spine, a qualifier row 38 asks to travel with the claim. Case39 has no export yet. They are the recorded sweep for the talk ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md)), and the conditions on any latency shown with them are in [docs/memory/latency-and-the-recorded-sweep.md](../../docs/memory/latency-and-the-recorded-sweep.md) and chapter 20; the registry names the whole set "the test set and the demo".

### What is known and unknown about C-ArmLSpine's provenance

The publication, device, open release and licence are known; who is in the set is not. State of the literature gives 149 cases, 148 cases or 120 distinct subjects, and the atlas calls them 149 acquisition sessions "not provably one person each". The contradictions page cannot tell how many people stand behind them (row 2): the case counts agree once scoped, the 148 being the project's bank before a repair. The 120 has two readings, the distinct subjects [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) counts by the split's own keying and, in the atlas, the cases one render bank covers; [the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md) carry "149 cases, 120 distinct subjects by the split keying, person count unverified" (row 2). Whether the subjects were living is not stated in anything the project has read, nor is the ethics approval for the collection; State of the literature lists all three as open. A person-level key from the release, its authors or the dataset's ethics record would settle the count, and the paper's ethics statement, read on the page, the other two. Until then a planner counts C-ArmLSpine in sessions, not people. The cases scored in the project's cross-validation, 148 in one artifact and 141 in another, are a different count that the ledger leaves open ([rows 0 and 104](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-104-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-in-the-five-fold-cross-validation-behind-the-982-exact-level-figure)); the fold files decide it ([chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)). The open question is carried in [chapter 27](27-open-questions.md).

### DeepFluoro and its three copies

DeepFluoro (Grupp 2020) is six cadaveric lower-torso specimens imaged on a Siemens Cios Fusion, each with one CT. Its H5 release holds 366 registered frames at 1536 by 1536 pixels and 0.194 mm, each with its ground-truth pose. The vertebrae are one merged class, so it carries no per-vertebra truth of its own, and the field is centred on the pelvis; the atlas calls it the reference for projection geometry, not for level identity. 160 of the 366 frames are stored upside down. The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) warns of two denominators in circulation: the test split carries 83 kept and 229 dropped (out-of-field) vertebra boxes, while older evaluations scored against all 312.

The vendor deposit adds 2,659 DICOM frames and 19 CT scans, 72.7 GB unpacked. Of its frames, 2,293 have no released pose, some carry the FTRAC fiducial and could be posed, and no written criterion excludes them; both statements are carried (row 30). Its PatientID is wrong, so it is keyed by specimen directory. A third copy of the same specimens sits in a Hugging Face collection, so a split keys by specimen across all three copies. The licence reads CC BY-NC 4.0 in the H5 licence file and CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 in its datasheet, and the deposit's listing and README disagree the same way; it is non-commercial on either reading, and which text is right is not settled.

### COBRA2026

COBRA2026 (Thummerer 2026) is the challenge set of kV CBCT projections taken around living radiotherapy patients, released on Zenodo (record 21322350) under CC BY-NC 4.0. It holds 692 public training cases of 867 from six centres on two vendors' machines, 467 Elekta and 225 Varian, with 375,618 projections; each case ships per-projection geometry and a deformably registered planning CT, and no anatomical label. The other 175 cases (52 validation, 123 test) are embargoed until 1 March 2032 (row 74). The field is the pelvis with part of the lumbar spine and sacrum, and whether L5 and the sacral ala are in it is unverified. It is radiotherapy CBCT, not fluoroscopy, though recorded by the same physics, as the [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) puts it.

Its size goes with its method (row 0): 771.1 GB at the fetch's verification and 754 GB by du on 25 September (atlas), 808.615 GB for its folder on the grid (registry) and 809 GB of apparent bytes (the holding report's census). Read again, these are different measures, not a contradiction (collision 7); 808.615 GB is 753.1 GiB, which du rounds up to 754, so two of them may be one size in two units. The Elekta cases in a frame bank are 145 in the registry, the [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) and the atlas's own notes, but 150 exports in the atlas's annotation line (row 1); the ledger leaves both open ([row 129](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-129-number-of-cobra2026-casesexports)), so neither is quoted as current, and the COBRA labelling manifest, listing exports by case, would settle it. No Varian case has been exported or looked at, though their geometry was solved on 23 September. The same two automatic labellers, run on the planning CTs, agree on 818 of 900 vertebrae, with 5 cases carrying an L6 call and 13 a T13 (OR Readiness Ledger), and a border-variant screen of the 692 planning CTs gave 91 calls nobody has read (atlas).

### Geneva, Ljubljana and SpineSAC

The Geneva hip gold standard (D'Isidoro 2021) is one resin phantom in 116 frames over 19 views on a Philips BV Pulsera image intensifier, with gold rigid transforms and the projection matrix and no vertebra labels; whether the phantom holds a lumbar column is unverified. The registry gives it the part of "the unseen-device test". Its licence is CC BY-SA 4.0 on the repository and CC BY-NC 4.0 in the paper.

The Ljubljana 2D-3D gold standard (Tomazevic 2002) is one cadaveric L1 to L5 segment in 18 radiographs taken on a turntable with a Trixell PIXIUM 4600 flat panel, not a C-arm, with fiducial gold-standard poses for each vertebra and an 8-bit CT of unknown window. It is the one held set with per-vertebra truth by construction, but it is one specimen, under CC BY-NC-ND with a signed agreement also cited, and sits in no split or bank: its parameters have been read and no truth bank built.

SpineSAC holds 3 sawbone phantoms in 59 scenarios: 637 frames inside 5,490 held files of 4.15 GB, from five or six C-arms of five makers, with projection matrices (row 31). It has no vertebra truth, no paper, no stated data licence, and processed files that reuse a template DICOM header. No frame has been scored, and the registry marks it excluded; [chapter 24](24-decision-log-data-licences-and-splits.md) holds the decision.

### What the holding does not cover

The Any-View Spine Plan finds every paired spine set in its table, held or not, lumbar or lumbosacral but one, and the registry records that no public set of real cervical or thoracic C-arm frames with named levels exists. The exception is a digital-twin cadaver (arXiv 2412.08020), 80 Loop-X frames from 46 viewpoints with four CTs from mid-femur to T2, which the plan lists as a fetch word, not a holding; outside its table, the request-only LevelCheck radiographs and UCLA kV pairs reach the thoracic spine. The IPCAI 2025 set (Flepp 2025), five cadaveric distal femora with 50 calibrated X-rays and five CTs under a research-only licence (inventory), was fetched on 21 October; once its page and paper showed it to be the femur, its entries in the training registry, the fetch chain and the bank recipes were removed on 23 October, and its source record stays only as a registration benchmark, which settles the notes that still call it "pelvis or spine, to confirm".

## Video and cine

The held video is small and mostly unlabelled. The registry's public clips on the grid, twenty folders and 262.801 GB in all, include four Bern myelography videos (Piechowiak 2021), three PMC supplementary clips, three Wikimedia Commons items and YouTube re-encodes of 28 videos. The Bern videos are four pulsed runs at 7.5 frames a second with the detector moving cranially, which the registry calls "the only overlapping travelling frames found" and the atlas the closest public case, beside a Wikimedia barium-swallow clip whose field also pans down the spine at an unknown step (row 38). Their captions name T7/8, T9/10 and L5/S1; they are CC BY 4.0, but re-encoded and cropped, with frame timing and geometry lost, and their native DICOM is request 3. The YouTube copies have no permission until a request is answered and are kept for private screening only (chapter 24). The Bournemouth cervical corner tracks (BORDaR 385) are 252 flexion and extension sequences from 63 people at 15 frames a second, with four-corner templates named C0 to T1 on every frame and no pixels, under CC BY 4.0; the tracks are on the grid, but the record's reach stays "get", since their cine is request 1. The DysphagiaBank UNM corpus, 87 files and 19 GB of swallow studies, is held under CC BY-NC-SA after the owner's registration (the registry of 20 October still says get, row 37); its terms allow programmatic processing only and bar any language-model use of its text, and it has never been scored.

The cine that carries level truth on its frames is not held. The Maastricht cervical cine (van Santbrink 2026) is 112 lateral recordings at 15 frames a second with C0 to C7 contours on every frame, on request under a Dutch WMO and GDPR transfer agreement (request 2). The Bournemouth lumbar cine (Breen 2021), 127 subjects from L2 to S1, has only per-frame angles in public, and its level truth depends on whether corner templates exist as they do for the cervical set; it is asked for in request 1. The Pittsburgh swallow corpus holds about 1,500 lateral clips of 265 patients and 70 volunteers, with named corners on C2 and C4 only; the ledger marks its 1,518 (Zhang 2021) and 1,488 (Li 2024) current as restatements of one number ([row 126](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-126-number-of-pittsburgh-videofluoroscopic-swallow-clips)), and a University of Pittsburgh agreement is expected (request 4).

The other letters ask clinics, vendors and teaching sites for the originals of videos seen online: twelve Seattle Science Foundation teaching videos (request 5), two native kyphoplasty series from Mayfield with a birth date burned in (6), DMX motion X-ray (7), Radiopaedia's spine cases (8), The Procedure Guide's clips (9, by contact form), Konica Minolta's dynamic radiography, which is not fluoroscopy (10), the original of a Jefferson stimulation video whose article file is already held among the Neurosurgical Focus videos (11), a Manual Therapy Foundation of India lumbar coupling video (12), a pain practice's clips (13) and a DARADIA vertebroplasty video (14). The held Neurosurgical Focus videos are CC BY 4.0 under a publisher's footer reserving text-and-data-mining rights, and the one article checked shows saved stills, not cine.

## Sets that need a letter, a form, a fee or an agreement

### The sixteen letters and where they stand

The atlas lists sixteen sources that can be had only by asking: the fourteen video and cine requests of 26 September, the Balgrist letter of 21 September and the SX-Stitch letter of September. It says each letter is ready and that eleven also exist as unsent Outlook drafts, .eml files in a folder it names; on 20 October the access note found them neither in git nor on the owner's Desktop, and the requests note finds no such folder in git, so the claim is not confirmed. The current rule ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)) sends dataset letters from the owner's Wayne State Outlook on his word, short and to verified addresses; he signs any data use agreement, and no external text says or implies that a model, a neural network, machine learning, AI or training is built or used. The requests note rewrote the September SX-Stitch and Balgrist drafts, which used words the rule now excludes, keeping their facts (its sections 13 and 14).

By 21 October four of the sixteen had moved. The Balgrist letter was sent on 20 October and refused on 21 October: the ex-vivo set has been licensed to a startup, is proprietary and cannot be shared, so its reach is now none. The Bournemouth letter (request 1) was forwarded to a further address on 21 October, and request 13 was sent the same day. For Radiopaedia (request 8) the owner signed the non-commercial machine-learning licence and paid its fee on 21 October, USD 250 in the inventory's record, which supersedes the atlas's note of 17 October that the fee could not be confirmed; it waits on Radiopaedia's countersignature, and the cases are never fetched by script (rows 35 and 61). SchwingNet's own records still mark the other video letters and the SX-Stitch letter drafted and never sent. In spine-datasets, the record of request state, the 86 records stood at 52 to do, 26 requested, 5 granted, 1 fetching, 1 fetched and 1 refused on 21 October; which of the sixteen are among the 26 cannot be read from this repository, and the status fields of the fourteen video sources there would settle it ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 51).

SX-Stitch (Li 2024) is the only published collection of overlapping intraoperative C-arm frames of the spine (row 38): 1,032 images in consecutive series overlapping by 20 to 90 percent, with vertebra masks; [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md) says what such series are worth. Its letter was drafted in September (the 19th in the registry, the 23rd in Research directions; row 34 carries "drafted in September 2026, not sent"), rewritten as section 13 of the requests note, and not sent. The paper's data statement says no datasets were generated, so success is judged unlikely, and its patient count is open in State of the literature.

### The intraoperative cohorts the literature names

State of the literature states the gap: what matters most and is missing is labelled real intraoperative frames from living patients, and every verified set of that kind the literature names is request-only or unreleased. The requests note drafts a letter for each of six such sets (its sections 15a to 15f). The LevelCheck clinical cohort (De Silva 2016; Ketcha 2017; Manbachi 2018) is the largest: 178 patients with a preoperative CT and 398 intraoperative radiographs, with a 62-image reader set that [docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md](../../docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md) counts among the 398 as its difficult cases, and 24 more patients with 61 radiographs, under a data use agreement only. It is the one of the six recorded here as asked: on 21 October the cohort's former senior author answered that it is held at Johns Hopkins and that sharing was never arranged, a follow-up went the same day to a Johns Hopkins faculty member, and the pipeline records no reply yet.

The other five are drafted and, in this repository's notes, not sent: Vanderbilt (Chan 2025), 146 living patients with 336 AP pelvic frames whose landmarks include midline L5/S1; Huashan (Shao 2024), 140 living patients with a preoperative CT and intraoperative AP and lateral lumbar films; PUMCH (Liu 2026), 43 scoliosis patients with four to six overlapping AP exposures each and no labels; NIT Warangal and NIMS Hyderabad, nine living patients whose level-named CT centroids and 8-bit overlays beside renders are public, an open download the atlas records as unverified; and CM Spine, whose Google Drive file downloaded empty and has never been identified. The note's order of value is LevelCheck, Huashan, the Balgrist form (since refused), Vanderbilt, NIT Warangal, SX-Stitch with PUMCH, then CM Spine once a source exists.

The atlas lists further real spine projections out of reach for now: a vendor's 632,385 unreleased intraoperative X-rays (about 124,000 of the spine, none labelled); the thoracolumbar CBCT sweeps of 55 living patients, 190 to 390 projections a volume, never released (Jaganathan 2023); and 3,184 stereoscopic kV images of 429 spine radiotherapy patients, by request under IRB approval and six institutional data-sharing agreements (Petragallo 2023). One small open source is not held: TCIA's CMB-MML and CMB-AML collections carry 35 interventional frames of 16 living patients, only one a spine procedure, with same-patient CT and DICOM geometry.

### Who acts on each kind of step

The access note sorts every step by who may take it. A letter is drafted by a session and goes out once the owner approves that one letter, since sent mail cannot be recalled, now from his Wayne State Outlook rather than the note's Gmail. A web form with no signature is given to the owner to paste. An account, a click-through or signed agreement, a credentialing attestation or a fee is the owner's own hand, because an agreement binds the person who accepts it. A plain download is a grid job, run only on the owner's word and number. The atlas's access column gives 78 of the 217 as open downloads, 53 as not available, 40 as by request and 21 with no route, the other 25 behind a registration, a form, a fee or an agreement. For the sets in this chapter the agreements expected are a UK data-sharing agreement (Bournemouth), a GDPR transfer agreement (Maastricht), a Swiss transfer agreement (Bern's DICOM), a University of Pittsburgh agreement (the swallow corpus) and a data use agreement (LevelCheck); the one fee is Radiopaedia's, and the one embargo COBRA's.

## The ten gaps worth getting next

The atlas ranks ten gaps "by value to CT-to-C-arm parity": by how directly a set pairs a CT with a real X-ray of the same body, best with known pose (chapter 13 holds what that parity is for). The list as it stood on 17 October, with what has changed since:

1. COVID-19-NY-SBU: the project holds a CT subset of this TCIA collection, and 439 of its patients have both a CT and CR or DX radiographs, mostly chest, so fetching the radiographs gives same-patient pairs with no request, under CC BY 4.0.
2. LUMOS (Shi 2025): 1,620 lumbar AP and lateral films with 280 lumbar CTs from 803 living patients, 46.4 GB on Zenodo; which film goes with which CT is not stated and a pairing by id is unverified, and the films are diagnostic radiographs with no pose and no level labels. Its CC BY-NC 4.0, in the atlas and the Any-View Spine Plan, is contradicted by no record but read on its page by none: the registry records no licence, and the inventory and the access note leave it to the page. Never fetched as of 21 October, it is queued in the fetch chain ([docs/memory/the-xray-fetch-chain.md](../../docs/memory/the-xray-fetch-chain.md)).
3. MIDRC Open Data: 10,100 cases with both a CT and a radiograph, about 300 of them spine studies, behind free registration and a click-through agreement, with no pose.
4. Balgrist ex-vivo (Jecklin 2024): whole-torso cadavers on a Siemens Cios Spin and two Ziehm units with fiducial poses, "the best fit that exists" in the atlas's words. The letter counts 13 specimens plus six for validation, the follow-up paper six with at least 50 X-rays each; since the request was refused on 21 October, the count no longer matters to a plan.
5. UCLA kV/MV (Charters 2024): 254 living patients with 2,486 paired orthogonal kV images of the thoraco-abdominal spine registered to the planning CT, by request only.
6. PENGWIN Task 2: 50,000 unwindowed renders in C-arm geometry with simulated hardware and sacrum masks, CC BY 4.0 and 35 GB; renders, not real frames, of CTs the project already holds and could render itself.
7. SyntheX: simulated hip images matched in geometry to real C-arm frames from two systems, CC BY-NC 4.0; whether such a matched set is already held is unknown.
8. SPARE (Shieh 2019): CC BY-NC 4.0 and 291 GB; one sweep reads it as real Elekta and Varian kV projections with geometry and a prior 4D-CT, and its contents are unresolved between two sweeps.
9. LIDC-IDRI: 290 patients with a CT and a chest radiograph, open on TCIA.
10. The femur multi-view set (Flepp 2025), taken up above: a registration benchmark only.

None of the ten ships a level name; six pair a CT with diagnostic or radiotherapy images rather than intraoperative frames, two are chiefly renders and one is a femur; and the one entry with real C-arm spine frames and pose on more than one device, Balgrist, is refused. Real level truth on intraoperative frames has to come from the literature cohorts above or from the project's own collection below.

## The retrospective collection at the Detroit Medical Center

### What the protocol of record asks

The source the project means to build for itself is set out in the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), canonical in the OR-study cluster: a draft eProtocol for Wayne State's IRB, not yet submitted. It would collect the archived intraoperative C-arm stills and cine loops, taken on the hospital's own devices, of one neurosurgeon's spine operations at the Detroit Medical Center from 2021 to 2026 (the window's ends still to confirm), with the operated level taken from the operative note and, where present, post-operative imaging. It asks for Expedited review under Category 5 because the data may support a future FDA submission, which in the protocol's words is not eligible for exemption, and completes the Exempt Category 4 paragraphs only as a fallback; [the OR-study contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md) carry Category 5 (rows 17 and 18) and note that the stated reason cites no regulation. It asks for waivers of consent and of HIPAA authorization and a DMC ancillary review. It has no funding at submission: the [AIDaS Seed Grant Proposal](../../artifacts/aidas-seed-grant-proposal/) is the planned application, to be amended in if awarded (row 26). A protocol of its own is needed because the determination HPR 2026-269 covers public de-identified data only (row 28) and its supplement HPR 2026-330 covers VinDr-SpineXR ([docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md](../../docs/memory/vindr-is-covered-by-hpr-2026-330.md)); archived hospital images are neither.

The protocol sizes the collection by its primary endpoint at about 1,100 labelled vertebral bodies in the held-out set (row 22); how many cases that takes waits on a pilot PACS query of 20 cases, and neither earlier yield estimate is settled (row 24). What leaves the hospital is coded images only: identifiers come off at export on a DMC workstation, keeping the year of surgery, the C-arm's make and model and the acquisition parameters, burned-in text is masked on every frame, the key stays at DMC, only aggregate results are published, and the seed grant says public release of the corpus is not a deliverable.

The protocol states one limitation in advance: archived stills were taken for clinical use, so the share of cases on which a full count can be scored is unknown and expected to be a minority, and cine loops are expected to supply most count endpoints (chapter 10 holds why). A shadow-mode study in the operating room would follow in a second protocol, only if this one meets its endpoints (row 25). Here it is a source not yet obtained.

### What the superseded first draft alone holds

[Protocol 1, the eProtocol draft](../../artifacts/protocol-1-eprotocol-draft-mkmh7p/) of 15 September, is superseded by the retrospective protocol in the clusters, as the earlier draft of the same first protocol (OR-study row 17), though the later draft never says it replaces it, so the datasets page carries each with its own cohort and review type (row 42). It asked for Exempt Category 4 for a wider pull, an unconfirmed 40,000 imaging studies of about 25,000 patients from 1 January 2006, with CT level-naming, registration-label and fracture aims, about 1,500 verified vertebrae per stratum and Protocol 2 submitted alongside, none of which the later draft carries; its figures here are a record, not a final value. It alone still holds practical advice for the PACS pull: ask Radiology whether C-arm saves and navigation screenshots reach PACS at all, since at one university centre 90 of 190 consecutive cases had their intraoperative images only on film or outside PACS (as Protocol 1 cites it, a record, not a final value); get a PACS count by modality, service and year before writing a number on the form; ask also for fluoroscopy of patients with no CT, and for post-instrumentation captures explicitly; and budget to look at every frame for burned-in text, since the DICOM flag for it is optional and absent on many C-arm objects.

Its claim that no public intraoperative spine fluoroscopy set with vertebra-level labels exists is not carried unqualified. No verified public set ships such labels, and C-ArmLSpine's are the project's own (datasets row 39), but on review the OR-study page confirms the contradiction (row 37): the atlas records an unverified open download of nine level-named patients on a real C-arm, NIT Warangal and NIMS Hyderabad's, with names from preoperative CT centroids and frames only as 8-bit overlays beside renders, and VerSeg-Net's 50 lumbar fluoroscopy patients with per-level masks, sharing route not recorded. Reading the Warangal repository would settle whether it meets the draft's definition.

## What we know

The project holds six sets of real C-arm or C-arm-like frames. Only C-ArmLSpine is what State of the literature calls the one real intraoperative human set with a volume, and whether its subjects were living is not stated. Its level names are automatic, lumbar only and read by no person: two labellers agree on 814 of 910 vertebrae, with three one-labeller L6 cases and no T13. DeepFluoro and COBRA2026 give exact pose or per-projection geometry with a CT but no per-vertebra truth of their own; Geneva is one phantom, Ljubljana one cadaveric segment with per-vertebra poses on a flat panel, and SpineSAC has no truth at all. Every paired spine set the Any-View Spine Plan lists but one cadaver is lumbar or lumbosacral, and no public cervical or thoracic intraoperative set with named levels is known.

Of the video, the project holds level-named corner tracks with no pixels, a few licensed clips whose captions name three levels, and screening copies; every cine with level truth on its frames is behind a letter. Of the sixteen letters, Balgrist was sent and refused, the Bournemouth letter forwarded, request 13 sent, and the Radiopaedia licence signed and paid, awaiting countersignature. Of the literature's intraoperative cohorts only LevelCheck is recorded here as asked, and its follow-up has no reply yet. None of the ten gaps the atlas ranks ships a level name. The project's own route to in-domain truth is the retrospective protocol, a draft not yet submitted.

## What we do not know

We do not know how many people stand behind C-ArmLSpine's 149 sessions, whether they were living, or under what ethics approval the images were taken. We do not know which of spine-datasets' 26 requested records are the video letters, nor whether the eleven Outlook drafts the atlas names exist anywhere. The current licence texts of DeepFluoro and Geneva are unsettled, LUMOS's CC BY-NC 4.0 has not been read from its record page, and SPARE's contents are unresolved; no release page could be read from the container, so the plan's check of licences and sizes against release pages is still owed. The record leaves open how many frames the case134 sweep holds (301 or 302), how many Elekta cases are banked (145 or 150), how many cases the cross-validation scored (148 or 141), what the C-ArmLSpine folder holds beyond the release, and whether COBRA2026's sizes are one size in different units. Nobody has confirmed whether L5 and the sacral ala are in COBRA's field, whether Geneva's phantom has a lumbar column, or whether the 2,293 unposed DeepFluoro frames can be posed. Balgrist's specimen count, 13 plus six or six, stays unresolved and no longer matters. Still to be learned are how many cases the retrospective collection would yield, whether the hospital's C-arm saves reach PACS at all, and whether LevelCheck, Huashan, Vanderbilt or SX-Stitch will share.

## What would change our mind

A person-level key from the release or its authors would change how C-ArmLSpine is counted: if the 149 sessions are fewer people, every interval that treats them as independent people is too narrow. A human read of C-ArmLSpine's levels that disagreed with the two labellers would change what the set can be trusted to test. A yes from LevelCheck or Huashan would give the project its first real intraoperative frames of living patients beside a preoperative CT, a second route beside the retrospective collection; raw level-named frames in the Warangal repository would make nine patients the first public labelled intraoperative set. A pilot PACS query that found most C-arm images outside PACS, as at the centre Protocol 1 cites, would shrink that collection and make the letters matter more. Reading spine-datasets' records would show whether the statements here that a letter was "drafted, never sent" are stale. Reading the release pages would settle the licence texts, and a size listing of the C-ArmLSpine folder, a byte count of COBRA's beside its verified file list and a census of the case134 sweep the size and frame questions; none would change which sets exist.
