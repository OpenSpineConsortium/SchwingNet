# 12 Detectors, the architecture and the version 1 training plan

This chapter settles, for whoever plans the next training run, what each learned component of SchwingNet is, what it was trained on and how it is scored; which architecture decisions are locked, and how far the stack that was built follows them; how far detection and naming carry across C-arm devices and views, and which lever moves each; and what version 1 is as the repository plans it, with what the census must count first. ## The stack as built, and the design it was built against

The record holds two descriptions of the architecture. The first is a design. [Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/), undated but later than the board of 11 September, locks "one network, four heads": a shared backbone carrying vertebra detection with four corner keypoints, a level head, a lesion head and later Cobb landmarks, with each head's loss masked to zero on images not annotated for it. The second is what was trained. The [handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) of 8 October says "the SchwingNet model" is five pieces: a film-trained keypoint body detector, a real-frame vertebra and sacrum detector, a level encoder, its recurrent head and, for the fine-tuned numbers only, five fold models re-initialised from the encoder. Beside them sit a count and its coupling to the names, which learn nothing. The contradictions page confirms the conflict between the locked design and the separate models actually built (row 2); no artifact reports the shared backbone trained, so every result below comes from separate models, and version 1 inherits the separate stages.

### The body detector, from detect_v1 to the film detector the stack uses

The first detector described in full is stage 1 of the [Vertebra Detector Field Report](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/), `detect_v1`. It is a YOLO11m-pose model with one class and four keypoints at the body's corners, trained at 1024 px with AdamW for 111 epochs on one H200. It trained on 958 images, 6,073 instances and 727 patients, nearly all lateral, from NHANES II, the Mendeley set and a partial BUU-LSPINE download, split by patient. Three lessons from it still bind any corner detector. Horizontal flipping is off, because a flip silently swaps the anterior and posterior corner slots and the keypoint head then fails to converge. Two parsing bugs were caught: BUU's edge lines pair at a step of two, so consecutive pairs made a quadrilateral for every disc space, and Roboflow's duplicate exports had made 107 duplicated instances. And 25 unit tests hold the corner geometry: the slot order is invariant to rotation and winding, a self-intersecting quadrilateral is refused, and no level is recorded without saying how it was known.

The report scores per source, by box and by corner, never as one pooled recall. Validation box mAP50 is 0.980 and corner mAP50 is 0.922. On the held-out test of 558 instances, AP50 is 0.957, and PCK, the share of corners within 0.1 and 0.2 of the vertebra's own body height, is 0.768 and 0.917. The table gives the sources; the page gives no intervals, and BUU's 15 instances make its row no measurement.

Recall is not reported globally, because a "missed" vertebra on a partially labelled source may be one nobody annotated, and detections outside the trusted-background region are neither credited nor penalised. Inference takes 6.8 ms per image, on hardware the report does not name. The report's own verdict stands: "None of these are cross-site." Every test image came from a source the model trained on, and cropping stood in for ignore masks. The ignore-region mask in `src/ignore.py` was written and unit-tested but never wired into the loss, so the union-with-ignore ablation had not run as of the report (row 75).

The detector then grew. The decisions page gives mAP50 0.991 and PCK at 0.1 of 0.812, on a split it does not name, for a class-agnostic model trained on 4,327 images; the contradictions page reads it as superseding the [field report's](../../artifacts/vertebra-detector-field-report-1jljc6/) 0.957, which is therefore a record, not a final value (row 31). Retraining was planned on about 34,000, the target that replaced the board's 13,500 (row 38). The stack uses `detector_v5z_final`, a YOLO11m-pose body detector trained on zoomed film windows from CSXA, BUU AP and lateral films and the 543 annotated NHANES films, 12,709 films in all (row 30). The [naming page](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) of 18 September reports the class-agnostic detector of its day finding 97.6 percent of bodies on held-out real film. It counts the misses as three in 243, two of them the sacrum, and gives a miss rate of 2.42 percent at confidence 0.25, 0.10 and 0.05 alike, so a lower threshold finds nothing more. The [OR readiness ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) sums up the film keypoint detector v5z as finding 97.7 percent of thoracic bodies on AASCE's 608 films at confidence 0.25, but its table pools films the detector trained on. On the 128 BoostNet test films and the 180 films outside its training and validation lists, recall by thoracic band runs 95.0 to 99.0 percent. L5 is weakest, at 83.6 percent, where the pelvis overlaps it on erect AP films. None of these figures has an interval.

### The sacrum detector and the real-frame detector

Two of the three misses were the sacrum, the count's anchor, so the naming page gives the sacrum its own single-class detector, split by patient, at precision 98.2 percent and recall 96.9 percent with no interval. The page says 4,526 training images in one place and 5,110 in another, so the count is open (row 50). It is the later design, against the single network (row 3).

The stack's real-frame detector, `rx_lodo_carmlspine_1024`, is a YOLO11m box detector for the vertebra and the sacrum. It was trained on 3,094 real frames, from 118 COBRA Elekta cases and five DeepFluoro specimens, and initialised from a detector trained on TotalSegmentator renders. The [demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) counts its model card's training set as 5,408 synthetic and 896 real entries, a unit the record does not reconcile with 3,094 frames. Real C-arm data is barely used: the handoff counts only 145 of COBRA's 692 cases, all Elekta, in any frame bank, and the [retraining plan](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) has the sacrum defined on only 18.2 percent of 609 COBRA test frames.

### Box repair between the detector and the names

Box repair learns nothing, but it is part of what the detector must deliver. It measures spacing in the frame, never assumes a count, fills at most two bodies per gap and never extrapolates past the ends of the chain. Held out, it takes duplicates from 3.03 to 0.61 percent, and naming from 83.0, 85.6 and 90.2 percent to 87.2, 90.4 and 91.4 percent with two, three and four vertebrae in view. Linking frames by majority instead of unanimity took duplicates from 21.8 to 3.0 percent and chain naming from 67.4 to 91.8 percent (chapter 10). No n or interval is given.

### The level encoder and its recurrent head

The encoder, `allspine_v9`, is a ResNet-18 over 27 classes: C1 to C7, T1 to T13, L1 to L6 and the sacrum. It was trained on CSXA's 4,963 films, 3,603 BUU-LSPINE laterals, the 543 NHANES films and renders of CTSpinoPelvic1K, VerSe 19 and 20 and TotalSegmentator v2, and a bidirectional GRU head reads runs of two to nine bodies. On C-ArmLSpine it is used zero-shot. It holds real plain films, but that C-arm's intraoperative appearance reached it through renders alone, since no other labelled intraoperative fluoroscopy was available. The fine-tuned numbers come from the five fold models.

The [level context ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) of 13 September says what moved the encoder. One-vertebra naming on real films went from 84 to 97 percent in one night, all of it from data, when CSXA's 4,963 cervical patients and 7,200 BUU lumbar films entered training. Architecture, size in millimetres, learning rate, epochs and masking moved it a few points at most; on CT, ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-tiny sat within two points of ResNet-18 while ViT-B/16 was 47 points worse, a comparison not repeated on films. Lumbar one-vertebra naming was 7 percent with no lumbar film in training, 81 with about 380 annotated NHANES lumbar films, 83 with BUU and 85 at 96 px. On 8,422 held-out crops the ladder gives 96.7 percent at 48 px and 96.9 at 96 px, the naming page gives 97.0, and the last tenth is unsettled (row 46). All of these September film figures predate the 22 September audit that found NHANES II entered twice across the older banks' splits, and neither page names its bank, so whether they are clean is not stated. The naming page adds that the encoder and its run head name the whole chain right on 100 percent of held-out films and 95.0 percent of renders, with no n.

### What carries no learned parameter

The count learns nothing. In the [counting-levels proposal](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/), the only learned stage finds bodies and the anchor, and the count reads box positions, never a class. Given the annotation's boxes, twelve arms of 16 to 801 spines count exactly on 99.17 to 100 percent of the walks that reach their target. By region, counting from the sacrum is 99.7 percent exact in the lumbar spine and 95.5 in the thoracic, and the one arm that reaches the neck is 83.8 percent there. In the built stack, the count from the detected sacrum names 97.0 percent of anchored bodies, and coupling it to the names adds 1.2 points, with disagreement as the stop signal. The contradictions page carries this latest account over the earlier pages' opposite single principles (row 4). Where the count is exact given the boxes, deployment is a detection question.

## The architecture decisions and how far they held

The decisions page says it records twelve decisions and itemises nine (row 48). Three of them shape the detectors.

One network with four masked heads is locked, "not separate models, not one flat class list". The reason is that a flat list teaches false negatives. A lesion film has no corners and a cervical film no lesion boxes, so in one class list every cervical film would teach "no osteophytes here", though nobody looked. The page cites UniDet (Zhou 2022) and LENS (Yan 2021, plus 49 percent sensitivity), neither checked against its paper. The stack never built the shared backbone, so masked heads are untested here.

Level identity goes on a head and never on the detector, also locked. A detector with level classes can score near-perfectly in training by reading the source, since some levels occur only in some sources. The page backs this with a ResNet-50 that tells the source from chest X-ray contours at 86.6 percent F1, which it does not cite (row 72). Its example, that C3 appears only in CSXA, is contradicted by the [X-ray data handoff](../../artifacts/spine-x-ray-data-handoff-bddbdd/), whose NHANES masks run C2 to C7 (row 23); the general point does not need the example. In the design the head reads pooled features inside the box, so it sees only the vertebra.

The built stack keeps both body detectors class-agnostic. The exceptions are anchors the count needs: the sacrum class, the C2 class of the whole-spine arms and a nine-class cranial detector trained on renders. The one comparison favours the rule: on 1,653 films and 8,422 vertebrae, a level-as-class detector of the BUU family, re-implemented on the same split, names 64.0 percent exactly with 7.6 percent abstained, against 97.0 percent with none for the encoder and its head on one frame (the naming page's figure; the ladder gives 96.9 on the same crops, row 46). A 33-point gap does not depend on that tenth. One plan departs from the rule without saying so. The screening detector in the [annotation-format note](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md) is a box-and-four-keypoint model over the 27 levels, whose per-body posteriors the counting prior decodes as one column, and no page reconciles it with the lock. Version 1 has no detector stage yet ([the v1 flow memory](../../docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md)), so the tension is still only on paper.

Adding CSXA is locked so that cervical levels have two independent sources, CSXA and NHANES, and a head trained on one can be tested on the other. Surviving the swap means morphology; collapsing means the source's signature. No artifact here records the result. The page's other six decisions concern the CT folds, the CT dataset paper ([chapter 7](07-ct-collections-their-labels-and-the-border-variants.md)) and data governance, not the detectors.

## How far detection and naming transfer

The held-out device below is C-ArmLSpine, absent from the training frames, the render poses and the style terms. Naming and detection behave differently there.

### Naming across devices and views

Lateral naming transfers. The zero-shot encoder names 595 of 617 lateral vertebrae, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4] (demo plan, A2.1). Here and below, "A" rows are rows of spine-program's STATUS.md as the [demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) quotes them; the plan ran nothing, and no result file behind them has been read, so they are Reported. The 96.4 supersedes the 96.27 percent (594 of 617) of 22 September in [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/), a record, not a final value (row 13). Against the arm fine-tuned on the device's own folds, the paired difference is plus 1.0 point [-1.9, 4.0] (A3.7), so fine-tuning buys nothing the interval can see. The fine-tuned whole-frame figure is unsettled. The handoff's catalog gives 97.4 percent, while the ledger and [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) give 98.2 percent, and the October pages give 98.2 percent to the coupled count. Its case count is 148 or 141. The main contradictions page leaves the figure to the catalog and the count to the fold record, and the concept ledger holds the count open (rows 0 and 104). With one body in view the current figure is 74.8 percent, replacing the [ledger's](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) 72.8, a record, not a final value.

AP naming does not transfer: 276 of 631, 43.7 percent, current in the [concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md) (ledger row 107), with no interval; the 37.88 percent of [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) it supersedes is a record, not a final value. The whole column shifts one level cranially. That is a counting error rather than an appearance error, so its levers are a counting prior and AP renders with anchors.

Fine-tuning on one device costs the others. On COBRA laterals with truth boxes, the ledger gives the zero-shot model 79.9 percent [74.5, 84.6] and the C-ArmLSpine fine-tune 43.5 percent [36.4, 50.1], each over 904 scored calls. With the held-out detector's own boxes, the zero-shot model reaches 89.2 percent [84.7, 92.5] over 240. Counting from the detected sacrum, device held out, reaches 80.0 percent [72.1, 86.8] over 240 scored calls on COBRA's near-AP frames and 84.3 percent [80.0, 93.9] over 185 on DeepFluoro's six cadavers, a cell the ledger flags for the initialising detector's exposure to the test specimen. Naming on oblique views is measured only on renders, about 58 percent on 89 spines (row 33). The ledger's one real oblique cell is a count from the sacrum on COBRA frames at 30 to 60 degrees, with a detector that saw the device: 98.8 percent [96.1, 100] over 163 scored calls. The catalog adds that renders raise film naming by 6 to 22 points at 5 to 25 training persons.

### Detection across devices

The current held-out figure is the catalog's. Trained without any C-ArmLSpine frame, the real-frame detector finds 231 of 287 bodies at confidence 0.74, 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8], and 93.9 percent on the repaired denominator (A6.1, A6.5). The [ledger's](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) 90.6 percent is an earlier rescore of 287 bodies on 56 frames at outline IoU 0.5 and confidence 0.25 (92.7 at its best threshold, 90.9 under the centre rule), a record, not a final value (row 5); neither later page states the catalog's convention.

That rescore holds the sharpest scoring lesson. The first held-out figure, 5.2 percent, measured a box convention. C-ArmLSpine's truth boxes are vertebral bodies, while the COBRA and DeepFluoro truth the detector learned are whole-vertebra outlines. Matched, the same predictions read 90.6 percent, and the same-device control moves the other way: 90.6 percent against body boxes, 40.8 against outlines and 91.6 under the convention-free centre rule. A detector learns its truth's box convention, and a test must score in that convention or by a rule that has none.

With 24 C-ArmLSpine frames added, detection reaches 84.0 percent. The plateau is about 82 to 85 percent, and 95 percent of it comes by 10 to 12 frames (A6.9 to A6.12, A8.2, no interval). No held-out cell meets the pre-stated 97 percent target (A6.13). The ledger lists counting-grade detection, 97 percent or more on a device held out entirely, as not yet supported, and [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) gives the reason: recall compounds over eight to twelve bodies.

From renders alone the value is open, though the direction is not. The [any-view plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) gives 0.3 percent. The demo plan ties it to 1 of 287 at confidence 0.74, and gives 31.7 percent at 0.25 (A6.12) and 7.0 percent with no device frame (A8.1). The ledger gives 2.4 percent by body IoU, 31.7 by outline, 54.7 by centre and 17.1 in its device table. The contradictions page calls it cannot-tell until the catalog row's convention, threshold and render generation are read (row 6). Every value is far below what other devices' real frames give.

On film, the counting-levels proposal gives a film-trained detector 99.14 percent per-body recall and the same architecture trained on renders only 71.67 percent, with no interval; Counting From The Sacrum puts that training set at 6,650 renders and no real image. With render-trained boxes, the AIDaS proposal reports count exactness falling from 100 to 30.06 percent on 79 held-out spines. A detector trained on renders of DeepFluoro's own CTs finds the sacrum on 90 to 97 percent of its real frames in the counting-levels table (six specimens held out in turn, three seeds each) and on about 96 to 98 percent in AIDaS; the concept ledger holds the pair open (row 10). Trained on renders of living whole-body CTs, it reaches 64 and 77 percent under two renderings. A refiner trained on the real pairs, a high-pass version of it and a training-free low-band swap added about eight to ten points each, every one smaller than the 26-point seed spread.

### Which lever moves which

Naming is moved by labelled real data and by the count. The ladder's night of CSXA and BUU films moved it most. The naming page also reports that 83 real six-lumbar images moved L6 naming from 67.4 to 73.5 percent on held-out patients, after re-weighting, oversampling, simulated pretraining and two pseudo-label rules had moved nothing. AP needs a counting prior, and renders carry naming to an unseen device.

Detection is moved by real frames, other devices' first and then a few of the device itself, by matching the truth's box convention, and by covering the device's scale. Renders alone have not carried it anywhere. Whether a better renderer could is the question of the physics-renderer ablation (#69), and the published answer is mixed: SyntheX (Gao 2023) matched real-data training on 366 real cadaver hip X-rays (landmark error 6.44 against 6.46 mm) but fell short on chest lesions (85 against 94 percent).

## Known failure modes

In-distribution headlines come first: the field report's 0.957 and the decisions page's 0.991 say nothing about another site. Leaked families come second. Allspine v2, v6 and v7 pooled NHANES twice across the split, `anchor_drr` put 301 of 801 persons on both sides, and vindrps had a 77.8 percent validation leak. So allspine v2's 99.02 percent shortcut-free film number, from the [handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), is a record, not a final value, as is the 99 percent selective accuracy from that bank. The G3 person-disjoint split replaced them on 23 September. But 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons still sit in the current encoder's lineage, so no transitional number on it is held out ([chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md)).

Magnification is third. The resolution ladder of [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/), a record, not a final value, coarsened films from 836 to 139 px at a cost of 0.00 points of recall at confidence 0.05. Magnified OR fields do cost. Under the centre rule, the held-out detector finds 35.9 percent of vertebrae in a three-vertebra field (64.0 at confidence 0.05; 175 crops, 566 vertebrae) and 18.6 percent in a one-vertebra field (287 crops), against 82.5 percent for the same-device control. In the ledger's words, "Scale is a device-transfer lever of its own" (row 35).

Orientation and layout are fourth. Inversion or a 180-degree flip breaks every detector, since orientation is assumed. Noise to 300 photons per pixel, read noise to sigma 0.06, rotation to 15 degrees and the tested gamma, collimation, shutter and instruments each cost under 10 percent of sacrum recall. [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/) found 160 of DeepFluoro's 366 frames stored upside down, a record, not a final value. A fixed box, the training set's median sacrum box, hits the true sacrum at IoU 0.5 or more on 85.7 percent of C-ArmLSpine test frames, 58.5 of COBRA's and 13.5 of DeepFluoro's, so "sacrum found" on C-ArmLSpine partly measures how surgeons centre the field.

Renders and seeds are fifth. [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/) records a two-class detector, trained on 597 calibrated synthetic frames, finding the sacrum on 76.9 percent of 365 real DeepFluoro frames, a record, not a final value. The ledger ties 76.9 percent to the `anchor_none_r2` generation and gives 64.0 percent for v2, the generation every real fine-tune started from (row 32). It also says the 100 percent DeepFluoro sacrum figure is not the manuscript's method but a same-device detector, and gives the clean render-only figure for that specimen as 88.5 to 91.3 percent at confidence 0.05 over three seeds. Three seeds of one dataset on one specimen spanned 26 points, so single-run differences between methods do not exist. The eight domain-randomisation arms of Ninety-Three Pixels, cut from 11,007 labelled films and a record, not a final value, were each validated on their own degraded distribution, so no ablation can be read off them, and no page here records the cross-domain run queued behind them.

The C2 arm is sixth, and void. In [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/), a record, not a final value, 2,519 of 2,593 walks were refused, 74 reached the target and 6 were correct. The C2 box lands on C2 in 218 of 219 renders, so the failure is coverage: C2 is found on 99.5 percent of full renders and 31 percent of 93 px sweep frames, and it is in the frame at all on only 15 percent. On real film, the ledger has the real-trained whole-spine arm finding 0 to 8 percent of 25 labelled C2 instances and the render-trained arms none. Last, fine-tuning overfits to its device, as the COBRA figures show, and pseudo-labels cost anomalous accuracy, nineteen points on some pages and twenty on others, a figure the concept ledger holds open (row 182).

## The any-view plan and what the census must count

The any-view plan of 21 October grades three goals. Paired X-ray and CT as gold truth is strong, since projecting a CT's segmentation through known geometry gives every vertebra's level and corners with no reader. Detection and naming at any position is feasible, with a measured gap. AP-to-lateral image translation is hard as stated. Nothing published turns a spine AP into a lateral; XraySyn (2021) moved the view about 9 degrees. And what an AP does not show, a model cannot recover, so a synthesised lateral is a display aid, never a film.

The working answer is structured transfer: predict the lateral's levels, corners and sagittal alignment from the AP, and score them against the same person's real lateral, with pixel synthesis only as an auxiliary task. LatXGen (2025), which makes a lateral radiograph from images of the back's surface, trained on about 2,600 real pairs and carried sagittal angles at R-squared 0.85 to 0.90. The plan takes that as its expectation for global alignment, not for focal findings.

For any position the plan asks for accuracy per C-arm angle, from COBRA's 692 real rotations for the low lumbar spine and sacrum (#60) and from renders elsewhere. Its gold sets are COBRA's 375,618 projections, C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases with CBCT, DeepFluoro's six cadavers, an 18-frame cadaver standard and one digital-twin cadaver reaching T2. LevelCheck (Lo 2015; 20 patients, 10,000 perturbed views) labelled 100 percent of views at about 26 s, which is why registration labels count as gold. A person used to check the prior is never used to fit it.

The detectors will read one format, set in the annotation-format note. Each body gets four corners in a fixed order (superior-left, superior-right, inferior-right, inferior-left, head up), a level from the 27 classes and a visibility flag. The box is computed from the corners; the column count and transitional findings are recorded once per image; and every annotation names its reader. Every held set that marks the bodies themselves marks their corners, or points or edge lines that include them, and CT projection gives corners exactly. The note's pseudo-labeller keeps a teacher's label only where per-body confidence, test-time augmentation and a column the counting prior accepts all agree. It writes pseudo-labels as shards of their own and never labels a test person.

The census came first. It ran on 22 October over 140 groups, 7,008,826 files and 7.3 TB ([the census record](../../docs/research/data-manifest/README.md)). By region, the only real AP-and-lateral pairs held are BUU-LSPINE's 3,600 lumbar people. The held X-rays with a CT or a model of the same subject are the plan's gold sets less the digital-twin cadaver, which is not yet fetched, plus a hip phantom, and all are lumbar, lumbosacral or pelvic. So nothing held pairs the thoracic or cervical spine, and NHANES II, VinDr-SpineXR and FluoroCap await the next run's split by region.

The plan also estimates what makes each approach testable. AP-to-lateral transfer needs about 1,000 real pairs per region to fine-tune and about 100 test persons, which gives a 95 percent interval near +/-6 points at 90 percent accuracy. Detection on a new device needs about 25 real frames of it. Checking the prior needs about 100 persons per region, and an anomaly screener needs every film plus about 50 confirmed cases per class. Only the lumbar and lumbosacral regions reach 100 persons. The first steps need no census, but every decision to train waits for it.

## Version 1 as the repository plans it

Version 2 of the retraining plan, from the owner's words of 20 October, replaces version 1's premise. The reported stack is not a baseline: the first model this workflow trains to work is version 1, and every later version is measured against it on the same frozen test persons. Nothing reruns from scratch when data is added, each stage has one model and one seed, and evaluation is drawn on the same real frames at every checkpoint.

### Data, splits and stages

Data arrives as a registry of shards. Each source has a file under `data/registry/` with its licence as recorded, its identity level and its person-key rule, and each shard is content-hashed with a manifest and never rewritten. Crops are derived, keyed by the shard hash and the detector's version, since a retrained detector makes every crop stale. Leak checks run at ingestion: the person-key chain (CHK-6 and CHK-7, never yet run on the real corpus) checks each new shard against every registered one, an item of a test person goes only to a test shard, and a shard that mixes test persons with others is refused. The split is a salted hash of the person key, so persons added later fall the same way. Test persons are a frozen list, `data/splits/test-persons.json`, extended only by pull request. The only real L6 persons the record holds are three of those test persons, and the plan's list of sources to register ends with the synthetic L6 renders.

Each stage keeps a checkpoint registry, `models/<stage>/<version>.json`, with the weights' hash, the shards seen, the parent and the commit. An incremental run resumes from the latest checkpoint over all shards, with the new ones oversampled; the validation witness frames pick the epoch, never the test frames. A `rebase` from declared ImageNet weights for the encoder, or COCO weights for the detectors, over every shard is the deliberate step. Version 1 is one rebase per stage on the registry as it stands, using the reported stack's trainers by path with their `--init` options. The schedule puts the registry by 23 October, the re-keyed films by 25 and version 1 by 28, at about 8 hours for the detector, 14 for the encoder and 10 for the head, each after the owner's word and number. Latency and the recorded sweep follow by 29 October, the provisional on 30 October and the talk on 2 November.

### The two training rules

The two training rules usually quoted for version 1 come from [Research Directions](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) of 8 October, not from the repository's plan, which restates neither. Training is mixed by default, renders and real films in every batch, because pretrain-then-fine-tune lost 25.6 anomalous points; the same page asks whether that arm, from a leaked bank, was ever rerun on a clean one. And the clean encoder, a two-channel ResNet-18 with no pseudo-labels, starts from ImageNet on G3 training persons only, because 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons sit in the v8 and v9 training splits; that page calls it an evaluation arm, not the flagship training, and gives it three seeds. G3 splits ordinary persons 80/10/10 and transitional ones 50/10/40, with every real transitional subject in test, and the handoff records that its leak audit ran CHK-1 to CHK-5, CHK-8 and CHK-9. The retraining plan agrees in effect on the first rule, since a rebase trains over every shard at once, and on the ImageNet start, but it splits by a salted hash of re-keyed persons with its own frozen test list and does not name G3, and it keeps one seed. Whether version 1's split honours G3's transitional assignments is therefore not settled by any file read here.

### Evaluation and latency

The validation witness set is 24 C-ArmLSpine frames of validation persons, scored at every epoch. The test witness set, 24 frames of test persons, the three L6 cases and 8 frames of each recorded sweep, is drawn at every checkpoint but read only at a version boundary. Film rows join the test set only after the v9 films are re-keyed and a rebase is run, since every CSXA, BUU and NHANES person is in the v9 base's training or selection set. Every number is correct over n with a Wilson interval on person-keyed n, the transitional stratum apart ([chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)). Latency is measured per version, with ONNX outputs matched to PyTorch on the witness frames, against 97 ms per frame end to end at the sweeps' 10.3 frames per second. The record's only CPU figure is 3,350 ms per frame, which the demo plan gives for the encoder alone.

### What has run

The flow has run on a synthetic bank only ([the dry-run record](../../docs/research/v1-demo/README.md)). Run `a767a466f5c1` of 21 October trained the level stage for four epochs over truth boxes and scored 556 of 556 test crops from 5 persons, 1.000 (0.993 to 1.000), after validation at 0.665 (0.624 to 0.703, n 546) at epoch 1. Its vertebrae are bright rectangles barred by level index, so the run shows that the workflow works and says nothing about the spine. Run `cd37124c487d` showed what witness frames are for. It named 85 of 93 validation witness bodies at epoch 1 and 38 and 41 at epochs 2 and 3, while the training loss sat far below its epoch 1 value (0.022 and 0.040 against 0.278), then all 93 at epoch 4. The detector stage is not yet in version 1, and every row says it is over truth boxes ([the v1 flow memory](../../docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md)). No real frame has been read, and the grid run waits on the owner.

## What we know

Every real-frame number comes from separate models: a film corner detector, a real-frame vertebra and sacrum detector, a 27-level ResNet-18 with a recurrent head, and a count that learns nothing. The masked-head backbone is locked but unbuilt. The one comparison in the record (64.0 against about 97 percent) supports keeping level identity off the body detectors, and data, not architecture, moved naming.

Lateral naming transfers to an unseen C-arm at 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4], and AP collapses to 43.7 percent as a one-level counting error. Detection reaches 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8] with other devices' real frames and about 84 with 24 frames of the device. That is short of the 97 percent counting needs, and renders alone give far less. Scoring must match the learned box convention or use the centre rule. Magnification, orientation and seeds move results more than most method changes do, and the leaked families are records only. Version 1's workflow is built and tested, but on a synthetic bank only.

## What we do not know

Six figures stay unsettled until the files behind them are read. They are the fine-tuned whole-frame figure (97.4 or 98.2 percent) with its cases (148 or 141), the render-only detection value with its convention and render generation, the population-matched sacrum range (90 to 97 or 96 to 98 percent), the sacrum detector's training count (4,526 or 5,110), the pseudo-label cost (nineteen or twenty points), and the convention behind the 80.5 percent.

The C-ArmLSpine figures rest on spine-program's STATUS.md as the demo plan quotes it; no result file behind them has been read. Whether the September film figures (the ladder, the naming page, the level-as-class comparison) came from a bank the 22 September audit later found leaked is not stated, and whether version 1's hash split keeps G3's transitional assignments is not settled. The record holds no result for the union-with-ignore ablation or the CSXA-NHANES swap. It has no clean-bank test of mixed against staged training. Nor does it measure naming on real oblique frames, C2 on any real image, paired thoracic or cervical truth, or a shared backbone against separate stages. Nobody has checked UniDet, LENS, XraySyn, LatXGen, SyntheX or LevelCheck against their papers, or sourced the 86.6 percent, or checked the field report's 0.980, 0.917 and 6.8 ms against spine-program's files. None of the levers in the [CT-priors note](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md) has been measured on a real frame, and version 1 has no number about the spine.

## What would change our mind

Each of these results would change what this chapter says:

- A detector trained on renders alone that reached the 80 percent held-out band on C-ArmLSpine under a stated convention would reverse the rule that detection needs real frames; the physics-renderer ablation (#69) is the run that could show it.
- A held-out detection cell at or above 97 percent on a device held out entirely would make counting deployable without device frames.
- A level-as-class detector that matched the encoder on held-out sources would reopen the level-on-a-head decision, and with it the screening detector's departure from it.
- A shared masked-head backbone that beat the separate stages on the same frozen test persons would make the locked design worth building.
- A clean-bank rerun in which pretrain-then-fine-tune matched mixed batches would change the default schedule.
- Structured AP-to-lateral transfer that failed on about 100 held-out BUU persons would retire the any-view plan's first goal.
- Paired thoracic or cervical films found by the census would let the 3D prior be checked above L1 without renders.
