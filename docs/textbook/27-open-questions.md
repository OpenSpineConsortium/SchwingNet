# 27 Open questions

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it lists questions and the results they wait on, and leaves how each stage works to Part III.

This chapter gathers the open questions of the owner's 69 collected artifacts into one digest, ordered by what each would decide, so that a planner can see what the programme does not yet know, what has been answered since an artifact was written, and who can answer the rest. The records hold 480 open questions. Many repeat across a handoff, its draft and the page that superseded it, many concern one afternoon's running job, and a large group concerns Symphony and Beethoven; consolidated, those that bear on a SchwingNet experiment come to about sixty. Each is given with its source artifact, whether it is still open as the repository stands on 24 October 2026 (read against docs/research/ and docs/memory/), who can close it, and the chapter it would change. One whole class is left out on purpose: the records raised questions about permissions, approvals, agreements, consent and the scope of determinations, and every question of that kind is settled by the owner and closed, so none is listed below. [Chapter 28](28-the-ranked-experiment-backlog.md) turns the questions that need a run into a ranked backlog; this chapter only says what is open and why it matters.

## How the questions were sorted

### Three tests applied to every question

Each question went through three tests. The first was whether a later artifact or a repository note answers it; the main sources are [docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md) (23 October), which names what nobody has measured, and [docs/research/training-review.md](../../docs/research/training-review.md) (24 October), which says where the workflow stands stage by stage. A question one of them answers is marked closed with the pointer, or partly closed with what remains. The second was whether it still matters: one about a run the plan of 20 October no longer builds on ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)) is marked superseded. The third was who can answer: a session alone, a grid run on the owner's word and number of jobs, or the owner or an outside party.

### What was dropped and why

Four kinds were dropped: the permission class, closed as the opening says; the Symphony and Beethoven questions (the taxonomy's R1 to R10, the protocol's validation findings, the store release, the VS Code migration), left to Part S, chapters 29 to 32, except the two a run waits on; outreach and funding, in [chapter 26](26-outreach-and-student-funding-by-role.md), where the event's year stays open; and working notes (a figure still rendering, a projected arrival time), unless the work still matters and no page reports its outcome.

### The state of the record in one paragraph

One fact frames every theme. Every measured number about SchwingNet on real fluoroscopy still comes from the reported stack of the earlier programme, not from the new workflow, and the new workflow has run once on real frames, on a small labelled set, with a test part of 21 crops from 4 persons whose interval runs from about 0.85 to 1 and which therefore carries no claim ([docs/research/training-review.md](../../docs/research/training-review.md), section 2). No detector stage of the new workflow exists yet, so a frame taken off a C-arm cannot yet be named end to end. So "open" below mostly means the number does not exist, not that it is bad.

## Labels and reads

The most repeated open question in the records is that no person has read a real fluoroscopic frame for its level. The later notes confirm it is still open: on 20 October the demo plan counts no transitional accuracy on any real image with a human read and 7 at-risk persons with a frame, none verified ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), section 5), and on 23 October the state-of-knowledge ledger has no row on a transitional spine read by two readers. What has changed is the route: an annotation format with a named reader on every file now exists ([docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md)), and workstream O (#127) plans gold masks on the paired CT and X-ray sets, where 30 verified cases give an interval near plus or minus 10 points on a per-body rate at 90 percent. Only a reader's time closes this, and it changes [chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md) and every real-frame claim in [chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md).

## Method

The method questions are named here by what each would decide; the mechanism behind each lives in Part III.

### Whether a single frame carries L6

[Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/) asked whether local appearance on a single AP or lateral frame can tell L6 from L5, T13 from L1, or a lumbarised S1 from L5 with the anchor in view, and whether the abstention set is defined by field of view alone. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) asked the same in two forms: whether 2-D morphology carries L6 identity at all, and how many distinct L6 cases are enough, since at most 90 gave 46 percent on rendered L6 crops and twelve-fold duplication gave nothing; that 90 rests on a VerSe L6 tally the ledger marks open (row 43). [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) adds that its own tests of a global anomaly signature were ambiguous and that it found no field of view at which all four spine classes sort. This is the central method question of the programme and it is open. The later notes reframe it rather than answer it: the only real persons with an L6 are the three frozen test persons, so every rare class the model learns before the talk comes from CT ([docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md), section 1). It needs a grid run and, for a real answer, readers; it changes [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md).

### The AP shift and the levers meant to fix it

The largest error in the record is not a rare class but a whole-column shift by one level on AP views: zero-shot AP naming of 43.7 percent (276 of 631 bodies) against 96.4 percent lateral (595 of 617, interval 92.3 to 98.4) on the held-out C-arm, as reported from the earlier programme's status file ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), section 0); the concept ledger marks the 43.7 current and the September page's 37.88 superseded ([the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md), row 107). Whether the counting aid built from CT removes that shift (workstream A, #57, the subject of [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)) is open: the code is built and has not run on the grid ([docs/research/training-review.md](../../docs/research/training-review.md), section 2), and its dry run on synthetic data tested the plumbing, not the spine. The state-of-knowledge note ranks it first among the unmeasured questions, by how many later steps wait on it, and costs it at CPU minutes once one CT statistics job has run.

### What explains the C2 failure, and how to reach C2

The superseded [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/) board asked what explains the C2 failure, first naming the render-trained cranial detector (0.740 recall against the film detector's 0.991) as the likely cause, then, in its own C2 arm, whose number it calls void, finding that C2 is inside a sweep frame at all on only 15 percent of frames; the [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) reports C2 found on 0 to 8 percent of 25 real-film truths, calls it unknown on fluoroscopy and proposes labelling C2 in the BUU and CSXA cervical films; the [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) asks how to reach C2 from the lumbosacral gold. The repository now has a programme for the last of these (workstream P, #135, with its prior art in [docs/research/lumbosacral-to-cervical-extrapolation.md](../../docs/research/lumbosacral-to-cervical-extrapolation.md)), which finds no paper that trains a level namer on one region and tests it on another, and judges naming on a mid-cervical spot frame with no anchor in view as possible but doubtful. So the question has moved from why C2 failed to whether the cervical spine can be reached at all, and it is open. It needs grid runs and changes [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) and the scope statements of [chapter 1](01-the-goal-and-the-constraints.md).

### Whether labels from registration transfer

[Lift Before Label?](../../artifacts/lift-before-label-jzjq8t/) asked how the render-to-real gap for a discrete level label scales with the number of registered real frames, noting that no learning curve exists for any spine task, and whether a pose-aware identifier helps on real frames; it also asked whether editing CT to make transitional variants transfers at all, with no prior work. All three are open. The route to the first is named, real COBRA projections labelled from their planning CT (workstream D, #60) and gold masks on paired sets (O, #127), both todo. The nearest measured curve is for detection, not the level label: on the held-out C-arm the reported stack's ladder runs from 0.3 percent (1 of 287 bodies) with renders alone (a record, not a final value, cannot-tell on the detectors page, row 6), to 80.5 percent with other devices' real frames (the held-out figure the record has not settled, below), to 84.0 percent with 24 frames of the device, 95 percent of a plateau near 82 to 85 percent reached by 10 to 12 frames ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), sections 0 and 1). These change [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) and [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md).
