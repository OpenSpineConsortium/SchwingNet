# 14 Transitional anatomy: two anchors, disagreement and abstention

Label: confidential-method. Results may be shown, mechanism may not, until the provisional is filed; this chapter stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere.

This chapter states in full how the programme means to find and name an extra or missing vertebra (L6, T13, a lumbar rib, an absent twelfth rib, a sacralised L5) when almost every spine it learns from is ordinary. It settles for a planner why a level name is the wrong target and what replaces it; which anchors survive which variant, and how a lumbar rib is told from a sacralisation; what has been measured on transitional spines, at what n; what the system abstains on, and why by position; and which arms are planned and which gates and stop rules decide whether they run. ## Why a level name is the wrong target

The design page begins with the target. Ask a network for L5 and it has a defect no capacity repairs: a sixth lumbar vertebra is rare, so the model is rewarded for never predicting one, and a model that learns ordinary anatomy well calls every lowest lumbar vertebra L5 because that prior is almost always right, "a property of the target, not the backbone". The page's "roughly 2% of people" carries no source. The contradictions page gives 3.2 percent (Sun 2025) as the L6 prior and 2.0 percent, Wilson 1.3 to 3.3, as CTSpinoPelvic1K's own read (row 15); the same page puts any lumbosacral transitional vertebra at 9 percent (Hanhivaara 2020) to 18 (Nardo 2012), though the reality check gives Hanhivaara's 3,855 CTs 28.6 percent with type I counted, so the 9 percent may be types II to IV alone; BUU's 4.0 percent is a dataset floor, not a prevalence (row 33). At every one of these rates the prior favours the ordinary name.

The record bears it out. As a voxel class on CT, L6 scored Dice 0.000 in every epoch of ten runs, one reaching epoch 83, with its 18 cases duplicated twelvefold ([Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/), Research directions); class-aware sampling stays untried (row 44), and the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) reads the failed duplication as showing that distinct cases, not copies, are the limit. On renders and film the class failed in the prior's direction. The superseded [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) named L6 on none of 68 single crops, 41 of them called L5; all-spine v1 named it on 46.4 percent, 366 of 913 called L5 ([FluoroCap Overnight Report](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/)); and [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) named it on 44.9 and 48.3 percent of isolated-stack windows, all records, not final values. The naming page adds that its pseudolabelled third held no L6, since its decoder had only the ordinary alphabet: every transitional spine there "is actively teaching the wrong answer".

What replaces the name is a pair of counts: the network predicts where each vertebra sits against two anchors, and the name is a deterministic function of the pair.

## Two counts, one sum and the flag it gives

For every vertebra voxel the design predicts two ordinal fields: `n_up`, how many vertebrae this one sits above the sacrum, and `n_down`, how many it sits below the lowest rib-bearing vertebra. Each anchor, the page argues, can be identified locally without knowing the count. On an ordinary column `n_up + n_down` is a constant K all the way down; on a transitional column the counts disagree.

Two consequences put this at the centre. Both counts are learned from the ordinary cases, about 800 of each, so the anomaly is detected as a violation of consistency between two things the network learned well, not recognised as a rare category it saw a handful of times. And one quantity is both flag and loss: `|n_up + n_down - K|` is a per-case scalar near zero on ordinary anatomy and near one at a transition, "a calibrated flag, not a softmax over a class that barely exists", and, penalised against the case's own constant, a free self-supervised constraint on every case, labelled or not. An ROC on that scalar is "the cleanest single figure this design can produce".

This is not the [Vertebral Count Anomaly Rule](../../artifacts/vertebral-count-anomaly-rule/) of 22 September, which [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md) holds. That rule counts between fixed ends: with C1 excluded an ordinary column holds 23 bodies from C2 to L5, so the counts above and below any target sum to 22, and on a mismatch "the ordinal is reported and the name is withheld". It flags the column, not the level, passes a column carrying one extra and one missing body, and needs C2 to the sacrum in view, where the two-anchor design needs the sacrum and the lowest rib. The two-anchor design counts between movable anchors defined by morphology, which lets it place the variant; the contradictions page rules them different rules, not rivals (rows 22 and 23). Both report the counts and withhold the name on a column that does not add up.

## Four anchors and their two signatures

The design page states the objection that breaks its first version: every anchor moves with exactly the variants it should detect. A lumbar rib moves the lowest rib-bearing vertebra down one and an aplastic twelfth rib moves it up; a sacralised segment moves the top of the sacrum up and a lumbarised one moves it down; and a lumbar rib and a sacralisation both cut the sum by one, so a design resting on it "would flag both correctly and name both wrongly, half the time." The answer is to define anchors by morphology, which does not move when the feature moves, and to take four whose failures fall on different variants: A, the costal facets on the vertebral body; B, the vertebra a rib articulates with; C, the sacroiliac articulation; and D, the sacral promontory.

| Variant | A: costal facets | B: rib articulates | C: sacroiliac articulation | D: promontory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical | T12 | T12 | S1 | S1 |
| Lumbar rib | T12 | L1, down one | S1 | S1 |
| Stump or aplastic twelfth rib | T12 | T11, up one | S1 | S1 |
| Sacralisation, Castellvi II | T12 | T12 | S1 | up one |
| Sacralisation, Castellvi III to IV | T12 | T12 | may move | up one |
| Lumbarisation | T12 | T12 | may move | down one |

The rib signature is A - B, which is 0, +1 or -1: an aplastic twelfth rib leaves its costal facet behind and a lumbar rib grows on a vertebra that never had one, so A is B's control and the sign names the rib variant. The sacral signature is C - D, zero or not: the auricular surface belongs to the ilium and, the page holds, is not given up when a lumbar segment fuses above it, so D moving alone and C and D moving together mark different Castellvi grades. The two families are orthogonal, so a case carrying both decomposes where a single-anchor design sees one net shift: no anchor is reliable alone, and the pattern of disagreement identifies the variant.

### Telling a lumbar rib from a sacralisation

Both variants shorten the interval by one but fall on different pairs. A lumbar rib moves B down against an unmoved A and leaves C and D at S1: rib signature non-zero, sacral zero. A sacralisation leaves A and B at T12 and moves D up, with C too at the higher grades: the other way round. A stump rib is told from a lumbar rib by the same pair, B moving up rather than down, and length cannot do it. In the reality check lumbar ribs had a median length of 45 mm against 38 mm for 98 stump twelfth ribs (the lumbar-rib count itself is open between 13 and 16, [the concept ledger, row 49](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-49-ctspinopelvic1k-cases-with-a-rib-on-a-lumbar-vertebra)), and the border page's bimodal rib 12, near 0.68 or 0.32 of rib 11's length, marks a hypoplastic rib but not its vertebra. The design names the costotransverse joint, 2 to 4 mm across, for that decision. Two premises under the sacral pair are unsourced, that the sacroiliac articulation is unchanged by fusion and the audit's "Castellvi II-IV by definition" for ilium contact (row 12): plausible anatomy, not measurement, for the first sacral experiment to test.

### The anchors on the data

Sacral foramina, the textbook sacrum-intrinsic anchor (four pairs normal, five an absorbed lumbar segment, a rule stated without a source, row 11), failed: on a release the design page does not name (row 27), the cases labelled normal gave 2 pairs per side in 211, 3 in 324, 4 in 163 and 5 in 49, overlapping the labelled variants completely. The design page blames truncation of the field and the border page the detector, which returned 3 left and 7 right on one symmetric sacrum; the contradictions page keeps both (row 10).

The rib anchor needs correctly numbered ribs. The [Rib Anchor Audit](../../artifacts/rib-anchor-audit-t7wr2h/) followed 11,579 ribs from v4 to v5: numbering errors fell from 170 to 109 and the systematic -1 shift from 129 to 71, while ribs on a lumbar vertebra rose from 21 to 25, as a review that keeps anomalies should. Two of its bugs teach any rib anchor. Left-right asymmetry read 230 of 801 cases until a rib number was dropped from both sides whenever either side's was cut, and then 15, since 57 percent of ribs are clipped by the field: a rib phenotype not computed on the paired, uncut subset measures positioning. And marking a vertebra rib-bearing whenever a rib passed within 10 mm marked L1 rib-bearing in 61 cases where strict incidence found 14 lumbar ribs: proximity is not articulation, and articulation is anchor B's definition. The border page's renumbering, with lumbar ribs given classes of their own, took ribs on the wrong vertebra from 109 to 19 and misnumbered cases from 16 to 2; these are v5 counts, no source checks that they reached v11 (row 14), and seven cases still needed a person (row 28).

The audit also scored the clinicians' own quantity, the count of non-rib-bearing vertebrae between the lowest rib-bearing body and the sacrum, normally five. Against a radiologist it agreed on 2 of 22 cases, missing all 7 sacralisations and 12 of 14 lumbarisations, because it was counted over labelled vertebrae and the labels give most transitional cases L1 to L5 and a sacrum whatever the anatomy (row 9): it "recovers the labelling convention, not the patient", and the border page's Cohen's d of 0.8 for it only separates labels by a measure derived from them (row 7). Ribs settle the thoracolumbar border, but this corpus's transitional cases live at the lumbosacral junction, where ribs cannot anchor the count; rib-12 length is only weakly associated with the label there (d 0.55), through co-occurrence (row 8). The audit's two tests for that boundary are anchors C and D in mask form: whether the L5 transverse process reaches a slightly dilated ilium, and the height of the sacroiliac articulation against the labelled S1 endplate; "where they disagree, that is the transitional cohort, and it triages itself." No source computed either, and the border page's proxies, lateral span and gap to the ala, were flat (d under 0.2), perhaps the proxy's fault (row 13).

## The type head: the vertebra names the rib

Counting cannot reach the case most worth detecting: when the twelfth rib is aplastic there is no lowest rib, and `n_down` has no origin. So a third head predicts thoracic or lumbar type per vertebra from patch-local morphology: costal facets on the body, thoracic only and present whether or not a rib grew; the costotransverse joint, a joint space where a lumbar transverse process is simply continuous with the pedicle; and process shape, short and club-like in the thorax, long and blade-like with mammillary processes in the lumbar spine. A vertebra with facets and no rib is a T12 with an aplastic rib, and one with no facets and a small rib an L1 with a lumbar rib: "the vertebra names the rib, not the other way round." Every thoracic vertebra in training carries the facet, so the head is learnable as the counts are.

The evidence offered is T12 against L1 at an AUC of 0.990 and 97.3 percent accuracy over 1485 vertebrae. Research directions qualifies it (row 24): five of the eleven features are divided by the patient's median, so it is not a single-vertebra classifier, and its dominant feature, the transverse-process span, is coronal and invisible on a lateral. Its n is open: 1485 is the design page's count for the 0.990, and Research directions' 1,486 from 798 cases is the planned sample, not the published run's ([the concept ledger, row 52](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-52-t12-versus-l1-vertebrae-used-for-the-auc-0990-shape-test) and [row 171](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-171-number-of-t12-and-l1-vertebrae-in-the-t12-versus-l1-shape-analysis)). At the lumbosacral junction no 3-D type classifier exists, so the extra body there is left to the sacral count.

PAR-1, a planned hour, settles the first doubt. It drops the five case-median features and runs T12 against L1 grouped five-fold by case; fits a seven-level shape model from T11 to L5 on the 643 ordinary records and applies it to the six-lumbar spines; and sets L6 against ordinary L5, 13 positives against 757 rows, leave-one-case-out with a permutation p-value. At an AUC of 0.95 or above a single-vertebra target exists; from 0.90 to 0.95 it is one body and a neighbour; below 0.90 a window of three or more. With 13 positives the L6 arm can tell no signal from some signal, nothing finer.

## Heads, losses, the patch and the five arms

The design trains five heads on one network.

| Head | Type | Loss | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| semantic | per-voxel classes | Dice and cross-entropy | the segmentation |
| `n_up` | dense ordinal | ordinal cross-entropy | count from the sacrum |
| `n_down` | dense ordinal | ordinal cross-entropy | count from the ribs |
| type | per-voxel binary | binary cross-entropy and Dice | thoracic or lumbar morphology |
| rib-bearing | per-voxel binary | binary cross-entropy and Dice | already built |

The counts are ordinal regression, which the page holds respects small ordered integers as an L2 loss does not. That an ordinal loss bought nothing in the 2-D line, cross-entropy and CORN within 0.6 points ([chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)), a record, says nothing yet about a dense count in 3-D.

The patch is the real tension. `n_up` at L1 needs the sacrum in view, about 265 mm from T11 to the S1 endplate. A tall narrow patch of 112 x 112 x 320 voxels at 1 mm spans it in 4.01 million voxels, cheaper than nnU-Net's default of 192 cubed at 7.08 million, but the pelvis needs 309 mm of width at the 95th percentile, and going anisotropic to reach it blurs the costotransverse joint. The page resolves it with a cascade: a low-resolution stage locates the sacral anchors against the whole pelvis and hands its prediction to a full-resolution stage that resolves the facet.

Arm 1 is ResEnc-L with the tall patch and the semantic heads only, the baseline for what a column-spanning field alone buys. Arm 2 adds the ordinal heads and the consistency loss; arms 1 and 2 run as a matched pair on the same folds, and their delta is the result. Arm 3 is `3d_cascade_fullres`; arm 4 is PrimusV2 on the winner, since the finding that CNNs beat Transformers came from organ segmentation, where long-range ordering is not the task, and here it is the whole task; arm 5 is the existing staged count-free pipeline. The measures keep Dice from hiding a failure: rib-free count accuracy split by typical and transitional anatomy, since "a model 97% accurate overall and 0% on transitional cases is the null result in costume"; the ROC on the disagreement scalar; stump-rib recall; and whether each error is an off-by-one traceable to a named vertebra or a silent misnumbering.

None of it has run. Research directions carries it as CT-7, ranked fourteenth of sixteen and waiting on a canal-closing fix, the pre-registration freeze and readers, its ROC set as 26 transitional-labelled records at counts four and six against 757 normal-labelled ones at four and five, with 7 transitional records at count five and 9 normal at six the cells a count alone cannot separate (row 26). CT-6 asks at how many distinct six-lumbar cases L6 Dice leaves zero, at 18, 36 and 62 on a 1,176-case dataset, scored by the rib-free count; CT-8 asks whether the costal facet separates a hypoplastic twelfth rib from a lumbar rib, on 30 read positives.

## What morphology carries on film

The plan's four facts about film come from September and are records, not final values.

L6 is named right less than half the time on real film: 44.9 percent in bank v2 and 48.3 in v5 on the naming page, whose 168 L6 training patients were all renders. The sweep reached 57.1 percent [25.0, 84.2] on renders with three vertebrae in frame, on n = 7.

The normal-L5 band does not transfer. An external report's "Wigh squaring index", normal from 1.20 to 1.45, held 5.1 percent of 3,456 ordinary laterals and is not in the literature. The genuine criterion, a superior over inferior endplate ratio of at most 1.37 (Konin 2010), fires on 99.9 percent of ordinary laterals and as a continuous index sits at chance for six-lumbar spines, an AUC of 0.54 [0.39, 0.68]. Only sacral slope separated them, at 0.73 [0.61, 0.84] on 15, and the L5 to S1 gap's 0.99 on four-lumbar spines was the annotation schema, since BUU's authors put the fifth set of corners on S1 of a sacralised spine.

The flag's learning curve is flat past forty positives: [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) ran it from 10 to 160 positive patients, 0.652 at ten, 0.704 at forty and 0.713 at 120, and read the ceiling as label quality and render fidelity: "stop harvesting and start verifying".

And the flag reads age and exposure, not anatomy. A control trained on the same renders to predict sex scored 0.829 on 3,600 real radiographs, so the renders carry information that transfers; yet on those films the flag retrieved 5 of 144 known cases in its top 144, where chance is 5.8, and its ten highest-scoring films averaged age 63 against 32 and brightness 0.36 against 0.22 for the ten lowest. On renders its AUC was 0.577 [0.48, 0.67] with 34 positives in the 802 CTs, 0.725 within VerSe with 110, and 0.510 at VerSe's thoracolumbar border with 91, a negative result, not an underpowered one. Framing beat data: only the tight lumbosacral view's interval excluded chance, at 0.656, against 0.509 for the standard lumbar film and 0.406 for the wide field showing the twelfth rib, a whole-frame 2-D finding that does not contradict the 3-D count (row 21).

## What has been measured on transitional spines, and at what n

Neither figure the plan names is a held-out number for the current model. The best rendered L6 recall, 56.1 to 72.4 percent on ten held-out persons, spans the alphabet-loss arms' 56 to 61 and 67 to 72 percent, though no source says it is their range; it is Reported, on renders, and under the lineage warning ([the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 23). The 49.9 percent [41.5, 58.9] is bank v6's interior-window figure on 128 anomalous films against 89.9 [87.1, 92.7] ordinary, a record, not a final value, whose partition the pages dispute (level-naming row 25).

On real frames with a human read the count is zero: zero verified real-frame transitional test persons against 35 needed, and seven at-risk persons with a frame, none verified, whose best case is a Wilson bound of 64.6 percent. The one real C-arm set holds three L6 persons, C-ArmLSpine case42, case121 and case136, each one labeller's read and all frozen test persons, with case139 and case44 completing the demo plan's transitional stratum ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md), [docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)); the three are to be reported "as agreement, never correctness". On 18 September [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) had eight held-out transitional spines, the one six-lumbar among them named wrongly, and quoted no figure, since eight "cannot distinguish 60% from 95%"; its cross-validation over 376 transitional patients has no outcome in any source, and how many such patients exist is open between 190 and 376 ([the concept ledger, row 55](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-55-transitional-patients-available-across-all-sources)). The State of the literature counts 51 at-risk CT test persons (L6 43, sacralised L5 or four lumbar 5, T13 3), a bound of 93.0 percent if all were right, though most sit in the encoder's training lineage.

On the real C-arm the reported stack's naming and its count from the detected sacrum disagree where a frame is wrong: that disagreement, the stop signal, caught 5 of 6 erring frames on C-ArmLSpine ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), no interval given), and zero-shot AP naming at 276 of 631, 43.7 percent, one level cranial, which the ledger marks current ([the concept ledger, row 107](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)), is the whole-column shift a count should catch. Neither is a transitional figure; both show the count and the name failing differently, which a design built on their disagreement needs.

The comparators come from the [feasibility review](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/) and Research directions. Kwak 2025, the only 2-D transitional model, reached 76.4 percent and an AUC of 0.84 on 313 held-out lateral films at 62.3 percent prevalence, below an orthopaedic specialist at 83.9. Readers type Castellvi on radiographs at 32 percent sensitivity against 76 on CT (Hanhivaara 2024); 32 of 91 AP-radiograph subtypes disagree with CT (Hou 2020); level nomination on outpatient lumbar radiographs or MRI was wrong for 38 of 71 transitional patients (Nakagawa 2017); and Farshad 2013's lateral rule reached 94 percent sensitivity and 74 specificity on fused types only. The programme holds 147 real positives against Kwak's 3,116 films (the State of the literature), and Research directions draws the test: a single-lateral flag that beats these on an unenriched set has found something or has leaked.

## Abstention by position, never by name

The overnight page of 16 September found a telling defect. Its safety rule required L5 or L6 in frame, and four sacralised Castellvi IIIb patients have no L5 at all, so the rule abstained on exactly the patients it protects; it now asks for the lowest mobile lumbar segment by position, never by name. The reason generalises: a name presupposes the count a transitional spine breaks, so a precondition written by name makes abstention depend on the answer it guards. Every abstention rule here should name what is in the pixels (a sacrum, a rib-bearing body, a mobile segment), never the level it would output. IIIb is visible, too: Hou 2020 typed it correctly on every AP radiograph of its series, no n given for the cell, so the failure was the rule's, not the image's.

Several sources say what to abstain on. The feasibility review promises from a single frame only a binary flag that triggers a whole-spine count or a CT check, abstaining when the sacrum, the odontoid or the twelfth rib is outside the field or a numerical variant is suspected. The reality check writes two abstain states into its target, "rib-bearing border, ambiguous" and "count-changing variant suspected", and reports the morphological name, the last mobile vertebra, beside the enumerated one with their disagreement flagged, since surgeons act on the first and MRI reports carry the second. The counting prior of [docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md) shows "?" when the four-, five- and six-lumbar hypotheses lie close, takes its odds from readers' CT calls on collections screened whole and sets its margin on validation frames only ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)).

Castellvi type I is the case abstention has to absorb: count-preserving, a coronal transverse-process height of 19 mm or more, unrecoverable from a lateral by construction, and inside every negative set the programme uses, so the overnight page proposes an explicit abstain class for it and the standing determination targets types II to IV. Its share, "about two thirds" on the overnight page and 68 percent in Research directions, is unsourced at both (row 36); the reality check cites a study of 3,855 abdominal CTs, which the State of the literature names as Hanhivaara 2020, in which 68 percent of transitional cases were type I. That is very likely the origin, though this chapter could not check it against the paper.

## The standing determination, the gates and the stop rules

The standing determination of 23 September is narrower and stronger than "anomaly-aware": name the level correctly on a transitional spine (L6, T13, sacralised L5, four lumbar) from C-arm exposures, at parity with an ordinary spine, and report "not at parity" plainly if that is the result. The record expects the first clean model to fail its validation screen, itself a result.

The design under it, set out on 8 October, names per vertebra from detected boxes in a limited field, with the count from the detected sacrum as the second mechanism and their disagreement as the stop signal, scored as exact level on a transitional spine, person-clustered. The G3 split puts transitional persons 50/10/40 with every real transitional subject in test; the clean encoder starts from ImageNet on G3 training persons; renders stay in training as the only labelled L6 and T13, variants in 25 to 35 percent of rendered CTs by the choice of CTs, never by duplication, with no decoder pseudolabels; and renders and films mix in every batch, since pretrain-then-fine-tune lost 25.6 anomalous points. The version 1 plan keeps the transitional stratum in its own rows and the three L6 test persons in the witness set ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)), and the demo plan adds a synthetic arm with exact truth, never pooled with the real series ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)).

### The stop rules

FLU-2 decides whether the fluoroscopy arm runs at all. A ResNet-18 two-channel encoder trained as the design says is scored on the 7 render-scoreable of the 11 G3 validation L6 persons. Zero or one miss proceeds to three seeds and two heads; two or more stops the arm, and the manuscript says "not at parity" with the number. At a true recall of 72.4 percent the screen passes 38 percent of the time and at 85 percent 72 percent, which the recomputation reproduces (38.3 and 71.7). A second gate asks the count to catch at least 80 percent of run-shifted films. No test person is scored until paper 2's ledger carries "H1 seen", and if the read confirms fewer than 22 carriers with an exported frame, no fluoroscopy transitional sensitivity is claimed and the render number is reported alone.

On the 3-D side, PAR-2 projects every label at exact pose on the 802 v11 volumes, counts a feature as represented in a view at an ICC of 0.75 or above and partly from 0.50, and calls the junction at parity for a view at a T12 against L1 AUC of 0.97 or above, partial from 0.90; if lateral and AP together stay under 0.90 on exact-pose renders, no 2-D model on real frames will, and the claim is withdrawn before any network is trained. That is parity between 3-D and 2-D features, not between transitional and ordinary spines. CT-3, the whole-cohort Castellvi read, asks a II to IV rate inside 9 to 18 percent, type I near 68 percent of calls and a second-reader kappa of at least 0.63; CT-4 asks a precision lower bound of 0.90 on lumbosacral L6, at most 3 errors on 88.

### What the arms wait on

The order is measurement, then human reads, then models: PAR-1 and PAR-2's pilot run in week one beside the reads. A one-hour job projects the sacrum and L5 labels of ten COBRA planning CTs onto their own projections, to see whether COBRA can serve the sacrum anchor; a 63-volume reader queue (57 field-supported COBRA calls, 3 re-read candidates, 3 C-ArmLSpine CBCTs) is then read as confirmed on CT and scorable on a real frame, since only 7 of 91 COBRA calls have an exported frame. The expected yield is about 5 L6 at the labeller's precision of 10 of 24, at most 8 four-lumbar and almost no T13, at most 19 verifiable, so 22 carriers cannot come from COBRA alone; the 11 field-supported COBRA L6 calls are "the single most decisive afternoon in the holding", since they can retire the real-frame transitional claim before any model is built. FLU-2 is read on the validation side in weeks two to six beside the rest of PAR-2 and CT-3; CT-4, CT-6 and PAR-5 follow CT-1 and the re-reads, with CT-7 among the last. The programme of 21 October feeds readers' CT calls to the counting prior's odds and extends the synthetic arm with spines edited in 3-D, always scored apart ([docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md)). ## What we know

A level name is the wrong target on a transitional spine: every arm that made the rare level a class named it at most about half the time on real windows, leaning to the ordinary name, and only real examples and more context moved the figure. The replacement on record is a design, not a result: two ordinal counts from the sacrum and the lowest rib-bearing body, a type head that repairs the rib anchor when the rib is missing, and the counts' disagreement as the flag, learned from ordinary anatomy. Four morphological anchors fail to different variants, so the rib and sacral signatures tell a lumbar rib from a sacralisation where the sum cannot. Ribs must be assigned by articulation and judged on paired, uncut levels; sacral foramina fail; a count over labelled bodies reproduces the labels. On film the records say L6 is named less than half the time, the geometric cues do not transfer, and a whole-frame flag plateaus by forty positives and reads age and exposure. Abstention is written by position. The gates cannot be met today: zero verified real-frame transitional persons against 35, zero real carriers against 22.

## What we do not know

We do not know whether the two-anchor design works: CT-7 has not run, and no source read says whether arms 1 and 2 ran in spine-program. We do not know whether the 0.990 survives as a single-vertebra figure, nor the published run's exact n; whether the sacroiliac articulation is fixed under fusion and ilium contact is Castellvi II to IV by definition; or whether the audit's sacral tests were ever computed. The variant counts on the current release are open: L6 at 18, 16 or 29 by definition, lumbar ribs at 13 or 16, and the non-normal, Castellvi and flag-positive sets each by its own definition. Type I's 68 percent has no citation where it is stated, only a likely origin in Hanhivaara 2020. The 376-patient cross-validation has no outcome, and the number of transitional patients is unsettled. Above all, no transitional figure exists for a model whose lineage excludes its test persons, and nothing measures the count and the name agreeing or disagreeing on a real transitional frame.

## What would change our mind

A CT-7 delta near zero between arms 1 and 2 would mean the formulation buys nothing over a column-spanning field, leaving the anchors only the flag; an ROC near chance on the 26 against 757 records would take the flag too. PAR-1 below 0.90 would make the type head a window method, and PAR-2 below 0.90 on lateral and AP together would withdraw the 2-D claim before any network is trained. The ilium-contact test agreeing with, or failing on, the labelled sacralisations would settle whether anchors C and D can come from masks that exist. A COBRA read yielding 22 or more verified carriers with exported frames would make the 85 percent claim reachable; fewer leaves the render number alone. Two or more FLU-2 misses of seven would stop the fluoroscopy arm and print "not at parity". A source giving type I another share, or a CT-3 read far from 68 percent, would change what the abstain class must hold. And a film flag whose top scores tracked anatomy rather than age and brightness would reopen the whole-frame route.
