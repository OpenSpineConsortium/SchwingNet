# 09 Splits, leakage and licences

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter states the project's rules for splits, leaks and licences and what each check found, names no stage's mechanism (Part III holds that), and stays in this private repository, as everything does until the provisional is filed on 30 October 2026.

A number is worth quoting only when the persons behind it were never seen by the model or by any model it grew from, and a dataset is worth building on only under terms someone has read and written down. This chapter settles, for whoever plans the next run, how a split is made and audited and which tools refuse a leaked list; which numbers the two historical leaks made into records, and how a leaked lineage is repaired; where a person's identity is still in doubt; what each licence says as the records state it, and which readings are settled; what may never leave the grid; and where the data requests stand. It reads the artifacts against the repository's split files, catalog, flow and licence classifier as they stand on 24 October 2026. The sets themselves are [chapter 4](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md) to [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md), the decisions are logged in [chapter 24](24-decision-log-data-licences-and-splits.md), and who may act on what is [chapter 23](23-who-may-do-what.md).

## The hold-out rule and what counts as one person

The rule every later check serves is the owner's, of 23 September 2026, as the [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) records it: an evaluation item is held out for a checkpoint only if its patient key is in neither the training set nor the validation set of that checkpoint, nor of any ancestor it was initialised from. A derived image, whether a render, a styled frame or a crop, inherits the person of its source. Only declared public ImageNet and COCO weights are exempt as ancestors. Unknown is not clean: an unkeyed item, an unresolved lineage link or a check that did not run counts as a failure. An item is one acquisition rather than one file; splits are enforced on the patient key and duplicates on the item key; validation counts as seen, since choosing a checkpoint on a person fits to them; and identity comes from metadata first and from the file name last.

The rule is only as strong as its keys, and the handoff says so: three kinds were still unverified on 8 October, the person counts behind AASCE's index groups, CSXA's assumption of one image per patient, and the number of VinDr studies per patient. The [retraining plan](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md) and the files under [data/registry/](../../data/registry/) give each source an identity level: patient for the plain films and for COBRA; cadaver for DeepFluoro's six specimens; source volume for renders; and study for VinDr-SpineXR at worst, since its release keys only images, series and studies. C-ArmLSpine keys one case as one patient on the archive's own statement, and its two demo sweeps take the same key as their case's spot frames, so a sweep and its case cannot be split apart.

Whether the archive's statement holds is the first open question here. The [concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md) counts 149 C-ArmLSpine cases, [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) counts 120 distinct subjects by the split's own keying, and [the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md) (row 2) carry "149 cases, 120 distinct subjects by the split keying, person count unverified", to be settled by the dataset's subject keys or its ethics record. Every case-level audit of C-ArmLSpine below inherits that question.

## How a split is made

### The G3 split

The programme's working split since 23 September is G3, applied that day with a leak audit that passed. It is person-disjoint and stratified: ordinary persons go 80, 10 and 10 percent to training, validation and test, transitional persons 50, 10 and 40 percent, and every real transitional subject goes to test ([Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)). The handoff lists it as frozen and never rerun in place; Research Directions adds that if the second reads of transitional cases move a record, the split changes and the leak audit runs again before anyone is scored. In the [Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/), AASCE is a thoracic test set only and Spinal-AI2024's 20,000 synthetic films of 44 identities sit wholly on training in G3 ([the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 33), so neither set's repeated identities can cross the split.

### The catalog's split

SchwingNet's catalog, which replaces the hand-built banks for version 1, splits by a rule that extends without a reshuffle: [data/splits/rule.json](../../data/splits/rule.json) sends each person to validation or training by a salted hash of their key, a tenth to validation, and a person on [data/splits/test-persons.json](../../data/splits/test-persons.json) is test whatever the hash says. The frozen list names five persons, all of C-ArmLSpine: the three real L6 cases, case42, case121 and case136, the transitional stratum of the test set, and the two demo sweeps, case134 and case39, which are never trained on. A person added later lands on the same side whoever ingests them, and the frozen list grows only by an explicit pull request.

The retraining plan names three consequences. Resuming from a checkpoint makes every later model's ancestry the whole shard history, so the test list may grow only by a person no training shard has ever held, anything else forcing a rebase, and a re-keyed person cannot cross the hash boundary once a checkpoint has seen them. The hash does not stratify, so a rare class's persons, or all six DeepFluoro cadavers, can fall to one side, and a class with an empty validation side is assigned by hand, recorded, before its first training run. And validation grows with each shard, so only the test rows of the evaluation table compare across versions ([chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)).

The two splits are different decisions, and nothing in the repository maps one onto the other: G3 sends a tenth of the ordinary persons and two fifths of the transitional ones to test, the frozen list names five C-ArmLSpine persons. No registry entry holds a shard yet, all 24 files list none, so nothing is lost; but a G3 test person registered before the frozen list names them falls where the hash puts them, nine times in ten into training, and cannot then join the test list without a rebase. Keeping G3's test persons held out means extending the frozen list by pull request before their shards are cut. For the planned in-house fluoroscopy, the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/) sets the same principle from the start: cases split by patient, never by frame, and a held-out set drawn and sealed before any training ([chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md)).

## How a split is audited, and what refuses a leaked list

### Nine checks

The audit behind G3 is nine checks, numbered as the programme's leakage note numbers them; the handoff gives each and whether it ran on the G3 split:

| Check | What it tests | On the G3 split |
|---|---|---|
| CHK-1 | item disjointness: no acquisition on two sides | run |
| CHK-2 | alias closure: one item under two names counted once (the NHANES failure) | run |
| CHK-3 | patient closure, derived items included: a person and all their renders on one side | run |
| CHK-4 | derivation closure, parent and child on one side: 8,082 training renders sit under the poses of validation, test or held real subjects, 2,720 of them under test poses | run, as a warning |
| CHK-5 | a held-out device or corpus is held out whole | run |
| CHK-6 | near-duplicates across splits, by perceptual hash and correlation | not run |
| CHK-7 | undeclared aliases, by store key or pixel key | not run |
| CHK-8 | orphans and coverage: 17 unkeyed AASCE coronal films | run, as a warning |
| CHK-9 | every quoted number checked against selection and lineage, 563 rows | run |

The two that never ran, the content-hash chain, are the checks that read images rather than keys. On 8 October the evaluation-gate tests passed 58, the patient-key and statistics tests passed, and the leak-check suite failed on one stale Geneva fixture. The two warnings stand, and CHK-8's 17 unkeyed films count, under the rule, against any number that could include them.

### The tools that refuse

Each generation of the work has a tool that refuses a leaked list. Before SchwingNet, the list builder of the fleet-wide detector, as Counting From The Sacrum describes it, refuses to write anything if a subject or a specimen lands on both sides of the split, and the patient-key module the handoff names refuses names it cannot key. In SchwingNet the refusals move to the door. The ingest ([docs/research/data-catalog.md](../../docs/research/data-catalog.md)) refuses the same bytes under two persons, leaving to a person which one they are; it refuses a training or review shard that holds a frozen test person, duplicates included, and a test shard that holds anyone else; and it lists the frozen persons a bank never showed, so a case spelt otherwise than the frozen list spells it is seen before a training shard is cut. The smoke test of 21 October on synthetic banks recorded a test bank ingested as a training shard being refused, its three persons named.

The catalog then applies the REFORMS split check, the split rule of the reporting checklist of Kapoor 2023: no person in two parts, no frame's bytes in two parts, every test person frozen, and every frozen person present held out, counted per source at its identity level. A version that fails exits 1 and no training step reads it. The flow repeats the person and frame test before training, and its run record refuses a split that is not disjoint or a tree with uncommitted changes, so the check is a gate and not advice ([tools/catalog.py](../../tools/catalog.py), [tools/flow.py](../../tools/flow.py), [src/schwingnet/flow.py](../../src/schwingnet/flow.py)).

### The pixel checks, run only on synthetic data

CHK-6 and CHK-7 now exist as code (issue #9): rules in [src/schwingnet/leaks.py](../../src/schwingnet/leaks.py), a reader and writer in [tools/leaks.py](../../tools/leaks.py), and a grid array, [grid/run-leaks.sh](../../grid/run-leaks.sh), run after the X-ray chain's third stage ([docs/memory/dicom-banks-and-leak-checks.md](../../docs/memory/dicom-banks-and-leak-checks.md)). CHK-6 confirms a near-duplicate across the split at a correlation of at least 0.97 on a reduced copy; CHK-7 fails frames that share bytes or pixels across persons or sources nobody declared to be one; and a frame no key was taken from, a shard with no fingerprint or a search past its budget fails the run. [grid/README.md](../../grid/README.md) records the synthetic test of 21 October in a cloud container: copies resized, brightened, changed in contrast or gamma, or saved as JPEG at quality 75 were caught; a 5 percent crop fell to a correlation of 0.61 to 0.84 and was not, as the definition leaves it; and two synthetic shards with two planted copies failed on exactly those two rows.

Three limits follow. The pixel checks have never run on the real corpus as far as the repository shows: it holds no leak verdict from a real shard, and no registry entry holds a shard to check. A crop passes CHK-6 by definition, so crops are caught only by keys, CHK-3's work, which depends on every crop carrying its source's person. And the verdict binds nothing yet: the [SchwingNet grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/) page says it holds training back by instruction only, and the code agrees, since the flow reads the split check before training and no training tool reads the leak report. Until one does, a failing row stops a run only if someone reads it.

### The C-arm audit and its scope

The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 22 September audited the C-arm banks from disk. Its splits are disjoint by case: C-ArmLSpine 105, 15 and 28 cases, 148 in all, as in the 296-frame bank from before the repair that added case134; COBRA 110, 8 and 27; DeepFluoro four, one and one specimens. It found no case in two splits, no crop outside its case's split across 4,620 crops and 5,212 multi-set images, no fixed-test patient in any fine-tune bank, and the five-fold rule verified fold by fold. Its near-duplicate search at 0.97 found none across C-ArmLSpine's splits (highest 0.962) or DeepFluoro's (0.934), 74,364 cross-case pairs in COBRA, which it explains as one gantry angle's layout in different patients, and no pair between a real test frame and 37,165 film images, 29,484 FluoroCap panels or 1,445 images of another bank. Its claim that FluoroCap's article list holds no DeepFluoro, C-ArmLSpine, COBRA or Geneva paper shows no query, and the verification contradictions (row 76) want one committed before it is cited.

The audit's unit is the case, so while C-ArmLSpine's person count is open its case-disjoint split is not known to be person-disjoint. Its zero also covers only these banks: the verification contradictions (rows 40 and 41) set it beside the NHANES leak in the older level banks and the transitional persons inside the current naming lineage, and warn against reading it as no leakage for that lineage.

### Unknown is not clean, in practice

The clause on unknowns comes from experience. [Ninety-Three Pixels](../../artifacts/ninety-three-pixels/) records a silent hold-out failure: freeing 260 GB deleted five crop banks that about 90 scripts named as their held-out bank, an unreadable bank left the list of trained-on cases empty, and evaluation ran over cases the model was fitted on without raising, after which the hold-out tool was made to raise. The [Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) says stopping on an empty, missing, ambiguous or defaulted input would have prevented 16 of the 29 incidents tallied on 27 September, an empty held-out set that flattered every result among them. The verification contradictions (row 69) ask that held-out numbers produced between the deletion and the fix be flagged for re-checking; no list of them exists.

## The two historical leaks and the numbers they made into records

Both leaks were one failure, a set entering the banks under two names split independently, which CHK-2 now tests. NHANES II entered twice, so 48 of 54 validation persons of the older banks were also in training, found on 22 September. CTSpinoPelvic1K entered twice, as its own renders and as the `anchor_drr` bank, putting 301 of 801 persons on both sides. The handoff draws the consequence: allspine v2, v6 and v7, `anchor_drr` and `vindrps`, the last with a 77.8 percent validation leak, are leaked families and every number from them is record only; the fix was G3 with its passing audit and the patient-key module. The retraining plan traces the leaks to banks rebuilt by hand: NHANES twice, FluoroCap panels, which carry no patient key, and VinDr split per image.

The list of families may be shorter than the list of banks the double entry reached. The corpus table of [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) of 17 September, whose rows sum to its v5 bank's 253,763 crops, carries two rows of 543 NHANES films each, `nhanes` and `nhanes_drr`, the second one of the two names under which the handoff says NHANES entered; and it holds 584 Mendeley films beside BUU's 3,603 laterals, though the atlas finds about 500 of Mendeley's films to be BUU persons. The handoff's list does not name v5, and no record says whether those rows were split together, so under the rule a number on that bank is not held out until CHK-2 has run on it.

The headline numbers of the leaked families stay in the book only as records. The shortcut-free film number of allspine v2 was 99.02 percent ([handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)), a record, not a final value. The selective-accuracy claim of 16 September was right on 99 percent of the vertebrae it named while naming 35.7 percent of them, on renders ([Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/)), a record, not a final value; Research Directions says it needs a re-run, and [the FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md) (row 10) require any such claim to be rerun on G3 and pass the evaluation gate before it is quoted. Any score on `anchor_drr`'s validation side is record only by the same ruling (row 42). The handoff forbade quoting them at all, naming a clean replacement beside each; the owner's answer of 23 October lets this book quote them as records only.

The leaks reach further than the leaked banks, through lineage. Research Directions finds 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons inside the v8 and v9 training splits, so any transitional number on the current lineage is not held out, and no held-out render number for a v9-lineage model is quoted except through the list of unseen cases. It also asks whether a comparison of 17 September, on which a design default rests, was ever re-run on a clean bank, since it ran on a leaked one. The fold banks behind the five-fold C-ArmLSpine naming numbers were built from allspine v7 and still hold both NHANES copies and 2,100 FluoroCap crops per fold bank; the crops carry no patient key, and under the rule an unkeyed item counts as a failure, so those banks cannot pass the orphan check, CHK-8, however clean the near-duplicate search came out. The handoff names cells free of those crops, the 250 cells of its Figure 5b, every one passing the evaluation gate with held out true.

One film number shows how far a leak's doubt spreads. The [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) of 15 September called its film validation, 98.7 percent single-crop on 8,422 crops from 1,653 held-out patients, 982 of the crops from NHANES, the one sound held-out number. Whether that set is person-disjoint was never re-verified after the NHANES finding a week later; the FluoroCap contradictions (row 37) mark it cannot-tell and ask for CHK-2 and CHK-3 on its bank, so the record has not settled it and it is no current value.

Some numbers are clean because of what was never trained on: Research Directions holds the v9 zero-shot numbers on C-ArmLSpine clean, and the handoff's inventory shows why, since the v9 level bank holds no real C-arm frame. VinDr-SpineXR, absent from that bank too, is foreclosed as a held-out benchmark another way: its release test split was consumed in building the programme's labels ([Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/)). The atlas also records that every C-arm bank was built from the 296-frame bank before its repair, without case134, the second lateral of case39 and a view correction of case118, and that `carm_drr` and the `carm_cv` family must never be pooled, because up to 50 people sit on different sides of their splits.

## How a leaked initialisation is fixed

The handoff's ninth rule is short: a leaked initialisation is fixed by retraining, never disclosed as a caveat. Its fifth says what the retraining must achieve: held out means never seen by the checkpoint or any ancestor, every quoted score goes through the evaluation gate, and nothing is quoted from the leaked banks. The record carries two applications. The clean-lineage arm of Research Directions starts again from declared public weights, the one kind of ancestor the rule exempts, and trains on G3 training persons only, since the current lineage has seen the transitional held-out persons; its first held-out render number would be the first quotable one, or the sentence that says the arm is not at parity. SchwingNet's version 1 goes further: one run of each stage as a rebase on the registry as it stands, with nothing of the old fold banks reused, since every shard is made again from its source.

Later versions that resume from version 1 inherit the catalog's rule on ancestry, and a leak found later is fixed by another rebase, not by a footnote. The handoff applies the same discipline to memory: a fresh project loads the standing context, not the archived memory index, whose one-line summaries carry leaked numbers. How each stage is retrained is [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md)'s and [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md)'s.

## Aliases and the id-is-a-person audit

Every leak above was an alias, one person under two keys, and the records name more aliases than the checks have closed. The most serious is still a question: CTSpinoPelvic1K is built from CT colonography, which scans each patient prone and supine; if two release ids are one human being the corpus is mis-split, and Counting From The Sacrum says that would be larger than either leak already found and fixed. An audit was running on 22 September, not assumed to be fine, and its outcome is not in the records. The holding report gives 802 records, 422 supine, 377 prone and 3 decubitus, against 801 distinct subjects by the split keying (verification contradictions, row 19). The two rows of [the detectors contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md) that meet it disagree, one carrying Research Directions' one record per patient (row 37), the other calling that the open point (row 53); this chapter takes the second, since the audit that would show it has not reported.

The other aliases are recorded facts waiting for their keys, as Research Directions, the holding report and the atlas give them. CTSpine1K's 782 colonography volumes are CTSpinoPelvic1K persons. TotalSegmentator's ids s1406 to s1429 in v2.0.1 are byte-identical to different ids in v3.0.0, and 1,118 v3 CTs are byte-identical to v2; the holding keys the set by release id and accepts up to 24 merged or split people until an alias map is built ([Imaging data holding: manifest, migration and index](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/)). Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 redistributes about 500 BUU persons and 12 VinDr studies; the registry's line that it adds no new person conflicts with the atlas's 71 films that duplicate neither, and [the datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) (row 22) hold neither reading without a recount, as the ledger holds its film count, 714 or 716, open (row 20). DeepFluoro's vendor DICOM carries a wrong PatientID, so the set is keyed by specimen across its three copies. COBRA's 5,013 duplicates put a person under two keys, one person only once the key chain has joined them, so no shard is registered before its chain run is green. Spinal-AI2024's 44 identities include one appearing 1,270 times, harmless only while the set sits wholly on training. CSXA's 4,963 films are all in the v9 level bank, 4,214 in training and 749 in validation, on the release's untested claim of one image per patient. And the CT acquisition tiers merged 127 exact folds but left 1,186 near-duplicates unmerged.

Against these, [data/splits/aliases.json](../../data/splits/aliases.json) declares no alias. That is correct for a registry with no shards, and it fixes the order of the next work: since CHK-7 fails undeclared aliases, the first real run over TotalSegmentator, over CTSpine1K beside CTSpinoPelvic1K, or over the DeepFluoro copies will fail until the aliases are declared or the keys merged, which is the check doing its job.

## FluoroCap: one figure, one group

FluoroCap, the literature corpus of published spine panels described in [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md), leaks differently because it holds figures, not persons. The [FluoroCap Pipeline Map](../../artifacts/fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs/) gives the rule: the same figure arrives by three routes, so panels are grouped as near-duplicates and split by group, and no copy of a figure sits on both sides. Duplication is common: the Reality Check counts 11,446 images in 10,233 near-duplicate groups and 243 duplicate groups among decoded panels, and the atlas and registry agree that 72.8 percent of panels are reproductions with no pixel residual. The release is frozen with checksums and a lock record before any claim, and the weights registry refuses public destinations.

Grouping settles duplicates and leaves two leaks a group cannot see. The labels are captions, and text is printed in the pixels: of 2,815 panels scanned, 67.5 percent carry printed text and 3.6 percent a printed level label, which the Reality Check calls a leak a network will learn. And a panel carries no patient key, so nothing closes it against corpora a paper may reproduce; the OR ledger found no pair at 0.97 between real test frames and 29,484 panels, but a crop passes that search by definition, and the article-list claim waits for its query. The Reality Check concludes that the mined figures are a test set, not a training set, and an external test set only after a two-reader read of a stratified sample. The [FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/) keeps reader labels pending for every panel, and what FluoroCap's evidence does for the method is [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md)'s.

## The licences as the records state them

### The classifier and its classes

Since 23 October every dataset's licence is a class in [data/licences.json](../../data/licences.json), proposed by [tools/licences.py](../../tools/licences.py) and rendered as [data/LICENCES.md](../../data/LICENCES.md). Each entry copies its record's licence line word for word, so a changed line is caught, and the rule proposes the strictest class any phrase supports. Only the owner's reading may make a class more permissive than the rule's; counsel holds two questions, whether a trained network is a derivative under share-alike or no-derivatives terms, and whether an agreement allows any use beyond the project's. The classes say what the words say, never what a court would, and "open access" or a bare "open" says the files can be read, not what may be done with them. The table gives the classes and the 113 entries as they stand on 24 October; four of the open ones were reviewed by the owner on 23 October, Aariz, C-ArmLSpine, its sweeps and KUB-StoneX.

| Class | What the licence's words allow | Entries |
|---|---|---|
| open | commercial use and derivatives, at most attribution | 23, 4 reviewed by the owner |
| share-alike | commercial use, derivatives under the same licence | 1 |
| non-commercial | no commercial use | 15 |
| agreement | the terms of a signed agreement, a credentialed licence, a request form or competition rules; never commercial | 25 |
| no-derivatives | no adapted material shared | 3 |
| unknown | not recorded, to confirm, or read two ways | 46 |

### Which readings are settled

A reading is settled when the words admit one class, when the owner has reviewed it, or when a determination names the set. It is open in three ways. The owner's own reading may be pending, as BUU's is in so many words and NHANES's and CSXA's are by the classifier's unknown. The records may conflict, as the holding report finds for six sources: CTPelvic1K (CC BY or non-commercial), VerSe, DeepFluoro, the Geneva hip set (CC BY-SA or CC BY-NC) and Ljubljana each have two readings, and SpineSAC states none; the index records both readings and marks redistribution unknown until each is settled against its release page. Or the question may be counsel's: the two derivative questions, and the commercial use of NHANES and of COBRA. The board of 11 September, repeated on the decisions page, counted CSXA, the Mendeley films independent of BUU, and NHANES if counsel agrees as clean for commercial use; the classifier now proposes unknown for CSXA and NHANES, and only the owner's reading may loosen them.

## The research track and the commercial track

The Lab Recipe states the rule in one line: students, the grid and non-commercial data are the research track, and nothing moves from it to a commercial track. [Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/) records its first enforcement in the earlier code base: a licensing module made the exporter unable to select a forbidden source, because nothing in a checkpoint records what it saw, so a README is not a control; Spinal-AI2024, which has no licence file, was tagged research-track so the gate keeps it out of any commercial lineage; and VinDr-SpineXR is research-only by its PhysioNet licence, which any weights that touch it inherit, permanently.

SchwingNet's classifier carries the rule into every run. Four recipes under [data/recipes/](../../data/recipes/) name the classes each takes: a network meant to be sold or licensed, from open sources the owner has confirmed; one that may carry share-alike data if counsel holds the weights may be offered under the same licence; an academic non-commercial one; and the study's own network, kept inside the project under each agreement's terms, whose weights are shared with no one until each agreement and no-derivatives licence is read for it. A catalog is cut to a recipe, and every run writes PROVENANCE.json beside its record, giving each source's licence, class and counts per part and the run's own class, the strictest among the sources it trained or selected on. The flow refuses a catalog with a source no licence entry covers, or one outside the run's recipe. The boundary between the tracks as an institution draws it is [chapter 23](23-who-may-do-what.md)'s.

## Who signs, who reads, and what never leaves the grid

Who signs is settled without exception. An account, a click-through or signed agreement, a credentialing attestation or a fee is the owner's own hand, always, because an agreement binds the person who accepts it ([docs/research/data-access-requirements.md](../../docs/research/data-access-requirements.md)); the owner signs any data use agreement, and request letters go from the owner's university mail on his word ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)). An agreement between institutions is the institution's as well: Bournemouth's UK data-sharing agreement, when it comes, is signed by the university's institutional official. A session drafts each letter and the owner approves each before it is sent, since sent mail cannot be recalled; and a plain download is a grid job that runs only on the owner's word and number.

Who reads a licence follows from the classifier: the rule proposes from the words, only the owner's reading may loosen a class, counsel takes the derivative questions and an agreement's reach, and the holding report leaves settling the six conflicting sources to the owner alone; the owner's reading of BUU is the one the records name as pending.

What may never leave the grid is built from several rules. The images stay: nothing of the corpora is copied into the repository ([docs/project.md](../../docs/project.md)), the catalog's steps read a version by path and pass its hash ([docs/memory/the-data-catalog.md](../../docs/memory/the-data-catalog.md)), and only aggregate counts of the sample index leave ([docs/memory/sample-index.md](../../docs/memory/sample-index.md)). Protected health information stays, the person key being a study-local pseudonym, and case134's DICOM headers, which carry a real patient identifier, are never read into a key. VinDr-SpineXR, BUU-LSPINE and every bank holding their pixels are copied owner-only from the first byte, approved students read the rest, and a student who needs those two applies for their own credentialed access. No weight trained on BUU leaves until the owner has read its licence. The private-forever list, the level-identification code, FluoroCap, model weights, the CT segmentation code base and XRSpinoPelvic1K, is never in a public repository or on the website; CTSpinoPelvic1K's volumes and FluoroCap's figures are never redistributed; and credentials are read from files, never echoed, never passed in a job's command and never committed.

## What we know

The hold-out rule is settled: a person is held out only if no checkpoint in the lineage saw them in training or validation, derived images inherit their source's person, only declared public weights are exempt, and unknown counts as a failure. G3 is person-disjoint, applied on 23 September with a passing audit and never rerun in place. The catalog's split is a salted hash with five frozen C-ArmLSpine test persons, and the ingest, the split check and the flow each refuse a leaked list. The two historical leaks were aliases, NHANES II and CTSpinoPelvic1K each entering twice, and they make every number of allspine v2, v6 and v7, `anchor_drr` and `vindrps` a record. A leaked initialisation is fixed by retraining, and version 1 rebuilds every shard. FluoroCap is split by duplicate group, and its printed labels make it a test set. Every licence has a class only the owner may loosen, counsel holds the derivative questions, the owner signs every agreement, and the images and protected health information stay on the grid, VinDr-SpineXR's and BUU-LSPINE's pixels owner-only.

## What we do not know

The record has not settled whether C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases are 149 persons or the 120 the split keying finds, so no case-level audit of it is yet a person-level one, nor whether two of CTSpinoPelvic1K's 802 records are one person, since that audit reported no outcome. CHK-6 and CHK-7 have never run on a real shard. Whether the 8,422-crop film validation is person-disjoint is cannot-tell; whether the v5 bank's NHANES rows and its Mendeley and BUU films were split together is not recorded; and the held-out numbers produced between the 260 GB deletion and its fix are not listed. The six licence conflicts were not settled against their release pages, which this session could not reach; the VinDr tier wording waits on a fresh read; the owner's readings of BUU, NHANES and CSXA are not recorded; and counsel's questions are unanswered. Which agreements the owner signed rests on the owner's word of 14 October and the dated states of 21 and 22 October; the plan's wish to confirm each with the owner was not met here. Nothing yet decides when G3's test persons join the frozen list, or when the flow will read the leak verdict.

## What would change our mind

A person-key table mapping C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases to fewer persons would make the OR ledger's case-level zero a person-level question and force folds by person; one mapping them to 149 would close it. Paired ids in the CTSpinoPelvic1K audit would re-split G3 and every number keyed on it, the case Counting From The Sacrum called larger than either leak; none would let one record per patient stand. Failing rows in a first real run of CHK-6 and CHK-7 would show that the key checks missed copies. CHK-2 and CHK-3 on the films-only bank would settle the 98.7 percent either way, and CHK-2 on the v5 bank would say whether its numbers join the leaked families. A release page read for each conflicting source, the owner's reading of BUU, NHANES and CSXA, or counsel's answer on derivatives would move classes; and a flow that reads the leak verdict before training would close the gap that instruction now covers.
