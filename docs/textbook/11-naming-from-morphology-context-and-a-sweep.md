# 11 Naming from morphology, context and a sweep

Label: confidential-method. Results may be shown, mechanism may not, until the provisional is filed; this chapter stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere.

This chapter states in full the line of work that names a vertebra by what it looks like rather than by counting to it: the crop encoder and its recurrent head, the context they need, what they do with no anchor in frame, and the sweep-and-match chain of 18 September that carries names from a landmark to a spot film that shows none. For a planner it settles why the headline moved from 84 to 97 percent, that the anchor-free stack is not underdetermined on an ordinary spine and what one exposure costs on an anomalous one, what each of the chain's six steps contributes and which of its figures hold, and where the line fails. Counting is [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md), transitional spines [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md), detectors and the version 1 plan [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md), renders [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) and the choice between the two lines [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md).

## The naming line

A detector returns an ordered stack of vertebral-body boxes. A ResNet-18 encoder reads a crop of each body at a fixed anatomical scale, 96 px per body on a 320 px canvas, with a target-marker channel so that it cannot read size as identity. A bidirectional GRU reads runs of 2 to 9 vertebrae, trained on windows of mixed length so that a film's span is not a cue. The output is a posterior over a 27-class alphabet, C1 to C7, T1 to T12, T13, L1 to L6 and the sacrum, where T13 and L6 are variant classes an ordinary spine skips. The name is the argmax, the system abstains when the top posterior falls below a threshold tau, and a sequence decode can read a run against the variant alphabets: ordinary, L6, T13, both, and four lumbar ([Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)).

The thesis of 18 September was that nothing counts: the landmark fixes the chain and each bone is named by its appearance. [The level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md) read the record as successive designs (row 13): naming from morphology on 18 September, then counting from the detected anchor ([Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/), 22 September), with the handoff's combined model as the current state: a body detector, `detector_v5z_final`, for the naming boxes; a vertebra and sacrum detector, `rx_lodo_carmlspine_1024`, for the anchor; the `allspine_v9` encoder and its BiGRU head; fold models fine-tuned for the C-ArmLSpine cross-validation; and a count and coupling with no learned parameters that give the ordinal from the sacrum and the stop signal. Research directions' design names from morphology, keeps the count as a second mechanism and takes their disagreement as the stop signal; this chapter is its naming half. The handoff and Research directions, both of 8 October, restate the same figures, so their agreement is not independent confirmation (row 61).

## How much of the spine a model must see

### The ladder, and what moved the headline

The [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) of 12 and 13 September scaled the target vertebra to the same pixel height in every crop, so that size could not be used, and shrank the visible context from five vertebrae to one on a collimated field, holding out 30 percent of the patients of every annotated source. On 8,422 held-out real-film crops (CSXA 7,440 and NHANES 982; 410 lumbar, 46 sacrum and 66 thoracic among them), one vertebra was named exactly in 96.7 percent and within one level in 99.7 percent, with no interval given. Cervical reached 97.7 percent at one vertebra and 99.3 at three (the headline chip's 99.5 is the table's figure at five, row 52), lumbar 91.9 at three; the thoracic and sacrum columns are bands, not rankings.

The page credits the move from 84 to 97 percent in one night to 4,963 CSXA cervical patients and 7,200 BUU lumbar films entering training. Its table shows what moved: the NHANES-only column, the old 84.2 percent headline, read 84.0 in the same 48 px arm, while CSXA, 7,440 of the 8,422 crops, entered training and test together. The move is mostly a change in the population scored, toward cervical films with ample annotated data, not a gain on the lumbar films the old figure measured; 96 px targets lifted the NHANES-only column to 88.6. Read 96.7 as a cervical-weighted figure and 81 to 85 percent as the September bound on a lumbar vertebra seen alone.

### Lumbar is bounded by its training count

Single-vertebra lumbar accuracy tracked the real lumbar films in training: 7.4 percent with none (CT only), 80.7 with about 380 annotated NHANES films at 48 px, 82.7 with the 7,200 counting-labelled BUU films added, and 85.4 with the same 380 or so films, without BUU, at 96 px, against 97.7 cervical with about 5,000 annotated patients. Nothing else moved a vertebra more than a few points: ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-tiny came within 2 points of ResNet-18 on CT, ViT-B/16 was 47 points worse, size in millimetres cost 1.7 points in its third trial, and learning rate, epochs and masking did nothing that mattered. The residual lumbar error is almost all one level (L2 against L3, L3 against L4, L1 against T12). That the count bounds lumbar naming is an inference from one test set, not a learning curve, but it agrees with the 18 September finding that only real examples ever moved the six-lumbar figure.

### Where more context stops helping

On CT projections with the whole scan visible, exact naming was 82.7 percent on VerSe and 91.8 on all CT sources, against 63.5 and 73.7 at one vertebra. Of the whole-scan arm's 188 errors, 80 percent were whole-case shifts, every vertebra of a case one level off together (151 errors in 24 of 234 cases), and only 4 of the 17 shifting VerSe cases carried an atypical count in their labels. At wide context the ceiling is enumeration, not morphology, which counting from an anchor ([chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)) addresses. The outline alone named 72.5 percent on CT but 0.1 on NHANES, a mismatch of shape definitions rather than a result. None of this is fluoroscopy.

A single-crop figure needs its model named. The ladder's 96.7 percent comes from arms that join films to synthetic crops; the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) of 15 September gives 98.7 percent on the same crops for a films-only model, its context rung not stated and its person-disjointness cannot-tell, a record, not a final value; and the 18 September page gives 97.0 percent for one frame of its own system beside the ladder's 96 px regional row (cervical 97.6, thoracic 95.5, lumbar 85.4, sacrum 93.5). [The contradictions page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#how-far-the-verdicts-hold) carries each with its model and page, neither replacing the other.

## The isolated stack is not underdetermined

### The claim and the test

A feasibility report, not among the artifacts, argued that with no anchor in frame (its State C, the isolated stack) a five-lumbar stack resembles a six-lumbar one with its top body cropped off, so that naming there is "mathematically underdetermined", L2 to L4 are "largely exchangeable", and a system "should formally refuse" and use geometric indices. [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) of 17 September answered that the lack of a deterministic inverse says nothing about whether the posterior carries information, and measured that posterior on real film, against 1,113 CT spines with junction flags read from the label voxels and the BUU-LSPINE authors' reads of 144 radiographs (129 four-lumbar, 15 six-lumbar). Its models were trained on bank v2 and on bank v5, 30 percent of whose 253,763 crops were the decoder's own pseudolabels; both banks carry the NHANES duplicate, so every model figure in this section is a record, not a final value, read from that page.

### Labels against pixels

Windows of 2 to 9 vertebrae assigned to State C by their labels were named in 97.2 percent [96.9, 97.4] of ordinary vertebrae (n = 14,492) and 87.2 percent [84.3, 89.7] of anomalous ones (n = 572), and v5's pseudolabels lowered the anomalous figure to 66.8 percent [62.9, 70.4]; these intervals ignore clustering by film, and the anomalous test set was 20 films. The page retracted that reading itself: a five-body lumbar window is the whole annotated stack, whose names the convention fixes, and shorter windows still touch an end whose crops show the sacrum or C2. These are records, not final values.

So the page defined the isolated stack by pixels, as windows that exclude both ends of the annotated stack, scored with a film-cluster bootstrap of 2,000 resamples. Ordinary lumbar naming was 91.8 percent [89.4, 94.1] on 455 films in v2 (89.8 at two bodies, 93.8 at three) and 93.5 percent [91.4, 95.5] in v5; cervical was 99.6 and 99.8; anomalous lumbar was 66.0 percent [39.6, 87.2] on 12 films in v2 and 57.3 percent [29.0, 78.6] on 17 in v5. A placement-blind baseline scores 0 percent, since with no anchor in view a count cannot start, a floor true by construction (row 74); a placement-aware one that sees the film's edge scores 100 percent, since BUU's names are the count, and the model, shown interior pixels only, recovers 92 to 94 percent of that convention. These too are records, not final values.

### The shape of the errors

The report named the mechanism correctly and its size wrongly. Of isolated-stack errors, 83.0 percent were exactly one level, 52 percent too low and 31 percent too high, and 44.9 percent of errored windows carried one coherent offset across every vertebra, the five-against-six degeneracy, which came to 8.3 percent of all isolated-stack windows. L2, L3 and L4 scored 87.6, 86.4 and 86.2 percent in v2, so the mid-lumbar bodies are not exchangeable. L6 failed, at 44.9 percent in v2 and 48.3 in v5, a data problem first: the page counts 168 L6 training patients, all renders, and 13 for T13 (counts later records replace, below), and its pseudolabelled 30 percent holds no L6 at all, since the decoder that named those films has only the ordinary alphabet and files every transitional spine as ordinary. Masking the body of an interior L3 cost 2.3 points and its upper neighbour 1.9 (intact 90.0 percent, n = 261), so identity is redundant and spread along the run. All are records, not final values.

### The cost on an anomalous spine

Bank v6 moved all 144 anomalous BUU studies out of training (124 had sat there as L1 to L5), duplicated the anomalous CT renders four times in training, and changed nothing else. On 4,026 label-defined State C windows from all 144 studies it named 81.3 percent [80.0, 82.4] against 97.3 on ordinary spines, so the page judged the 20-film 87 percent optimistic by six points and the pseudolabelled 67 percent pessimistic by fourteen. On interior windows it named 49.9 percent [41.5, 58.9] on 128 anomalous films against 89.9 percent [87.1, 92.7] ordinary: with no junction in the pixels, one exposure names a transitional spine at about a coin toss between its two candidate identities. [Research directions](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) gives the same pair but calls the 144 a validation set, and the contradictions page leaves the partition unsettled (row 25). All are records, not final values.

So the isolated stack is not underdetermined on an ordinary spine, where interior pixels carry identity far above the 0 percent floor. On an anomalous spine one exposure costs about 25 to 40 points in these records (91.8 against 66.0 percent, 89.9 against 49.9), on few films and leaked banks, and nothing has re-run the measurement on G3. That cost, not underdetermination, is what the sweep was built to remove.

### What else the page settled

The report's geometric substitutes did worse than the network. Its "normal L5" squaring band held 5.1 percent of 3,456 ordinary laterals, with thresholds not in the literature; only sacral slope separated six-lumbar spines, at an AUC of 0.73 [0.61, 0.84] on 15 films; and its stump-rib numbers have no source; how the two border anomalies co-occur is [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md)'s. Among the models, cross-entropy and CORN lay within 0.6 points (94.79 to 95.35 percent on film), so an ordinal loss bought nothing; ordinal focal loss and DINOv3 did worse; logit adjustment bought three points on ordinary spines and nothing on variants, on 128 anomalous films; two-view fusion lost, the AP view alone scoring 40 to 47 percent with a lateral-trained encoder; and a transition-role head, at 89.5 percent balanced accuracy on its own, left the alphabet score identical to three decimals at three weights. Of six interventions the page aimed at the transitional figure only the sweep moved it; domain randomisation, pretrain-then-fine-tune and two pseudolabel-admission rules had failed earlier. These are records, not final values.

## The sweep, first measured on renders

The page's remedy was the surgeon's own: exposures from a landmark, here at a 9-inch field, about 14 cm at the spine, linked by a correlation restricted to the step the protocol asked for, since unrestricted the periodic column locks one body off, the failure the page says caused all 47 failures in Otake 2012. On 801 abdominopelvic renders a two-body frame scored 69.6 percent alone and 95.1 registered; on 76 whole-spine renders swept from C2, a thoracic three-body frame went from 31.1 to 99.3 percent, on about 133 vertebrae. Decoding the chain as one sequence against four alphabets beat per-node argmax by 9.7 points (95.5 against 85.8), and counting from the landmark gave 93.8. Field size barely moved naming (94.9, 93.6 and 93.5 percent for a collimated, a 9-inch and a 12-inch field) while chain completeness rose with it (92.5, 94.1 and 96.5), since a wider field needs fewer links; overlap bought the last four points. The render arms measured the linker and the decoder, not the whole pipeline, since the real-film detector's boxes completed the chain on 0.4 percent of these degraded renders. These are records, not final values ([Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)). The contradictions page carries the 18 September held-out figures in their place (rows 15 to 17), reading onto them the superseded summary's audit of an earlier version of itself, which had scored training patients from image directories that carry no record of the split, a leak that inflated the lone-frame baseline rather than the sweep ([Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/)). The page's search found no published method that chains frames from a landmark to name what lies between them, a claim about naming, not stitching; its operating point matches the one clinical stitching series, four to six exposures at 20 to 30 percent overlap (row 54).

## The sweep-and-match chain of 18 September

[Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) walks all six steps with a before and after for each, on held-out spines. A spot film showing three vertebrae and no landmark is named correctly 93.9 percent of the time once matched onto the sweep, against 85.7 percent alone, and 90.4 percent with the automatic detector in place of human boxes. The page says steps 1 and 5 carry the advantage and steps 2 to 4 must simply not fail; it gives n for its regional arms and no intervals.

| Step | What it does | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Sweep | exposures from a landmark, two bodies of overlap | chain intact on 88.3 percent of spines at half a body | 97.5 percent at two bodies |
| 2 Detect | a class-agnostic box per body, the sacrum's own model | 97.6 percent of bodies found, unsettled (row 18) | sacrum recall 96.9 percent |
| 3 Repair | merge and fill from the frame's own spacing | duplicates in a frame 3.03 percent | 0.61 percent |
| 4 Link | correlation in the protocol's window, a majority rule | duplicates across frames 21.8 percent | 3.0 percent |
| 5 Name | the encoder per body, the head over the run | chain fully named 67.4 percent | 91.8 percent with the step 4 fix |
| 6 Place | the spot film matched onto the map | 85.7 percent named alone | 93.9 percent on the sweep |

### Steps 1 and 2: the sweep and the bodies

The radiographer starts with a landmark at the edge of the field, the sacrum going up or C2 coming down, and moves the C-arm toward the level of interest, leaving two bodies of the previous image in view at one field size: three exposures for most lumbar work, five or six to the mid-thoracic spine. With one body of overlap the periodic column can align a frame one level off; with four, every extra frame is another link to join wrongly. The superseded summary's column is a record, not a final value ([Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/)); the contradictions page keeps its exposures and accuracy and flags its chain rates (row 15).

| Overlap | Chain intact, canonical page | Exposures and chain intact, superseded summary |
|---|---|---|
| Half a body | 88.3 | 2.5 exposures, 86.7 |
| One body | 96.7 | 2.6 exposures, 95.0 |
| Two bodies | 97.5 | 3.0 exposures, 96.7 |
| Four bodies | 82.5 | 6.9 exposures, 80.0 |

A class-agnostic detector boxes each body and names nothing. By the canonical page it found 97.6 percent of bodies, missing 2.42 percent at confidence 0.25, 0.10 and 0.05 alike, so the misses do not sit just below the cut, and two of its three counted misses were the sacrum, the anchor. The recall is unsettled: three misses in 243 would be 98.8 percent, the superseded summary gives 99.2, a record, not a final value, and row 18 carries neither until the detector's evaluation file is read. [Collision 70](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#number-collisions) prefers the canonical 2.42 percent miss rate to the summary's 2.2 and says nothing of 99.2, and collision 116 cannot tell 2.42 from another detector page's 98.71 percent recall at 0.25, so no recall is current. A lower threshold doubles the spurious boxes and costs the chain, which by the canonical page survives on 8 percent of films against 40 at the 0.25 in use, and by the summary, written while its tolerant linking rule was in testing, on 20 percent with the detector's boxes against 100 with annotation boxes on 25 held-out radiographs. The ledger leaves 20, 40 and 100 open ([the concept ledger, row 206](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-206-sweep-chain-survival-with-the-detectors-own-boxes)), and the evaluation's run log would settle 20 against 40 (collision 206). So the sacrum got its own single-class detector, split by patient, with precision 98.2 percent and recall 96.9. The page says it was trained on the 4,526 images that carry a sacrum box and elsewhere counts 5,110 such images; whether the first is the training share of the second, the training list and the bank manifest would show (row 21).

### Steps 3 and 4: repair and linking

Bodies in one frame sit at nearly constant spacing, which the detector ignores. Box repair measures the typical gap in the frame, merges two boxes much closer than it and inserts one where two are much further apart. It fills at most two bodies into one gap, since a run of invented boxes is worse than an honest hole, and never extrapolates past the ends of a run, since inventing a missing sacrum would fabricate the landmark. Duplicates in a frame fell from 3.03 to 0.61 percent, and naming at two, three and four vertebrae rose from 83.0, 85.6 and 90.2 percent to 87.2, 90.4 and 91.4, more than half the gap to human boxes. Repair never uses a count, unlike the published lumbar baseline, which decodes exactly five boxes and, by its authors' report, drops a vertebra on a six-lumbar spine and puts the fifth box on the sacrum of a fused one.

Consecutive frames are matched by correlation inside a window around where the protocol says the C-arm went. The original rule accepted a shared block only if every pair in it was confirmed, so one bad box collapsed the overlap and re-entered every shared vertebra as new. A majority rule took duplicates across frames from 21.8 to 3.0 percent and chain naming from 67.4 to 91.8 percent, and it agrees with unanimity to the second decimal on human boxes, which made it safe to adopt. The superseded summary's state before the fix, 17.9 percent duplicated (a baseline that does not match 21.8, collision 71) and a sweep worse than none (66.0 against 72.3 percent at two vertebrae), is a record, not a final value ([Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/); row 19).

### Steps 5 and 6: naming and placement

Each vertebra of the chain is named from its own pixels and the head reads the run; nothing counts. Every vertebra was named correctly on 100 percent of held-out real films and on 95.0 percent of renders, with no n given. A later limited-field exposure is then matched onto the stitched map by correlation and takes its names from it, which replaces CT registration and costs milliseconds; by row 55, detection and naming take a quarter to half a second on one CPU core and the detector 6.8 ms per image on a GPU. A frame placed on a broken map inherits its error, which is why steps 2 to 4 matter.

### Against methods on the same data

The page re-implemented the counting paradigms and scored them on the same films at the tightest field.

| Method, on 1,653 films and 8,422 vertebrae | Exact | Abstains |
|---|---|---|
| Count from a reference vertebra | 1.8 | 97.3 |

The counting paradigms refuse because a tight field holds nothing to count from, and that refusal is the clinical problem. A fourth row, the BUU-LSPINE authors' per-level detector (Klinwichit et al.) trained on this split, was announced as running, and no outcome appears in any source read.

### Transitional spines, and what the page decided

After every training spine was excluded, eight transitional spines remained, one of them six-lumbar and named wrongly, so the page quotes no transitional figure. A cross-validation over the transitional patients, 376 by these pages, was running; the ledger leaves that count open ([the concept ledger, row 55](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-55-transitional-patients-available-across-all-sources)), no outcome appears in any source read, and the handoff's zero verified real-frame transitional test persons, against 35 needed, is a different count (row 40).

The page recorded its decisions: name from morphology, never from a count, since every published failure traces to a fixed cardinality or a tally a transitional segment corrupts; repair from local spacing; link by majority; delimit at the disc, not the fusion, since bridging is mixed on a third of these spines and expert readers disagree on which endplate is sacral (kappa 0.32 to 0.38); and give the sacrum its own model. The first was later qualified, since the record now keeps naming and counting together.

## What the superseded 18 September summary alone holds

[Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/) shares the canonical page's date and chain. The clusters mark it superseded because its own text says the sacrum was still being given a detector of its own, a tolerant linking rule was in testing, earlier that day the sweep had been worse than none, and an earlier version of it had scored training patients, where the canonical page reports the detector and the linking fix done. Its numbers are records, not final values, and it alone holds four things.

The first is the argument for the disc rule. The largest vertebra dataset did not segment vertebrae partly fused to the sacrum, and the main lumbar radiograph dataset boxes none; sixteen spine surgeons agreed on which endplate is sacral about a third of the time; joint-like and solid bridging coexist in about a third of cases; and a plain-film grade is wrong against CT 35 percent of the time. Hence the rule: a body that a disc still separates from the sacrum, even under a bridging transverse process, is a vertebra and counts; without the disc it is sacrum; and a disc between the first and second sacral segments makes the top segment separate.

The second is the threshold table. The misses never move, so finding more vertebrae and making the system safer are different goals, which is why 0.25 stands.

| Confidence | Vertebrae missed | Boxes on nothing | Map intact, films | Three in view, named |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25, in use | 2.4 | 8.5 | 40 | 85.6 |
| 0.10 | 2.4 | 13.9 | 24 | 84.4 |
| 0.05 | 2.4 | 18.2 | 8 | 81.3 |

The third is its correction of what the literature's figures measure, which the next table gives as the summary states it. Two later readings qualify it. The contradictions page splits the first row by study: 99.998 percent is the Otake line's registration convergence and 100 and 99.3 percent by computing budget are Lo 2015's, both registration success rather than naming and, by the feasibility review, not verified (row 2). The repository's reading qualifies the last row, since those images are Long-Film, an extended-field tomographic mode (below). No method releases code or weights, none addresses transitional anatomy, and a perfect registration still carries a miscounted CT faithfully onto the radiograph.

| The figure quoted | What it measures |

The fourth is timings and two counts. It alone gives the CPU figure above, against three to five seconds per image for CT registration on a GPU once a CT has been acquired, segmented and labelled, and nothing ran on an embedded module; the retraining plan records 3,350 ms per frame as its only CPU figure ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)), and latency belongs to [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md). It also gives the n behind the L6 gain, 105 held-out patients, and counts 845 six-lumbar spines in a rare-variant set whose images hold no landmark to sweep from.

## Prior claims: refuted, contradicted and still standing

The report's isolated-stack claims fell as above, except two that were confirmed: its mechanism, the one-level offset, and its warning that CT-tuned thresholds collapse on film.

The deep-research verdict of 15 September, [Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/), held that nothing verified gives a per-level accuracy for a registration-free single frame on real images, that Huang 2022's 88.3 percent and 3.3 mm did not survive verification, and that a single frame's realistic promise is a transitional flag with abstention, "not an authoritative level name at the lumbosacral junction". The project's measurements overtook its first clause on ordinary spines, through the interior-window records and the 96.4 percent on lateral C-arm frames below; its last clause stands, since the chain has no transitional figure. The superseded summary contradicted its Huang verdict, and the canonical page tables 88.3 percent as the comparator. The repository's reading of 22 October found the abstract as printed: 297 CTs rendered for training, 50 AP and 50 lateral Long-Film images of 13 cadavers tested, no region named, the full text closed, and 88.9 and 91.2 percent with and without instrumentation, which cannot both partition 88.3 ([docs/research/lumbosacral-to-cervical-extrapolation.md](../../docs/research/lumbosacral-to-cervical-extrapolation.md)). The contradictions page carries 88.3 only as Huang's figure on 100 cadaveric Long-Film images, unverified until the full text is read (row 0); whatever it measures, it was measured over an extended field on cadavers, not on a spot film.

"Best published" is unsettled too. [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) gives 73.3 percent, which the anchor-free page identifies as VDVM 2023, a C-arm frame matched to the same patient's full-length film on 31 images; the canonical page gives 88.3; the ledger leaves both open ([the concept ledger, row 123](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-123-best-published-registration-free-vertebra-naming-accuracy)); and the overnight page's 96.5 percent on MRI carries no citation (row 3). A planner names one comparator with its modality, never a best.

The ceilings that stand are clinical and anatomical. Surgeons name the level 69.6 percent of the time unaided on difficult C-arm films and 97.6 percent with a CT-registration overlay (Manbachi 2018), but only where a CT existed and was labelled; every figure in this chapter is the machine alone, and the clinical claim needs a reader study ([chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md)). A lateral-film transitional flag reached 76.4 percent accuracy and an AUC of 0.84 on 313 films at 62.3 percent prevalence, below a specialist's 83.9 (Kwak 2025). Radiographic Castellvi typing reaches 42 percent accuracy, and 35.2 percent of subtypes disagree with CT (Hanhivaara 2024; Hou 2020). Level nomination on outpatient lumbar radiographs and MRI was wrong for 38 of 71 transitional patients (Nakagawa 2017). And a whole lumbar film with the sacrum in it is a counting problem: against Cina 2021's 98.4 percent, the anchor-free page's own model scored 99.96 percent [99.9, 100] on 520 films, exactly what a sacrum rule scores, a record, not a final value ([Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)).

Two proposals of that week also fell. [Lift Before Label?](../../artifacts/lift-before-label-jzjq8t/) advised a direct 2-D identifier with two-view fusion over a reconstruction stage; the reconstruction verdict stands, but fusion lost on these films. [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) proposed an ordinal loss, which bought nothing, and its 99 percent accuracy at 35.7 percent coverage came from a leaked bank, a record, not a final value (row 39).

## On a real C-arm

The reported stack's held-out C-arm result, as [docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) copies it from another repository's status file, so Reported here, is where this encoder meets intraoperative frames of a device it never saw. Zero-shot lateral naming on C-ArmLSpine was 595 of 617 vertebrae, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4]; fine-tuned five-fold it was 97.4 percent, with no interval given and a record, not a final value (below), a paired difference of +1.0 points [-1.9, 4.0] over zero-shot; the count from the detected sacrum was 97.0 percent. Zero-shot AP naming was 276 of 631, 43.7 percent with no interval given, a one-level cranial shift and the ledger's current value ([the concept ledger, row 107](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)), which the repository reads as a counting error, not an appearance error ([docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md)). The handoff gives 74.8 percent with one body in view (row 7). Whether the 98.2 percent of 22 September was replaced by the 97.4 the October pages give for whole-frame naming, or is the coupled count (97.0 plus 1.2 points), the record has not settled; that is chapter 10's question.

## Where the naming line fails

Transitional spines, above all L6, are the first and largest failure. Rendered L6 spines fail mostly by a whole-run shift one level cranial on 26 to 28 percent of films, the sixth lumbar body taking the fifth's name, exactly the error a count catches ([Research directions](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)). The naming component's L6 accuracy moved once, from 67.4 to 73.5 percent, when 83 real six-lumbar images entered training, after re-weighting, oversampling, simulated pretraining and two pseudolabel rules had moved nothing; each L6 figure belongs to its own setting (row 23). The record settles the thin counts as far as it can. For L6 it carries the 18 September count, 213 distinct training patients with 83 real, over the previous day's 168, all renders, the training manifest to show why the render count fell ([collision 56](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#number-collisions); row 22). For T13 it carries Research directions' seven persons in all sources, none real, as the latest count, and the 18 September page's 73 with 65 real as unexplained until the bank's person keys are checked (row 24). The repository counts three real L6 persons, all frozen test persons ([docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md)), and the latest L6 figure is Research directions' rendered L6 recall of 56.1 to 72.4 percent on ten held-out persons (row 23). ## What we know

On plain films one vertebra at a fixed scale names a cervical level about 98 percent of the time and a lumbar level 81 to 85 percent, three vertebrae take lumbar to about 92, and lumbar accuracy follows the annotated lumbar films in training rather than the architecture; the move from 84 to 97 percent was mostly a change in the population scored, and at wide context the residual error is enumeration. With no anchor in its pixels an ordinary spine is not underdetermined, its errors mostly one level and its mid-lumbar bodies not exchangeable, while an anomalous spine loses 25 to 40 points in one exposure and L6 is named less than half the time, both on leaked-bank records. On held-out spines the sweep-and-match chain took a three-vertebra spot film with no landmark from 85.7 to 93.9 percent, or 90.4 with the automatic detector, the gain growing with distance from the landmark; two bodies of overlap is the working point, majority linking was worth 24 points and box repair closed more than half the gap to human boxes. The detector's misses do not move with its threshold, and the ones that matter are the sacrum's. On a real C-arm the current encoder names lateral frames zero-shot at 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4] and AP frames at 43.7 percent. Only real examples and the sweep have ever moved the transitional figure.

## What we do not know

We do not know whether any September figure survives the G3 split, since nothing after 27 September changed the banks or the models. We do not know how the chain does on a transitional spine: eight held-out spines gave no figure, the cross-validation and bank v7's real-film arms have no outcome in any source read, and the number of transitional patients is open. The detector's per-body recall, the thoracic figure on twelve studies (4.3 or 8.3 percent alone), the chain's survival with the detector's own boxes, the sacrum detector's training count and whether whole-frame C-arm naming stands at 97.4 or 98.2 percent are unsettled, and the twenty-point cost of pseudolabels has never been measured on a clean split. Nothing has been measured on real oblique frames or real C-arm sweeps. The BUU authors' detector has no score on this split, Huang 2022's full text is closed, and nothing here measures whether a surgeon assisted by this system matches one assisted by CT registration.

## What would change our mind

A G3 re-run of the interior windows that put ordinary naming well below 92 percent would mean the leaks carried the anchor-free result; one putting anomalous naming near the ordinary figure would weaken the case for sweeping transitional spines, and one putting it lower would strengthen the case for counting. A transitional figure for the chain below the single-crop figure would mean the chain inherits the variant failure instead of removing it. A real C-arm sweep whose chain fails to complete, or whose spot-film gain disappears, would turn the chain from a method into a hypothesis, and real oblique frames near the rendered 58 percent would make projection the first problem. Lumbar accuracy that did not rise as annotated lumbar films were added would refute the count-bound reading of the ladder. The BUU authors' detector scoring near this work on the same split would remove the advantage claimed on ordinary spines, and Huang 2022's full text, if its 88.3 percent is per-vertebra naming in a field like a C-arm's, would make it the comparator. A reader study in which wrong suggestions overturn more correct reads than right ones rescue would make the system a hazard whatever its accuracy alone.
