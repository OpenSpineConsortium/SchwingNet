# 19 What a valid comparison needs: leakage, conventions, comparators and power

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members and stays in this private repository; it gives results and rules, and leaves how each stage works to Part III.

This chapter sets the conditions under which two SchwingNet numbers, or a SchwingNet number and a published one, may be set side by side. A planner reads it before putting any figure on a slide, in a table or in a grant: it says which checks must pass first, which published comparators stand, which were refuted and which are ceilings rather than rivals, how many persons, devices or frames each kind of claim needs and which claims have near-zero power today, and what pre-registration requires and where a departure is written down. It closes with the six-step method the project uses to test any claim, its own or a rival's. Chapter 18 ([Metrics, intervals and witness frames](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)) says how a single number is computed and bounded; this chapter says when two of them may meet.

## Four ways a comparison goes wrong

The record of September and October 2026 shows four distinct failures, each of which made a number look comparable when it was not. A test frame or test person also sat in training, so the number measured memory. Two arms scored their boxes against truth drawn under different conventions, so one arm looked broken when it was not. A pooled figure stood where a held-out-device figure should have, so the reader took same-device performance for transfer. And a comparator was quoted without its denominator, its material or its own interval, so the gap between it and SchwingNet had no meaning. The sections below take these in turn, with a short section on denominators and populations before the comparators, say what the record found and state the rule that follows; the section after them turns the rules into numbers of persons and devices.

## Leakage: no test item may sit in any training corpus

### What the September audit checked, and what it could not

The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 22 September is the canonical audit of the verification cluster. Its leakage section reports three separate checks. Splits were disjoint by case on all three real C-arm sets (C-ArmLSpine 105/15/28 cases, COBRA 110/8/27, DeepFluoro 4/1/1 specimens), with no crop in a split other than its case's and no fixed-test patient in any fine-tune bank. Near-duplicate frames across splits, at a normalised cross-correlation of 0.97 or more at two scales, numbered zero on C-ArmLSpine (maximum 0.962) and DeepFluoro (maximum 0.934); COBRA showed 74,364 cross-case pairs that the ledger explains as the same gantry angle on different patients with no bone at lateral angles. And the real test frames were set against every external corpus, 37,165 film images, 29,484 FluoroCap panels and 1,445 frames of an older keypoint set, with zero pairs at 0.97 or more.

That is a strong pixel-level check of the real C-arm test frames, and nothing later contradicts it within its scope. Its scope is the point. Later findings looked as though they overturned it, and the contradiction pages rule that they do not, because each answers a different question ([the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), rows 40 and 41; [the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 30). The handoff of 8 October records that NHANES II entered the older plain-film level banks twice under two names, so that 48 of 54 validation persons of those banks were also in training, which made the allspine v2, v6 and v7 families leaked, and that CTSpinoPelvic1K entered under two names, putting 301 of 801 persons on both sides of its split ([Handoff: SchwingNet, CTSpinoPelvic1K and the imaging programme](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)). And [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) counts 92 of 97 transitional validation and test persons inside the v8 and v9 encoder's training lineage. The first two are persons duplicated under two source names inside training banks; the third is a person-level overlap in a model lineage. A pixel near-duplicate test of real C-arm frames can find neither.

So the September zero is a true statement about one kind of leak and says nothing about the others. A planner who reads "leakage: 0 pairs" on the ledger's verdict strip should read it as "no real C-arm test frame is a near-copy of anything in training", not as "the evaluation is clean". The ledger's own sub-claim that FluoroCap's article list holds no DeepFluoro, C-ArmLSpine, COBRA or Geneva paper is shown with no listing or count behind it (verification row 76), so it stays Reported.

### The three leak checks a comparison needs

Read together, the audits give three checks, and a number is quotable beside another only when all three have passed for the data behind both.

The first is a person-key check: no person whose identity the source's identity level defines (patient, study, cadaver, source volume) is on two sides of a split, counted across every source, including the same source entered under two names. This is the check the NHANES double entry and the CTSpinoPelvic1K alias failed. [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) raised a sharper form of it on 22 September: CTSpinoPelvic1K is built from CT colonography, which scans each patient prone and supine, so if two release ids are one human being the corpus is mis-split, which that page says would be larger than either leak already found and fixed. The record has not settled whether a CTSpinoPelvic1K record is one person: Research Directions gives 802 records, one per patient, whose supine and prone counts sum to 799, while the handoff and Counting From The Sacrum count 801 persons ([the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 29, cannot-tell); until the patient-key file settles it, a CTSpinoPelvic1K split is person-disjoint only as far as its ids are persons.

The second is a content check: no frame's bytes appear in two parts under two persons, which a person table alone never shows. The third is a near-duplicate check of test frames against every external corpus, the one the ledger ran.

The repository now holds the first two as code that runs before any training step reads a frame. tools/catalog.py's `split_check` reports, over one catalog version, every person in two parts, every frame hash in two parts, every test person not on the frozen list and every frozen person found outside test, and its verdict is disjoint only when all four lists are empty ([tools/catalog.py](../../tools/catalog.py)). The split itself is decided by a salted hash of the person key with a stated validation share, and the test persons are a frozen list extended only by an explicit pull request ([data/splits/rule.json](../../data/splits/rule.json), [data/splits/test-persons.json](../../data/splits/test-persons.json), [docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)). The leak checks proper, the person-key chain with its CHK-6 and CHK-7, run at ingestion as a separate job after the shards are built ([docs/memory/dicom-banks-and-leak-checks.md](../../docs/memory/dicom-banks-and-leak-checks.md)); the retraining plan notes they had never run on the real corpus before this workflow. The near-duplicate check against external corpora is not yet code in this repository. Chapter 9 ([Splits, leakage and licences](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md)) holds the split and its history; this chapter needs only the rule that the checks pass before a number is compared.

The rule that follows: a number from a leaked family is a record, never a comparator. Allspine v2's 99.02 percent, a record, not a final value ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)), and the 99 percent selective accuracy at 35.7 percent coverage (2,956 answered, lower bound 98.7 percent) of [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/), a record, not a final value, both come from banks the handoff rules leaked (verification row 9), and Research Directions says the second needs a re-run; they may be quoted only as records, with a link to the page they come from, and never set beside a clean figure as if the two measured the same thing.

## One convention across devices

### The 5.2 percent that became 90.6 percent

The clearest lesson in the record about conventions is one table cell. The ledger's overnight table gave a detector trained with C-ArmLSpine held out a vertebra recall of 5.2 percent on 56 whole C-ArmLSpine test frames, which read as a device that transfers not at all. At 06:50 the same morning the ledger re-scored it. C-ArmLSpine's truth boxes are vertebral-body boxes; COBRA's and DeepFluoro's are whole-vertebra outlines; the held-out detector, trained on the other two, had learned outlines. Scored against outline truth on the same 287 vertebrae, recall was 90.6 percent at the working threshold (92.7 at its best), and under a convention-free mutual-centre rule 90.9 percent ([OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/), section 3).

The ledger's control is what makes the re-score credible. The same-device pooled detector, trained on body boxes, fell from 90.6 percent under body truth to 40.8 percent under outline truth and stayed at 91.6 percent under the centre rule. A convention gap that moves both arms in opposite directions, and vanishes under a rule that ignores box extent, is a convention gap and not a performance gap. The thoracic films showed the same thing in another form: one detector trained on real films found 97.7 percent of thoracic bodies on AASCE films, while every box-scored arm scored 0 to 7 percent against the same truth at IoU 0.5, which the ledger calls "partly a box-convention gap again" and rules not comparable at that threshold (section 6).

The rule that follows: two detection numbers are comparable only when both were scored against truth of one convention, or under a rule that does not depend on box extent, and the table says which. A recall without its truth convention, IoU threshold, matching rule and confidence threshold is not a comparator.

### Which held-out recall the book carries

The 90.6 percent is itself not the figure the book carries as current. The handoff's results catalogue of 8 October gives held-out-device detection recall as 80.5 percent with no numerator, denominator or truth convention printed (the State of the Literature places it on a held-out-device test of 56 frames), and the verification cluster's verdict carries 80.5 percent, keeping 90.6 percent as the ledger's earlier matched-convention re-score ([the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 3; [the main contradictions page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md)). The OR-study cluster and the programme cluster read the same pair as cannot-tell, to be settled by paper 1's claims file naming which device was held out for 80.5 percent, under which box convention and over which denominator ([the OR-study contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md), row 33; [the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 13); chapter 20 ([Readiness for the operating room](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md)) gives both. For comparison purposes the consequence is narrow and firm: whichever of the two is quoted is quoted with its convention, and the two are never set side by side as a gain or a loss.

## Same-device beside held-out-device, always

Every C-arm is its own domain, and the ledger's device table shows how far apart the two kinds of number can be. The pooled three-device detector found 98.2 percent of sacra and 90.6 percent of vertebrae on C-ArmLSpine, its own device; on Geneva's BV Pulsera image intensifier, which it never saw, it found 32.2 percent of sacra and 27.0 percent of vertebrae. On COBRA laterals, a level model never trained on C-ArmLSpine named 79.9 percent of levels exactly with boxes given (interval 74.5 to 84.6), where the model fine-tuned on C-ArmLSpine named 43.5 percent (36.4 to 50.1), 904 scored in each arm: fine-tuning on one device cost the model the others ([OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/), sections 3 and 5). A pooled figure therefore says almost nothing about a device outside the pool.

[Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) turned this into the standing rule: each C-arm is its own domain, and a pooled figure is never quoted without the device-held-out arm beside it. The demo plan applies a stricter form for the talk, a device held out three ways, absent from the training frames, the render poses and the style terms ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)). The ledger's DeepFluoro row shows why the stricter form matters. Its 100 percent sacrum figure on that specimen, 104 frames, came from a detector whose starting point had already been exposed to the test specimen, 255 of its 569 training renders (44.8 percent) lying under that specimen's own poses and its device terms fitted on real frames that included all 104 of the specimen's; the ledger itself rules it "not the manuscript's method" and gives 88.5 to 91.3 percent, over three seeds, as the clean render-only figure for that specimen. A number whose device was held out of the frames but not out of the poses or the fitted terms is a held-out number in name only, and the ledger's status chips ("device held out, init exposure") exist to say so.

The rule that follows has three parts. A same-device number is labelled as such. Wherever a same-device number appears, the held-out-device number for the same stage appears beside it. And a held-out number states what was held out, frames, poses and fitted terms, so a reader can see whether any route let the test device back in.

## The denominator and the population

Two smaller conventions decide whether two naming numbers measure the same thing. The first is the denominator. The ledger found DeepFluoro's test split carrying 83 kept and 229 dropped out-of-field boxes, with older evaluations scoring recall against all 312; the fix went into the loader, but the whole-spine curves still used 312 (section 1). Research Directions asks that any anchored number print the strict all-sacrum-frames denominator beside it, and the paper-2 endpoint scores a person wrong for a missed body, an unanchored frame or an abstention, not only for a wrong name; on ordinary development frames that strict endpoint held on 57.0 percent ([State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/)). A naming accuracy over named frames and one over all frames are different numbers, and the second is the one a surgeon meets.

The second is the population and view. The records show zero-shot lateral naming on C-ArmLSpine far above zero-shot AP naming, the AP figure itself revised from September to October ([concept ledger, row 107](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)); chapter 18 gives the figures and the rule that a pooled view hides both. Two figures from different views, different strata (ordinary against transitional) or different units (bodies, frames, persons) are not set beside each other, and where the same measurement is printed at two roundings or with two case counts, as the ledger's 148 cases and Counting From The Sacrum's 141 cases for the September whole-frame figure, the record is unsettled and neither is a comparator until it is (the concept ledger marks both counts open, [row 0](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-0-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-scored-in-the-five-fold-lateral-whole-frame-evaluation-982) and [row 104](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-104-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-in-the-five-fold-cross-validation-behind-the-982-exact-level-figure)). That September whole-frame figure, 98.2 percent on the ledger (interval 95.7 to 100) and 98.22 percent on Counting From The Sacrum, is a record, not a final value; the October catalogue gives 97.4 percent, which the cluster verdicts set beside it without calling it the replacement ([the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), row 61).

## The comparators: which stand, which fell, which are ceilings

The artifacts name about a dozen published figures as things SchwingNet might be measured against. They fall into four groups.

### Ceilings, not rivals

LevelCheck (De Silva 2016 and the papers before and after it) is the clinical state of the art, and it is registration-based: it registers the patient's own preoperative CT to the intraoperative radiograph and overlays the CT's labels ([Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/)). Counting From The Sacrum states the consequence plainly, "it is a ceiling, not a rival, and it costs a CT". What LevelCheck's numbers measure is itself a trap. The figures quoted for it, 398 of 398 radiographs within a projection distance of 30 mm in a 178-patient series (after 21 films were excluded), 99.998 percent, and 100 percent at about 26 seconds and 99.3 percent at 7 seconds in Lo 2015, are registration-convergence and projection-distance results, not per-level naming accuracies; the feasibility study voted down the reading of them as near-perfect identification accuracy, and the naming page of 18 September tables 99.998 percent as "registration converged - not naming" ([the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 37, overturned on review; [the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md), rows 1 and 2). LevelCheck's clinical evidence is confidence support: in the three-surgeon reader study it was judged helpful in 42.2 percent (168 of 398) of cases and improved confidence in 30.6 percent (122 of 398), and no surgeon's unaided read conflicted with it; the feasibility study notes that the published per-region parts sum to 157, not 168, and the cluster verdict reads 42.2 percent as a three-surgeon mean (range 31.7 to 53.3), so even this figure is a reader's rating with an internal inconsistency, not an accuracy. So the registration-based ceiling is unquantified as a naming accuracy, and SchwingNet is not said to approach or beat it.

Manbachi 2018 supplies the human figure on hard intraoperative films that paper 2 froze as a comparator: 69.6 percent unassisted and 97.6 percent assisted by registration ([Research directions](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/); [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/)); the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) gives the same study as a median error of 30.4 percent unaided falling to 2.4 percent with the overlay, on 62 hard radiographs. No page gives the number of readers. The 97.6 percent is a ceiling of the same kind as LevelCheck's, since it is a surgeon reading with a registered CT, and the State of the Literature lists among its unknowns that it has no published denominator, so its own sampling error is unknown.

### Refuted or unsourced

Huang 2022 (a multi-perspective region network trained on images synthesised from 297 CTs and tested on 50 AP and 50 lateral long films from 13 cadavers) is the only registration-free intraoperative labeller the verified search found, and its accuracy figures, 88.3 percent among them, did not survive verification; the full text was paywalled and the feasibility study keeps only its protocol ([Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/); verification rows 6 and 7). Three days later [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/), the superseded summary of 18 September, and the canonical [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/) still tabled 88.3 percent as the registration-free comparator; that older use is what the refutation overrides, and the summary alone holds it. Counting From The Sacrum names 73.3 percent as "the best published registration-free naming" and gives no source, and two pages give two different "best published" figures (verification row 8; [concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md)). Both 88.3 and 73.3 percent are therefore unverified as comparators. The claim that both 73.3 percent and LevelCheck import identity from the patient's prior imaging is sourced for LevelCheck and not for the 73.3 percent (verification row 73).

The priority claims rest on this weak ground. "Naming solved to a standard nobody has published", "71.67 percent has no peer" and the claim that no published work uses renders to supply level identity to a 2-D model are made with no search record behind them, and Research Directions itself lists Li 2016, Li 2017 and ABLSpineLevelCheck 2024 as unread because IEEE Xplore was blocked (verification row 74; [the datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), row 56). Any of the three could hold a C-arm naming result. Until they are read, a novelty sentence is Inferred at best and carries the record of where the search went.

### Comparators that stand, with their scope

Kwak 2025 is the one 2-D lumbosacral transitional model: accuracy 76.4 percent, AUC 0.84, on a 313-film test set drawn from 3,116 films at a 62 percent positive rate, lateral view, single centre, with report-text labels and the test set also used to choose the backbone ([Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/)). The 313 is the test set and 3,116 the dataset; pages that called 3,116 the test n are wrong (verification row 39; datasets row 40). Research Directions adds that Kwak's figure must never be benchmarked on a balanced set, since its prevalence is enriched. Hanhivaara 2024 gives the human radiograph ceiling for transitional anatomy, sensitivity 32 percent and kappa 0.22 on radiographs against 76 percent and 0.66 on CT, and Farshad 2013 the one lateral rule, sensitivity 94 percent and specificity 74 percent for fused types only. Research Directions draws the right conclusion from the three: a single-lateral flag that beats all of them on an unenriched set "has found something or has leaked", and the first thing to check is the second.

For detection, Rao 2024's 0.964 is the published class-agnostic C-arm recall, on 578 images from three C-arms with a test set of 20. The State of the Literature names it as in-domain and notes it may be cadaver and phantom only, and the cluster verdict carries it as "Rao 2024, 0.964 in-domain, possibly cadaver and phantom only" beside SchwingNet's held-out-device recall (verification row 5). Counting From The Sacrum's earlier "published 0.96 to 0.98" names no material, and is replaced by that line. The comparison it supports is limited: an in-domain figure on 20 test images set against a fully held-out-device figure on 287 vertebrae says the held-out detector is below in-domain published detection, not that SchwingNet's detector is worse than Rao's.

### The rule for a comparator

A comparator stands when its source is read, its numerator and denominator are known, its material (patients, cadavers, phantoms, renders), its device and its view are stated, and it measures the same quantity as the SchwingNet figure beside it. A comparator that measures registration convergence, a confidence rating or an in-domain result is quoted as what it is. A comparator whose source was not read is Reported, and one whose figure failed verification is not used. And because the comparators themselves are samples, the frozen ones carry an interval too; the State of the Literature lists the sampling error of the frozen comparators, 97.6 percent with no published denominator and Peh 1999's numerator assumed, among the unknowns that change a decision.

## How many persons, devices and frames each claim needs

### The arithmetic behind the gates

The October records state a small set of pre-stated targets, carried once from one set of power calculations ([the datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md), row 46; [the transitional contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/transitional-vertebrae-and-border-variants.md), row 43). The handoff says an independent pure-arithmetic check reproduced 35 and 22, 53 and 34, 69 and 45, 113 and 74 at zero, one, two and five errors, and matched every printed value on 8 October. The rules can be re-derived from a two-sided 95 percent bound. With every one of n persons correct, the Wilson lower bound is n / (n + 3.84). It first passes 90 percent at n = 35 (35 / 38.84 = 0.901) and 85 percent at n = 22 (22 / 25.84 = 0.851). For the superiority hypotheses the records use an exact binomial bound, 0.025 to the power 1/n for n of n correct: it first passes 82.2 percent at 19 persons, 92.6 percent at 48 and 97.6 percent at 152 (0.025^(1/152) = 0.9760). Those are exactly the records' 19, 48 and 152, and this chapter's re-derivation agrees with them.

The numbers say what each kind of claim costs:

- Exact level on transitional real frames with a lower bound of 90 percent needs 35 at-risk persons named with no error.
- Variant sensitivity with a lower bound of 85 percent needs 22 carriers with no miss.
- Paper 2's first hypothesis, superiority over its frozen 82.2 percent comparator, needs 19 at-risk persons; the records here do not name the study behind 82.2 percent.
- Superiority over Manbachi's 97.6 percent needs 152 persons with no error, and 708 for 80 percent power at a true 99 percent; at a true 98 percent it is never reached.
- Non-inferiority to 92.6 percent on ordinary spines needs at least 48 persons.
- A device-level claim needs devices, not frames. With four devices a two-sided sign-flip test has 16 equally likely sign patterns, so its smallest P is 2/16 = 0.125 whatever the effect; five devices give 0.0625 and six give 0.031, the first at or below 0.05. Research Directions states the rule: six devices are needed for any two-sided P at or under 0.05.

### Where the holding stands against them

The [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) tables the power of every comparison the programme has named, and the picture is stark. Verified real-frame transitional test persons number zero; seven COBRA2026 two-labeller at-risk persons exist, unverified, whose best-case bound if all were right is 64.6 percent. The 51 CT at-risk test persons (L6 43, sacralised L5 or four lumbar 5, T13 3) would give a bound of 93.0 percent if all were correct, but most sit inside the current encoder's training lineage (49 of 54 L6 persons). The 43 L6 CT test persons give power of 0.36 to 0.93 at a true 95 to 99 percent and never reach the gate at the measured render L6 recall of 72.4 percent; at that recall the chance of 22 of 22 is 0.0008. T13 has three CT test persons and none real, below the 22-carrier floor, and about 55 T13 people would be needed under the 50/10/40 split.

So every transitional claim has near-zero power today, because every powered statement assumed planning accuracies of 95 to 99 percent that no transitional measurement supports; the measured figures are render L6 recall of 56.1 to 72.4 percent, L6 named 44.9 percent with no anchor on anomalous films, and 49.9 percent on anomalous film windows. The State of the Literature's sentence that the 97.6 percent figure "cannot be exceeded at any reachable n" is the claim the cluster rules refuted as worded: its own power table gives zero power below 152 persons, 708 at a true 99 percent and never at 98 percent, which is a statement about the n the programme can reach, not about any n (verification row 38; level-naming row 33). The planner's version is that superiority over 97.6 percent is unreachable with any data the programme holds or has requested.

Two comparisons are decided. Held-out-device against same-device detection on one device, 28 cases with 8 better and none worse, is decided at its n (Holm P 0.0078). And the film difference, 49.9 percent on 128 anomalous films against 89.9 percent on 453 ordinary films, is the one powered transitional statement available, needing 74 per arm at a design effect of 4. Everything else in the table is a target. A device-level gain of fitted device cards over uncalibrated physics has a fold-level P of 0.00023 over 24 folds and zero power at the device level with four devices, which is the difference between a statement about folds and one about devices.

### Clustering

Frames within a case are not independent, so n for an interval is persons or cases, not frames, and every interval is clustered at the case. Counting From The Sacrum states that a naive interval would cover only 70.8 percent of the time; chapter 18 records that this figure is stated by that one artifact with no file or method named, and that the repository's own evaluator today prints a per-vertebra Wilson interval with no clustered interval or bootstrap ([docs/research/evaluation/README.md](../../docs/research/evaluation/README.md)). The power arithmetic above assumes a design effect of 1 unless it says otherwise; the measured design effect on the transitional film arms is 3.84 to 4.47, which turns 19 persons an arm into 74. A comparison whose n was counted in frames is re-counted in persons before it is quoted.

## Stop rules

A stop rule is written before the first number, so that a negative result has a sentence waiting for it. Research Directions gives the clearest one, for the clean-encoder screen on the seven render-scoreable validation L6 persons: zero or one miss proceeds to three seeds and two heads; two or more stops the fluoroscopy arm, and the manuscript says "not at parity" with the number. It states the screen's own operating characteristics, passing 38 percent of the time at a true recall of 72.4 percent and 72 percent of the time at 85 percent, and a second rule: if the read confirms fewer than 22 carriers with an exported frame, no fluoroscopy transitional sensitivity is claimed and the render number is reported alone. The parity programme has its own: a feature not readable in every view at exact pose with no noise leaves the inventory, and a pair whose separating features are all in that state is routed to the count and its parity claim withdrawn.

Two further rules guard comparisons directly. No test person is scored until paper 2's ledger carries an "H1 seen" entry; whether the test witness frames the retraining plan draws at every checkpoint breach that bar has not been ruled (chapter 18). And the paper-2 history is the warning: its blinded gate was cancelled on 27 September and the open gate scored names only because of a path bug, so the frozen comparator stands with no valid SchwingNet number against it ([Handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)).

## Pre-registration: the freeze, the hash and the deviations file

[The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) of 27 September sets out the chain. PREREG.md is written before any script produces a number. It holds the question in one sentence a clinician would recognise; the population and inputs by release, DOI and file hash, with exclusions and reasons; each measurement as the proposal states it; each hypothesis with its bar, taken from a published value or a power calculation and never from the data; what counts as failure and the sentence that will be written if it fails; the analysis plan, with held-out sets and who reads what blind; and a freeze block. It is frozen with its SHA-256 in PREREG.md.sha256 and never edited; every producing job checks the hash first and stops on a mismatch. A departure goes in DEVIATIONS.md, dated, with the reason, and a second version is a new file with its own freeze. Each quoted number has a manifest key naming its results file, its job and the pre-registered item it answers.

The recipe gives its own reason: pre-stated rules make a negative result reportable. Its two examples are a render-ablation rule written before the numbers, whose contrary cell then went into the manuscript, and a bar of 0.50 for a primary endpoint that failed and is recorded as failed.

Two things should be said about where this stands in SchwingNet's repository. No PREREG.md or DEVIATIONS.md exists in it today; the recipe is the programme's rule for projects, and this repository carries its parts in other forms: the frozen test-person list and split rule, the evaluation page that the required check holds to the recorded runs, and the plan's statement that test frames are read once at a version boundary ([docs/research/evaluation/README.md](../../docs/research/evaluation/README.md); [docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)). And the [REFORMS compliance checklist](../../artifacts/reforms-compliance-checklist-bpzdoi/) maps all 32 REFORMS items to gates, of which 20 can be verified by code and 12 only required as written fields; its split items (a split file with its group key, hashed into the run's id, and a gate that fails if model selection read the test split) are the ones the catalog's split check now meets in part. A SchwingNet experiment that will be compared with anything should therefore start with a frozen PREREG.md in the repository, by pull request, so that its hash is in git before the first job runs. Chapter 21 gives the run's mechanics and chapter 32 ([Beethoven](32-beethoven-the-repositories-the-roadmap-the-reforms-gates-and-the-store-release.md)) the gates.

## Testing any claim in six steps

The [Symphony and Beethoven Taxonomy Manual](https://claude.ai/artifact/RE4jtXqsFFgiZxhQLamUDR) was written for Symphony's own uniqueness claims, but its method is the one this chapter's comparators needed and in several cases did not get. Write the claim with its scope: one sentence, with the version, the material and the date it holds for. Write what would refute it before looking for support. Look for the refutation first, searching by mechanism rather than by product name, since a rival rarely uses the project's words; for SchwingNet that means searching for registration-free level labelling, vertebral counting on C-arm frames and level naming from radiographs, not for "SchwingNet". Run it when it can be run, since a claim that can be settled on the project's own data is cheapest settled there. Record the search even when it finds nothing, where, with which terms and on what date; a search that finds nothing makes a claim Inferred, never Verified. And when the claim fails, narrow it or drop it, changing the spec first and then every talking point that rests on it.

The manual's source ranking matters here too. The thing itself, a result file or code at a commit, gives the strongest grade; a paper with its data or code supports the counts one can re-derive from it, and the manual's advice is to quote counts rather than percentages; a blog, a search summary or a model's answer is a lead to the primary source, never the source. The comparator history of this chapter is that ranking ignored: 88.3 percent was quoted from a paywalled abstract, 73.3 percent from nowhere, and LevelCheck's convergence rate as if it were a naming accuracy. Each was caught by a later verified search, and the cost of each was a week of pages that set a SchwingNet number against nothing.

## What we know

A pixel near-duplicate check found no real C-arm test frame copied in any external corpus, and the catalog's split check now refuses a person or a frame in two parts before training; but person-level leaks (NHANES twice, a CTSpinoPelvic1K alias, 92 of 97 transitional persons in a lineage) were found by other checks, so all three are needed. A detection recall without its truth convention is not comparable, as the 5.2 percent that re-scored to 90.6 percent shows. Pooled and same-device figures say little about a device outside the pool, so a held-out-device figure, held out of frames, poses and fitted terms, sits beside every same-device one. LevelCheck and Manbachi's assisted 97.6 percent are ceilings; Huang's 88.3 and the unsourced 73.3 percent are not comparators; Kwak 2025, Hanhivaara 2024, Farshad 2013 and Rao 2024 stand within stated scopes. The power targets of 35, 22 and 19 persons, the 152 behind 97.6 percent and the six devices behind any device-level P re-derive exactly. Every transitional claim has near-zero power today; two comparisons are decided.

## What we do not know

Whether a CTSpinoPelvic1K record is one person. Which convention and operating point lie behind the 80.5 percent catalogue figure, and so how it relates to 90.6 percent. Which case count lies behind the September whole-frame naming figure. The source of the 73.3 percent comparator and of paper 2's 82.2 percent; the denominator behind Manbachi's 97.6 percent and Peh 1999's numerator; what Li 2016, Li 2017 and ABLSpineLevelCheck 2024 report. Whether Rao 2024's 0.964 is cadaver and phantom only. The true person-level transitional naming accuracy of any arm on real frames, which every power row needs as its effect size. How much clustering widens the intervals on real frames, beyond the design effect measured on the film arms. Whether the 70.8 percent naive coverage figure holds on the files. Whether the near-duplicate check against external corpora will be made code here.

## What would change our mind

A read of Li 2016, Li 2017 or ABLSpineLevelCheck 2024 that reports a registration-free naming accuracy on C-arm frames would replace the comparator section and the novelty sentences. A CTSpinoPelvic1K audit finding prone and supine records of one person under two ids would void every split that treated them as two, and re-open every figure trained on such a split. A results-catalogue row naming the same convention for 80.5 and 90.6 percent would turn the pair from two conventions into two operating points. A verified real-frame transitional cohort of 35 or more persons would end the near-zero-power statements; a seventh device in a held-out test would make a device-level P possible. A design effect on real C-arm frames well above 4 would raise every person target in this chapter.
