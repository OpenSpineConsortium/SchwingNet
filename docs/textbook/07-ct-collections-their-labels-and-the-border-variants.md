# 07 CT collections, their labels and the border variants they hold

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's own members: it cards the CT collections, their terms and how their labels and variants are counted, describes no stage of the method, and stays in this private repository until the provisional is filed.

A planner who reaches for a CT collection needs four answers before trusting a label from it: what is held and under which licence, how good its level and rib labels are, what its border variants say about each other, and which of its records must stay out of any truth pool. This chapter settles those four as far as the record allows and says where it does not. How the method uses ribs, sacra and transitional spines is Part III's ([chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md), [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md) and [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)); the whole holding is [chapter 04](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md), and the splits and leaks [chapter 09](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md).

## What is held

### CTSpinoPelvic1K, the project's own release

CTSpinoPelvic1K is the lab's own release of 802 abdominopelvic CTs, built from TCIA's CT COLONOGRAPHY (825 patients, CC BY 3.0), CTSpine1K and CTPelvic1K, with expert per-level labels for the vertebrae, ribs, sacrum, hips and femora (the holding report). v10 is dated 7 September 2026 and v11 18 September, and the records split 422 supine, 377 prone and 3 decubitus. In v11, by [Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/), the vertebrae come from a radiologist, the sacrum and hips are expert where they were fused, the ribs were corrected by a student, and a Castellvi consensus covers 33 records. No field reaches T1.

The reproduce map ([docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md](../../docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md)) places the release's Zenodo records, which the datasets verdict could not place from the artifacts alone (datasets row 25): 22797014 for v11, 22642578 for v10, 22139642 for the concept record and 22647933 for the build archive. The licences agree wherever they are stated: the labels are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, the CT CC BY 3.0 and the code Apache-2.0, and the share-alike clause is inherited and cannot be dropped ([the lab recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/)). The release page, which needs the network, was not checked.

The April 2026 first release held 1,153 records over 802 patients: 342 fused, 351 x 2 in separate mode, 89 spine-only and 20 pelvic-only (the reproduce map). On 22 September [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) left open whether two release ids can be one person, since colonography scans each patient prone and supine, and said an audit was running. Research directions (8 October) gives v11 as one record per patient, with the second acquisitions of 351 prone-and-supine pairs kept for a later study, and the cluster's verdict carries that over the earlier question ([the detectors contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md), row 37). Two loose ends remain: no record gives the audit's result, and the keying the train and validation split uses finds 801 distinct subjects among the 802 records, a gap no record explains; whether that mis-splits the corpus is chapter 09's concern.

The label traps are recorded because most once cost a result:

- Two label schemes exist, and mixing them raises no error. The wrong map returns T4 for `femur_left`, and pelvic incidence was once computed for the whole release against the legacy map. So identifiers come from the release's `dataset_labels.json` through `labels_for`, never from a fixed map.
- Identifier 29, v10's separately carved S1, is retired in v11.
- Volumes are stored P,I,R and are never reoriented when written.
- Case ids are zero-padded, so `zfill(4)` comes before any join (the lab recipe; [the handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)).

[The OpenSpine Board](../../artifacts/openspine-board-t3gfds/) of 11 September adds one more. Two label directories have voxel-identical vertebrae but rib blocks that differ by one, and hard-coded rib bases, right for one and wrong for the other, moved a rib-12-to-crest measure in 237 of 802 records, worst by 36 mm (a record, not a final value).

The release's spinopelvic means carry a version trap. The v11 release table gives a mean sacral slope of 36.6 degrees, against 36.5 in Veilleux's published series (the handoff; research directions). Two earlier values survive, each a record, not a final value: the board's 33.2, the mean of its Table II on 563 records after the plate fix of 11 September rejected 140 plates, set beside 34.1 in the one matched supine-CT series; and an estimator note's 33.1 from before that fix, whose 3.4-degree gap to Veilleux research directions finds to be 0.1 on v11. The cluster verdicts carry 36.6 with its version named (the detectors contradictions, row 39), and the number verdict, read again, keeps the board's 33.2 beside it with its own scope (number collision 173 on [the contradictions page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md)). The ledger sets no sacral slope current, and the first step of CT-2, recomputing the v11 means beside Veilleux, is not recorded as run, so no sacral slope here is final, and the board's reading of its gap as one of cohort, not method, waits on it.

Integrity is checked by content, never size, because CT 0533 once shipped truncated at a plausible size. The release rebuilds from TCIA by series identifier, verified over all 802 CTs, and 0533 was rebuilt on 23 September. The census found the release's group at 802 volumes in 2,052 image files, 1,006 with labels by a rule that matches annotation files to images by name and is no check of label truth.

### The public collections beside it

Its authors list 24 lumbarisations and 13 sacralisations, and 782 of its colonography volumes are CTSpinoPelvic1K persons (research directions). CTPelvic1K (Zenodo 4588403) adds pelvic labels. Its licence reads CC BY in one record and non-commercial in another, and no record reconciles its 172 CTs in the registry with the census's 178 image files (163 with labels).

VerSe 2019 and 2020, unified as VerSeFusion, holds 374 scans of 355 patients, with expert masks from C1 to L6 that include T13 and a variant table by two neuroradiologists (Loeffler 2021). Ten VerSe'20 series were never downloaded. Its licence reads CC BY 4.0 in the VerSeFusion readme and CC BY-SA 4.0 in a memory note, which the registry follows, so until the release page settles it the index marks redistribution unknown. Research directions records three traps:

- the `has_T13` field is wrong, 18 against the 6 T13 recorded;
- 39 of the 85 L6 masks it counts lack identifier 25;
- `vf_813` is never used for whole-spine work.

VerSe's L6 count has four readings: 85 scans (Liebl 2021), 47 cases (Sekuboyina 2021) and 87 read series on the literature-state page, and 50 in research directions. No file has counted the raw masks, so the record carries all four with their sources until CT-3's count settles them (datasets row 29). Its other variant figures differ in definition: research directions gives T13 6, sacralised 13, Castellvi II 27 and lumbar rib 12, and [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) gives four lumbar 3, Castellvi II to IV 109 and stump ribs 90, each to be carried with its own definition (transitional row 19).

TotalSegmentator is held in two releases. v2.0.1 (Zenodo 10047292, CC BY 4.0) has 1,228 subject directories. v3.0.0 (Zenodo 22688904) is carried as 1,935 scans, the registry's count, with the holding report's 1,939 subjects flagged beside it, since the units differ and only the release's own metadata would settle it (datasets row 28). The census counted 1,631 and 1,936 image files, and the split keying finds 1,170 and 1,735 distinct subjects (Counting From The Sacrum). 1,118 v3 CTs are byte-identical to v2 ones, and v2.0.1's ids s1406 to s1429 are byte-identical to different v3.0.0 ids, so keying by release id accepts up to 24 people merged or split. The labels are refined model output with no class for L6, T13 or a lumbar rib, and the sacrum excludes S1, so every variant here is a labeller's call, and two labellers agree on 14 L6 (research directions).

RibFrac holds 660 chest CTs with rib fracture labels under CC BY-NC 4.0, with expert rib masks from RibSeg v2 and the Moller masks (CC BY 4.0), and T13 4 called by a single labeller. RSNA 2022 cervical, 2,019 studies in a 203 GB zip under the Kaggle competition rules with per-level fracture labels, is the only source anchored in voxels at C1, C2 and C7, but it has never been unpacked, a header census refused every study for a junction count, and no model uses it (the registry; research directions). Of the project's own segmentation training sets, Dataset813 holds the 802 records with 18 L6 and stale flags, and Dataset815 1,176 cases with 62 L6 and 4 T13 by its manifest, never preprocessed. None of the project's CT segmentation runs has scored L6 above zero Dice ([the decisions page](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/); research directions), so L6 is carried by the labels but not yet learned. The open routes, CT-6 and CT-7, are [chapter 28](28-the-ranked-experiment-backlog.md)'s, and the method's handling of L6 is [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md)'s.

The literature-state page's 20,280 is not the harvest's size. So 20,280 is every counted CT block outside VerSe, C-ArmLSpine and the phantoms.

No radiologist has read any acquired tier-1 CT (datasets row 58): the harvest holds calls from two automatic labellers. Calls are not truth: a one-labeller screen matched reader truth in CTSpinoPelvic1K on 10 of 24 L6 and 0 of 52 T13, and on TotalSegmentator's 70 outside-read CTs the screen found only 12 to 17 of 28 L6, so a screen that misses that many yet flags about as many as the priors expect must carry false positives. Two expectations have already missed: AMOS kept about a third of its predicted people at 3 mm or thinner, and the NLST pilot's 9.3 percent T13 rate was five to fifteen times its prior and wrong.

The second tier, Spine-Mets-CT-SEG (54), Spinal-Multiple-Myeloma-SEG (67), LumVBCanSeg (185) and MedPelvis3D (99), waited on 8 October under the plan's stopping rule 4, and the reproduce map lists them as still to get, yet the census found a spine-metastasis set of 35,527 DICOM files, a difference no record reconciles. ## How far the rib and level labels can be trusted

### What the v5 review fixed

The [Rib Anchor Audit](../../artifacts/rib-anchor-audit-t7wr2h/) asks whether each rib sits on the vertebra its number claims, before and after two review cohorts, 169 rib cases and 107 of 113 spine cases finalized with no rejects, were merged into v4; in v5, 29 cases took both, 555 were unchanged and 802 label files were written. The merge was anatomy-pure, each cohort writing only its own identifier range, and it avoided laying the v2 spine ledger within reach, whose ids mean different bones, over v4, which would have silently relabelled vertebrae.

The review fixed the numbering:

- ribs on another thoracic level fell from 170 to 109 (36 percent fewer);
- the targeted off-by-one shift fell from 129 to 71, while the opposite shift barely moved (32 to 34), the signature of a real correction;
- ribs on their own vertebra rose from 5,369 to 5,536;
- ribs with no vertebra within reach fell from 38 to 12;
- cases with a consistent misnumbered run fell from 24 to 16.

Ribs on a lumbar vertebra rose from 21 to 25, so the review kept anomalies rather than smoothing them into normal.

[Spinal Border Variants](../../artifacts/spinal-border-variants-fye5ef/) then finished the job: ribs on the wrong vertebra fell from 109 to 19, misnumbered cases from 16 to 2, lumbar ribs still labelled rib 12 from 14 to 1, and the +1 offset family, which the review had left at 34, to 1. The cause was the rib labels' origin: TotalSegmentator numbered the cages from the bottom, so a thirteenth rib on L1 took the T12 rib's identifier until lumbar ribs got classes of their own (`rib_left_lumbar` and `rib_right_lumbar`, 74 and 75). The border page's before column repeats the audit's after column, so for these counts it supersedes the audit, 170 to 109 staying the record of the first step (transitional row 14). The misnumbered-case count is settled for v5: the number verdict, held when read again, carries 2 as the later page's update in the chain 24, 16, 2 (number collision 51 on the contradictions page), though [the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md)'s row 51 lists only the 24 and the 16, both open. v11 is not settled: no record says whether the renumbering reached the release, so 19 and 2 are not yet the error counts of the labels a model trains on.

### Truncation is the larger defect

The audit's main finding about the data is that 57 percent of the ribs in CTSpinoPelvic1K are clipped by the field of view. Its first pass found unequal left and right rib counts in 230 of 801 cases, far above any anatomical rate, and the pattern showed why:

- in 228 of 236 unmatched ribs, the rib present touches the top slice;
- the missing ribs peak at rib 5, not rib 12;
- only 3 cases differ in the last rib alone.

The patient lies askew to the gantry, so the field stops one side a level early. Dropping truncated ribs singly left 112 cases asymmetric; dropping them by rib number on both sides left 15 (1.9 percent), worth a human look, and took ribs with no vertebra within reach from 913 to 38. Any rib-count phenotype here must therefore be computed on the paired, uncut subset, or it measures positioning, not anatomy.

### What the non-rib-bearing count measures

The audit counted, per case, the non-rib-bearing vertebrae above the sacrum. Over 787 determinate cases (98 percent) it found 26 at four (3.3 percent), 734 at five (93.3 percent), 26 at six (3.3 percent) and one at zero, after a first version had put 71 at four before its rib-bearing rule was corrected. The border page later gives 734 at five, 35 at four, 26 at six and one more over 796 complete cases, which transitional row 6 reads as cell totals, not labelled LSTV cases: nine more determinate cases, all at four. The audit also says 20 cases failed its determinacy test, which does not square with 787 of 802.

The count fails as a check on the labels. On the 22 cases where a radiologist's column holds the same count, it agrees twice, missing all 7 sacralisations and 12 of 14 lumbarisations, because the label volume assigns most transitional cases L1 to L5 and a sacrum: the count recovers the labelling convention, not the patient, and its 93.3, 3.3 and 3.3 percent split is no phenotype prevalence. The audit says this happens regardless of anatomy, but 18 records carry an L6 label and its table has 26 at six, so the record reads it as most cases, not all (transitional row 9). The border page's standardised difference of 0.8 for the LSTV label sets a label against a count built from labels that already encode the variant (an L6 label alone separates at 4.7), so the audit's verdict stands (transitional row 7).

A lumbosacral field has no imaging gold standard for vertebral identity, which only a count from C2 on whole-spine imaging settles, so these scans cannot resolve it in principle (transitional row 8). The two sacral-boundary tests the audit proposed next were never recorded as run (transitional row 13); what the method uses instead is chapter 14's subject.

### Cases flagged for a person

The border page lists seven cases that need a person:

- 0008, labelled sacralisation, with its readers disagreeing, shifted by one in the renumbering, which is wrong if its L5 is sacralised;
- 0389 and 0720, with no thoracic vertebra labelled;
- 0344, whose left ribs reach L3 and L5, a segmentation defect;
- 0179 and 0412, whose cages disagree with themselves;
- 1153, whose fragmented right cage must be re-annotated.

These seven and the six spine reviews the audit left unfinalized are open as of v5, and no record says whether any was resolved before v11 (transitional row 28). The 15 asymmetric cases the audit marks for a human look have no recorded outcome either.

## Border variants: what the counts say

### Each count with its definition

The variants are counted under definitions that change from page to page, and the record's rule is to name the definition beside each figure, not to reconcile figures by arithmetic.

L6 in CTSpinoPelvic1K has three counts, each with its definition: 18 by release label, 16 by the readers' own count, and 29 persons in the registry of calls, the anomaly scan's list of labeller calls; seven records on which label and readers disagree are out of every truth pool until re-read (the literature-state page). The record settles the release's figure at 18: the data registry gives 802 CTs and 18 L6, with 31 agreeing passages (the concept ledger), research directions gives Dataset813 18 L6, and the verdicts carry 18 by release label with 16 by the readers (number collision 47, held when read again; transitional row 16; datasets row 26). The ledger does not use that collision, its quote from the decisions page carrying too few anchors, so it neither confirms nor overturns it, and the anatomical count waits on the re-reads. The decisions page's "about 22 of 802, roughly 2.7 percent" is an undated, approximate count of the training labels, not an earlier value of this figure, to be quoted only with its label version (datasets row 26). Where read, L6 is 2.0 percent (Wilson 1.3 to 3.3 percent; 16 of 789 countable), against a prior of 3.2 percent (Sun 2025); the unsourced "roughly 2 percent of people" on [Counting Against Two Anchors](../../artifacts/counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu/) is the corpus rate, not a prior (transitional row 15). The overnight page's 31 Castellvi II to IV and 34 flag positives may be nested within the 33 records with a non-normal LSTV label rather than rival counts; no record gives their membership (transitional row 18).

Lumbar ribs have no settled count. The border page gives 13 cases (v5, renumbered), Counting Against Two Anchors sixteen from a release it does not name, and the overnight page tables 16 while warning that its source disagrees with its review sheet; the ledger holds the figure open (row 49). Other figures count other things: 14 lumbar ribs by the audit's strict incidence check, 21 and then 25 ribs on a lumbar vertebra, and 10 lumbar ribs at or under 50 mm (transitional row 1). None of these is a denominator yet.

Rib 12 is bimodal. As a fraction of rib 11 its length falls into a main mode near 0.68 and a hypoplastic mode near 0.32, with 67 cases below 0.30, the shape a discrete developmental variant should take. But those 67 are a measurement, not a label: nobody has read them, the project's CTs hold no labelled stump-rib case, and with 57 percent of ribs clipped some are short only because the field cut them (transitional row 20).

The handoff adds a finding: a 3-D shape test separates T12 from L1 at AUC 0.990, which Counting Against Two Anchors gives with 97.3 percent accuracy over 1485 vertebrae. Research directions plans its own first stages on 1,486 T12 and L1 vertebrae from 798 cases, calls the published figure within-patient, not a single-vertebra result, and would report it only as a ceiling (transitional row 24). The record carries 1485 with the result and 1,486 only as a planned n, cannot tell whether the two are one set, and the ledger holds both open (rows 52 and 171); the one-hour rerun research directions puts first would fix the count. What the figure means for naming is [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)'s.

### Co-occurrence across the two borders

The border page's headline is an odds ratio of 19.6: of 796 complete cases, 667 are typical at both ends, 60 have a thoracolumbar variant only, 25 a lumbosacral variant only and 44 both, where independence predicts 9. The page calls this an association in segmentation-derived measures, not a causal or clinical result, and never states how it defines a thoracolumbar variant. [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) of 17 September asked the same question with flags read from label voxels and found co-occurrence "supported, smaller than first tabulated":

- CTSpinoPelvic1K: 4.00 [1.00, 16.03], on a both-cell of two;
- 280 fully graded VerSe subjects: 3.61 (exact 95 percent interval 2.08 to 6.27);
- morphology-only flags: 3.2 [1.8, 5.8];
- the 115 with both junctions in the field: 2.1 [0.9, 5.0].

Its first all-rows figure, 4.30, was inflated because the two flags shared vertebral numbering, and it puts published whole-spine series at 1.9 to 13.7. The record's verdict is that the graded estimates are the programme's number and that 19.6 stands only under its own definition and caveat. The two pages cannot be put in order, so this is a contradiction, not a supersession (transitional rows 3 and 4), and their thoracolumbar counts must not be merged either: 104 over 796 against 113 over 1,113 (802 CTSpinoPelvic1K and 311 VerSe) (row 5).

What the co-occurrence does not say matters as much:

- It is no prevalence: VerSe is enriched by design, and CTSpinoPelvic1K is a colonography cohort aged 50 and over.
- Its reference labels are, by the border page's own word, imperfect, so every effect size inherits their errors.
- The abdominopelvic field cannot see the upper ribs, so the thoracolumbar flags are partial by construction.
- The lumbosacral measures are proxies. The Castellvi span and gap are flat (under 0.2) and are not graded reads, and the sacral foramina counts are unusable as anatomy, with 3 left and 7 right on one symmetric sacrum, from truncation and defects of the counting step (transitional row 10); the four-pair rule both pages assume has no citation (row 11).
- Rib 12 length separates the lumbosacral label only weakly (d 0.55), which shows the co-occurrence a second way but does not locate the junction (row 8).

The X-ray programme ([docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md](../../docs/research/xr-from-3d/README.md)) takes variant odds only from collections screened whole ([docs/memory/from-3d-to-x-ray.md](../../docs/memory/from-3d-to-x-ray.md)), so what this corpus owes is a whole-cohort read: CT-3, the planned Castellvi read of all 802, is the test that settles co-occurrence here.

### Expected against called

The literature-state page sets calls beside the acquisition plan's priors: any LSTV at 9 percent (Hanhivaara 2020) to 18 percent (Nardo 2012), L6 at 3.2 percent and four-lumbar at 1.6 percent (Sun 2025), and T13 at 0.64 to 2.0 percent. CTSpinoPelvic1K's 789 lumbosacral-countable people among its 802 CTs should hold 25 L6, 13 four-lumbar and no T13, since the colonography field holds no whole rib cage; its readers have called L6 16 to 18 and four-lumbar 9, with 33 Castellvi-graded against 71 to 142 expected, a gap that is an unread cohort, not a measured shortfall. TotalSegmentator v2's 483 countable people among its 1,228 CTs should hold 16 L6 and 3 to 9 T13, against L6 22 and T13 4 in the registry of calls. Across the holding outside VerSe, C-ArmLSpine and the phantoms, about 11,770 countable people should hold 377 L6, 188 four-lumbar and 44 to 138 T13, against L6 146 and T13 14 in the registry of calls.

Two conclusions follow. L6 is short not in number but in verification, since no acquired CT has a read. T13 is scarce everywhere: the low end of its expectation falls short of the roughly 55 needed even before any call fails, and research directions counts seven T13 persons in all sources. Transitional persons have no settled count either: the literature-state page gives 194, about 190, 202 or 306 across all sources, by date and definition, and [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/) of 18 September cross-validates over 376 patients with transitional anatomy, giving no definition or source list. The record cannot tell whether these count the same persons (number collision 55), and the ledger holds every one of them open (row 55).

## Records out of the truth pools

The truth tiers are written down in the handoff: a read ranks above two labellers, two above one, and one above morphology alone; a segmenter's call is never promoted without a read; and a CT that cannot count is `no_count`, never ordinary. The record puts the first group below out of every truth pool and the tiers put the second below read truth; the other three are this chapter's inference, records whose labels or fields should wait for a person's look.

1. The seven CTSpinoPelvic1K L6 records on which release label and readers disagree, which the record does not list by id. Research directions sends them, with two more, to a third reader blind to the first two, and sets record 0033 aside as disputed in its planned L6 analyses; a re-read that moves a record changes the person split and reruns the leak audit before anyone is scored.
2. Every acquired harvest CT, which holds a call, not a read. One-labeller calls are not trusted at all.
3. The audit's 20 cases that are not determinate, excluded from its count because a missing level would fabricate a sacralisation, and the cases that wait on a person: the seven the border page flags and the 15 asymmetric ones.
4. Records whose own fields mislead: VerSe's `has_T13` and its 39 L6 masks without identifier 25, Dataset813's stale flags, and the census's labelled column, which follows a file-linking rule and shows TotalSegmentator unlabelled.
5. RSNA 2022's studies, refused for any junction count, and the 101 unidentified IDC series that failed conversion.
