# 10 Counting from an anchor along a sweep

Label: confidential-method. Results may be shown, mechanism may not, until the provisional is filed; this chapter stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere.

This chapter states SchwingNet's counting line whole: a level found by counting vertebral bodies along a sweep of overlapping C-arm exposures from an anchor the surgeon already trusts, the sacrum or C2, rather than by naming what one frame shows. It settles what a planner needs before spending on the line: what each stage does and which is learned; what the exactness figures measure, since every walk reported as 99.17 to 100% exact was scored with the annotation's own boxes and so tests the linker and the protocol, not deployment; what the decline rule and the R + C = 22 rule would guarantee, neither yet measured; which figures of 22 September may be quoted; and where the line fails, almost entirely in finding the bodies and the anchor on a C-arm the detector has not seen. No count has yet been made on a real stepped C-arm sweep with the project's own detector, and that is the experiment the line waits on.

## The counting line in one paragraph

The radiographer puts the anchor at the edge of the first field, the sacrum when sweeping up and C2 when sweeping down, and exposes, then moves the C-arm toward the target by one field minus two vertebral bodies, so two bodies of the previous image stay in view, and exposes again until the target is in the field. A detector, the one learned stage, finds the bodies and the anchor in each frame. Consecutive frames are linked by normalised cross-correlation restricted to within 0.6 of a body of the commanded step and form one chain, a panorama with one node per body; the target is the k-th node from the anchor, and the method asserts "four from the sacrum", never "L4". A last exposure with an instrument on bone is correlated along the chain: the body under the peak is the level, and the drop to the peak one body away is its confidence. When a frame fails to link or the anchor is not found, the walk is declined rather than guessed.

That is the method of the September proposal, [Counting Levels Under Fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/). The October [AIDaS Seed Grant Proposal](../../artifacts/aidas-seed-grant-proposal/) and the [Level Counting Visual Abstract](../../artifacts/level-counting-visual-abstract/) carry the same paragraph and figures, read as one description by [the OR-study contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md) (rows 51 and 52) and [the level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md) (row 58). The proposal is cited here for the method and the grant, which adds intervals and regional figures, for the results.

## The chain, stage by stage

### Expose

One field size is kept throughout, with no zooming part-way, an instruction only the superseded 18 September summary keeps. At the field of a 9-inch image intensifier, about 14 cm at the spine, three exposures cover most lumbar work and five or six reach the mid-thoracic spine ([Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/); [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/)). The step is commanded, not measured: nothing in the line reads where the C-arm went, which is why the overlap and the linking window carry so much of the design.

### Detect: the one learned stage

The detector is YOLO11m initialised from COCO, 20.1 million parameters, run on one 1,024-pixel exposure with three classes only, vertebral body, sacrum and C2, and asked no level name ([SchwingNet Figures, 22:50](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-xyz6kr/), Figure 6). On 18 September two of the three bodies the general detector missed among 243 on held-out film were the sacrum, the landmark the chain stands on, so the sacrum got a single-class detector of its own, trained on 4,526 sacrum-box images split by patient: 98.2% precision, 96.9% recall.

Box repair sits between detection and linking. It measures the typical gap between boxes in the frame, merges two boxes much closer than that and fills a much wider gap with the body its neighbours say is missing; held out, it took duplicated bodies from 3.03% to 0.61%. It uses the frame's own spacing and never a count, fills at most two bodies into one gap, and never extrapolates past the ends of a run, so a missing sacrum is never invented. In the reported stack of the [8 October handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/), the counting anchor comes from a YOLO11m vertebra and sacrum detector trained on 3,094 real frames from a render-trained start.

### Link: the restricted correlation and the alias

A column of vertebrae is periodic at the body pitch, and the matcher, as row d of [SchwingNet Figures, 20:12](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-jshksq/) says, has no labels and no anatomy outside the field. Unrestricted, correlation aligns a frame one body off as readily as on it, putting every name above it one off; the 17 September report calls this the single documented failure mode of automatic vertebral localisation, behind all 47 failures in Otake 2012.

The line searches only a band of +/- 0.6 of a body around the commanded step. Row d marks the margin itself: the alias peaks lie a whole body either side of the true match, so 0.4 of a body separates the band's edge from each alias, and because the step is commanded and not measured, a move off by more than that lets the alias in and the frame may lock one body off, a wrong-level operation. A planner should read the window's tolerance as 0.4 of a body of step error, not 0.6: between the two, the true peak and one alias both lie in the band and only their heights separate them, and past 0.6 only the alias is left.

The project's largest single error was here: the first linking rule accepted a block of shared bodies only if every pair was confirmed, so one bad box collapsed the overlap and every shared body was entered again as new. Requiring a majority took cross-frame duplicates from 21.8% to 3.0% and chain naming from 67.4% to 91.8%; with a human's boxes the two rules agree to the second decimal.

### Chain and count

The linked frames make one chain, one node per body, and the count is an integer traversal of it from the anchor; in the proposal's testable words, the count consumes box positions and never reads a class. The artifacts number the chain differently. The 22 September composite numbers the sacrum 0 and the bodies above it 1 to 8; the 22:50 build's Figure 3 numbers 23 bodies from the sacral anchor; the [Vertebral Count Anomaly Rule](../../artifacts/vertebral-count-anomaly-rule/) counts C2 as the first body, making T1 the 7th and L1 the 19th; and another page counts C1 and puts T1 at the 8th (level-naming row 42). Every count in a test, a table or a readout must state its convention, or two correct counts will differ by one.

### Confirm: the instrument frame and its margin

A final exposure with an instrument on bone is placed on the chain by correlation. In the 22:50 build's Figure 4 the instrument frame, three bodies wide, is correlated with the panorama at every position; the peak gives the body by its count from the anchor, 4, and the margin, the drop to the alias peak one body away, is 0.18. Row f of the 20:12 build says why the margin is the confidence: on a periodic column the peak is always high, and what places the field is how far it stands above the best peak one body away, there 0.11 under a peak of 0.96.

## Which stage is learned, version by version

The artifacts give three answers, successive designs rather than an open disagreement (level-naming row 13). On 17 and 18 September the answer was a name. The 17 September report decoded a linked chain as one sequence at 95.5%, against 85.8% for each node's own argmax and 93.8% for counting from the landmark (a record, not a final value), and judged the shape reading at least as good as counting; the 18 September results page said nothing counts from the landmark, which only fixes the chain, and the superseded [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/) summary that the method never counts (OR-study row 36).

From the runs of 21 September the proposal, then the grant, reversed that: only the detector is learned and nothing learned decides the level. The 22 September report, [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/), sits between. Its composite shades detect and count as learned, labels count "ordinal from the anchor; named by the level model", and draws that model in row e, a ResNet-18 encoder over a crop of each body and a bidirectional recurrent head over two to nine vertebrae, with a posterior over 27 levels; the 20:12 build's row b shades detect alone. The 8 October handoff holds the current state: two learned detectors, a learned level encoder and recurrent head, and a count and coupling with no learned parameters, giving the ordinal from the detected sacrum and a stop signal. [Chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) holds the naming line and [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md) how the two coexist.

## Why the count is exact wherever the boxes are right

The count has no estimation step of its own. If every body in every frame is boxed, the anchor is found and each link lands on the true offset, the chain is the column's bodies in order and its k-th node is the k-th body, so reading the target is integer arithmetic. Error enters in only three ways: a box missed or added, which moves every node beyond it by one; a link that lands a body off, which does the same; and a walk that never reaches the target. The restricted window addresses the second and the decline rule the third; nothing in the count addresses the first. That is why the proposal calls deployment a detection question, and why exactness scored with the annotation's boxes measures the linker and the protocol, not the system. Three rulings read the C2 walks against C2 detection, the counting grade against held-out detection, and the ledger's naming headline against its sacrum-anchored count as figures of different stages (OR-study row 34; [the detector contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/detectors-architecture-and-the-training-plan.md), row 44; level-naming rows 43 and 45).

Exactness given the boxes is still not 100%: four of 1,209 walks from C2 went wrong, and thoracic and cervical exactness fall well below lumbar with the same boxes, so the link, the annotation or the rule that decides a walk reached its target also fails; no artifact breaks the wrong walks down. Nor is any of it a claim of superiority over surgeons assisted by CT registration at 97.6% (Manbachi 2018), as level-naming row 33 rules.

### With the annotation's boxes

The proposal and the grant give five arms, scored with the annotation's boxes on public labelled CT renders and real radiographs. A walk is exact when the mark lands on the body truly k from the anchor, among walks that reached the target, and the C2 arms used three bodies of overlap.

These all come from a manuscript in preparation and no page ties them to a result file: the 22 September report's 54 checked claims are its own numbers, not the arms, and neither that file nor the manuscript's numbers manifest is in this repository, so they stay Reported. The C2 arms say nothing about finding C2, which no detector has yet done on real film (The anchor, below).

### With a detector's boxes

Few counts have used a detector's own boxes, and none a stepped C-arm sweep. With the boxes of a detector trained on renders alone, exactness fell from 100% to 30.06% on 79 held-out spines; that detector found 71.67% of bodies on real film. The grant sets that against 99.14% for the film-trained detector, a figure the record has not settled: the 18 September pages give 97.6% and 99.2% for the same three misses in 243 (level-naming row 18). On 17 September the film-trained detector, run on degraded renders, gave a complete chain on 0.4% of spines, so that report's render arms measured the linker and the decoder, not the pipeline.

The superseded 18 September summary alone records the sweep with the detector's boxes before the linking fix, a record, not a final value: the chain survived on 20% of 25 held-out films instead of 100%, and sweeping did worse than not, 66.0% against 72.3% at two vertebrae and 56.2% against 93.8% at four, with 17.9% of bodies duplicated. The ledger leaves chain survival with the detector's boxes open, 20% there against 40% in the results page ([the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-206-sweep-chain-survival-with-the-detectors-own-boxes), row 206). After the fix and box repair, the results page names 90.4% of three-vertebra spot films on held-out real radiographs with the detector's boxes, against 93.9% with a human's (level-naming row 19), a name placed on the sweep, not a count.

The record's counts on real C-arm frames are all within one frame, from a detected sacrum. The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) gives sacrum-anchored counting on the same device's 56 test frames at 96.3% of frames exact, and on devices held out 80.0% (72.1 to 86.8) of 240 scored calls on COBRA near-AP frames at 41.9% coverage and 84.3% (80.0 to 93.9) of 185 on DeepFluoro's six cadavers at 68.8%. The handoff says the count alone names 97.0% of anchored bodies, the coupling with the level model adding 1.2 points, about three cases (level-naming row 5), and the [demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md) gives the same 97.0% on C-ArmLSpine, neither with an n or an interval. That is the record's nearest approach to a deployed count, within a frame that already shows the sacrum.

## The decline rule

The proposal states the rule in three sentences: when a frame fails to link, or the anchor is not found, the system declines that walk; a decline is a repeat exposure, not a wrong level; and wrong answers are converted into declines at a rate the operator chooses. In place of a fixed accuracy it offers a measured bound on wrong-level output at a stated decline rate on held-out cases, re-measured whenever the device, protocol or population changes, and an audit trail of each case.

A decline is a repeat exposure because a broken link leaves the walk short of the target and a missing anchor leaves nothing to count from: there is no ordinal to report, so none is reported, and the operator exposes again from the last frame that linked or recentres on the anchor. A guess across the break would be a wrong level, the never event the line exists to prevent.

The rule is unmeasured. No artifact gives a decline rate at any operating point, the bound on held-out cases, or the share of walks that never reached the target, all deliverables of the grant's phantom, cadaver and shadow-mode work; and the guarantee says nothing about how many cases allow a count (OR-study row 49). The one measurement of what a repeat buys concerns naming and is a record, not a final value: on 17 September, 4,792 frames re-shot a quarter vertebra away scored 81.3% against 82.8% for those they replaced, so that page answered a declined name by counting or sweeping further, not by reshooting. In the reported stack the refusals are the stop signal, which caught 5 of 6 erring frames (the demo plan, no interval), and the abstention the demo overlay marks "?".

## The R + C = 22 rule

The [Vertebral Count Anomaly Rule](../../artifacts/vertebral-count-anomaly-rule/), a canvas stamped 20:40 UTC on 22 September, makes counting check itself. With C1 left out, as the datasets leave it, an ordinary column holds 23 bodies, C2 to L5, and for any target the bodies above it to C2, R, plus those below it, C, must number 22. A sixth lumbar or thirteenth thoracic body gives 23 and a missing body 21; either is reported as an anomaly, with the ordinal from C2 and from the sacrum and no name, because a transitional column cannot be named by counting alone.

The page states its limits: it cannot say which vertebra is extra, so it flags the column and not the level; a column with both an extra and a missing vertebra sums to 22 and passes; and its error is the counting error compounded over the whole column. Two more follow from the arithmetic: it needs both ends counted along one chain, which only a whole-column sweep or a whole-spine film gives, so a lumbar sweep from the sacrum, observing C and never R, cannot apply it; and it inherits every detector miss across 23 bodies, not the few between anchor and target.

Whether the rule is in the method is not settled (level-naming row 41). The composite stacked at 19:31 drew it on an ordinary column and on one with a thirteenth thoracic body, flagged at 23; at 20:12 the figures page re-rendered those rows to remove it; the canvas's stamp is UTC and the pages give no zone, so the two cannot be ordered; and no later page read, the 22:50 build, the proposal, the grant or the handoff, mentions it. Its flag rates are unmeasured. The two-anchor design of the sacrum and the lowest rib-bearing body belongs to [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md); [the transitional contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/transitional-vertebrae-and-border-variants.md) rule the two different rules, not rival claims (rows 22 and 23).

## The sweep protocol and what its knobs were measured to do

The field, the overlap and the bodies in a frame were measured twice, and the later set is the one to carry: the 18 September summary says an earlier version of its page had scored training patients and that all was re-measured with the exclusion enforced by the tool, and the level-naming contradictions read the 17 September figures as superseded (rows 15 to 17).

Overlap is the knob. On held-out spines the canonical 18 September page gives the chain complete on 88.3% of spines at half a body of overlap, 96.7% at one, 97.5% at two and 82.5% at four, at more than twice the exposures, and the superseded summary 86.7%, 95.0%, 96.7% and 80.0%. Level-naming row 15 flags that column, so neither set is settled, but both put the best at two bodies and the worst at four, for the page's reasons: one body gives a single vertebra to agree on, enough to align a frame a level off, and above two every extra frame is another link to join wrongly. Two bodies is the protocol, and the step of one field minus two bodies follows from it.

The superseded summary alone keeps the exposures and two-vertebra accuracy beside each overlap, the table row 15 carries for those columns: 2.5 exposures and 92.1% at half a body, 2.6 and 97.5% at one, 3.0 and 98.3% at two, 6.9 and 89.8% at four. It alone keeps the field-width trade-off at one body of overlap, 3.8 exposures for a collimated 100 mm field at 97.1%, 2.6 for a 9-inch field at 97.5% and 2.0 for a 12-inch field at 97.1%, so width matters less than the exposures it implies. The 17 September report alone holds two-vertebra naming at five overlaps, 86.6%, 93.6%, 95.1%, 95.2% and 93.2% from half a body to four, with its knee at 1.5 to 2 bodies; it is the protocol's operating point, not the knee, that matches the only clinical stitching series, four to six exposures at 20 to 30% intended overlap (row 54). All of these are a record, not a final value.

Four vertebrae in the frame answers the thoracolumbar junction: on the 17 September renders a thoracolumbar-variant spine was named at 50% at T11 and T12 with two vertebrae in view and 100% with four, in cells of 3 and 7 vertebrae from the evaluation re-measured the next day, so the rule rests on a record, not a final value.

Whether a person can hold the step is unmeasured. The grant lists the distribution of manual step error from the phantom sweeps, tested against the 0.6-body window, among its deliverables (OR-study row 51); by row d's geometry the test should be made against 0.4 of a body.

## The audit of the synthetic pipeline

The 22 September report audited the synthetic data behind the detectors. The device cards do sample real distributions: of 114 device x parameter pairs, 51 are drawn per frame from a fitted Gaussian copula conditioned on water-equivalent thickness and 9 come from DICOM headers. But a clip on 8 to 11 correlated terms at once saturated rather than truncated, pinning out-of-band draws to the boundary, so a styled frame reached only 1.7 to 5.1% of each device's joint distribution. A replacement that rejects only combinations a device never produces, by the typicality statistic the cards are validated against, reaches 99% of the joint; it was not adopted, and no page reports the A/B meant to decide (OR-study row 64; [the verification contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 91). Which devices had fitted cards that day the record cannot tell (detector row 11). The audit bears on counting only through detection; [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) holds the renderer.

## Where the counting line fails

### Detection on a device held out

Detection is the binding constraint, as the [22 September report](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/) says: finding the bodies, not naming them, is what fails, and recall compounds over the eight to twelve bodies of a walk. On C-ArmLSpine held out, the reported stack's record gives 231 of 287 vertebrae, 80.5% (74.1 to 86.8), at confidence 0.74, 93.9% on the repaired denominator ([the demo plan](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md); [docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md)); the 22 September ledger and report give 90.6% of the same 287 at confidence 0.25 under outline truth, where body-box truth gave 5.2%, and 83.9% of sacra. Level-naming row 9 carries 80.5% as the later figure, but [the renders contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) cannot tell the two apart until the run record behind 80.5% is read (row 12), so neither is settled as the held-out number. Both miss the 97% target set in advance, as every held-out cell does.

Adding 24 frames of the device gives 84.0%, and the curve plateaus at about 82 to 85%, 95% of it reached by 10 to 12 frames; renders alone find 0.3% of the bodies (1 of 287) at 0.74 and 31.7% at 0.25, a record, not a final value, since the detectors contradictions cannot tell it from the ledger's render-only values (row 6). On held-out film a lower threshold did not help: both 18 September pages give one miss rate, about 2.4%, at confidence 0.25, 0.10 and 0.05, while chain survival fell from 40% to 8% of films, figures the record holds open until the evaluation file is read (verification row 14; the concept ledger, rows 116 and 206).

If misses were independent, an eight-body chain would carry every body about 18% of the time at 80.5% recall, 45% at 90.6% and 78% at 97%; box repair and the overlap's second look raise those figures by an amount never measured on a real sweep. The report's comparison with published C-arm detection at 0.96 to 0.98 carries no source, and its 28-point sim-to-real tax said to match the field is refuted: a page of the day before had withdrawn the one matching citation, which measured surgical phases, and says 71.67% has no published peer. The comparator to carry is Rao 2024's in-domain 0.964 on 578 images with 20 in test, possibly cadaver and phantom only (detector rows 17 and 68; [the programme contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/programme-direction-handoffs-and-onboarding.md), row 74).

### The anchor

The anchor is the body most often missed, as the 18 September misses showed. On a device held out entirely nothing reaches counting grade: the ledger's best clean detection numbers run from 18 to 50% on COBRA and 32% on an image intensifier to 84% for the sacrum and 91% for vertebrae on C-ArmLSpine under outline truth, and 88 to 91% for the sacrum on one DeepFluoro specimen with a render-only detector over three seeds, which the record has not settled (renders row 6). Some of what reads as finding the sacrum is layout: the training set's median sacrum box, held fixed, hits the true sacrum on 85.7% of C-ArmLSpine test frames.

C2 is not demonstrated on any real image: render-trained arms find 85 to 88% of C2 on renders and none on film, and the real-trained arm 0 to 8% of 25 instances, so every C2 walk in the arms rests on the annotation's box. Orientation is assumed, not inferred: an inversion or a 180-degree flip breaks every detector at once. A missed anchor is the decline rule's second trigger, so these figures set how often the line would decline before it counts anything.

### Transitional columns and the lumbarised S1

An ordinal becomes a name only through an inventory. Row a of the 20:12 build shows the problem: the window that holds L4 to L2 above the sacrum of an ordinary spine holds L5 to L3 above a lumbarised S1, with the same count from the sacrum. Row f shows the effect. Its column carries a sixth lumbar body; the readout names the target T6, the 12th body from C2 on the ordinary inventory, and gives it as the 13th counting up from the caudal end, which on the ordinary inventory would be T5. The anchors disagree by one level, as R + C = 22 exists to catch: R is 11, C is 12, the sum 23.

The proposal's answer is that no name is formed and transitional anatomy is counted the way it is operated on, which holds only if the operative plan is itself an ordinal from the same anchor; no artifact tests that. A partially lumbarised S1 has no label anywhere in the corpus, 5,110 images carrying a sacrum box and none an S1 box, so no detector can yet be asked where a lumbarised S1 ends and the sacrum begins, the call that decides the ordinal; readers were drawing them on 18 September, and no later page reports them done. That page's rule for the call is to delimit at the disc, not at the fusion, since expert readers disagree about which endplate is sacral (kappa 0.32 to 0.38). [Chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) holds the two-anchor design and abstention.

### The error compounding over the column

Every miss shifts every node beyond it, so error grows with distance from the anchor, as the regional exactness shows even with the annotation's boxes. On whole-spine films the ledger's film keypoint detector completes a T1 to L5 chain on 73.2% of all 608 AASCE films and on 54.7% and 57.8% of the 128 and 180 outside its training, breaking at L5, which the pelvis overlaps on erect AP films, and at a single missed mid-thoracic body. That is a film, not a sweep, but it is the compounding the R + C = 22 page names. No number exists on a cervical or thoracic C-arm frame (state-of-knowledge).

### The AP view

The reported stack's largest error is a counting error. Zero-shot AP naming on C-ArmLSpine is 276 of 631, 43.7%, a whole-column shift one level cranial, against 595 of 617, 96.4% (92.3 to 98.4), on lateral frames (the demo plan); the ledger marks 43.7% current, superseding the 22 September report's figure ([the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine), row 107). The [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) and [docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md](../../docs/research/ct-priors-for-2d-detection.md) read it as a counting error, not an appearance error, and target it with a counting prior and AP renders that carry anchors; that the prior fixes the shift is not yet measured.

## What version 1 carries, and the recorded sweep

Version 1 carries two pieces near the line, not the line. [src/schwingnet/chain.py](../../src/schwingnet/chain.py) is the counting prior: it names one frame's bodies as one ordered column by a Viterbi pass over hypotheses of 11 to 13 thoracic and 4 to 6 lumbar bodies, adding each body's level posterior, the spacing likelihood of each neighbouring pair under a prior fitted on the segmented CTs, and the hypothesis's prior. A missed body is a two-level step paid for by a penalty, and the decoder abstains when the best labelling beats the best different one by less than a margin, so a close call between a standard column and one with L6 shows as "?". It counts within a frame and links none. [src/schwingnet/sweep.py](../../src/schwingnet/sweep.py) is the recorded sweep's causal tracker, joining each frame's boxes to tracks by overlap.

The talk's recorded sweep is C-ArmLSpine's case134, a cone-beam projection sweep of 301 usable frames with the gantry turning from +122 to -52 degrees around the patient, not stepping along the spine, and with no detector in version 1 its boxes are truth boxes ([docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md](../../docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md)). No real stepped sweep is held; the request letters of 21 October ask for the only two collections of overlapping C-arm exposures described anywhere ([docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md](../../docs/research/xray-dataset-requests.md)). The proposal's phantom sweeps, with the linker and the detector running live on an anthropomorphic lumbar phantom, are the first place the whole line could run.

## What we know

The counting line is a design with one learned stage: expose from an anchor, detect, link within a restricted window, chain, count an ordinal, confirm by a margin, and decline when a link or the anchor fails. Since 8 October the reported stack pairs it with naming: its count and coupling have no learned parameters, while its detectors, level encoder and recurrent head are learned.

With the annotation's boxes the count was exact on 99.17 to 100% of reached walks in the four tabled arms of walks, 1,205 of 1,209 from C2 on real radiographs among them, falling to 95.45% thoracic and 83.84% cervical; these Reported figures measure the linker and the protocol, not deployment. Both 18 September pages find the chain most often whole at two bodies of overlap and least often at four.

Detection is the binding constraint: on a device held out the record gives 80.5% or 90.6% of vertebrae by threshold and truth convention, both short of 97%; the sacrum is the body most often missed, and C2 has not been found on real film. The counts on real C-arm frames are within one frame: 96.3% of frames exact on the same device, 80.0% and 84.3% of scored calls on two devices held out, and 97.0% of anchored bodies for the count alone. The unfinished 22:50 figure state is the one to carry.

## What we do not know

Nobody has run the line on a real stepped C-arm sweep with the project's own detector. No artifact gives a decline rate, the bound on wrong-level output at a stated decline rate, or the share of walks never reached. Only five rows of the twelve arms are tabled, and no result file behind them is in this repository. Whether an operator's step stays within 0.4 of a body is unmeasured, as are the flag rates of R + C = 22, whose place in the method is not settled.

How a sacrum-anchored count behaves on a transitional column in the operating room is unknown: no S1 label exists, and nothing tests whether the operative plan maps to an ordinal from the same anchor. The record has not settled the held-out recall, the film-trained detector's recall (97.6, 99.14 or 99.2%), the miss rate across thresholds, chain survival with the detector's boxes, the chain-intact rates by overlap, the case count (148 or 141), which image Figure 5 carries, the typicality A/B, or which devices had fitted cards on 22 September; A8 was still open on 8 October. Whether the counting prior fixes the AP shift is untested, and no number exists on a cervical or thoracic C-arm frame.

## What would change our mind

If the phantom sweeps show an operator's step error often above 0.4 of a body, the commanded-step window is unsafe as designed and the line needs a measured step or more overlap. If a stepped sweep with the deployed detector gives per-walk exactness near the annotation-box figures at a decline rate a surgeon accepts, deployment is only a detection question; if exactness falls well below 99% on walks whose detections were right, the linker is the problem.

If a detector reaches 97% per body on a device held out entirely, the constraint moves to the anchor and the transitional column. If the manuscript's numbers manifest disagrees with the five tabled rows, the arms here must be corrected. If the typicality sampler's A/B shows better held-out detection, the device cards become a lever on detection. If the counting prior removes the AP shift, the within-frame count becomes version 1's answer on AP and the sweep's place narrows to fields with no anchor in view. If S1 labels and a two-reader transitional series show sacrum-anchored counts misplacing L6 columns, the line must withhold the name more often or count from two anchors. If verse_drr is re-rendered or shown correctly spaced, row d's millimetres become quotable.
