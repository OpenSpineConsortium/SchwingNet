# 20 Readiness for the operating room: device transfer, latency and the in-domain study

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter is for the project's members only: it gives results, plans and review routes, names each method by what it is for and leaves how any stage works to Part III, and it stays in this private repository until the provisional is filed on 30 October 2026.

This chapter settles three things a planner needs before promising anything about an operating room. First, whether the stack can count on a C-arm it has never seen: not yet, because naming carries across devices far better than detection, and no held-out device reaches the 97 percent counting grade. Second, what the talk's recording will and will not show: a causal replay of a real C-arm sweep with per-frame compute time against a 97 ms budget, a number that exists only where the exported model matches the reference. Third, what it takes to find out where the method would be used: the in-domain case the proposal, the seed grant and the two IRB drafts make, from a retrospective collection with a sealed test set and a per-body recall endpoint to phantom, cadaver and shadow-mode steps in 2027. It also names the numbers the record ruled out of the manuscript's method, and what the superseded Protocol 1 alone still holds.

## The question the ledger asked

The [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 22 September 2026, canonical in the verification and OR-readiness cluster, asks the question this chapter inherits: can it count vertebrae in an operating room it has never seen? Its last section answers yes only for the device it was trained on, where naming on real operating-room laterals is high, cross-validated and free of any leakage found; on a device held out entirely, counting-grade detection, at least 97 percent, is "not supported yet". The October record agrees: the pre-stated 97 percent detection target "is met by no held-out cell" ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), section 0, which cites the earlier programme's status file by row). The ledger is a snapshot of one morning with trainings and re-scores still running, and by the handoff of 8 October nothing after 27 September had changed the banks or the models ([Verification benches](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md), row 82), so none of its running lines is known to have reported a result.

## Device transfer

### Naming carries across devices; detection does not

Naming, the stage that gives a found vertebra its level ([chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)), was scored on C-ArmLSpine, the one real intraoperative human set held, with that device never seen in training: 595 of 617 lateral vertebrae, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4] (status file A2.1). Adapted to the device by five-fold training on its own cases it reaches 97.4 percent, a paired difference of +1.0 points [-1.9, 4.0] (A3.7). On AP frames zero-shot naming gives 276 of 631, 43.7 percent, a one-level cranial shift, which the concept ledger marks current ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine)). These are the reported stack's figures as the demo plan gives them with their status-file rows; the status file itself was not read.

The ledger's September naming figures are records, not final values: 98.2 percent exact on whole lateral frames, five-fold over 148 cases [95.7, 100], 92.9 percent [89.6, 95.6] with three vertebrae in view and 72.8 percent with one. The one-vertebra figure has been replaced: the October record gives 74.8 percent with one body in view ([Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/); [How far the verdicts hold](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#how-far-the-verdicts-hold), collision 6). The case count is a confirmed contradiction, 148 against 141 on Counting From The Sacrum ([the ledger's line](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-104-number-of-c-armlspine-cases-in-the-five-fold-cross-validation-behind-the-982-exact-level-figure)), and whether 98.2 percent was replaced by 97.4 percent or measures the coupled count, the record cannot tell (the same section). Neither the 98.2 percent nor its case count is quoted here as current.

Naming also holds on populations no level model saw. On COBRA, radiotherapy patients imaged on an Elekta XVI, a level model that never saw the device named 90.3 percent [86.1, 93.9] of 599 scored lateral calls, and 89.2 percent [84.7, 92.5] of 240 when a detector that never saw the device found the vertebrae. Adapting a level model to one device cost it the others: with the boxes given, the model adapted to C-ArmLSpine named 43.5 percent [36.4, 50.1] of 904 COBRA lateral vertebrae against 79.9 percent [74.5, 84.6] zero-shot. A model adapted to the hospital's devices will have to be scored again on every device it was not adapted to.

Detection, finding each vertebra in the frame, is the binding constraint. With C-ArmLSpine held out of the detector's training, recall is 231 of 287 vertebrae, 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8], and 93.9 percent on a repaired denominator, with no count or interval (A6.1, A6.5). The ledger's re-score of the same 287 vertebrae gave 90.6 percent under a matched box convention, at another operating point; two cluster pages read the October figure as superseding it, and the OR study's page reads the pair cannot-tell until the manuscript's claims file names the device, convention and denominator behind 80.5 percent ([The case for the OR study](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/the-case-for-the-or-study-proposal-seed-grant-and-irb-protoc.md), row 33). As in [chapter 3](03-what-is-measured-and-what-is-claimed.md), 80.5 percent is graded Measured and 90.6 percent is quoted only as the September re-score, a record, not a final value.

Other devices are further away. The ledger's best clean held-out figures, September records, not final values, are 18 to 50 percent on COBRA, 32 percent on an image intensifier, 88 to 91 percent for the sacrum on one DeepFluoro specimen with a render-only detector over three seeds, and on C-ArmLSpine 84 percent for the sacrum and 91 percent for vertebrae, the 90.6 percent record. Counting from the sacrum on a held-out device named 80.0 percent [72.1, 86.8] of 240 COBRA near-AP vertebrae while attempting only 41.9 percent of them, and 84.3 percent [80.0, 93.9] of 185 on DeepFluoro's six cadavers at 68.8 percent coverage, with the exposure described below. Coverage belongs beside each of these, since a count that declines on most frames is at once safe and of little use, and the in-domain study's decline rate exists to measure that trade ([chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md) holds abstention). At the top of the spine, the grant's 1,205 of 1,209 walks from C2 on 262 real radiographs, 99.67 percent [99.16, 99.91], used the annotation's boxes, while the ledger's detectors found 0 to 8 percent of 25 labelled C2 instances on real film, two quantities rather than a contradiction (row 34): counting from C2 in an operating room waits on a detector that finds C2.

### The levers: real frames, frames of the device, and scale

The ladder the demo plan copies (A6.9 to A6.12, A8.2) runs from renders alone, 0.3 percent (1 of 287), to 80.5 percent once real frames of other devices are in training, and to 84.0 percent with 24 frames of the held-out device added, with a plateau of about 82 to 85 percent and 95 percent of the gain by 10 to 12 frames; no rung but 80.5 percent has an interval. The canonical [Any-View Spine Plan](../../artifacts/any-view-spine-plan/) reads it as "real frames from any device carry most of it" and sizes the test of a new device at about 25 real frames. The ladder shows no route to 97 percent: a few dozen frames of the device buy three to four points and flatten, so the remaining twelve points or more must come from something it did not try, which the protocols bet is in-domain data at archive scale. That reading is Inferred; [chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md) and [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md) say what each rung trained on.

Scale is a lever of its own, separate from noise and display. On whole frames the held-out detector finds most of C-ArmLSpine's vertebrae; on the magnified windows that stand in for a surgeon's field it loses most, while the same-device control keeps most. The ledger's re-scored crop cells give 35.9 percent of 566 vertebrae in three-vertebra crops and 18.6 percent of 287 in one-vertebra crops, against 82.5 percent for the control, but its own list of what is not supported calls the crop re-score pending, so these are to be run again, not the stack's recall in a surgeon's field. Downsampling whole films costs an in-domain detector little; magnified fields cost a detector that never saw the device most ([the renders page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md), row 28). The in-domain study should report its primary endpoint by field size, which its protocol does not yet list among its strata.

### Robustness, orientation and a phantom on six C-arms

One corruption at a time, at five severities on the real test frames, the pipeline degraded gracefully: quantum noise to 300 photons a pixel, read noise to sigma 0.06, rotation to 15 degrees either way, and any gamma, magnification, obliquity, collimation, shutter or instrument in the tested range each cost under a tenth of sacrum recall. An inverted image and a frame turned through 180 degrees broke every detector at once, the same-device sacrum rate falling from 98.2 percent to 14.3 and 37.5 percent, so the unit that captures the C-arm's video must deliver frames the right way up and with the right polarity, and the prototype's bench log should show it. The only real frames of many devices held are a sawbone phantom on six C-arms (SpineSAC) with no projected truth, which the ledger calls "a sheet to look at, not a number to quote"; on it a detector adapted to C-ArmLSpine alone boxed vertebrae on three of the five well-populated machines while the pooled detector, at 90 to 98 percent on its own devices, mostly did not.

### Styled frames are not yet real frames

The ledger's route to one network for any C-arm, a synthetic corpus styled per device and tested by leaving each device out, stopped short: styled frames were "not yet statistically inside the real domain". On the Cios Fusion card the real-to-styled noise-power distance was 0.111 against a real-to-real floor of 0.015, the grey histogram 3.1 against 0.14, the gradient 0.47 against 0.11 and the halo 0.23 against 0.16; on the Pulsera card noise power was 0.20 against 0.04. The page contradicts itself on whether any device beyond the Cios Fusion was fitted, and whether the styled trainings ever finished the renders page cannot tell (row 53). [What Is Left After Physics](../../artifacts/what-is-left-after-physics/), canonical on renders, and chapter 13 hold what a renderer leaves.

### What the leakage audit covers

The audit found no pair above its similarity bar between test frames and 37,165 film images, 29,484 FluoroCap panels or 1,445 detector-training images, and splits disjoint by case (C-ArmLSpine 105, 15 and 28; COBRA 110, 8 and 27; DeepFluoro 4, 1 and 1 specimens). It audits pixels and cases, not persons: the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) records C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases as sessions not provably one person each. One fixed box finds the sacrum on 85.7 percent of C-ArmLSpine test frames, 58.5 percent of COBRA's and 13.5 percent of DeepFluoro's, so a C-ArmLSpine sacrum rate is partly layout; the ledger's explanation, that surgeons centre the field the same way every time, has no source and is dropped (Verification benches, row 71). The retrospective protocol's split by patient is the stricter rule ([chapter 9](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md)).

## Numbers ruled out of the manuscript's method

The ledger ruled one headline out in its own words: "The 100% DeepFluoro sacrum number is not the manuscript's method." That detector had the test device's frames in training and was built on a render-trained model whose renders and calibration had both seen the test specimen: 255 of 569 training renders, 44.8 percent, were made under its poses, and the calibration was fitted on 255 real frames including all 104 of its own. The specimen's clean render-only figure, 88.5 to 91.3 percent over three seeds, cannot be set against the grant's 96 to 98 percent, since the ledger does not say what those renders were made from (renders page, row 6), and the clean retrain running that morning never reported. The same box gives the manuscript's 76.9 percent pooled DeepFluoro sacrum recall to an earlier detector generation and 64.0 percent to the one every real fine-tune started from, and whether the grant's "64-77% depending on round" describes those generations cannot be told (OR study page, row 4). All three are records, not final values.

The overnight 5.2 percent held-out vertebra recall scored predicted whole-vertebra boxes against vertebral-body truth; against outlines the same predictions gave the 90.6 percent record. The retrospective protocol's primary endpoint uses "the same box-matching criterion as the manuscript", still marked to confirm, and it has to be fixed with its truth convention before the test set is sealed.

For latency the retraining plan rules out every old figure: the runtime cost file does not exist and "the '17 ms' is banned" ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md), section 5). The one CPU figure on record is 3.35 seconds per frame for one part of the naming stage, from the earlier programme's model card. The superseded [Vertebral Levels Without CT](../../artifacts/vertebral-levels-without-ct-a4rydj/) alone still gives timings, a fraction of a second per image on a desktop graphics card and a quarter to half a second on one CPU core, against three to five seconds for CT registration, and calls its embedded-module claim "an inference from model size, not a measurement"; with no file behind it and the model card against it, that is a record, not a final value. The demo plan also names two claims the record cannot make: any ratio of synthetic to real training data, since none was swept, and anything about a device at the hospital.

## Latency: the budget, the rule and the recording

### The budget and where it comes from

The budget is one frame interval of the recorded sweeps: about 10.3 frames a second, from the earlier programme's cine demo build plan as the demo plan cites it, gives 1000 / 10.3 = 97.1 ms, which the code holds as `BUDGET_MS = 97.0`, rounded down ([src/schwingnet/latency.py](../../src/schwingnet/latency.py)). Real-time means the 95th percentile of per-frame compute strictly under it, taken by nearest rank so that every p95 shown is a measured frame ([the latency note](../../docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md); [docs/memory/latency-and-the-recorded-sweep.md](../../docs/memory/latency-and-the-recorded-sweep.md)). Checked as the plan asked, the budget traces to that frame rate, which comes from documents: the grid probe of 21 October reads no DICOM header. It is a property of these recordings, not a clinical requirement. The code holds 97 ms as a constant beside a function that turns any frame rate into its interval and a budget check that takes the budget as an argument, so another device's budget can follow from its own rate.

The slide's claim is fixed word for word: per-frame compute on a named GPU, models resident, batch 1, excluding acquisition, DICOM decode and display, as p50 and p95, and "real-time at 10 fps" only if the p95 is under 97 ms. It covers the stack's frame path and nothing between the X-ray tube and the screen; the frame grabber's delay, the C-arm's video delay and the display are unmeasured.

### Parity before any number

No latency number exists for a runtime whose outputs differ from the reference: every predicted level equal and every probability within 1e-5 of PyTorch on the CPU in float32, on validation crops, never test crops, the evaluation page's validation witness frames first ([chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md)). An arm without its own passed parity for that device, checkpoint and exported file is refused, and the headline holds the fastest arm whose parity still holds when it is written. FP16 and TensorRT change the outputs and are not measured. The earlier programme's own timing tool is written and has never run (demo plan, W6).

### The dry run

The only latency measured is the dry run of 21 October, on a model trained briefly on the synthetic bank and timed on four CPU cores of a cloud container, which says nothing about SchwingNet on fluoroscopy. Parity passed on 64 validation crops of 5 persons, the largest probability difference 1.9e-6. Over 300 timed frames (59 distinct, 3.6 boxes a frame) PyTorch gave p50 20.5 ms and p95 31.3 ms, and ONNX Runtime p50 9.4 ms and p95 15.1 ms; a 12-frame synthetic sweep ran at p50 10.7 ms and p95 18.1 ms. On those unrelated pictures the frame-to-frame step joined boxes that were not the same vertebra, so 29 of 46 were right with it, 63.0 percent [48.6, 75.5] with 9 abstaining, against 38 of 46, 82.6 percent [69.3, 90.9], frame by frame; the real sweeps decide whether the step keeps its settings.

### What the recording shows

The demo is a recorded overlay of a real C-arm sweep with measured latency on screen, not a live run: C-ArmLSpine's case134 and case39 raw sweeps, 301 frames each at 1024 x 1024 and about 10.3 frames a second, under CC BY 4.0, real motion from lateral through oblique to AP. Each tracked vertebra carries its box, level and probability, or "?" where the stack abstains, and the corner shows the frame counter, the gantry angle, the frame's compute time, the p95 so far against 97 ms, the run, the runtime, the device and the source of the boxes. Each frame's output uses only the frames before it, so the recording is what a live run would have shown.

Two limits belong on the slide. Version 1 has no detection stage, so the boxes are truth boxes from the registered CT and the overlay says "boxes: truth" on every frame: it shows naming and latency, not detection, the half that does not yet transfer. And naming collapses toward AP, which the plan says to show rather than cut, with the stack's abstention and stop signal carrying it; the stop signal is credited with catching 5 of 6 erring frames (A5.3), with no interval given and too few frames for a narrow one. A live laptop run is not recommended: no laptop latency exists and the quicker runtimes change the outputs.

The status on 24 October is thin. The probe of 21 October ran in 13 seconds and found case134's export as expected, 301 frames each with its metadata; case39 has no export and no poses, and its folder holds 696 files, not 301, so only case134 can be shown from version 1 ([SchwingNet grid pipeline](../../artifacts/schwingnet-grid-pipeline/)). The timing and sweep jobs wait for version 1, requesting at most 3 GPU-hours for one version and one sweep. The schedule puts version 1 by 28 October, latency and the recording on 29 October, the provisional on 30 October and the talk on 2 November, with the CPU latency and the export first to drop; the record step does not yet copy the sweep off the grid; and the owner's answers keep the demo private until the filing and the method off every slide.

### What latency is not measured

No machine in a surgeon's room has been timed, nor a laptop, nor the module the grant budgets for: a lossless frame grabber on the C-arm's video output feeding an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin that runs the stack outside the C-arm's software, with a bench log of latency per frame as its deliverable. The code's rules would carry to it unchanged, a Jetson number counting only with parity passed on the Jetson and against the frame rate of the C-arm it is plugged into; that is Inferred, and nothing has run there.

## The case for in-domain data

### What synthetic and cadaver data cannot predict

[Counting Levels Under Fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/), the September proposal for the department's leadership and canonical in the OR study's cluster, states the premise every later page repeats: "The one number no synthetic or cadaver data can predict is how it performs on our machines and patients", the per-case wrong-level rate on the hospital's own patients with its own machines. The [AIDaS Seed Grant Proposal](../../artifacts/aidas-seed-grant-proposal/) and the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/) share one body of preliminary results (OR study page, rows 52 and 53): with the annotation's boxes the count was exact on 99.17 to 100 percent of reached walks in twelve arms of 16 to 801 spines; a detector trained on real films finds 99.14 percent of bodies on real radiographs and the same design trained on renders 71.67 percent; and with render-trained boxes exactness fell from 100 to 30.06 percent on 79 held-out spines. The risk is detection, not counting, and synthetic fluoroscopy is "close in physics but tuned to one C-arm and one cadaveric population" (row 45). The shared paragraph's DeepFluoro figures are not this chapter's to settle: its sacrum range is open ([ledger row 10](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-10-sacrum-recall-on-real-deepfluoro-frames-detector-trained-on-renders-of-deepfluoros-own-cts)), and its 80.9 percent variance share, which the concept ledger's rule marks current ([ledger row 12](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-12-share-of-a-real-deepfluoro-frames-variance-explained-by-the-physics-renderer-alone)), is the figure from before the proposal's own correction to 86 percent by the number ruling and three cluster pages ([How far the verdicts hold](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#how-far-the-verdicts-hold), collision 12); chapter 13 holds both.

What the method can promise is "a measured bound on wrong-level output at a stated decline rate, on held-out cases, re-measured whenever the device, protocol or population changes", a decline rather than a guess where a count cannot be completed, and an audit trail per case. The proposal expects a detector fine-tuned on a few thousand in-domain frames to reach the film detector's range, about 99.14 percent, and the protocol sets its bar at 97 percent, an expectation beside a floor (row 23); both are Inferred, since no in-domain frame is annotated.

### The retrospective study

The canonical protocol, a draft for the university's eProtocol system that the inventory dates to January 2027, collects the archived intraoperative C-arm stills and cine loops of the spine operations a clinical collaborator, a DMC neurosurgeon, performed from 2021 to 2026, with the operated level from the operative note, or from post-operative imaging where the note is ambiguous or a re-localisation is documented. Its aims are a de-identified, coded dataset; annotated bodies, anchors and instruments, with the detector fine-tuned on a patient-level training split and per-body recall on held-out cases as the primary endpoint; and, on series with an anchor and overlapping frames, the count's exactness, the decline rate and agreement with the surgeon's confirming instrument frame as secondary endpoints. Its hypotheses are per-body recall of at least 97 percent with a target half-width of one point (H1), count exactness consistent with the 99.17 to 100 percent seen on public data (H2), and agreement consistent with 99.31 percent of 5,385 and 98.57 percent of 6,343 public confirmations (H3).

Cases are split by patient; a fixed held-out test set is drawn before any training, stratified by region and prior instrumentation; the rest serve for fine-tuning and patient-level five-fold cross-validation; and the analysis code is frozen and the test set sealed before fine-tuning. Recall is reported with a patient-level cluster-bootstrap interval, overall and by region, hardware and still against cine, against the render-only detector on the same frames. The size follows from the normal approximation, 3.8416 x 0.97 x 0.03 / 0.0001, about 1,118, so about 1,100 held-out bodies, or about 1,520 to hold 99 percent to half a point; a pilot query of 20 cases fixes frames per case, the held-out fraction and the number of cases before sealing. Its stated limitation: archived stills were not taken under the method's protocol, so the share of countable cases is unknown and expected to be a minority, cine loops carrying the count endpoints while the primary endpoint does not depend on them. Extraction and annotation take three months after approval and fine-tuning and evaluation three more; only aggregates leave the team.

Three points are Inferred from the protocol's own text. The arithmetic is right, but it sizes an estimate, not a test: at a true recall of exactly 97 percent an interval of a point either side, 96 to 98, cannot show "at least 97 percent", so H1 needs a true recall nearer 98 percent or a larger set. The 1,100 bodies come from a formula that assumes independence while bodies cluster within frames and patients; the protocol says so and promises to report the achieved width, but only the pilot's bodies per patient can say before sealing how far clustering will widen it. And the matching criterion must be fixed before sealing, for the 5.2 percent's reason.

The route requested is Expedited Category 5, records collected solely for treatment (45 CFR 46.110 in the protocol's citation), for four reasons: a coded master list kept during abstraction, identifiable burned-in text and headers handled before de-identification, cine loops as clinical video, and the rule that research which may support an FDA submission "is not eligible for exemption", since the data may enter a future 510(k) pre-submission. The Exempt Category 4 paragraphs, under 46.104(d)(4)(iii), are completed only in case the IRB routes it there, with waivers of consent and of HIPAA authorization requested; the grant's "Exempt Category 4 or Expedited Category 5" is consistent with this (row 18). The protocol has no funding at submission and would add the seed grant by amendment, with notification expected on 16 December 2026 (row 26), and it is still a draft whose pilot values are unfilled (row 58).

### What Protocol 1 alone still holds

The [Protocol 1 eProtocol draft](../../artifacts/protocol-1-eprotocol-draft-mkmh7p/) of 15 September is superseded by the retrospective protocol, the later Protocol 1, and alone holds the broader design not carried forward: Exempt Category 4 with limited review; 40,000 imaging studies, marked unconfirmed, from about 25,000 patients between 1 January 2006 and approval, across spine CT, radiographs, MRI, archived fluoroscopy and the trauma registry; five hypotheses, naming on CT, registration-derived labels agreeing with the record on at least 98 percent of vertebrae, registration-free intraoperative naming by stratum, fracture detection and trauma outcomes; and about 1,500 verified vertebrae in each of four strata, 6,000 in all, for 99 percent +/- 0.5 points, from 1,500 to 2,500 images of 800 to 1,500 patients, with the shadow-mode protocol submitted alongside. Its warning that one university centre kept intraoperative images outside PACS in 90 of 190 cases is a fair question for radiology, though it comes from another centre (row 41). Its claim that no public intraoperative spine fluoroscopy with level labels exists is not to be carried unqualified, since the atlas records an unverified open download of nine level-named intraoperative patients (row 37), and its "every event was at L4-5 or L5-S1" is dropped (row 9).

### The 2027 validation plan and its cost

The seed grant asks $20,000 for 1 January to 31 December 2027, under AIDaS area 4, AI for Health. In the first quarter the retrospective protocol goes to the IRB, since the university's existing determination for the project's public, de-identified data does not cover hospital records, and phantom sweeps on the university's C-arm measure whether a hand-moved C-arm stays within the tolerance the count needs ([chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)). The second brings cadaver validation through the Body Bequest Program, the count read against a needle at a known level in an intact thoracolumbar spine; the detector's fine-tuning on the DMC fluoroscopy; and the prototype. The third is a shadow-mode study under a second protocol that shows its count to no one; the fourth an FDA pre-submission and an NIH NIBIB R21, the grant warning that "FDA clearance is not achievable within 2027 from a seed grant". The deliverables are the de-identified corpus; held-out per-body recall with its interval, set against 99.14 and 71.67 percent; the count's exactness on phantom, cadaver and shadow-mode cases with intervals and the share of walks not reached; the manual step-error distribution; a prototype with its latency bench log; and the pre-submission and a submitted R21. Public release of the corpus is not a deliverable.

### The shadow-mode protocol

Protocol 2, planned as a later, separate submission, would capture the images the surgeon already takes through a frame grabber on the C-arm's video output, run the count on the external unit and show it to no one during the case, with no added exposure. Its primary endpoint is agreement between the count and the documented level, with the decline rate; secondary endpoints are per-body recall on captured frames, time from first exposure to confirmed level, the number of exposures and a short workload questionnaire for surgeons. It expects an IDE-exempt study of a non-significant-risk diagnostic device under 21 CFR 812.2(c)(3) and a waiver of consent under 21 CFR 50.22, the IRB's written determination to be sought, and it waits on Protocol 1's endpoints, the phantom and cadaver steps and the hospital's biomedical engineering approval to connect a frame grabber.

### What else the case rests on

The proposal and the [Level Counting Visual Abstract](../../artifacts/level-counting-visual-abstract/) agree on the business case, about ten operating-room minutes a case, $140,000 to $500,000 a year at 300 to 1,000 cases (row 16), whose weakest input is the 15 minutes of a thoracic localization by needle and count, from two small series. The fiducial's $1,750 to $1,950 is 51.9 minutes of operating-room time at the 2014 rate, not a device price (row 14), and navigation's $1 million to $2.5 million is vendor pricing labelled an assumption. The background registry of 34,544 operations gives wrong-level surgery at 0.12 percent [0.09, 0.16], 0.42 percent thoracic, with intraoperative disorientation behind 81.3 percent of events (Furuya 2026), a figure whose reading as a count gone wrong is unsettled (row 8). The clinical comparator, from the canonical [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/), is a surgeon: 69.6 percent unaided on difficult C-arm films, 97.6 percent with a CT-registration overlay where a labelled preoperative CT exists. The claim an operating room needs is a surgeon with this aid against a surgeon with that one, which "nothing here answers yet"; the report's reader study would give the assisted accuracy and the rates at which suggestions rescue wrong reads and overturn right ones ([chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md)).

### What the regulatory reasoning still owes

The plan asked for the categories and the 510(k) reasoning to be checked against the regulations; with no network this chapter could not read them, but it can say where the reasoning is thin. The categories are the ones the protocol quotes, but its decisive step, that FDA-bound research cannot be exempt, cites no regulation or university policy (row 48); the place to look is the IRB's own policy and FDA's rules in 21 CFR 50 and 56, whose exemptions are not the Common Rule's, a pointer that is Inferred. No page names a predicate device, a classification or why a 510(k) is the route, the grant saying only "the device pathway", and a pre-submission asks FDA for feedback and clears nothing. The fifteen business days of radiation-safety paperwork rest on a policy named but never cited (row 42), and the claim that cadaver work needs no IRB has no policy behind it (row 47).

## What we know

The stack names levels on real lateral operating-room frames at about 97 percent on the device it saw, and its lateral naming carries to a device it never saw, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4] zero-shot, within a point of the adapted figure; AP naming does not carry (43.7 percent), and adapting a level model to one device costs it the others. Detection on a device held out entirely is 80.5 percent [74.1, 86.8] on C-ArmLSpine and lower elsewhere, and no held-out cell reaches the 97 percent counting grade; real frames of other devices are the largest lever, two dozen frames of the device add three to four points before a plateau, magnified fields hurt a detector that never saw the device most, and an inverted or upside-down frame breaks every detector. The 100 percent DeepFluoro sacrum is not the manuscript's method and the 5.2 percent was a box convention. The latency rules are in code: a 97 ms budget from the sweeps' frame rate, the p95 strictly under it, nearest rank and no number without parity; the only latency measured is a synthetic dry run, and the recording will show naming and latency on truth boxes from case134. The in-domain study is designed, with Expedited Category 5 requested, a sealed patient-level test set, per-body recall as the primary endpoint and about 1,100 held-out bodies, and the 2027 plan costs $20,000 in the artifact and $19,987 in the final version a checkpoint note records.

## What we do not know

We do not know whether 80.5 and 90.6 percent are one detector at two operating points, which case count lies behind the September naming figure, or whether 98.2 percent was replaced; what a held-out detector finds in a surgeon's magnified field; or whether the styled corpus, the device fits and the clean DeepFluoro retrain ever finished. No latency exists on fluoroscopy, on the H200, a laptop or a Jetson, nor between tube and screen, and the 10.3 frames a second has not been read from the files. For the study we do not know how many bodies an archived case gives, how many cases allow a count or how much clustering widens the interval; whether the IRB accepts Expedited 5 and on what basis exemption is closed; which FDA route and predicate apply; whether the grant was submitted and will be funded; whether a hand-moved C-arm keeps to the count's tolerance; and whether a surgeon with the aid does better, or worse, than without it.

## What would change our mind

A device held out entirely reaching at least 97 percent per-body recall under a stated criterion, or a styled corpus within the real-to-real floor, would reopen the claim of one network for any C-arm. The in-domain study missing H1 on the hospital's own devices would say the gap is not only a matter of data from the device, and send the detection stage back to Part III. The results catalog row behind 80.5 percent would settle the held-out figure this book carries. A real p95 at or above 97 ms on the H200 with parity passed would end the "real-time" wording. A pilot query with too few bodies a patient or too few anchored series would change the study's size or endpoints before sealing; an IRB ruling for Exempt 4, or a policy that leaves exemption open to FDA-bound data, would change its route; phantom sweeps outside the count's tolerance would change the count before any cadaver or shadow-mode step; and a reader study in which wrong suggestions overturn right reads more often than right ones rescue wrong reads would change the clinical case itself.
