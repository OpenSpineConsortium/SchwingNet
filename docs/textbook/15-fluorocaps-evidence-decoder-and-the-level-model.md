# 15 FluoroCap's evidence decoder and the level model

Label: confidential-method. Results may be shown, mechanism may not, until the provisional is filed; this chapter stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere.

This chapter states how a panel mined from the published literature becomes a level proposal, and what the level model trained beside that pipeline proves. A planner reads it to decide which parts of the machinery to reuse, which numbers may be shown and with what words, and what must be run again before a number is current. It answers four questions: how a panel gets a level and what decides when its evidence disagrees; what the level model proves on renders and real panels, and at what coverage; what broke in the night of 14 to 15 September and how each fault was fixed; and why the corpus does not retrain the level model. FluoroCap as a dataset is [chapter 8](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md); the renderer is [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md); transitional anatomy and abstention are [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md); the method decisions are logged in [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md) and the benches as instruments in [chapter 17](17-benches-and-human-reads.md).

## How to read the numbers here

Five pages carry this chapter, written within three days. The [FluoroCap Pipeline Map](../../artifacts/fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs/) of 14 September is canonical for the structure and claims no accuracy. The [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) of the same day measured the corpus on that day's decode; the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/), read on 15 September from 06:55 to 07:35, re-measured the same decode and supersedes it, so the audit's figures appear below only as records. The [FluoroCap Overnight Report](../../artifacts/fluorocap-overnight-report-xnhgdo/), updated at 08:30 that morning, yields to the reality check on corpus counts but is the later record for the all-spine v2 model and the translator's job ids. [Overnight: The Honest 99%](../../artifacts/overnight-the-honest-99-kantj8/) of 16 September is canonical for selective prediction, which the [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) ruled record only.

Four kinds of number appear. The first is agreement between automatic sources, such as a decoded run agreeing with the author's caption; it says the lines cohere, not that they are right. No real panel carries a human-verified level: the reality check found `adjudicated_gold_panels 0` and the 120-panel reading set and 65 reading pages built and unread, and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) still lists human review pending on all 21,603 manifest rows on 17 October, so there is no accuracy on real panels anywhere in this record. The second is render scores, which overlap training unless a split filter, first added on 15 September, excluded the training cases. The third is anything from the families the handoff ruled leaked, allspine v2, v6 and v7, `anchor_drr` and `vindrps`, after NHANES II entered the banks twice (48 of 54 validation persons also in training, found on 22 September) and CTSpinoPelvic1K twice (301 of 801 persons on both sides); the G3 person-disjoint split followed on 23 September, and each such number below is marked a record, not a final value. The fourth is the one held-out number on real images, from a model trained on films and measured on radiographs. No result file was read for this chapter, so nothing is graded above Reported.

## Six lanes and the filesystem contract

The map draws six lanes. Sources gathers every captioned spine radiograph a script can reach, with its legal basis on each row: Europe PMC and the PMC open-access set, the aggregators Open-PMC-18M, Open-i and MultiCaRe, OpenAlex PDFs searched in eleven languages, Wikimedia Commons and Openverse, YouTube through 905 queries as the only source of real C-arm monitor frames with spoken level cues, and Reddit, 799 Internet Archive texts, patents, manufacturer sitemaps, Common Crawl and three image archives. Network routes run on the workstation because, the map says, PMC throttles the grid's outbound address; no page measures that. Ingest makes one manifest row per panel: a logged fetch, the article's licence and context, a splitter that cuts montages at their gutters because the panel is the unit of labelling, a three-class panel classifier with a view head that screens out non-radiographs, a 110-field manifest with licence tiers A to E and a SHA-256 chain, and DINOv2 duplicate groups, since one figure arrives by three routes and splits must go by group.

Evidence runs on GPUs and yields independent lines per panel, none deciding alone. Decision turns them into one contiguous run of level names per panel with its reasons, through a caption parser, decoder v2, caption-free consistency checks, reader pages and a freeze. The learning loops, judged on held-out films, are detector self-training, FluoroGen, the anchor detector, the level-model arms and the CT segmentation folds that feed the renders. Serving puts detector, ensemble and labeller behind one FastAPI endpoint in Docker, with weights pulled from a private repository; the registry refuses public destinations, and the website sends pixels and draws the boxes and names it gets back, with no model file served. That rule was a repair: until 14 September the website shipped 121 MB of weights, as [the lab recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) records.

The lanes are held together by a contract, not a scheduler. Every stage is a Slurm job or a login-node orchestrator that polls for a file or a job name; outputs are JSON lines named by a run tag, so any stage can be rerun and every consumer finds the newest evidence; each stage skips what its done-set holds. The decoder waits for the inference meta file, the OCR and hardware jobs and the anchor weights, gives the vision-language read a bounded wait, then decodes with whatever exists and records what it used. A missing line delays nothing and shows in the record afterwards. The map and the card say the manifest regenerates byte-identical; neither names a command or a run that tested it, so the property stands unverified.

## The lines of evidence

### The corner detector

A YOLO11 pose model finds four corners per vertebra, fine-tuned on zoomed windows because collimated frames broke the film-trained scale. Every other line hangs off its boxes. Of the 3,294 panels the decode of 14 September ran, 2,528 had a box and 766 none; the 3,294 were 17.3 percent of the 19,043 panels then known, a share run through the decode rather than a detection rate.

### The level ensemble and its run labeller

Four single-vertebra ResNets read each box as a 96-pixel target with a marker channel showing which vertebra is meant; a bidirectional GRU run labeller for each model and context rung reads the crops as a run; flip test-time augmentation is averaged in; and the ensemble's entropy orders the reading queue. The crop comes from the [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/), which scales every target vertebra to the same pixel height so that size cannot be used, the design the reality check says to keep. On mined panels the fixed scale has a cost: the model upsamples the median panel 1.75x and the tenth-percentile panel 4.6x, which adds no edges.

### Printed labels

EasyOCR reads the labels authors print beside vertebrae: of 2,815 scanned panels, 67.5 percent carried printed text and 3.6 percent a printed level label. A label also points to a vertebra the detector missed, so a recovery step re-detects around it on a zoomed window and falls back to a SAM point prompt. The map calls printed labels the strongest anchor there is, with no measurement behind it; the only figure, the [audit's](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) 53.8 percent caption-held-out agreement on 13 panels (a record, not a final value), ranks OCR below the caption, the vision-language read, hardware and figure mates.

### The vision-language read

Qwen2.5-VL-7B makes a blind read of type, view, region and devices that never sees the caption, and a parse of the caption in any language. As the deciding term on caption-free panels it agreed with the caption on 73.1 percent of 93.

### Hardware

OWLv2 draws implant boxes zero-shot and flags which vertebrae carry metal, and the caption parser turns "screws in L3 and L4" into facts about single vertebrae rather than a set of levels. The audit counted such device-to-level constraints on 1,886 of 2,815 captioned panels and found the zero-shot implant detector, checked by hand on a strip of unrecorded size, at 33 percent recall with 28 percent false positives; each is a record, not a final value. Hardware does not make a panel easier: caption-free agreement was 55.1 percent on 283 panels with hardware against 62.9 percent on 97 without.

### Anatomical anchors

A YOLO11 anchor detector trained on more than 3,000 rendered views, DRRs from the labels of CTSpinoPelvic1K, VerSeFusion and TotalSegmentator, finds the sacrum, the femoral heads, the twelfth rib, a lumbar rib and the skull. In the map's design a visible sacrum names L5 without counting, which on a six-lumbar spine names the wrong body. The line decided one panel of 3,294, and its bank, `anchor_drr`, put 301 of 801 CTSpinoPelvic1K persons on both sides of its split, so its scores are a record, not a final value. The sacrum, which the anchors should make easy, shows the gap to real panels: the level model names it on 100 percent of renders that overlap training, while the decode recalls it on 11.9 percent of 67 caption-free real panels.

### Orientation, mates and duplicates

A four-way orientation net rotates sideways frames, and an upside-down call makes the decoder try both orders. Figure mates and video neighbours give a check that needs no caption: panels of one figure must agree, and neighbouring frames must not jump region. Duplicate groups decide which panels count as independent.

## Decoder v2: what decides when the lines disagree

### The scoring rule

Decoder v2 is a deterministic function of its input files that runs on a CPU in seconds. Over the boxes, ordered along the spine, it scores every contiguous run of level names by the model's floored probability for each name plus a term each for the caption, the printed labels, the hardware, the anchors, the vision-language read and the mates. The map gives no reason for the floor; its effect is that one vertebra the model scores near zero cannot alone rule out a run the other lines support. When the caption's levels fit a contiguous window over the boxes, containment is hard: the decoded run and the caption must nest one inside the other. The output is one run per panel and the term that moved it, `decided_by`. A changed weight or a new line costs a re-decode of seconds, not a retraining, and every run can be traced to its sources.

### Hard containment, and why the caption-free subset is not a random hold-out

Hard containment makes the constrained panels useless as a test: on 1,615 of them the run and the caption nest 100 percent of the time (mean Jaccard 0.522), by construction. The test has to be the rest, and the rest is not random, because `hard_caption` is false exactly when the caption's levels fit no contiguous window over the detected boxes: when, for example, the detector missed vertebrae, the caption describes another panel, or the panel is not what the caption says. The caption-free subset is selected by detector failure and caption misfit, and its labels are still captions. On the reality check's 380 such panels the run and the caption nest 57.1 percent of the time (mean Jaccard 0.341); the [audit's](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) 56.8 percent on 389 is the earlier record, a record, not a final value. Neither is an accuracy.

### Who decided, and how well

On the decode of 14 September the term that moved the answer was the model on 1,166 panels, the caption on 1,100, nothing on 803, the vision-language read on 94, OCR on 53, a figure mate on 43, hardware on 34 and an anchor on one. Against the withheld caption on caption-free panels the deciders rank the other way: the soft caption term 79.5 percent on 44, the vision-language read 73.1 percent on 93, the level model alone 37.9 percent on 169. The [audit's](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) ranking of the day before, a record, not a final value, puts figure mates at 80.0 percent on 10, hardware at 69.6 on 23, OCR at 53.8 on 13 and the model last at 38.3 on 175. The commonest decider is the weakest, every outside line beats it, and constraint decoding is doing real work. By level, caption-free recall was 63.5 to 72.2 percent at L1 to L4, 52.2 at L5 and 48.5 at T12, on 33 to 138 panels a level, and 0.0 to 41.9 at T2 to T11, on 15 to 47: a lumbar system.

Between panels of one figure, with no caption used, the decoder's runs agree on region 91.5 percent of the time over 519 pairs against 52.1 percent over 468 for the model alone, and overlap substantially (Jaccard at least 0.5) 68.2 against 30.8 percent; these are agreements, not accuracy. The [FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/) prints 88 percent for the decoder, a number typed into its script with no pair count and defined over mates that share the caption's levels, which the card itself says the shared caption partly induces; [the FluoroCap contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/fluorocap-the-corpus-its-pipeline-and-its-audits.md) read it as a different quantity from the caption-free 91.5 percent (rows 0 and 32), and the ledger leaves the decoder's figure-mate agreement out of its settled values (row 154), so the 88 percent is not to be quoted.

### The T13 and L6 columns

Until the morning of 15 September the decoder drew its runs from the ordinary sequence of names, so no decoded run could contain T13 or L6, and the films-only model had no T13 or L6 training crops to propose one: every decoded zero there was true by construction. That morning both decoders were changed to try the column with a T13 or an L6 inserted, against a prior penalty, having passed only a unit check. Tested with the all-spine v2 model on 376 held-out renders, the single crop called 68 of 108 L6 bodies L6 (63.0 percent, 29 called L5) while the caption-free decoder called only 39.8 percent of them L6, so the rare-column penalty was set too high; T13 was 0 of 16, 13 called T12. Both come from the v2 family and are a record, not a final value. [Research Directions from the Data Holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/) of 8 October repeats the 39.8 against 63.0 and records an alternative decoder, Star Temporal Classification, at 22 to 24 percent, refuted.

On the corpus, the new columns gave the first counts above zero: the Honest 99 page lists L6 on 338 decoded panels (145 named in a caption, 193 caption-free) and T13 on 254 (106 and 148), among 20,700 panels decoded and 11,377 carrying a level. The 20,700 is current ([ledger row 142](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-142-number-of-fluorocap-panels-decoded)); the L6 and T13 counts are caption-named or decoder-derived, and no reader has checked one.

## From proposal to claim: reader pages, benches and the freeze

The decision lane ends in people. Reader pages put the doubtful panels first, show the evidence and ask for one click and a level name per panel. The card's protocol asks for two readers blind to each other and to the proposal, a third to adjudicate, and a freeze of dataset, model and analysis plan before any performance claim; the freeze writes a SHA256SUMS file and a lock record naming the manifest hash, the registry ids and the counts. Version 0.1 was frozen; version 1 waits for reader labels that do not exist.

Two benches were built for them. The [Level Verification Bench](../../artifacts/level-verification-bench-mkjxsy/) draws 179 decoded panels and 865 vertebrae at random within each route with a fixed seed, an unbiased estimate that a set chosen by the model's uncertainty could not give, and tags each panel with its route, whether the caption constrained it, its deciding term and whether it was read bottom-up. The [FluoroCap Adjudication Bench](../../artifacts/fluorocap-adjudication-bench-rnsyir/) draws 120 panels stratified by deciding evidence and region from `decode_v2_v2.jsonl`, which predates the refetch re-split; its reader sees the film before choosing to reveal the caption, and the reveal is recorded. Both pages keep one record per panel in a shared store, so a second reader would overwrite the first and the card's two blind reads cannot be held. Neither exported page shows a verdict, and whether any were saved cannot be told. The quality report's arithmetic gives 545 reads for +/-3 percent around 85 percent, and the reality check puts a 99 percent claim within +/-0.5 percent at about 1,500 verified images in each reported stratum.

## What the level model proves

Three models and three domains are mixed in the numbers that circulate, so the statement goes model by model.

### The films-only model on films, renders and real panels

The model deployed on 14 September, trained on films only, scores 98.7 percent exact and 99.8 percent within one level on 8,422 held-out single crops from 1,653 patients (CSXA 7,440 at 99.4 percent, NHANES 982 at 93.6), and 99.4 percent with its run head. These are radiographs, not fluoroscopy; the page says no crop is thoracic, though the ladder counts 66 thoracic crops in the same set, a question the [contradictions page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md#how-far-the-verdicts-hold) leaves open; the lumbar rows rest on 82 validation crops per level; the source gives no interval; and person-disjointness was not re-verified after the leak finding of 22 September, which the FluoroCap contradictions rule cannot-tell (row 37), so 98.7 percent is a record, not a final value. The ladder's 96.7 percent on the same crops (96.9 at 96-pixel targets) is another arm's, annotated films joined to synthetic crops, beside the 98.7 rather than replacing it; the ladder also found that models trained on CT projections alone scored 7.4 percent on real NHANES films.

The same films-only model scores 35.7 percent on CT renders, which it never trained on: T10 and T11 at 0.0, T12 at 4.3, the sacrum at 5.3, L1 at 43.0, because its bank of cervical and lumbar films held zero crops at T2 to T11, T13 and L6. The decode of 14 September ran before all-spine v1 existed, so its 37.9 percent on real panels is, by inference, this model's too. One model, then, is near 99 percent on held-out films of the regions it saw, about a third right on renders, and in agreement with the caption on about a third of the hardest real panels.

### All-spine v1 and v2 on renders

All-spine v1, trained on films plus every CT render source, named the levels the films-only model had never seen, but on a diagnostic whose renders' CT cases sat in its training bank: its 92.7 percent across levels (T10 95.8, T11 93.1, T12 95.3, L6 13 of 13, the sacrum 600 of 600) is a training-set score. All-spine v2, registered at 08:13 on 15 September as `allspine_v2_r1`, scored 78.8 percent exact, 95.9 within one and 97.2 region on its own validation, 62.5 percent on cone-beam renders against v1's 50.0, and 52.3 on rare-anatomy renders; over 800 held-out renders, with 4,505 training renders skipped by the new split filter, it scored 64.2 percent. Held out, per level, it ran from 80.4 percent at L4 down to 37.5 at T9, with the sacrum 100.0 on 120, L6 50.0 on 10 and T13 0.0 on 1. Every v2 figure is a record, not a final value.

All these renders predate the defects the Honest 99 page found the next night, a scatter term that put 50 to 82 percent of the spine's pixel value into a blurred copy of the bowel and a photon budget of 0.9 to 12 detected photons at the vertebra, fixed and tested on one small matched comparison rather than a re-render ([chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md)).

### Accuracy as a pair with coverage

No published system names every level at 99 percent on plain radiographs, so the Honest 99 page reports a pair: how often the model is right on the vertebrae it answers, and how many it answers. On held-out renders from the allspine v2 bank it names the exact vertebra at 99 percent accuracy on 35.7 percent of vertebrae (2,956 answered, 95 percent lower bound 98.7) and the region on 55.7 percent; at 95 percent accuracy the two coverages are 58.0 and 95.7 percent, and at full coverage 78.8 percent are exact. Conformal prediction at a 1 percent error rate gives a mean set of 2.56 names, a single name on 33.6 percent of vertebrae at 99.2 percent accuracy and one region on 70.3 percent at 99.7 percent. All of it is a record, not a final value: the handoff names the claim record only because it comes from the leaked bank, and it must be re-run on the G3 split through the evaluation gate before it is current. At full coverage its exact figure equals v2's own validation, but its region figure, 93.4 percent, differs from v2's 97.2, and the [ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#settled-figures-not-used) leaves the pair unsettled (row 219), so which set the curve was drawn on is not known. The page says every number comes from a named file, and names none.

The form outlives the numbers: a pair is what a surgeon can act on and a reviewer can check. It needs real panels before a threshold is deployed, since conformal coverage has been measured collapsing from 90 to 7 percent under shift and class-conditional guarantees need about 99 real cases per class.

## What broke overnight, and how each fault was fixed

### The thoracic gap

The audit traced the films-only model's collapse through the thoracic spine to its bank, which held zero crops at T2 to T11, T13 and L6. It first recommended logit adjustment and class-balanced sampling, then withdrew the advice on its own evidence: a class with no examples cannot be re-weighted. The fix was to change the bank, merging the films with every CT render source in all-spine v1; the mid-thoracic levels, which only VerSeFusion supplies, still scored 43 to 58 percent on renders. One gap was separate from coverage and survived: at L1, with 3,843 crops in the bank, the films-only model scored 85.4 percent on its own films and 43.0 on renders, a gap of domain, not of counts.

### The training-set score

The 92.7 percent came from a diagnostic, `diag_level_model.py`, that did not filter renders by split, while `ctsp_drr` had contributed 6,091 training crops. A split filter added on 15 September skipped 4,505 training renders in v2's diagnostic and gave the 64.2 percent, and the reality check made the rule general: no render accuracy without a split filter, in that diagnostic and in the transitional evaluation alike.

### The link step

The training chain's last step ran `ln -sf` on a file whose name was already the link's name, which replaces the file with a link to itself. The v2 weights written at 05:22 were lost at about 05:24, the bank untouched. The step was guarded, the retrain resubmitted at 05:47, and the model landed at 08:13. The lasting fix was a model registry: each weight file content-addressed and read-only, ledgered, logged to MLflow and mirrored to a private repository, sixteen models backfilled, trainers registering on save, chains linking instead of running `ln -sf`. A stale v2-ready marker that would have started the corpus re-run on the v1 model was removed at 06:20. The reality check, read before 08:13, named the pending retrain job 40285209; that it is the run registered at 08:13 is inferred, since no page names both, and the spine-program records that would confirm it are not reachable here. The demo plan still lists registering the stack's weights with their sha256 as not done ([docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md), row W2).

### Bank v2

Bank v2 drops any vertebra the collimator cuts as a target, 8,949 of 28,270 cone-beam boxes (31.7 percent), and adds 1,482 rare-anatomy renders, kept after dropping frames with fewer than two unclipped vertebrae, oversampling the VerSeFusion cases with an L6 or a T13 and 34 CTSpinoPelvic1K cases with an L6 or a Castellvi variant at up to twelve poses in two views. The VerSeFusion count is 55 in the audit and 56 in the overnight report; the FluoroCap contradictions read 56 as the later count (row 24), but the ledger has not settled it ([row 43](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-43-versefusion-cases-carrying-an-l6-or-t13)). The reality check's caution applies: at L6, 90 distinct cases gave 46 percent and twelvefold duplication gave nothing, so new poses of the same cases are not new cases.

## The failure modes that remain

The clearest is the counting trap. On six-lumbar spines all-spine v1 gave each lumbar body the name of the one above it, reading the column as five lumbar bodies above the sacrum: L1 36.7 percent (called T12), L2 46.0, L3 45.0, L4 39.8, L5 30.6 (called L4) and L6 46.4, called L5 in 366 of 913, against 78.5 to 86.9 percent on normal spines, on a test not held out. v2's held-out test that morning still put six-lumbar T12 at 58.1, L4 at 47.2 and L5 at 38.9 percent, a record. The audit had shown the films-only version, a record, not a final value: L1 to L4 named as well or better on six-lumbar spines as on normal ones, but L5 at 23.5 percent, called L4, and L6 never named, called L5 in 41 of 68. Chapter 14 is the programme's answer.

The second is captions as labels. A caption is the author's count by a convention the record cannot see, since both readings of L5 appear in the captions, and the decoded run is the model's own proposal held to it. The third is the level model as the weakest term in its decoder. The sacrum's 11.9 percent caption-free recall may reflect a token convention (S1 against sacrum) or a failure; the record cannot say. The median panel's short side is 320 pixels, current by [ledger row 157](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-157-median-fluorocap-panel-short-side); a detected lumbar vertebra spans a median 55 pixels, about 0.55 mm per pixel, and the training renders are 2.6 times sharper per vertebra. Of 2,071 decoded panels carrying levels, 732 (35 percent) are ones the modality classifier calls not a spine radiograph, and 49 percent of the 18,119 panels then held (since superseded) score as not an x-ray; the modality head's 93.1 percent was measured on 2,143 film-manifest items, not on FluoroCap.

Two defects surfaced the next night. The abstention rule required L5 or L6 in frame, so it abstained on four sacralised Castellvi IIIb patients with no L5, exactly the positives it protects; it now asks for the lowest mobile lumbar segment by position. And label maps gave plausible wrong answers: label 25 means L6 in one scheme and T13 in another, so the VerSeFusion manifest claimed eighteen subjects with a T13 where the masks show twelve with no such label and six with an L6, matching the published table; separately, of the 802 released CTs only record 1043 breaks its chain, lacking L3. The transitional flag, a whole-frame variant detector beside the level model, barely moved with more positives (AUC 0.652 at 10 positive patients, 0.713 at 120) and retrieved known cases on 3,600 real radiographs at chance, reading age and exposure rather than anatomy; chapter 14 carries it.

## The learning loops and what each returned

Detector self-training, on the Noisy Student pattern, is drawn feeding confident corners on caption-consistent panels back into the detector as `detect_v6f`; no page reports what it did to detection. The anchor detector decided one panel, and its bank is leaked.

FluoroGen, SDXL with ControlNet and a LoRA, was to restyle perfectly labelled DRRs into C-arm frames without moving a vertebra. At strength 0.45 to 0.65 the output looked like fluoroscopy and had lost the vertebrae; the array was cancelled at 05:50 on 15 September after 12,896 images, none usable for level training, and a sweep over 300 cases found no usable strength, the render unchanged at 0.2 and the end-plates smeared at 0.35, so image-to-image restyling was dropped. The unpaired translator CUT carries labels over exactly by construction. Its first version trained toward the panel folders as they were, MRI slices and annotated screenshots among them, and is invalid; the second, restricted to the 2,991 panels the modality classifier passes at p of at least 0.7, was queued as jobs 40285316/7. By 8 October research directions records render-to-fluoroscopy translation (SDXL, CUT, CycleGAN, ADDA) as giving no gain in any arm and lists CUT and SDXL restyling among what the record excludes.

The level-model arms are Slurm arrays with one variable per arm and fixed seeds, registered by weight hash. Size in millimetres as a scalar feature gave no gain, three times. The Honest 99 page asked for an ordinal loss, since level is ordinal and was trained as 27 unordered classes; [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md) records that the ordinal losses tried later gained nothing over cross-entropy. A focused fine-tune with transitional captions and T13 and L6 weighted five times was queued on 15 September; no page reports its outcome, and the later record excludes decoder-named pseudolabels.

[Lift Before Label?](../../artifacts/lift-before-label-jzjq8t/), written that day from four literature sweeps (173 sourced findings), stands behind these choices: no reconstruction stage, the direct 2-D identifier with both views where the C-arm gives both, and registration as the way real frames get labels; the reconstruction verdict stands, but two-view fusion later lost on films ([chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)). Its adversarial verification pass had not run when it was written, and no page shows that it ran later; its sentence that the renderer is already at DeepDRR fidelity, which the reality check repeats, did not survive the physics audits that followed.

## Why the corpus does not retrain the level model

The method gives four reasons, each resting on a measurement. The labels are captions and no panel has a read, so nothing measures how often a caption or a decoded run is right. The decoded run is the model's own proposal held to the caption, and the model is the weakest decider; in the audit's words, the model would be learning largely from its own errors. The panels carry shortcuts and noise a network would learn: printed level labels on 3.6 percent, burned-in text on two thirds, the non-radiographs counted above, and panel boundaries that are themselves noisy because half of PMC figures are compound. And the labels the model most needs, T13 and L6, exist on the corpus only as captions or decoder output, unread.

The decisions followed in three steps. The audit allowed retraining with re-weighting and, on the same page, conceded that re-weighting cannot help a class with no examples. The reality check put training or fine-tuning any level head on caption-derived labels first among what to stop, and made the mined figures its Tier 4: an external test set after a two-reader read of a stratified sample, and a source for the modality and hardware heads, never for the level head. The record did not follow at once: two days later [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) brought 2,385 decoded FluoroCap panels, carrying 114 L6 and 78 T13 vertebrae, into bank v7, and the retraining plan counts "FluoroCap in" among the faults of the hand-built banks. Directive D1 of 23 September, recorded in the handoff and the registry, put FluoroCap out of training, a test set at most, and the registry adds that its crops were still inside the old fold banks ([data/sources/fluorocap.json](../../data/sources/fluorocap.json), [docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md](../../docs/research/datasets-reproduce.md)), and the retraining plan of 20 October refuses any shard that carries a source the directives exclude, FluoroCap among them ([docs/research/retraining-plan.md](../../docs/research/retraining-plan.md)).

What would change the corpus's standing is a read, not a model. Research directions puts the choice as annotating about 200 panels as a test set or writing the corpus off, and that test set does not exist. A two-reader, adjudicated read of the random bench would give the first real-panel accuracy with an interval; bounding it within +/-3 percent around 85 takes 545 reads, more than the bench's 179 panels, and such a read would make FluoroCap the external test set the reality check's Tier 4 asks for. The directive is the owner's, and the record names no condition under which it lapses.

## Superseded and companion pages

[The FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/), superseded by the reality check, alone holds the evidence-term ranking with its own counts, the 56.8 percent containment on 389 panels, the region head's 76.6 percent on lumbar panels against 44.8 thoracic and 12.6 sacral (90 of 135 sacral panels called lumbar), the films-only table on 1,175 renders with and without an L6, and the retraction of re-weighting, each a record, not a final value. The overnight report yields on corpus counts and holds the v2 state, the link incident, the registry and the translator's job ids. The dataset card is canonical for the reader protocol and the freeze; it was written as a release card for two datasets, A and B, which the later rule that FluoroCap stays private forever ([the lab recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/)) overtakes; its frozen v0.1 stays the authoritative copy. The pipeline map is canonical for structure; its unsourced sentences are not to be repeated as facts, and the system it draws is separate models, not the shared backbone with four heads that the programme's decisions page had marked locked ([chapter 12](12-detectors-the-architecture-and-the-version-1-training-plan.md)).

## What we know

A mined panel gets its level proposal through six lanes held together by files, eight lines of evidence and a deterministic decoder that holds the caption hard when it fits and records what it used. On the decode of 14 September the level model decided the most panels and was the weakest decider, 37.9 percent agreement with the caption on 169 caption-free panels against 73.1 percent for the vision-language read, on a subset selected by failure, so none of these figures is an accuracy. The films-only model is near 99 percent on held-out films of the regions it saw (98.7 percent on 8,422 crops, person-disjointness not re-verified) and 35.7 percent on renders. All-spine v1's 92.7 percent was a training-set score; v2's 64.2 percent held out and the Honest 99 pair are records, not final values. The night's three faults were fixed by changing the bank, by a split filter and by a guarded chain with a content-addressed registry. The decoder could not name T13 or L6 before 15 September, and afterwards its penalty cost L6 more than the single crop did. No real panel has a verified level, neither bench shows a verdict, and FluoroCap is out of training by directive D1, though the registry records its crops still inside the old fold banks.

## What we do not know

We do not know the model's or the decoder's accuracy on any real panel, because the random bench is unread. We do not know whether the films-only validation was person-disjoint, which set the Honest 99 curve was drawn on, or whether selective prediction survives the G3 split, the evaluation gate and the physics fixes. We do not know whether job 40285209 is the run registered at 08:13 or where the registry stands now; the spine-program records are not reachable here. We do not know what the self-trained detector, the focused fine-tune or the second CUT returned, whether the rare-column penalty was retuned, whether the sacrum's low recall is a token convention, which L5 convention the captions encode, how many VerSeFusion cases carry an L6 or a T13, or what a reader would name on a 320-pixel panel without its caption. The Noisy Student, conformal-prediction and logit-adjustment references were not checked against their papers, nor the Honest 99 page's scoping-review finding that 0 of 15 level-determination countermeasures were tested against an outcome (Fisher p = 0.024) against the review's own record, which is not in this repository.

## What would change our mind

A read of the level verification bench by two blind readers and an adjudicator, in a store that keeps each read, would turn this chapter's agreements into accuracies. If the decoder's runs proved right on the random draw at about the rate the vision-language read and the caption term suggest, the decoder would earn its place as the readers' starting proposal; if the model's 37.9 percent proved typical of the random draw rather than of a subset selected by failure, its weight in the decoder would need rethinking. Re-running the selective-prediction curve on the G3 split through the evaluation gate, on renders made after the physics fixes, would decide whether 99 percent at about a third coverage belongs to the method or to the leaked bank. A held-out L6 test on which the decoder named L6 at least as often as the single crop would show the penalty fixed. A reader study of 320-pixel panels shown without their captions would say whether the corpus can test exact levels at all, or only regions.
