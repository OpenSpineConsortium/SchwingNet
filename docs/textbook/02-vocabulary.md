# 02 Vocabulary: anchors, levels, devices and evidence grades

Label: schwingnet-internal. The chapter quotes the project's records for its own planners and stays in this private repository until the provisional is filed; it names each method only by what it is for, leaving how any stage works to Part III.

This chapter fixes the words the rest of the book uses, so that a planner can read any later chapter without a glossary. For each recurring term it says what the term names, what it is for and which artifact or repository file fixed that meaning, and, where the record uses one word for two things, which sense the book means. It lists the words the project forbids or replaces and defines the four evidence grades every provenance table carries. It defines and never explains: how a count is made, a name chosen, a render drawn or FluoroCap's evidence weighed belongs to Part III, chapters 10 to 16.

## How a meaning is fixed here

A term means what the canonical artifact of its cluster says it means, as [the clusters](../../artifacts/_synthesis/clusters.md) name them, unless the repository defines it in code or in a memory fact; then the repository wins, since its tools hold every record to that definition. A figure that illustrates a word obeys the book's rules for numbers: one that [the concept ledger](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md) or [the contradictions record](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions.md) has not settled is described, never quoted as a value. A word with several senses gets its spine sense in the body, and the Homonyms table gathers the rest.

## Levels, columns, counts and names

### Levels and regions

A level is the anatomical name of one vertebra. The vocabulary is fixed in code: the level encoder's 27 classes, C1 to C7, T1 to T13, L1 to L6 and the sacrum, held as `LEVELS` in [src/schwingnet/catalog.py](../../src/schwingnet/catalog.py) and taken over by the X-ray annotation format ([docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md](../../docs/research/xr-annotation-format.md)), so T13 and L6 are labels in their own right and not notes beside L1 or L5. The same file names T13 and L6 the transitional classes, "the rare classes the registry counts persons for separately", and gives each level a region: cervical, thoracic, lumbar, or the sacrum as a region of its own. A body a reader can locate but not name is written "?" with its corners kept.

### The column and where a count starts

A column is the stack of vertebrae of one spine. The [Vertebral Count Anomaly Rule](../../artifacts/vertebral-count-anomaly-rule/) fixed the ordinary column as 23 bodies, C2 to C7, T1 to T12 and L1 to L5, because "C1 is excluded from these datasets". Its check is named here by its purpose and its stated limit, "it flags the column rather than the level"; how it decides is [chapter 14](14-transitional-anatomy-two-anchors-disagreement-and-abstention.md)'s.

A count is an ordinal, and it means nothing until its starting point is said. Counting from C2 as the first body, the anomaly rule makes T1 the seventh, L1 the nineteenth and S1 the twenty-fourth; the [FluoroCap Reality Check](../../artifacts/fluorocap-reality-check-czudvb/) proposed a convention from C2 that makes them the eighth, the twentieth and the twenty-fifth, which holds only if C1 is counted. [The level-naming contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/level-naming-and-counting.md) confirm the clash (row 42) and ask that the project's label-convention document be checked. The annotation format therefore records every count with its basis, from C2, the first rib or the sacrum, and the [student onboarding](../../artifacts/spine-program-student-onboarding/) page warns that "two vertebra label schemes exist and mixing them raises no error", so names pass through the toolkit's `labels_for`, never a fixed map.

### Count against name

The column call is the image's record of its column, written once per image: the thoracic and lumbar count, T12L5 for the ordinary column and T12L6 or T13L5 for a variant, with the count's basis and the reader's confidence, sure, likely or cannot tell.

The distinction the book turns on is count against name. A count says how far a body lies from an anchor; a name says which vertebra it is. [Counting Levels Under Fluoroscopy](../../artifacts/counting-levels-under-fluoroscopy/) says "The method never asserts 'L4'; it asserts 'four from the sacrum'". The anomaly rule says the same of a column that is not ordinary: "the ordinal is reported and the name is withheld". The 22:50 build of [SchwingNet Figures](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-xyz6kr/), whose 23 bodies are "numbered from the sacral anchor, no names", supersedes the 20:12 [build](../../artifacts/schwingnet-figures-jshksq/) of that evening, which alone still describes the captions of rows a to j and the three audit figures. Counting is [chapter 10](10-counting-from-an-anchor-along-a-sweep.md)'s subject, naming [chapter 11](11-naming-from-morphology-context-and-a-sweep.md)'s, and [chapter 16](16-method-decisions-and-what-would-reverse-them.md) says how the two lines are meant to coexist.

A counting-inferred label is a level a dataset's authors assigned by position along the column rather than by reading the anatomy, as BUU-LSPINE's are. The [Level Context Ladder](../../artifacts/level-context-ladder-xd7eltc/) fixed the rule that such labels are "never scored", so they are never truth for an evaluation.

### How right a name is

The Level Context Ladder also fixed the accuracy words. Exact level means the name matches the truth, within one level that it is off by at most one, and region that it falls in the right region. A whole-case shift is an error in which "every vertebra in a case" moves "one level cranial or caudal together". The ladder called within one level "the honest primary metric for a level-confirmation tool", since at wide context most of its errors were such shifts. Vertebrae visible is its measure of context: one, three or five bodies in view, the target among them. Its own figures predate the leakage rule of 23 September and are left to chapter 11. The zero-shot AP result the concept ledger marks current, 276 of 631 C-ArmLSpine bodies named exactly, 43.7 percent with no interval given, is recorded as a one-level cranial shift ([ledger row 107](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-107-zero-shot-ap-exact-level-accuracy-on-c-armlspine); [docs/research/demo-plan.md](../../docs/research/demo-plan.md)).

## Anchors, sweeps, windows and declines

An anchor is a structure whose identity is known without counting, so that a count can start from it: "the anchor structures (sacrum, C2)" in the [Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol](../../artifacts/retrospective-fluoroscopy-protocol/), anchors "the surgeon already trusts" in the counting proposal.

A sweep is a short series of overlapping exposures taken along the spine from an anchor to the level in hand. A chain is the ordered run of bodies a sweep establishes. A walk is a count along the chain to a target, and "a walk is exact when the mark lands on the body truly k from the anchor, scored among walks that reached the target", so a walk score is read with the share of walks that reached it. [Counting From The Sacrum](../../artifacts/counting-from-the-sacrum/), the canonical report of the counting line, says why detection comes first: "Recall compounds over eight to twelve bodies." Hence counting grade, the [OR Readiness Ledger](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/)'s bar of a per-body recall of at least 97 percent, a target that, as docs/research/demo-plan.md tables the reported stack's status, "is met by no held-out cell".

Overlap is how much of the column two consecutive exposures share, in bodies; what it does to naming is chapter 11's (level-naming contradictions, row 15). A window is the run of vertebrae in one frame's view. [Naming Vertebrae Without an Anchor](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-an-anchor-3dfzmh/) cut films into windows of two to nine vertebrae and sorted them into the feasibility report's states: state A holds a cranial anchor, state B a caudal one, and state C, the isolated stack, holds neither sacrum, nor ribs, nor cervical spine in its labels. An interior window is stricter, since its pixels exclude both ends of the annotated stack. A naming result is quoted with its state, or it cannot be set beside another.

Coverage is the share of frames or walks a system answers, and a decline is one it deliberately leaves unanswered. The record uses decline in two senses that differ in what a decline costs. On the counting line a walk that cannot be completed is declined, and "a decline is a repeat exposure, not a wrong level". On the naming line frames are declined to reach a chosen coverage, and there "a decline is not a free retake", since retakes scored the same as the frames they replaced: "a decline means count, or sweep further, not move and reshoot". The transitional pages call the act abstention. The retrospective protocol's decline rate is the fraction of series given no count, with the fraction of cases that have no anchored series. No contradictions page sets the two senses side by side, so a reported decline rate says which one it counts.

Registration-free names a method that uses no preoperative CT. The [Registration-Free Level ID Feasibility](../../artifacts/registration-free-level-id-feasibility-uramo1/) review set the term against LevelCheck (De Silva 2016; Otake 2013), which "needs the patient's preoperative CT (or MRI) and 3D-2D registration". The review is a member of its cluster, not superseded; the one page recorded as contradicting it, the canonical [Naming Vertebrae Without CT](../../artifacts/naming-vertebrae-without-ct-hjy4vc/), tables as a published comparator a figure of Huang 2022 that the review found did not survive verification (level-naming contradictions, row 0).

## Transitional anatomy

### The variants

A transitional vertebra sits at the junction of two regions and carries features of both. At the lumbosacral junction it is an LSTV, a sacralised L5 joined to the sacrum or a lumbarised S1 freed from it; at the thoracolumbar junction it is a TLTV. A numerical variant is a column of 23 or 25 presacral segments instead of 24, put at 7.7 percent of whole-spine MRIs with no n beside it (Tins 2016 in the Reality Check; Protocol 1 gives it uncited), and the feasibility review names T13, L6 and a lumbarised S1 as the numerical variants. L6 is a sixth lumbar-type vertebra and T13 a thirteenth rib-bearing one. A lumbar rib is a rib on L1; a stump twelfth rib is a short last rib, which the [LSTV Verification Bench](../../artifacts/lstv-verification-bench-6m2qhr/) lists as an absent or hypoplastic twelfth rib; and a cervical rib is a rib on a cervical vertebra. The bench gives its readers this closed list.

Four lumbar and six lumbar are counts; sacralised and lumbarised are anatomy. A Castellvi sacralisation need not change the count (transitional contradictions, row 19), so the two vocabularies are not interchangeable, and the pages that report BUU-LSPINE's reads in one or the other give 129 and 130 for what may be one class, a pair the ledger keeps open ([row 24](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-24-buu-lspine-sacralization-four-lumbar-cases)).

### Castellvi's types

Castellvi's classification types an LSTV from I to IV, and the records define it only in part. Type I is "a coronal transverse-process height of 19 mm or more" ([Research directions from the data holding](../../artifacts/research-directions-from-the-data-holding-kcyckb/)), with no fusion and an unchanged count (the Reality Check), and type III is "bony fusion L5 to ala" (Research directions). The records use the subtypes Ib, IIa, IIb, IIIa, IIIb and IV, and the annotation format stores a type with its side, but no record defines the a and b suffixes or type IV. The usual reading of the published classification, type II a false joint, a one side and b both, and type IV a type II on one side with a type III on the other, is stated whole in no record and was not checked against the paper, so the book carries it as Unverified.

Typing is a CT question. The feasibility review reports radiograph typing at an inter-reader kappa of 0.16 to 0.32 (Hanhivaara 2024) and 32 of 91 radiograph subtypes, 35.2 percent, disagreeing with CT (Hou 2020), the readers "mostly under-calling bony fusion as pseudoarthrosis".

On which types change the count, the records disagree. Research directions takes types II to IV, "9 to 11 % of CTs"; the Reality Check takes types III and IV, "about 16% of LSTV"; and the [Rib Anchor Audit](../../artifacts/rib-anchor-audit-t7wr2h/) calls ilium contact sacralisation, "Castellvi II-IV by definition", which the transitional contradictions find stated with no source (row 12). A count of count-changing LSTV says which reading it uses.

### Prevalence, as the record carries it

The plan asks for the LSTV prevalence figure to be confirmed against its meta-analysis. A pooled prevalence of 15.3 percent across 83 studies is carried by the Reality Check, with a range of 4.5 to 35.6 percent, and by Protocol 1; neither names the meta-analysis's first author, Protocol 1 lists it under "References to be completed", and the paper was not read for this chapter, so the figure stays Reported and its confirmation is owed. The Reality Check also gives 28.6 percent of 3,855 abdominal CTs when type I is counted, 68 percent of those type I and 16 percent types III and IV, a series that [State of the literature](../../artifacts/state-of-the-literature-datasets-held-missing-size-and-power-8hg1ay/) names as Hanhivaara 2020. Two contradictions pages disagree on whether the 68 percent is sourced where it is used: [the transitional page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/transitional-vertebrae-and-border-variants.md) finds it uncited (row 36), [the verification page](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/verification-benches-overnight-audits-and-or-readiness.md) traces it to that series (row 35).

One misreading the record has settled. The [OpenSpine Board](../../artifacts/openspine-board-t3gfds/) called BUU-LSPINE's 4.0 percent "the population rate"; [the datasets contradictions](../../artifacts/_synthesis/contradictions/datasets-holdings-and-licences.md) confirm that it is a flag rate on lateral films and carry the pooled 15.3 percent and the planning priors of 9 to 18 percent (Hanhivaara 2020 low, Nardo 2012 high) as the population rate instead (row 41). The board is superseded by [Spine Program Decisions](../../artifacts/spine-program-decisions-qqhkqz/), which repeats the claim; the board alone still holds its grid job list and the dataset paper's five fixes. [Protocol 1](../../artifacts/protocol-1-eprotocol-draft-mkmh7p/) is superseded by the Retrospective Fluoroscopy Protocol and alone still holds its Exempt Category 4 request with hypotheses H1 to H5 and the aims the later draft dropped. The L6 rate is unsettled: [Counting Against Two Anchors](../../artifacts/counting-against-two-anchors-qkktgu/) gives "roughly 2%" with no source, State of the literature uses a cited prior of 3.2 percent (Sun 2025), and the transitional contradictions confirm the clash (row 15).

### Truth and its tiers

The [handoff of 8 October](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/) fixed the order of truth, "read above two-labeller above single-labeller above morphology-only", and a CT that cannot count is `no_count`, "never ordinary". In the annotation format every annotation names its reader, a second reader writes a second file and never an edit, and two readers' calls on one image are the transitional stratum's two-reader truth. A labeller need not be a person: C-ArmLSpine's "two labellers agreeing on 814 of 910 bodies", as the [SchwingNet Data Registry](../../artifacts/schwingnet-data-registry/) puts it, are two automatic labellers (datasets contradictions, row 5). FluoroCap's reference standard is explicit: its decoded level "is a proposal for the readers, not ground truth", to be read by two readers blind to each other and to the proposal, a third adjudicating, with the dataset, the model and the analysis plan locked "before any performance claim" ([FluoroCap Dataset Card](../../artifacts/fluorocap-dataset-card-xwd4wx/)).

## Images, renders and devices

A frame is one image, and the catalog names what made it with one modality word (src/schwingnet/catalog.py): `xa`, a C-arm spot frame; `rf`, fluoroscopy; `cbct_projection`, one projection of a cone-beam sweep; `dx`, a digital plain film; `cr`, a computed-radiography or scanned film; and `render`, "a DRR or other synthetic image". A render is a synthetic image made from a CT, a DRR, a digitally reconstructed radiograph, among them; a styled frame and a crop are derived images too, and each inherits the person of its source CT. How renders are drawn, and how far they fall from real frames, is [chapter 13](13-renders-physics-and-the-sim-to-real-gap.md)'s. A device is the machine that made a frame, by make and model; the catalog's device classes are its coarse families, so that a query spans makes and models without naming them: `mobile_c_arm`, `fixed_c_arm`, `cbct_gantry`, `film` and `synthetic`.

The OR Readiness Ledger fixed the labels that say how far a result sits from training. Device held out means the test device gave no frame to training, as in the ledger's lodo rows, each leaving one device family out. Same device means the device was in training and only the test cases were held out. Init exposure marks a held-out cell whose test specimen still reached the model indirectly, as the ledger documents for one DeepFluoro specimen. Population held out means the test population never entered training, as in a zero-shot score. Boxes given means the result was scored on the truth boxes, so detection errors do not enter it. How far each label carries to the operating room is [chapter 20](20-readiness-for-the-operating-room.md)'s question.

The box convention is what a truth box encloses: a vertebral-body box, as in C-ArmLSpine's truth, or a whole-vertebra outline, as in COBRA's and DeepFluoro's. The [ledger's rescore](../../artifacts/or-readiness-ledger/) of 2026-09-22 scored one held-out-device detector on 56 C-ArmLSpine frames and 287 vertebrae at 5.2 percent against body boxes, 90.6 percent against outlines and 90.9 percent under a mutual-centre rule it calls convention-free, each a record, not a final value: the verification contradictions mark that recall superseded by the handoff of 8 October (row 3), whose 231 of 287 bodies, 80.5 percent with an interval of 74.1 to 86.8, docs/research/demo-plan.md tables. A detection comparison therefore names its convention ([chapter 19](19-what-a-valid-comparison-needs.md)). The position prior is the ledger's warning about layout: a fixed box, the training set's median sacrum box, hits the truth sacrum at an IoU of at least 0.5 on 85.7 percent of C-ArmLSpine test frames, with no interval given, so "sacrum found" on that set is "partly a statement about layout".

## Sources, reach and the catalog's words

### Source and registry

A source is one dataset as the project records it, and the word registry names two things. The dataset registry is [data/sources/](../../data/sources/), one JSON record per source, rendered as [docs/research/datasets-inventory.md](../../docs/research/datasets-inventory.md) by tools/sources.py, whose check fails when page and records differ; on 2026-10-24 it holds 111 records. The SchwingNet Data Registry artifact is its rendering of 2026-10-20, with 60 records, a count since updated (concept ledger, row 17). The catalog's registry is data/registry/, 24 entries, one for each source the catalog takes in, each with its identity level and person-key rule ([data/registry/README.md](../../data/registry/README.md)). The first says what a source is and how to get it; the second says how it enters the catalog. The dataset registry sorts its records into six groups titled in [src/schwingnet/sources.py](../../src/schwingnet/sources.py), from intraoperative fluoroscopy stills, "the target domain", to future directions. [Chapter 04](04-the-registry-and-the-holding.md) walks the registry source by source.

### The reach words

A reach word says how far a source is from the project's hands. The words are stored in lower case and printed in capitals, and `held` is printed HAVE (`REACH_WORDS` in src/schwingnet/sources.py). The inventory page defines them: HAVE, on the grid now; GET, public or by request, not yet fetched; GATED, behind a data use agreement, an application or a login the project does not hold; EMBARGOED, released on a date; NONE, does not exist publicly; EXCLUDED, held or seen and set aside for a stated reason. Of the 111 records on 2026-10-24, 29 are HAVE, 61 GET, 15 GATED, 1 EMBARGOED, 2 NONE and 3 EXCLUDED, counted here from the records; the artifact uses the same words over its 60. One NONE record is the gap itself: "no public set of real cervical or thoracic C-arm frames with named levels".

Checked against data/sources/ and tools/catalog.py as they stand, as the plan asked, the reach words and the catalog's terms match. [tools/catalog.py](../../tools/catalog.py) itself uses no reach word: the words belong to the dataset registry's renderers, tools/sources.py and [tools/reproduce_table.py](../../tools/reproduce_table.py), which carries its own copy of the mapping.

### Bank, shard, version and part

A bank is a folder of frames and labels in the builders' format, named by family and version, as the allspine v9 bank or the `anchor_drr` bank are; it is the unit the earlier work trained and scored on, and the catalog's ingest turns a bank into shards ([docs/research/data-catalog.md](../../docs/research/data-catalog.md)). A shard is one ingest of one source, named `<source>/<ingest date>-<12 hex>`, and it holds one kind only: train, test or review. A catalog version is one read-only SQLite file built from the shards' manifests, logged in VERSIONS.jsonl, the latest named by CURRENT and nothing ever rewritten. A part is where a person's frames go: train, validation or test.

### Persons, items, leaks and splits

The person key is the identity a split keeps on one side, and the identity level is what that identity is for a source: patient, study, cadaver or source volume. The item key names one acquisition, since "an item is one acquisition, not a file"; splits are enforced on person keys and duplicates on item keys. The leakage rule the owner set on 2026-09-23, as the handoff records it, defines held out: an evaluation item is held out for a checkpoint only if its person is in neither the training set nor the validation set of that checkpoint or of any ancestor it was initialised from. Validation is seen; a derived image inherits its source's person; public ImageNet and COCO weights are exempt as ancestors only when declared; and "unknown is not clean".

A leak is an evaluation item that breaks the rule, and a leaked family is a line of banks or models whose evaluations did. The handoff records two historical leaks, each a collection that entered the banks under two names, and names the leaked families allspine v2, v6 and v7, `anchor_drr` and vindrps. Every number from them is record only, allspine v2's 99.02 percent shortcut-free film number among them, quoted here as a record, not a final value ([the handoff](../../artifacts/handoff-schwingnet-ctspinopelvic1k-and-the-imaging-programme-rbcdmf/)). The fix was the G3 split, person-disjoint, applied on 2026-09-23 and never rerun in place, and `split-check` in tools/catalog.py now holds every catalog version to the REFORMS split rule (REFORMS, Kapoor 2024's consensus recommendations for science that uses machine learning). [Chapter 09](09-splits-leakage-and-licences.md) holds the splits, the leaks and the licences.

A fold is one part of a k-fold cross-validation, as in the ledger's five-fold scores on C-ArmLSpine, whose case count the concept ledger keeps open (rows 0 and 104). C-ArmLSpine's record counts 149 cases, 299 spot frames, 149 CBCT volumes and 3 real L6 cases; the cases are acquisition sessions, and how many people they are the record does not settle (datasets contradictions, row 2). The frozen test persons in [data/splits/test-persons.json](../../data/splits/test-persons.json) are five of its cases: the three real L6 persons, who form the transitional stratum, and the two demo sweeps.

### Manifests

Manifest has five senses, which the Homonyms table lists. In the data-platform sense it records how each source was obtained and how to obtain it again; the repository's copy under data/reproduce/ lists 211 sources ([docs/memory/the-dataset-is-obtained-again-from-data-reproduce.md](../../docs/memory/the-dataset-is-obtained-again-from-data-reproduce.md)), while the [Imaging data holding](../../artifacts/imaging-data-holding-manifest-migration-and-index-fqmtjs/) page of 8 October cards 212. That page superseded the [Spine X-ray Data Handoff](../../artifacts/spine-x-ray-data-handoff-bddbdd/) of 3 September, which alone still holds its access details: a download tool's folder cap, an un-ingested subset of 400 pairs and the login route of one cervical set.

## FluoroCap's words

FluoroCap is the project's corpus of published spine fluoroscopy and radiograph figure panels whose captions name the vertebral level. A figure is a published article's figure, and a panel is one image cut from it and written to one manifest row. The corpus holds 29,500 panels, the value the concept ledger marks current ([row 132](../../artifacts/_synthesis/concepts.md#row-132-number-of-fluorocap-panel-images)), and 72.8 percent of them are reproductions with no pixel residual, as the Data Registry and the [Fluoroscopy Data Atlas](../../artifacts/fluoroscopy-data-atlas/) both say. The licence tiers are A, CC0 or public domain; B, CC BY; C, CC BY-SA; D, a non-commercial licence; and E, other or unknown, held in a review queue until a person reads the article's licence statement. Dataset A, the research set, spans the tiers, and Dataset B, the clean set, keeps tiers A and B only. [Chapter 08](08-fluorocap-as-a-dataset.md) treats FluoroCap as a dataset.

An evidence line is one of FluoroCap's "independent lines of evidence per panel, none of which decides alone", and the decoder is what joins them into one proposed reading per panel, with its reasons ([FluoroCap Pipeline Map](../../artifacts/fluorocap-pipeline-map-h9rmbs/)). How it does so is [chapter 15](15-fluorocaps-evidence-decoder-and-the-level-model.md)'s; here it matters only that its output is a proposal and never truth.

A pseudolabel is a label a model wrote. The annotation format keeps pseudolabels apart from human reads: their reader is `model:<run id>`, they sit in shards of their own, and no test person is ever pseudo-labelled. What training on them costs the anomalous stratum the concept ledger keeps open (row 182).

The [FluoroCap Corpus Audit](../../artifacts/fluorocap-corpus-audit-9hjzzs/) of 14 September is superseded by the Reality Check of the next day, which re-measured the same corpus and model. The audit alone still holds that day's measurements, each a record, not a final value: caption-held-out containment of 56.8 percent on 389 panels, the level model alone right on 38.3 percent, and image size as the binding constraint.

## Records of a run

[The Lab Recipe](../../artifacts/the-lab-recipe-1t2gvp/) named the lab's records: "A paper is a chain of files, each written by a script that recorded what it read, and a number enters the manuscript only through that chain." A task is one script, one job script, one results file and the manifest keys that quote it. PREREG.md, what will be measured and what counts as failure, is frozen with its sha256 before the first number; DEVIATIONS.md dates each departure from it with the reason; JOURNAL.md records what ran; each results file carries a `_run` block from the script that wrote it; numbers_manifest.json maps each key to its results file; and the manuscript prints a number only as `\RESULT{key}`.

The repository's v1 flow keeps the idea under new names ([docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md](../../docs/memory/the-v1-flow.md)). RUN.json, the run record, is written before the first step, and the run id is a hash of the commit, the catalog's hash and the start. A result row gives correct over n with its 95 percent Wilson interval, the persons behind it, the run id, the commit, the clean-tree flag and the catalog's hash. The ledger row is one line per run in [docs/research/v1-demo/LEDGER.md](../../docs/research/v1-demo/LEDGER.md), appended by the record step and never by hand.

A witness frame is one of the fixed real frames every checkpoint is drawn on from epoch 0, so that improvement is a sequence of pictures a person reads rather than a curve they trust ([src/schwingnet/witness.py](../../src/schwingnet/witness.py); [docs/memory/evaluation-and-wandb.md](../../docs/memory/evaluation-and-wandb.md)). There are 24 validation frames of C-ArmLSpine across L1 to L5, which the trainer draws at every epoch, and, for the evaluator, 24 test frames plus two frames of each L6 case and eight of each demo sweep, which never choose a checkpoint. The first run on real frames freezes the list into data/witness/witness.json, against which every later run is checked; on 2026-10-24 that file does not exist yet. A checkpoint, in this sense, is one epoch's saved weights, and the one reported is chosen on the whole validation part, never on epoch 0. [Chapter 18](18-metrics-intervals-and-witness-frames.md) holds the metrics and the witness frames.

## Words that are forbidden or replaced

The plan asks which words are forbidden or replaced. The record gives five rules, and their scope matters.

1. No name is invented from a count: a count is an ordinal from its anchor, and a name is given only where the anatomy that fixes it is known (the counting proposal; the anomaly rule).
2. The French-derived word for where data came from is never used: "write origin, lineage or source" (the onboarding page). It heads the handoff's "binding rules for anything that leaves", so it binds every page, slide, caption and letter that leaves the lab; this internal book keeps the word as the heading of its closing tables, which a planner renames in anything copied out.
3. Version numbers are whole integers, "no fractional versions"; older records still carry fractional ones, the FluoroCap card's manifest 1.1 and dataset 0.2 among them.
4. What no file records is written "not recorded", never guessed.
5. A printed number enters through a manifest key, "never typed".

The same binding rules keep captions at or under 60 words and never state the commercial goal or name the product, and the dataset request letters carry no external text that "says or implies that a model, a neural network, machine learning, AI or training is built or used" ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)). Three replacements follow from this chapter: two-labeller never means two people unless the record says so, a flag rate is never called a population rate, and sacralised is never written for four lumbar.

## The four evidence grades

Every provenance table in the book grades each statement with one of four words, set by the book's preface ([the table of contents](../../artifacts/_synthesis/table-of-contents.md)). Measured is a number read from a result file, or computed by tested code on held-out data, with its n and interval, and a statement is graded Measured only where the chapter's writer read the result file or a research note that tables the run with its file. Reported is a statement an artifact or a paper makes without the file behind it being read. Inferred is a judgment drawn from measurements, or a design claim with no outcome measured. Unverified is a statement asserted with no source, or contradicted by another record. This chapter also grades Measured a definition or a count it read directly from the repository's code or data files; a count of records carries no interval.

The artifacts grade themselves with Verified, Inferred, Reported and Open, defined in section 1 of the [Symphony and Beethoven Taxonomy Spec](../../artifacts/symphony-and-beethoven-taxonomy-spec-lcbqro/) and taught by the [Symphony and Beethoven Taxonomy Manual](https://claude.ai/artifact/RE4jtXqsFFgiZxhQLamUDR). Verified means checked against a primary source opened on the as-of date, with a link or a file and line; Inferred, following from verified facts, with the reasoning beside it; Reported, taken from a secondary source because the primary was unreachable, to be re-read at the primary before anyone quotes it; Open, not yet checked, or unknown. Two of the manual's rules hold in this book too: "A grade rises only with new evidence, and falls as soon as a source contradicts it", and "A claim whose only source stays out of reach keeps the grade Reported".

The mapping is this. An artifact's Verified is Measured only where the book's writer read the file behind it, and Reported otherwise; Inferred and Reported keep their names; Open becomes Unverified; and whatever a contradictions page confirms as contradicted is Unverified, whatever its artifact called it. The preface's short form, Verified to Measured, holds where the file was read.

Three status vocabularies sit beside the grades. The results catalog the handoff of 8 October describes, which wins wherever another file differs, marks each result CURRENT, CURRENT-SEL ("measured on the split that chose the checkpoint"), HELD-OUT NOT QUOTED, SUPERSEDED, DISPUTED, REFUTED, RECORD ONLY, NOT RECORDED or OPEN, with the rigor marks of the audit of 2026-09-22, such as leaked, selection split, one seed and automatic truth. The concept ledger marks each value current, superseded or open. The contradictions record rules pairs of pages confirmed, superseded, refuted (they agree once read in context) or cannot tell, and pairs of numbers by words such as updated-later and same-number-restated. The book reads them by one rule: only a value marked CURRENT in the catalog or current in the ledger is quoted as current; a CURRENT-SEL number is never called held out; a SUPERSEDED, RECORD ONLY or leaked-family number appears only as a record, linked, with the words "a record, not a final value"; and an OPEN, DISPUTED or cannot-tell figure, or one the ledger keeps open, is described and never quoted. The contradictions record is itself checked: on review, 26 of the 30 verdicts that put a candidate on the confirmed list held, 87 percent with a Wilson 95 percent interval of 70 to 95, so a row is read before it is relied on.

## What we know

The level vocabulary is fixed in code: 27 classes from C1 to the sacrum, T13 and L6 the transitional ones, four regions, the annotation format using the same list. A count is an ordinal from a named anchor and never becomes a name; the ordinary column is 23 bodies from C2 to L5, and every count states its basis. The six reach words print `held` as HAVE, and on 2026-10-24 the dataset registry holds 111 records, 29 of them HAVE. The catalog's parts, shard kinds, identity levels, modalities and device classes are fixed in src/schwingnet/catalog.py. Held out means never seen by the checkpoint or any ancestor, in training or validation, and a derived image carries its source's person. The box convention, the position prior and the ledger's result labels say what a detection or naming figure measures and how far it sits from training. An artifact's Verified reaches Measured only where the file behind it was read.

## What we do not know

No record defines Castellvi's a and b suffixes or type IV, and neither Castellvi's paper nor the meta-analysis behind the pooled 15.3 percent, whose first author no record names, was read. The records disagree on which Castellvi types change the count, and the population rate of L6 is unsettled between an unsourced 2 percent and a cited 3.2 percent prior. Two contradictions pages rule differently on whether the 68 percent type I share is sourced where it is used. How many people C-ArmLSpine's 149 cases are, and how many cases the five-fold scores held, the record leaves open. The two senses of decline are not reconciled. The witness frames are chosen by rule but not yet frozen into a file, and whether the project's label-convention document settles the counting convention has not been checked.

## What would change our mind

Reading the LSTV meta-analysis would confirm or correct the pooled 15.3 percent and give it a first author, and reading Castellvi's paper would turn the usual reading of the suffixes and of type IV into a sourced statement or replace it. A label-convention document that counts from C1 would replace the anomaly rule's enumeration as the book's convention. A later rendering of data/sources/ would replace the tally of 111 records. The first recorded run on real frames would fix the witness frames in a file that can be cited. A final retrospective protocol that defines a decline by what it costs the surgeon would settle the word's two senses.
