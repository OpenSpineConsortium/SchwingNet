# 33 What to run next, by the weight of the claims

Label: schwingnet-internal. This chapter weighs the programme's claims and ranks the work that bears on them, for the project's members only; it names each method by what it is for, leaves how any stage works to Part III, and stays in this private repository until the provisional is filed.

[Chapter 28](28-the-ranked-experiment-backlog.md) ranked the backlog by the evidence's own order, measurement first, human reads second and models third, and then by the calendar. That order answers what the programme should learn next. The owner asked a different question on 24 October: which experiment bears most critically on the claims of novelty, functionality and integrity that the patent, the talk, the manuscripts, the grant and the operating-room study rest on. This chapter answers it. It writes every claim SchwingNet makes or means to make into one register, [docs/textbook/claims.json](claims.json), each with its kind, its statement in plain words, the evidence under it with a grade for each link, its own grade, which is the weakest link, and what falls if it fails. It writes every outstanding experiment and open question into the same file as an item that says which claims it bears on, how far it could move each, what it costs and whether it waits on the owner's word. And it ranks the items with a stated formula in [tools/claims_rank.py](../../tools/claims_rank.py), whose `next` command gives the top item a session can start alone and the top item that needs the owner, for the Claude integration of issue #220 to read. The ranking is a tool for a planner, not a verdict: it weighs claims, not knowledge, and its limits are set out below.

## How the register is built

### What counts as a claim

A claim here is any sentence a public or legal document of the programme says or plans to say, as the chapters of this book and the artifacts behind them record it. The register holds 36. Eight are novelty claims, statements that nobody has done something, which bear first on the provisional due by 30 October and on the talk's first-time sentence. Sixteen are functionality claims, statements that SchwingNet works to some accuracy and latency on some material, which bear on the talk, paper 1, paper 2, the grant and the study. Twelve are integrity claims, statements that the numbers are honest: the person is the unit of every interval, no test person leaked into training, comparisons are made on the same frames, the truth is read by a person, and every number the talk shows comes from a record in this repository. The book's chapters supplied each claim's words; the claim's own entry in the register names the chapter and the source.

### The grades and the weakest link

The grades are the book's four ([chapter 03](03-what-is-measured-and-what-is-claimed.md)). Measured means a number read here from a result file or computed by tested code; Reported, stated by an artifact, a paper or the earlier programme's records without the file behind it read here; Inferred, a judgment or a design claim with no outcome; Unverified, no source, or a source that contradicts the claim. A claim's grade is the weakest of its links, and the check refuses a register in which it is not. That rule is harsh on purpose. The lateral naming figure, 595 of 617 on a C-arm the stack never trained on, is Reported, but the claim that SchwingNet names levels on that device also rests on a person count the record cannot settle, so a claim that needs both is only as strong as the count.

A novelty claim cannot be Measured, since nothing measures an absence. For novelty the ladder is read this way: Unverified while a named prior-art item is unread or contradicts the claim, Inferred once the search is recorded and finds nothing against it, and Reported once counsel's written read says so. The check refuses an item that promises to make a novelty claim Measured.

### What falls if a claim fails

Each claim names its stakes, from seven: the patent, the talk, paper 1 (the C-arm naming manuscript held for technology transfer), paper 2 (SchwingNet against registration-assisted surgeons), the plain-film manuscript rows, the grant as submitted on 16 October, and the operating-room study planned for 2027. A claim's criticality is the sum of its stakes' weights. The patent weighs 5, because a provisional is filed once and a claim in it that prior art takes away cannot be put back; the talk 4, because it is public on 2 November; paper 1, paper 2 and the study 3 each, since each can be corrected before it is submitted or approved; the grant and the plain-film rows 2 each, the grant because it has gone in and its award is not expected before December, the film rows because the talk shows fluoroscopy. These are this chapter's weights, Inferred, and they are named constants a person can change in one line.

## The formula

For each item and each claim it bears on, the register gives the grade the item could bring the claim to and whether the item could refute it. The move is the number of grade steps the item could add, with the ladder Unverified 0, Inferred 1, Reported 2, Measured 3, or one step if the item could refute the claim and adds no grade, since finding a claim false before it is made is worth at least a step. The item's own weight is the sum over its claims of the kind's weight times the claim's criticality times the move. An item that other items wait on also carries half the weight of each open item that waits on it, so a free decision that unblocks a heavy experiment rises with it. The score is the weight divided by a cost factor:

`score = (sum over claims of KIND_WEIGHTS[kind] x criticality x move + 0.5 x weight of the items it unlocks) / cost_factor`

`cost_factor = 1 + 0.05 x session hours + 0.5 x days of work + 0.01 x CPU-hours + 0.25 x GPU-hours + 0.1 x readers' hours + 0.0005 x dollars + 2 for each unsized grid or reading cost + 3 when an outside party must act`

The three kinds weigh 1.0 each, since the owner named them together. The cost rates make four GPU-hours, a hundred CPU-hours, two days of work or ten readers' hours each double the cost of a free item; an outside party, counsel or an IRB, adds three to it, so a free item costs four; and where the record gives no size for a grid job or a read, the item carries a fixed penalty of 2 rather than a number this chapter would have to invent. A range counts at its midpoint. Every cost figure in the register names its basis, the chapter or note it comes from, and where the figure is this chapter's own estimate, as the session hours of a reading are, the basis says so and the figure is Inferred. The module's docstring states the same formula, and its constants sit at the top of the file under the names used here.

## The claims by kind

### Novelty

The novelty claims are the weakest part of the register, and the heaviest. The core claim of the disclosure's second part, naming each level from one intraoperative exposure with no registration and no other image of the patient (N1), stands on two partial searches. The read of seven Johns Hopkins items found none that names levels ([docs/research/unberath-lab-items-prior-art.md](../../docs/research/unberath-lab-items-prior-art.md)), and the read of the owner's fluoroscopy list found two papers that already name levels on real images with no CT, Huang 2022 on cadaver long films and Zeng 2024 on intraoperative AP images from one G-arm ([docs/research/fluoroscopy-list-prior-art.md](../../docs/research/fluoroscopy-list-prior-art.md)), so the claim holds only as the whole of it. And three titles that could hold a C-arm naming result, Li 2016, Li 2017 and ABLSpineLevelCheck 2024, have stood unread since September because the publisher's site was blocked ([chapter 19](19-what-a-valid-comparison-needs.md)). With a named item unread, N1 is Unverified.

The narrower claims fare differently. The talk's first-time claim (N2), a per-level naming rate on real C-arm frames at any angle, as a per-angle curve, with its own detector, no CT and a measured latency, is Inferred: no comparator row reports that whole ([docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md](../../docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md), section 2), though it is only as good as the functionality claims under it, which do not yet exist. The claim for the disclosure's first part (N3), a CT classifier that flags a transitional spine whatever segmenter produced the vertebrae, is Unverified, because VERIDAH (Moeller, arXiv 2026), which enumerates T13 and L6 on CT from an upstream localiser and predates the conception, is the closest prior art found and counsel has not read it against the claim. The next-shot element (N4) survives only narrowed: a granted Johns Hopkins family claims choosing and moving to the next X-ray from one image, and Kausch 2020 chooses a C-arm pose from one lumbar X-ray with no CT, so what is left is a shot chosen from the levels already named; it is Inferred, and freedom to operate is counsel's question, not this register's. Carrying a count along a chain of overlapping exposures (N5) has no recorded search beyond the seven items and is Unverified. The narrow claim that survives the transitional search (N7), naming on a single intraoperative image with the transitional column taken into account, is Inferred.

Two novelty claims should not be made at all. The draft's sentence that no surveyed method addresses transitional spines (N6) is contradicted by VERIDAH, by Kwak 2025 and by the VerSe labels, and the prior-art note already asks that it come out. And the September pages' priority sentences (N8), naming solved to a standard nobody has published, a figure with no peer, no published work using renders to give a 2-D model level identity, first to learn naming from renders, first to try it on C-arm frames, rest on no search record, and Huang 2022 and FluoroSAM contradict the last two ([docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md](../../docs/research/state-of-knowledge.md), section 6).

### Functionality

One functionality claim is Reported with a count and an interval: lateral naming on a C-arm the stack never saw, 595 of 617, 96.4 percent [92.3, 98.4] (F1), as the demo plan copies it and the catalog lists it current and held out. Everything else is weaker. AP naming on an unseen device (F2) is contradicted today by 276 of 631, 43.7 percent, the largest error in the record, which the concept ledger marks current; that a counting prior from CT fixes it is Inferred. Naming across gantry angles on real frames (F3) has only a render figure behind it. Counting-grade detection on a device held out entirely (F5), the claim behind "one network for any C-arm", is contradicted by the one held-out detector's 80.5 and 90.6 percent. Transitional naming on real frames (F6) has no verified test person. The disclosure's first part working on CT (F7) is designed and not run. Low latency (F8) is Measured only for a synthetic model with no detector on a container's CPU, p95 15.1 ms, so the claim is Inferred. Version 1 on the frozen real test persons (F9), the deliverable every talk number hangs on, has no number. Naming from C1 to the sacrum (F10) has no number above the lumbar spine on any C-arm frame, and C2 was found on 0 to 8 percent of 25 real-film truths. The decline guarantee of the study (F11) has as its only headline figure a number the catalog calls not quotable. The plain-film figures (F12), the grant's DeepFluoro paragraph (F13) and the sweep's gain on real films (F14) all rest on lineages the record rules leaked or record only. The bench claims of the study, that a hand-moved C-arm keeps the count's step (F15) and that recall reaches 97 percent on the hospital's own devices (F16), are design claims, Inferred, with nothing yet bought or pulled. The counting paragraph (F4) is Unverified for the catalog's reasons above.

### Integrity

The integrity claims are all Unverified, and almost all cheap to raise. The C-ArmLSpine person count (I1) is 149, 148 or 120 by three records; the new workflow trains on a catalog whose leak verdict nothing reads (I2), and the leak join has never run over the real shards (I3); whether one CTSpinoPelvic1K record is one person (I4) is cannot-tell; the evaluator prints a per-vertebra interval and no clustered one (I5); no comparator has been run on the same frames (I6); whether the test witness frames breach paper 2's rule that no test person is scored before its hypothesis is recorded as seen (I7) is unruled; no real-frame number about SchwingNet is in a record here (I8); the 81.3 percent registry figure the grant reads as miscounting is cannot-tell (I10); C-ArmLSpine's poses, from which version 1's truth boxes are projected, are unmeasured (I11); and no person has read a level on any real test frame (I12). The claim that no quoted figure is one the catalog marks superseded or record only (I9) is contradicted by this chapter's own partial read of the catalog, since the grant quotes the counting arms (C3.5) and the renderer's 80.9 percent (C4.2); it holds at most for what is said next, the talk and the book, and a full read would show whether it holds there.

## Claims the record cannot support today

Thirteen claims carry an action. Two should be withdrawn now, before the provisional is filed and before a slide is made: the transitional sentence of the draft (N6) and the September priority sentences (N8), since each is contradicted by a source already read. Eleven should be narrowed. The core naming claim (N1) may be made only as the whole of it, single exposure, no registration, no other image of the patient, a C-arm during surgery, never as level naming on real images alone, which Huang 2022 and Zeng 2024 already show. The next-shot element (N4) is narrowed to a shot chosen from named levels, and its freedom to operate goes to counsel. AP naming on an unseen device (F2) is not claimed; the lateral figure is. "One network for any C-arm" (F5) is not claimed; the held-out detector is described as below the 97 percent the count needs. Naming from C1 to the sacrum (F10) is narrowed to the lumbar spine and the sacrum, the only region with a real-frame number. The counting paragraph (F4), the selective-accuracy figure (F11) and the DeepFluoro paragraph (F13) are narrowed until a clean run replaces each, because the catalog marks their numbers record only or not quotable; the grant has already gone in with them, so the narrowing applies to what is said next. The sweep's gain on real films (F14) is narrowed for the same reason: its lineage is ruled leaked and record only, so the figures are not quoted until the clean re-run (E35). The plain-film figures (F12) are not quoted as held out. And the claim that nothing quoted is record only (I9) is narrowed to the talk and the book, since the grant has gone in with record-only figures.

These are this chapter's readings of the register, Inferred, and each is the owner's to make. None of them asks for a run: they cost a sentence each and remove the risk that a claim is made which the programme's own records contradict.

## How this ranking differs from chapter 28's

Chapter 28 puts the census first and the physics tests second; here the census is thirteenth and the physics correlation test is last, with a weight of zero. The difference is the question. The census decides where each model can be tested, and the physics test asks why real-frame detection is low; neither moves a claim's grade, so a ranking by claims puts them low, and a ranking by what the programme needs to learn puts them high. Both rankings agree on the COBRA L6 read, the counting prior and version 1, and both put the 2027 study last for its cost and its wait. The two lists should be read together: chapter 28 for what to learn, this one for what to say.

## The formula's honest limits

The formula weighs claims, not knowledge. An item that explains a failure without changing what may be said, the physics correlation test or the VerSe L6 count, scores zero, though either may decide which later experiment is worth running. It has no term for time: counsel's read must happen before 30 October and version 1 before 28 October, and the formula ranks them by weight and cost alone, so the dates are given beside the items in the register and are this chapter's to read, not the score's. It has no term for the chance that an item succeeds, or for how far a result would move a number rather than a grade; an item that could raise a claim by three steps scores the same whether that is likely or not. Grade steps are equal: the step from Unverified to Inferred counts as much as the step from Reported to Measured, though the second costs far more evidence. The weights of the stakes and the cost rates are judgments, and different weights reorder the middle of the list; the first few items hold under any weights this chapter tried, since they are both heavy and nearly free, but no sensitivity run is recorded. Unsized costs carry a flat penalty, so an item whose grid cost the record never sized is neither cheap nor dear but average, which flatters some long trainings and harms some short jobs. The unlock share credits an item for what waits on it, which can raise a cheap step that is not itself the bottleneck. And the register itself is this chapter's reading of 36 claims and 42 items from the book's chapters; a claim the documents make that no chapter records is missing, and the draft of the provisional, which sits outside the repositories, was not read.

## Where the book is read

The book is read in three places, each built from the same files. On GitHub, the chapters under docs/textbook/ with their figure records. On the members-only Pages site, which tools/textbook_site.py builds on every push to main and once a day, where every exported artifact page is copied in and shown on its own page, interactive, and each figure carries a button that opens the artifact it came from. And as one private claude.ai artifact, which [.github/workflows/textbook-artifact.yml](../../.github/workflows/textbook-artifact.yml) publishes from that same site and republishes to the same link after each merge that changes the book, where the exported pages open as pages of their own and an Ask Claude box on every page answers from that page's text through the artifact runtime, on the reader's own Claude account; what was published, the commit, its date and every file's hash, is state.json on the branch textbook-artifact. The book goes to claude.ai and not the other way round because a live claude.ai artifact cannot be framed by any other site ([docs/research/textbook-as-an-artifact.md](../research/textbook-as-an-artifact.md)). Nothing of the book is public before the provisional is filed, and the artifact stays private to the account that publishes it until that account shares it.

## What we know

SchwingNet makes or means to make 36 claims the book records: eight of novelty, sixteen of functionality and twelve of integrity. One functionality claim, lateral naming on an unseen C-arm at 96.4 percent of 617 vertebrae, is Reported with its interval; every integrity claim is Unverified; the novelty claims are Inferred at best and five of the eight are Unverified. Two claims are contradicted by sources already read and should be withdrawn, and eleven should be narrowed, one of them, I9, because the catalog shows the grant quoting record-only figures. The earlier programme's catalog, in the read-only clone, settles that 80.5 and 90.6 percent are one detector at two thresholds, that the September 98.2 percent lateral figure is superseded, and that the counting paragraph, the 99 percent selective figure and the DeepFluoro paragraph are record only or not quotable. By the formula, the work that bears most on the claims for its cost is reading the unread prior art, reading the catalog in full, searching the chaining prior art, the COBRA L6 read and two decisions only the owner can make.

## What we do not know

We do not know what Li 2016, Li 2017 and ABLSpineLevelCheck 2024 report, or what counsel will say of VERIDAH and the granted next-shot family. We do not know what the draft of the provisional claims in its own words, since it was not read. We do not know how many persons stand behind C-ArmLSpine's cases, what version 1 scores on a real frame, how fast the frame path runs on the demo's machine, or whether any transitional spine is named right on a real frame. We do not know how the ranking moves under other weights, or what the owner would weigh most among the stakes.

## What would change our mind

A read of the three unread titles that found C-arm naming without registration would turn N1 from a claim to narrow into one to withdraw, and drop E04's weight; a read that found nothing would raise N1 to Inferred and leave counsel as the next step. Counsel's read that VERIDAH discloses Part A would end N3. A version 1 number on the frozen test persons well below 96.4 percent would make F9 a claim to narrow before the talk. Eleven COBRA L6 calls read as false positives would retire the real-frame transitional claim until the retrospective study. A catalog row marking any claim of the grant current where this chapter read it as record only would restore it. And the owner's weights for the stakes, or a word that dates should count, would reorder the list; the formula is built to take them.
