The DRR domain-gap study (the owner's ask of 2026-10-25, #391): tools/domain_gap.py sets FleXray's DRRs and other synthetic sets beside the held fluoroscopy and film corpora, with the replication test of FleXray's renderer; two fetches and two GPU jobs, bench flexray_drr_seg then bench flexray_drr_gap; design and results in docs/research/drr-domain-gap/README.md.

- The groups are data/domain_gap/groups.json (salt schwingnet-drr-gap-v1, 22 groups): each picks files from the sample index, a folder walk, renders made in the job, or FluXray's ThunderDB, with a person rule; the pure rules (person keys, the salted choice, the words) are src/schwingnet/domain_gap.py under the gate, the numerics tools/domain_gap_stats.py, the renderer tools/flexray_render.py over tools/render_blocks.py.
- Rendering is a configuration of named blocks (src/schwingnet/render_blocks.py: DEFAULT is FleXray's released MOOSE recipe on the project's segmenter, FLEXRAY the same on MOOSE labels, PHYSICAL with the attenuation randomisation off, FLUXRAY_RAW the paper's 90-pose raw stream FluXray was edited from) run by one of two engines, this project's blocks or FleXray's own code; six render groups on the same 250 CTs, five pairs each isolating one thing, and the word "replicated" where the logistic C2ST AUC's lower bound is at most 0.6 and the MMD's q at least 0.05, the verdict being flexray_replica against flexray_reference in the flexray space.
- The segmenter is a block, `segmenter` (src/schwingnet/segmenters.py, the one home of the names, the two alphabets, the ids and the maps, imported by the render blocks and the training blocks): spineps, the default and the production runs', reads SPINEPS's existing masks in the VerSe alphabet from the data root's spineps and never runs SPINEPS; moose, only when replicating FleXray's own dataset, reads moosez's masks, which bench flexray_drr_seg makes under the data root's moose from the weights `fetch moose_weights` lands; TotalSegmentator is no value (the owner's words of 2026-10-26). A label volume of either scheme carries MOOSE stored ids, what FleXray's own code reads: L6 and the thirteenth ribs are in it and dropped by FleXray's remap, T13, the coccyx and the trachea left out and recorded as unsupervised.
- Images are decoded one way for every corpus (percentile window at 0.5 and 99.5, as the paper did, centre square of 0.9, 256), and the primary reading is inversion-invariant (the mean of an image's features and its inverse's), since fluoroscopy is often bone-dark and DRRs bone-bright.
- Separation is read against calibration: split-half nulls within a corpus and real-against-real baselines; a synthetic set is "closer than two real corpora" or "separable" by the pre-registered words, never by a map.
- Only summary.txt (at most 80 safe lines) leaves the grid; figures and map coordinates go to W&B where the key is found; results.json, the plan and render-subjects.json name files and stay on the grid.
- FluXray needs the fetch hf_flexray_data (the Realism thread's row, #406); without it the group is skipped with its reason and neither job waits for it. The moose_weights row rides the url route of grid/platform-fetch.sbatch (each zip's length by a one-byte range request before a byte lands), and the segment subcommand unpacks the zips into moosez's own models directory inside the job's throwaway environment with the model_version.json moosez reads, so moosez never downloads. Every label record says what is present, absent (the supervised names its files could have carried), dropped and unsupervised and the order the files were written in; the subject listing asks for every MOOSE model's file on the CT's own grid; and the study reads only the CTs every render group on a folder kept.
