Ask the book searches the textbook for the passages that answer a question, built as blocks chosen by name like the training blocks (src/schwingnet/rag.py, tools/rag.py, issue #250), and answers through the viewer's own Claude inside the claude.ai artifact, with no server and no key.

The owner asked on 2026-10-24 at 21:48 UTC: "A RAG trained on the textbook should help. Use a similar plug and play method package". The search has six parts, each a tuple of names in src/schwingnet/rag.py with version 1's choice first, chosen with `--block KEY=VALUE` as tools/train.py chooses its blocks, and `python3 tools/rag.py parts` lists them: chunk (section, each heading's section cut at about 300 words with a 40-word overlap; window, fixed 200-word windows; figure, the sections plus each figure's record as a passage), tokens (plain, lower-case letters and digits less a short stop list; stem, plus one light suffix rule), index (bm25 with k1 1.5 and b 0.75; tfidf, each passage scaled to length one), retrieve (top, the k best; spread, at most two passages a chapter), answer (cite, answer from the passages alone and cite each by its number, chapter and figure, saying when they do not answer; brief, the same in at most five sentences) and embed (none, the slot: a local embedding model would be a later name that runs in the train environment, never an API key, since the repository keeps none and the subscription token mints no embeddings). The package is pure logic under the gate; the tool reads the chapters and the figure records, and the scoring is the package's alone.

- `python3 tools/rag.py ask "the question" --k 8` builds the index in memory in under a second and prints each passage with its chapter, heading, file, line and site link; the research loop's brief tells it to ask the book first. The @claude assistant's run denies python3, so its brief says to Grep docs/textbook/ and Read by line range instead.
- `eval` scores docs/textbook/rag/questions.json (54 questions written from the chapters, each with the chapters that answer it and, for ten, a figure): recall at 5, MRR over the first ten and figure at 5, per block set with `--vary` or all 24 sets of the four search parts with `--grid`; `--write` records them, with the commit measured, in docs/textbook/rag/eval.json. Version 1 reached 0.877 and 0.764; the best sets and what each part changed are in docs/research/rag/README.md. `check` fails below the floor written in questions.json (recall at 5 0.8, MRR 0.7) and runs in scripts/check.sh.
- The index is never committed, since it would change with every chapter edit: tools/textbook_site.py builds it into the site as textbook/rag/index.json and chunks.json, beside rag/search.js, rag/box.js and rag/box.css, and `build --out DIR` writes it with a manifest (the blocks, the counts, the commit, the sha256 of every file read).
- The box on the front page and every chapter scores the question in the reader's browser with search.js, a JavaScript copy of the scoring that takes idf, the stop words and the suffix rules from the index itself; tests/fixtures/rag-scoring.json holds both to the same ranking and the same score to the last bit, checked by tests/schwingnet/test_rag.py and by node in tests/test_rag_tool.py. After a change to the scoring, `python3 tools/rag.py fixture` writes the fixture again. Sums of floats go through math.fsum, since sum() rounds differently from Python 3.12 on and the gate's pixi runs 3.11.
- On claude.ai, in the reader of #294, which hands the book's pages its own window.claude, `window.claude.use("sample")` resolves, and on the reader's click (never on load, since the first call asks the viewer's consent) Claude answers from the passages with the search as its tool search_textbook ({query, k}), streaming, its citations made links and a Sources list beside it; not_granted hides the answer part and keeps the search, rate_limited says to wait, and a null sample, as on the Pages site, leaves the search alone. Ask Claude in the box's row opens the issue with the question and the passages' files and lines. The box sits inside the row of class buttons, which the edition's own Ask box leaves out of the page text it sends.
