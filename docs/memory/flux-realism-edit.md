The Flux realism programme: FluXray is a zero-shot FLUX.2 klein-9B edit, reproduced on ENHANCE.PET CTs at FluXray's poses and measured against it before any adapter is trained.

No CSAIL-trained realism model exists: FluXray (VictorButoi/flexray-data, FluXray/, 138,063 images at 256 pixels, CC BY-NC 4.0) is flux.2-klein-9b run zero-shot (4 steps, bfloat16, guidance 1.0, one fixed five-sentence prompt, 90 fixed poses, SDD 1250 mm, 0.6 mm detector pixels, 1024 by 1024) over DRRs of the same MOOSE CTs, filtered by a paired soft-Dice rule whose raw-only U-Net is not released; the paper's appendix (arXiv 2609.26756v2, section appendix-dataengine-enhanced-drrs) is the one record, and schwingnet.realism.FLUXRAY holds it field by field with the deviations named (the flux000 member stands in for the filter's U-Net; the seeds are ours).

The programme is docs/research/flux-realism/README.md: tools/realism.py renders raw DRRs of ENHANCE.PET 1.6k CTs (the public bucket enhance-pet-1-6k, the CTs FleXray's MOOSE set came from) at the recorded poses with exact masks, edits them as the paper did, filters, exports FluXray's own images of the same subjects, and reads the gap in three feature spaces at FleXray's 256-pixel canvas (physics, FleXray's channel probabilities, ImageNet ResNet-18) with a grouped classifier test, an RBF MMD read at its Benjamini-Hochberg q over the spaces, Frechet and kernel distances on 64 components (the physics space's 57) and the second set's split-half null, the permutations by sample where the two sets show the same samples (DEVIATIONS.md), one verdict word per space (matched, indistinguishable, undecided, separable); the LoRA plan (schwingnet.realism.LoraPlan, klein-base-4b, Apache-2.0, rank 16, 2000 steps, ostris/ai-toolkit at 73ebae13) trains by subject split and is judged on held-out subjects.

Resolution first: at 1024 pixels a detector pixel spans 0.31 to 0.55 mm at the isocentre over the three source heights and one pixel of rise over a 45 mm lumbar endplate is 0.40 to 0.70 degrees; at FluXray's 256 pixels it is 1.25 to 2.21 mm and 1.6 to 2.9 degrees, so a model that learns from FluXray's images alone cannot measure an endplate angle to a degree; the edit stage's measure step reads the loss on the exact masks.
