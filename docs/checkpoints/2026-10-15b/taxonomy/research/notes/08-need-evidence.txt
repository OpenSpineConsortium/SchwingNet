## Takeaways

- The best quantitative evidence that agents sharing a codebase fail is CooperBench (Khatua, Zhu et al., Stanford and SAP Labs, arXiv 2601.13295, 2026-01-27, preprint): on 652 feature pairs from 12 repositories, two agents splitting a workload pass far less often than one agent doing both (GPT-5 315 vs 183 of 652, 48.3% vs 28.1%; Claude Sonnet 4.5 307 vs 168, 47.1% vs 25.8%; pooled 41% of solo capability lost). The paper states the gap three ways (30% in the abstract, "as large as 50%" in Sec 4, 41% in App C), so cite the counts, not a headline.
- What fails is mostly duplicated or incompatible work, not missing skill: of failed two-agent runs, 33.2% are work overlap (both agents build the same thing) and 29.7% divergent architecture; the root causes in 50 hand-read traces are expectation 42%, commitment 32%, communication 26%. Messaging eats up to 20% of steps and cuts merge conflicts but does not raise success. A plan in the first turn nearly halves the conflict rate (29.4% vs 51.5%), and successful runs name more lines and files. These are exactly the facts a claim with a plan and a "files to touch" list makes durable and checkable (Inferred).
- The "curse" is a property of isolation plus chat, not of parallelism as such. CooperBench's own later ablation (2026-05-19, codex with gpt-5.5, 50 pairs, one seed) puts messaging-only coop at 26%, solo 48%, a shared git remote 56%, and lead + shared task list + scratchpad 62%; removing the task list (-11) or the scratchpad (-16) drops the team below solo. Small 2026 preprints with explicit claims or schedulers report the same direction (Claim Plane static pre-write admission 23.3% to 50.0% pair pass; Shepherd supervisor 28.8% to 54.7%; NP-Bench planner 1/9 to 9/9 clean integrations in simulation). The need is a shared, visible substrate with claims and ownership; this is Symphony's design space (Inferred).
- In the field, agent pull requests already collide and are merged less often than human ones: AgenticFlict finds textual merge conflicts in 27.67% of 107,026 simulated agent PRs (authors cite 10-20% for human PRs); co-active agent PR pairs conflict 19.8% of the time within one agent and 41.7% across agents (747 replayed pairs), and 40.2% of AIDev-pop repositories have concurrent agent PRs. In AIDev-pop, feature PRs merge at 73.5% for humans vs 56.8% Claude Code, 54.4% Devin, 38.1% Copilot, 60.4% Cursor and 80.7% Codex (Codex's rate reflects self-merged personal work).
- Coordination is expensive in tokens: Anthropic measured single agents at about 4x and multi-agent systems at about 15x the tokens of chat, with token spend alone explaining 80% of BrowseComp variance; Claude Code's docs put agent teams at about 7x a standard session's tokens in plan mode; 16 Claude agents writing a C compiler took nearly 2,000 sessions, 2 billion input and 140 million output tokens and just under $20,000 over two weeks. Today's local coordbench pilot (3 pairs, anecdotal) shows every two-agent arm at about 2x solo's wall time and 3x its tokens.
- Practitioners and vendors converge on the same primitives: a claim or lock per task (Anthropic's lock files in current_tasks/ that git refuses to let two agents take; Claude Code's file-locked task claiming), file ownership ("Two teammates editing the same file leads to overwrites"), planner / worker / judge roles (Cursor), a single writer (Cognition, 2025 and again April 2026), and gates on review and tests (DORA 2025: AI raises throughput but lowers delivery stability; Faros: PR review time up 91%). Cursor's report that naive locks collapsed 20 agents to the throughput of 2-3 shows a lock needs expiry and release, which Symphony's 24-hour release provides (Inferred).
- Classic software engineering predicts all of this: Brooks's n(n-1)/2 communication paths (1975), Herbsleb and Mockus's finding that distributed work items take about 2.5 times as long as colocated ones (2003), and Cataldo et al.'s socio-technical congruence (2006: coordination that matches technical dependencies shortens resolution time), though a 2021 large-scale study only partly reproduced the congruence result.
- Evidence strength is uneven. Strong: MAST (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks, 1,600+ traces, kappa 0.88), METR's RCT (16 developers, 246 tasks, 19% slower), DORA 2025 (about 5,000 respondents), Stack Overflow 2025 (49,000+), AIDev (932,791 PRs), Herbsleb and Mockus (IEEE TSE). Medium: CooperBench (preprint, 652 pairs x 5 models), AgenticFlict, Veracode (vendor). Weak: engineering blogs (Anthropic, Cursor, Cognition), the CooperBench ablation (n=50, 1 seed), the 2026 claim/scheduler preprints, and the coordbench pilot (n=3). A 2026 noise-floor study finds 7 of 10 recent multi-agent coordination architectures report gains below a roughly 15-point run-to-run noise envelope.
- The gap Symphony could close as evidence: no peer-reviewed study yet measures a PR- and claim-gated protocol on a shared GitHub remote against a solo agent at a matched budget; coordbench's sdlc arm is built to do that and has only a 3-pair pilot (Inferred).

## Findings

### Q1. What fails when several agents share a codebase, and by how much

- CooperBench's setup: 652 evaluated feature pairs (199 features, 52 task sets) from 12 libraries in Python, TypeScript, Go and Rust; a feature averages 52.3 changed lines in 1.4 files; each agent works in its own VM on its own branch, capped at 100 actions, talks only through a messaging tool, and the two patches are merged once at the end with git merge-file; success = merged code passes both features' hidden tests. [Verified] CooperBench paper PDF (cooperbench/website 97dbb0a, public/static/pdfs/main.pdf) pp. 4 and 22.
- Solo vs Coop counts out of 652 (paper Table 5): gpt-5 315 vs 183; claude (Sonnet 4.5) 307 vs 168; minimax 236 vs 91; qwen coder 141 vs 87; qwen 41 vs 30; pooled 1,039 vs 558; AUC retention gpt-5 0.64, claude 0.60, minimax 0.46, pooled 0.59, i.e. "On average, 41% of Solo capability is lost". [Verified] paper PDF pp. 24-25.
- The headline is stated inconsistently: abstract "on average 30% lower success rates" (p. 1), Sec 4 "as large as 50% in the leading models" (p. 7), App C 41% (p. 24); the website's meta description says 30%, its key findings say "roughly 50% lower". The website leaderboard's coop scores (GPT-5 27.95%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 25.92%) differ slightly from Table 5 (28.1%, 25.8%). [Verified] paper PDF pp. 1, 7, 24; cooperbench/website src/pages/index.astro lines 7 and 356-358; src/pages/leaderboard.astro lines 403, 433.
- The coordination gap is largest at middle difficulty, where most real work sits; scaling from 2 to 3 to 4 agents on 46 tasks gave 68.6%, 46.5%, 30.0% (model not named, task set unpublished). [Verified] paper PDF pp. 7-8.
- Communication: agents spent up to 20% of steps messaging (Claude 20.0%, GPT-5 16.3%, MiniMax 13.6% of events); with-comm vs no-comm success difference not significant; communication significantly reduced naive merge conflicts. [Verified] paper PDF pp. 8-9 (Fig 5).
- What predicts fewer conflicts: a Plan message in the first turn (conflict rate 29.4% vs 51.5%; effect holds in 7 of 8 difficulty buckets, 39% reduction at the hardest); Plan:Question ratio 2.04 in conflict-free vs 1.31 in conflict trajectories; successful trajectories mention 32.6 vs 22.5 line numbers and 13.1 vs 10.0 file paths. The authors call conflicts "spatial" coordination (who edits which lines) and task success "semantic" coordination (what to implement), which messaging did not solve. [Verified] paper PDF p. 9.
- Failure symptoms over all failed Coop trajectories (GPT-5 as judge, Table 1): work overlap 33.2%, divergent architecture 29.7%, repetition 14.7%, unresponsiveness 8.7%, unverifiable claims 4.3%, broken commitment 3.7%, dependency access 1.7%, placeholder misuse 1.5%, parameter flow 1.3%, timing dependency 1.1%. [Verified] paper PDF p. 11.
- Root causes from 50 manually reviewed failed traces (Table 2): expectation 42% (one agent treats announced partner work as if it does not exist), commitment 32% (promises not kept, unverifiable integration contracts), communication 26%. The website blog says expectation "happens in 63% of failures", which conflicts with the paper's 42% and with the website's own index page (42/26/32). [Verified] paper PDF p. 12; cooperbench/website src/content/blog/curse-of-coordination.mdx line 127; src/pages/index.astro lines 389-398.
- Communication error rates by model (share of conversations): Claude repetition 37.1%, unresponsiveness 9.9%, hallucination 6.9%; MiniMax unresponsiveness 21.3%. [Verified] paper PDF p. 10 (Fig 6).
- Giving agents git (self-hosted server per pair, February 2026 update) moved cooperative success only 1-3 points and not significantly (Gemini 3 Flash, OpenHands SDK: 26.23% to 27.76% vs solo 48.60%; Mini-SWE: 12.27% to 15.18% vs 25.20%; Gemini 3 Pro: 20.40% to 21.78% vs 36.80%), and added failure modes: force pushes, botched merges, history rewrites, overwriting a partner's work, fabricated stories about what the partner did. [Verified] cooperbench/website src/content/blog/the-curious-case-of-miscoordination.mdx lines 43-140.
- CooperBench team-harness ablation (generated 2026-05-19, codex with gpt-5.5, "flash" subset of 50 pairs, Docker, 1 seed; eval: identical, else naive 3-way merge, else lead's patch alone): coop messaging only 13/50 (26%), solo 24/50 (48%), coop + shared git remote 28/50 (56%), team (lead/member, task list, scratchpad, MCP, auto-refresh, protocol) 31/50 (62%); one-feature-off: no task_list 20/50, no scratchpad 15/50, no protocol 35/50; authors: "Most multi-agent value = a shared code substrate, not the orchestration"; caveat "Single seed, n=50". [Verified] cooperbench/CooperBench 63b9d44 docs/team_harness_ablation_report.html lines 37, 56-66, 93-101, 119-122.
- Anthropic's 16-agent C compiler: while the work was one large task (building the Linux kernel), "Every agent would hit the same bug, fix that bug, and then overwrite each other's changes", and 16 agents did not help until GCC was used as an oracle to split the kernel into independent pieces; "Merge conflicts are frequent, but Claude is smart enough to figure that out." (Nicholas Carlini, 2026-02-05). [Verified] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler
- Anthropic's research system saw subagents "spawning 50 subagents for simple queries", "distracting each other with excessive updates", and duplicated work ("2 others duplicated work"); it says "most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research" and LLM agents "are not yet great at coordinating and delegating to other agents in real time" (2025-06-13). [Verified] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system
- MAST (Cemri, Pan, Yang et al., UC Berkeley; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks): 14 failure modes in 3 categories from 7 frameworks; the repository's taxonomy figure gives poor specification 37.17%, inter-agent misalignment 31.41%, task verification 31.41%, with the largest modes disobey task specification 15.2%, incorrect verification 13.61%, step repetition 11.5%, no or incomplete verification 9.16%, premature termination 8.64%, reasoning-action mismatch 7.59%, unaware of termination 6.54%, information withholding 6.02%, ignored other agent's input 4.71%. [Verified] MAST a70542e assets/taxonomy_v11_cropped.pdf (percentages mapped to labels by position; they sum to 100.0).
- MAST versions disagree on category shares: arXiv v2 figure reported as 44.2% / 32.3% / 23.5%, and a blog's 41.8% / 36.9% / 21.3%; dataset described as 150-200 traces for the taxonomy and 1,600+ (v3: 1,642) traces in MAD; Cohen's kappa 0.88. ChatDev reaches 33.33% on ProgramDev, and role and verification interventions give "+15.6% for ChatDev", which the authors call insufficient. [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657 (v2, v3), neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121528, sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/mast.
- AgenticFlict (Ogenrwot and Businge, UNLV; arXiv 2604.03551, ACM): 142,652 agent PRs identified across 59,412 repositories, 107,026 merge-simulated, 29,609 with textual conflicts = 27.67%, 336K+ conflict regions; textual conflicts only; authors state human PRs run "typically 10-20%" (no citation in the README). [Verified] AgenticFlict e050289 README.md lines 13, 51-59.
- Concurrent agent PRs (Xu, Subramanian, Karthik, arXiv 2607.04697, 2026-07-06, on AIDev-pop's 33,596 PRs): 40.2% of repositories had time-overlapping agent PR pairs covering 79.4% of agent PRs (one-week window: 53.4% and 95.0%); replaying 747 co-active pairs, textual conflicts in 41.7% of cross-agent vs 19.8% of same-agent pairs; cross-agent pairs are rare (0.5% of pairs, 122 repositories). [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/abs/2607.04697.
- AIDev (Li, Zhang, Hassan, Queen's University): 932,791 agent PRs (OpenAI Codex 814,522, Copilot 50,447, Cursor 32,941, Devin 29,744, Claude Code 5,137) in 116,211 repositories by 72,189 developers to 2025-08-01 (the July 2025 arXiv v1 reported 456K); curated AIDev-pop 33,596 PRs in 2,807 repos with more than 100 stars; MSR 2026 Mining Challenge dataset. [Verified] AI_Teammates_in_SE3 be60e52 README.md lines 33-45, 79-80; AIDev_preprint.pdf p. 1.
- AIDev-pop merge rate by PR type (replication notebook output): feat PRs merged Human 73.48% (n=1,946), OpenAI Codex 80.74% (n=10,019), Cursor 60.39% (616), Claude Code 56.80% (250), Devin 54.36% (1,904), Copilot 38.05% (1,661); fix PRs Human 82.40%, Codex 81.58%, Cursor 68.37%, Claude Code 57.39%, Devin 42.91%, Copilot 42.40%. [Verified] AI_Teammates_in_SE3 analysis/productivity.ipynb (stored outputs, "All AIDev-pop" cell).
- Why agent PRs are rejected (Peralta et al., MSR 2026 challenge, arXiv 2605.22534): 11,048 closed agent PRs, 717 inspected; of 353 rejected, 35.7% were agent failures, 31.2% not agent failures, 33.1% unknown; 15.4% of merged ones needed reviewer feedback or commits; Codex and Cursor PRs typically merged with minimal interaction. Ehsani et al. (arXiv 2601.15195, MSR 2026, 33k PRs): not-merged agent PRs are larger, touch more files and often fail CI. [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/pdf/2605.22534, arxiv.org/html/2601.15195v1.
- Semantic interference (Xia, Wu, Park, "Passes Alone, Fails Together", arXiv 2609.25396, EXPRESS 2026): only 1 of 834 runs on 417 mined Django PR pairs showed interference after a grading fix, but 97% of runs on constructed tasks around 12 Django helpers did; one message describing the concurrent change recovered 82%. [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/abs/2609.25396.
- Agent-written code quality: Veracode's 2025 GenAI Code Security Report (2025-07-30) tested 80 tasks on 100+ LLMs; about 45% of outputs had a security flaw, Java over 70% (72% in coverage), Python/C#/JavaScript 38-45%, XSS defenses failed 86%; no improvement with newer models. GitClear 2025 (211M changed lines, 2020-2024): duplicated 5+ line blocks up about 8x in 2024, moved (refactored) lines down 39.9%. [Reported] WebSearch: veracode.com press release, sdtimes.com, helpnetsecurity.com 2025-08-07; devclass.com 2025-02-20, leaddev.com.
- METR RCT (Feb-Jun 2025, arXiv 2507.09089): 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks on their own mature repositories, Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet; AI-allowed tasks took 19% longer while developers forecast 24% faster and afterwards believed about 20% faster. METR's 2026-02-24 update (57 developers, 143 repositories, 800+ tasks from August 2025): returning developers about 18% slower (CI -38% to +9%), new developers about 4% slower (CI -15% to +9%); 30-50% of developers withheld tasks they would not do without AI, so METR calls the evidence weak, believes speedups are now likely, and is redesigning the study. Secondary sources disagree on the sign. [Reported] WebSearch: metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study, andrewwegner.com/metr-ai-productivity-study-update.html.
- DORA 2025 "State of AI-assisted Software Development" (2025-09-23, nearly 5,000 professionals): 90% use AI at work; more than 80% believe it raised their productivity; 30% have little or no trust in AI-generated code; "AI adoption is positively related to software delivery throughput and product performance. It is negatively related to delivery stability"; strong automated testing, mature version control and fast feedback loops absorb the higher change volume. [Verified] https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report
- Stack Overflow 2025 (49,000+ responses): 84% use or plan to use AI; 46% distrust its accuracy (31% in 2024); 66% cite answers "almost right, but not quite"; agents: 14.1% daily, 9% weekly, 7.8% less often (about 31% using), 17.4% planning, 37.9% no plans; among agent users 87% worry about accuracy and 81% about security and privacy. Stack Overflow 2026 (released 2026-10-06, about 30,903 respondents): about 66% use coding assistants or agents, 26% automated agent workflows; of 12,255 answering, Claude Code 65.5% and Copilot 58.7% used in the past year; 48% trust AI when they can easily verify it, about 7% for important decisions. [Reported] WebSearch: survey.stackoverflow.co/2025, infoworld.com, devops.com; stackoverflow.blog 2026-10-06, visualstudiomagazine.com 2026-10-06, survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data.

### Q2. What coordination costs in tokens, time and money

- Anthropic research system: "agents typically use about 4x more tokens than chat interactions, and multi-agent systems use about 15x more tokens than chats"; token usage alone explained 80% of BrowseComp variance (three factors 95%); the multi-agent system beat single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research eval; parallelism cut research time by up to 90%. These are research tasks, not coding. [Verified] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system
- Claude Code docs: "Agent teams use approximately 7x more tokens than standard sessions when teammates run in plan mode"; "Token costs scale linearly"; start with 3-5 teammates; enterprise average about $13 per developer per active day, $150-250 per developer per month, under $30 per active day for 90% of users. [Verified] https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs and https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams (fetched 2026-10-15).
- C compiler with 16 parallel Claudes: nearly 2,000 Claude Code sessions over two weeks, 2 billion input and 140 million output tokens, "just under $20,000" in API costs, a 100,000-line Rust compiler that builds Linux 6.9 on x86, ARM and RISC-V. [Verified] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler
- CooperBench: communication consumes up to 20% of an agent's action budget with no success gain (Q1). In the ablation, median task time was solo 2m50s, coop 3m02s, coop+git 3m42s, team 3m56s; agents self-terminated after about 50-95 tool calls each; codex costs read $0 because its stream omits cost. [Verified] paper PDF p. 9; CooperBench docs/team_harness_ablation_report.html lines 56-66, 119.
- CooperBench core-subset team runs (10 pairs): claude_code (Sonnet 4.5) 5/10 for about $8.5, mini_swe_agent_v2 6/10 for $13.37, openhands_sdk 4/10 for $31.90, codex (gpt-5.5, 400k+ input tokens per agent); reaching those numbers took five reruns and four re-evaluations, each fixing a harness bug. [Verified] CooperBench docs/BENCHMARK_RESULTS.md lines 18-25, 50-80.
- coordbench curse-1 pilot (2026-10-15, claude-sonnet-5-5, Claude Code 2.1.300, pallets_click 2068, 3 complete pairs, one seed): per pair, solo 127 s wall, 1.26M tokens, $0.42; coop 261 s, 3.43M, $1.05; sdlc (Symphony protocol) 213 s, 3.49M, $1.05; coop-git 239 s, 3.71M, $1.11; team 211 s, 3.33M, $1.06 (list prices, estimated); "every two-agent arm took about twice solo's wall time and three times its tokens"; messages took 17.3% of coop's tool calls vs 8.4% for sdlc; the sdlc pairs had 6 claims, 6 PRs, 6 merges, 0 merge conflicts. No arm difference is significant at n=3. [Verified] /home/user/coordbench/bench/cooperbench/results/curse-1-pilot/README.md lines 94-126; report.md lines 21-25, 47-51.
- Faros AI "AI Productivity Paradox" (2025, telemetry from 10,000+ developers, 1,255 teams): high-adoption teams completed 21% more tasks and merged 98% more PRs, but PR review time rose 91%, PR size 154% and bugs per developer 9%, with no company-level change in throughput or lead time; vendor data, correlational. [Reported] WebSearch: faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering, blog.robbowley.net 2025-08-21.
- DORA "ROI of AI-assisted Software Development" (2026.01) models a J-curve with a productivity dip (its calculator assumes 15% for three months) from learning and a "verification tax". [Reported] WebSearch: infoq.com/news/2026/05/dora-roi-ai-assisted-dev-report, ingenire.com.
- Human baseline, Brooks (The Mythical Man-Month, 1975): "Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later"; n people have n(n-1)/2 communication paths (10 for 5 people, 45 for 10). [Reported] WebSearch: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mythical_Man-Month and secondary glossaries; book not opened.
- Human baseline, Herbsleb and Mockus ("An Empirical Study of Speed and Communication in Globally Distributed Software Development", IEEE TSE 2003; conference version ICSE 2001 with Finholt and Grinter): "distributed work items appear to take about two and one-half times as long to complete as similar items where all the work is colocated", mostly because more people are involved; replicated in a second organization. [Reported] WebSearch: st.cs.uni-saarland.de/edu/empirical-se/2006/PDFs/herbsleb03.pdf, cacm.acm.org/research/global-software-development.
- Human baseline, socio-technical congruence (Cataldo, Wagstrom, Herbsleb, Carley, CSCW 2006): higher fit between coordination requirements from technical dependencies and actual communication was associated with shorter modification-request resolution times; extended in Cataldo and Herbsleb, IEEE TSE 2013 (productivity and failures); a 2021 large-scale longitudinal study (arXiv 2105.08198) did not fully reproduce it and notes the originals covered about two projects. [Reported] WebSearch: patrick.wagstrom.net/publications/papers.bib, arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08198.
- Collaboration tax (Sun, Wang et al., arXiv 2608.22152, 2026): across 32 tasks and 11 models from 7 providers, two cooperating LLMs do worse than one alone, the tax shrinks with capability, and stems from a four-stage breakdown (ungrounded claims, not asking, not integrating, accepting without re-checking); a targeted prompt closes much of it. Nature Machine Intelligence 8:1157-1172 (2026, Kim, Gu et al.): over 260 configurations at fixed compute, the single-agent baseline best predicts whether coordination helps; a capability-saturation threshold predicted the sign in 94% of validation configurations on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench. [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22152; media.mit.edu and ubicomplab.cs.washington.edu publication pages.

### Q3. What practitioners say they need (locks, ownership, review gates, a board)

- Claims by lock file on a shared git remote: in Anthropic's C compiler harness each agent claims a task by writing a file to current_tasks/; if two claim the same task, git forces the second to pick another; agents pull, merge, push and remove the lock when done; each agent runs in Docker against a bare upstream repository. This is Symphony's "first push of claude/N wins" in miniature. [Verified] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler
- Claude Code agent teams ship a shared task list with dependencies, "Task claiming uses file locking to prevent race conditions", file ownership advice ("Two teammates editing the same file leads to overwrites. Break the work so each teammate owns a different set of files"), quality-gate hooks (TeammateIdle, TaskCreated, TaskCompleted, exit 2 to block), and the advice to start with research and review rather than parallel implementation; known limits include "Task status can lag" and one team per session, local to one machine. [Verified] https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams
- Cursor (Wilson Lin, "Scaling long-running autonomous coding", January 2026): equal-status agents coordinating through a shared file with locks stalled (agents held locks too long or forgot to release them; 20 agents ran at the effective throughput of 2-3); optimistic concurrency was simpler but agents became risk-averse; the working design separated planners (create tasks), workers (only execute) and a judge per cycle, running hundreds of agents for about a week on a browser of 1M+ lines; the follow-up reports about 1,000 commits an hour at peak. [Reported] WebSearch: cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents, simonwillison.net/2026/jan/19/scaling-long-running-autonomous-coding, zenml.io, addyo.substack.com.
- Cognition (Walden Yan, "Don't Build Multi-Agents", 2025-06-12): two principles, "Share context" and "Actions carry implicit decisions"; parallel subagents building a Flappy Bird clone make incompatible choices; default to a single-threaded linear agent. The April 2026 follow-up ("Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working") keeps the warning for parallel writers but endorses setups where extra agents add intelligence (a clean-context reviewer, a "smart friend") while writes stay single-threaded. [Reported] WebSearch: cognition.ai/blog/dont-build-multi-agents, cognition.ai/blog/multi-agents-working, yespress.io/walden-yan.
- CooperBench's successful runs show unprompted role division, resource division ("I will modify ONLY lines 68-84. I will NOT touch anything else.") and negotiation before coding; the authors still call scaffolding "a workaround, not a solution" and bet on native coordination ability, citing Cursor's planner/worker hierarchy as such scaffolding. [Verified] cooperbench/website src/content/blog/curse-of-coordination.mdx lines 160-215, 227.
- ParallelPilot (arXiv 2609.33113, 2026): a formative study of 14 developers who already run parallel AI coding sessions found most spend considerable effort deciding how to split work and isolate sessions to avoid conflicts; five supervisory practices: Planning, Isolating, Logging, Observing, Triaging. Practitioner reporting (Pragmatic Engineer) names review speed as the bottleneck when running parallel agents. [Reported] WebSearch: arxiv.org/html/2609.33113v1, newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com "programming by kicking off parallel AI agents".
- Review and stability gates: DORA 2025 ties AI's gains to strong automated testing, mature version control and fast feedback (Verified, Q1); Stack Overflow 2026 finds trust is conditional on easy verification (48%) (Reported, Q1); AIDev-based work finds reviewer engagement the strongest correlate of merging and force pushes associated with lower merge likelihood. [Reported] WebSearch: emergentmind.com/topics/aidev-dataset, arxiv.org/html/2602.19441v1.
- Explicit claims and schedulers in 2026 preprints: Claim Plane (Nikolaev, arXiv 2608.00947): on 30 CooperBench pairs x 3 seeds x 4 arms (360 runs), static pre-write admission raised pair pass from 23.3% to 50.0% and integration success from 65.6% to 96.7%; dynamic scope failed closed on undeclared scope in 46 of 90 runs (22.2% pass); relies on oracle-localized scope from gold patches. NP-Bench / Nerveplane planner (arXiv 2610.07261): declared disjoint scopes plus producer-to-consumer merge order raised clean integration from 1/9 to 9/9 simulated scenarios and cut conflicts 13 to 0; 0 of 5 live agents strayed from scope. Shepherd (arXiv 2605.10913, MIT, Northeastern/Stanford): a supervising meta-agent raised CooperBench pair pass from 28.8% to 54.7%. ## Claims worth verifying

- CooperBench's headline gap: which number to quote (30%, 41% or "about half") and the 63% vs 42% expectation-failure discrepancy; the paper's Table 5 coop counts (183, 168) against the leaderboard (27.95%, 25.92%).
- CooperBench's ablation result that a shared git remote (56%) and a task list plus scratchpad (62%) beat solo (48%): one seed, n=50, one model; a second seed and Claude would decide whether "coordination beats solo with a shared substrate" holds.
- MAST's category percentages by version: open the NeurIPS camera-ready and the arXiv v3 tables; the repo figure (37.17/31.41/31.41) may be superseded.
- AgenticFlict's claim that human PRs conflict "typically 10-20%": it carries no citation in the README; the comparison needs a measured human baseline under the same simulation.
- AIDev-pop merge rates: whether Codex's 80.7% feature merge rate reflects self-merges in the agent operator's own repositories (Peralta et al. say Codex and Cursor PRs merge with minimal interaction), so merge rate is not a quality measure.
- METR's 2026-02-24 update: the sign and CIs of the returning and new developer estimates, from METR's own post.
- Cursor's "20 agents slowed to the throughput of 2-3" under locks, from the primary post.
- Claim Plane's 23.3% to 50.0% and NP-Bench's 1/9 to 9/9, from the papers; whether the oracle-localized scope from gold patches inflates them.
- Stack Overflow 2026 agent shares (Claude Code 65.5%, Copilot 58.7% of 12,255) and the 48% / 7% trust figures, from Stack Overflow's own pages.
- Veracode's Java rate (72% vs "over 70%") and whether a 2026 edition exists.
- Anthropic's 15x multi-agent token multiple is for research, not coding; Claude Code's 7x is for agent teams in plan mode; neither is a measurement of PR-gated parallel work on GitHub.
- The coordbench pilot's "2x wall time, 3x tokens" for two-agent arms rests on 3 pairs with output tokens as a lower bound.

## Gaps

- No primary access to arXiv, METR, Cognition, Cursor, Veracode, Stack Overflow or GitHub's blog from this environment; those findings are Reported from search summaries and should be re-read at the source before publication.
- No peer-reviewed, matched-budget comparison of a claim- and PR-gated protocol on a shared GitHub remote against a solo agent; coordbench's full run (43 gradeable pairs listed, pilot stopped at 3) is the missing measurement.
- No field measurement of duplicated work between agents (two PRs for one issue) on GitHub; AgenticFlict and Xu et al. measure textual conflicts only, and "Passes Alone, Fails Together" finds semantic interference rare in mined, reviewed pairs.
- No study of coordination cost on subscription plans (shared five-hour windows across parallel sessions) as opposed to API dollars.
- No study of whether claims or locks lower agent PR conflict or rejection rates in real repositories.
- Classic sources (Brooks, Herbsleb and Mockus, Cataldo et al.) were not opened; their figures are from abstracts and secondary citations.
- DORA's 2026 State of the report, if any, was not found; only the 2026.01 ROI report and 2026 insight pieces.
- MAST's per-framework and per-mode figures in the final version, and its intervention numbers beyond ChatDev's +15.6%, were not read.
