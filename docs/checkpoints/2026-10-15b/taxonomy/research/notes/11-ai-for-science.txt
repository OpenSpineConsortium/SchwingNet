# Beethoven's neighbors: AI-scientist systems, reproducibility frameworks and HPC workflow tooling (as of 2026-10-15)

Scope: where Beethoven (the launcher: hpcworker + beethoven-extension; the engine: beethoven-qa, beethoven-agents, beethoven-research) sits among autonomous research systems, reporting checklists, workflow/lineage tools and HPC access tools, and how GitHub Actions (Symphony's home) relates to them. Method note: the shared WebSearch budget ran out after 12 searches in this session, so most facts below come from primary files opened directly (GitHub READMEs and LICENSE files via raw.githubusercontent.com, shallow git clones, PyPI and npm registry JSON, github/docs source, anthropic.com, code.claude.com, cloud.google.com); figures that rest only on a search summary are graded Reported. Writing note: Beethoven's own texts never use the word "provenance" (beethoven-research/CLAUDE.md, "Never use the p-word"); this note says lineage or origin record.

## Takeaways

- Every AI-scientist system examined runs on metered model API keys or a vendor's hosted platform (Sakana AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, Biomni, data-to-paper, Robin/Edison, Kosmos, AlphaEvolve on Google Cloud); none runs on a researcher's flat-rate subscription inside a university Slurm allocation. Beethoven's rule D1 ("no API key anywhere", the student's own flat-rate plan) plus Slurm/Apptainer is not matched by any neighbor found.
- Their verification is of four kinds: an LLM reviewer (Sakana), human expert grading after the fact (Kosmos, 79.4% of statements judged accurate), wet-lab follow-up (Robin, Google co-scientist), or a user-written evaluator program (AlphaEvolve, OpenEvolve). data-to-paper adds "data-chaining" (every number click-traced to the code line that made it). None enforces a domain reporting checklist (REFORMS, CLAIM, TRIPOD+AI, MI-CLAIM, DOME) as a gate.
- The reporting checklists are human artefacts: Word/PDF tables filled by authors and checked by reviewers after the fact (MI-CLAIM ships a .docx; CLAIM 2024 a .docx supplement). The gap between the two families is that nobody turns checklist items into machine-checked gates over an agent's run record. Beethoven's plan does exactly that for REFORMS (32 items: 20 code gates, 12 declared fields) and CLAIM, but only one module (the split-level leakage check, splits.py) is built.
- The documented failure modes of AI scientists are the very items REFORMS targets: data leakage, inappropriate benchmark selection, metric misuse and post-hoc selection bias (Luo, Kasirzadeh, Shah, NeurIPS 2025), 42% (5 of 12) experiments failing on coding errors and hallucinated numbers (Beel et al. 2025), and automated reviewers overrating generated papers by about 2.3 points (Agent Laboratory). Luo et al. find the failures are detectable from trace logs and code, not from the paper, which supports Beethoven's "keep every prompt, log, job script and commit" rule.
- Agents on HPC exist but as plumbing, not as verified-science engines: Anthropic's own guide (2026-03-23) runs Claude Code in tmux on a Slurm GPU node with a CHANGELOG and test-gated commits; Argonne/Globus Labs' Academy (IPDPS 2026, academy-py 1.0.0 on 2026-09-08) deploys stateful agents across federated research infrastructure; LLNL's converged-computing group ships hpc-mcp and fractale; small read-only Slurm MCP servers sit on npm. None of them carries a reporting checklist or symbolic proofs.
- metaflow-slurm, the orchestration Beethoven chose (D3), is thin and stale: version 0.0.4 on PyPI (2024-12-10, last commit 2026-03-25), it drives sbatch over SSH with a key file (asyncssh), needs a blob store for the code package, and does not run steps in containers; Metaflow core has no Slurm support of its own. Its credential model conflicts with Beethoven's "never touch a credential" rule unless the plain sbatch adapter Beethoven plans underneath replaces it.
- GitHub Actions cannot be the compute plane for this science: standard runners on public repositories are free and unlimited but are 4 CPU / 16 GB / 14 GB SSD, 6 hours per job, 20 concurrent jobs on Free, with no GPU; GPU runners (1x T4, 16 GB VRAM) are larger runners, always charged, even on public repositories. Actions fits as the control and verification plane (gates, checklists, board), with the HPC allocation as the compute plane. Beethoven already does this split: the gate runs on ubuntu-latest, and a grid lane on the cluster posts commit statuses back to GitHub because GitHub's runners cannot reach the grid.
- Beethoven's repositories are private and proprietary, so they do not get the free unlimited minutes (private repositories get 2,000 minutes a month on Free, 3,000 on Pro/Team, 50,000 on Enterprise Cloud); self-hosted runners are free. The open-core split used for Symphony (public runtime, free Actions) would apply to a public Beethoven gate library, not to the current private repos.
- Licences matter for Beethoven's medical domain: The AI Scientist Source Code License (v1.0, December 2025) forbids using it to "Diagnose a medical condition without human oversight" and to infer health aspects of a person, and requires prominent AI disclosure in any manuscript; Coscientist's code is Apache 2.0 with the Commons Clause (no commercial use); Biomni is Apache 2.0 but warns its bundled tools and databases may carry stricter licences.

### 2. The gap between AI-scientist systems and reproducibility frameworks

- AI scientists optimise for an artefact (a manuscript, a hypothesis, a better program); their checks are internal (LLM review, evaluator code) or post hoc (expert grading, wet lab). Reproducibility frameworks are constraints on the study's design and reporting (splits, leakage, uncertainty, data access), written for human authors and reviewers and filled after the work as tables [Inferred] from the READMEs and checklist sources above.
- The failures that matter are invisible in the paper: Luo et al. find leakage, benchmark cherry-picking, metric misuse and post-hoc selection in Agent Laboratory and AI Scientist v2, and that trace logs and code make them far easier to detect; they recommend venues require trace logs and code for the whole automated process [Reported] https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08713v2 , https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/126015 (search summary).
- So the missing layer is a gate that reads the agent's run record (not the paper) and fails the run when a checklist item is unmet, plus retention of the full record. No neighbor found implements it; data-to-paper's number-to-code chaining and AlphaEvolve's evaluators are the closest partial pieces [Inferred].
- Benchmarks confirm the capability gap is still wide: PaperBench (20 ICML 2024 papers) best score 26.0% +/- 0.3 (IterativeAgent o1-high, 36 h, 2025-04-02); its Code-Dev variant does not check that results replicate [Verified] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/preparedness/HEAD/project/paperbench/README.md lines 9-21, 288-294. MLE-bench any-medal reached 64.44% +/- 1.18 (Famou-Agent 2.0, Gemini-3-Pro-Preview, 2026-02-23) and 63.11% (AIBuildAI, Claude-Opus-4.6, 2026-03-06); new submissions paused 2026-04-24 pending a fairness process [Verified] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/mle-bench/HEAD/README.md lines 6-20. CORE-Bench (270 tasks from 90 papers in CS, social science, medicine; Python or R) moved to the HAL harness, which is no longer updated as its team turns to agent reliability [Verified] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/siegelz/core-bench/HEAD/README.md lines 12-16; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness/HEAD/README.md line 7. ScienceAgentBench: 102 tasks from 44 publications validated by 9 experts, calls for "rigorous assessment of agents on individual tasks ... before making bold claims on end-to-end automation" [Verified] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench/HEAD/README.md lines 34-36.

### 4. Documented failure modes of AI-scientist systems, with numbers

- Beel, Kan, Baumgart (arXiv 2502.14297, "Bold Claims, Mixed Results"): 5 of 12 proposed experiments (42%) failed on coding errors; runs that ran often gave logically flawed or misleading results; some papers had hallucinated numerical results; literature review weak (known ideas such as micro-batching for SGD called novel); median 5 citations, mostly outdated; placeholder text [Reported] https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297 , https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.14297v3 (search summary; n=12 is small).
- Luo, Kasirzadeh, Shah (arXiv 2509.08713, NeurIPS 2025): four pitfalls, inappropriate benchmark selection, data leakage, metric misuse, post-hoc selection bias, found in Agent Laboratory and AI Scientist v2; systems preferred models with better test performance despite worse training metrics; a paper-only audit did little better than chance [Reported] https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08713v2 (search summary; the "chance" figure is from a secondary summary).
- Agent Laboratory: automated reviewers scored generated papers about 2.3 points higher than human reviewers; the paper flags the agents' tendency to overrate their own work [Reported] https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-team-up-in-agent-laboratory-to-speed-scientific-research/ (search summary).
- Kosmos: 79.4% of statements accurate, so about 1 in 5 inaccurate; data-analysis statements about 85.5% versus synthesis claims about 58%; Edison says it "goes down rabbit holes" and chases statistically significant but irrelevant findings [Reported] https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02824v2 , https://rywalker.com/research/kosmos (search summary; the split is secondary).
- Sakana workshop experiment: 2 of 3 papers scored 3-4 averages and were rejected; the accepted one averaged 6.33 at a 70%-acceptance workshop [Verified scores; Reported acceptance and rate] sources in section 1.
- Code-execution risk: Sakana, Biomni and data-to-paper all warn that the LLM-written code they run can use dangerous packages, reach the network, spawn processes or touch credentials and sensitive data [Verified] READMEs cited in section 1. The Edison/Kosmos and Google systems run on the vendor's side, so the data goes to the vendor [Inferred].
