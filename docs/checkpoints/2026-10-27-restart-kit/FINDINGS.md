## 0. How to read it

- Every naming number in sections 1 to 5 is synthetic: columns drawn from Panjabi's tables by bordercall's generator (archive/bordercall/panjabi.py: rho 0.5, the correlation of one measure across the bodies of a column, a shared offset a patient, not a correlation between the measures of one body; a log size SD of 0.07; 1 mm of measurement error, transitional bodies as 0.5 blends of their neighbours). None comes from a patient or a real mask.
- bordercall paths are on its main at 7015802; everything older than 2026-10-27 05:32 UTC lives under archive/ (bb86584, moved unchanged).
- The seven measures a SPINEPS mask gives (SPINEPS_7, archive/bordercall/SPINEPS_MAPPING.md): upper and lower end-plate width (EPWu, EPWl), upper and lower end-plate depth (EPDu, EPDl), posterior body height (VBHp, h_post), canal width (SCW) and canal depth (SCD). An AP view holds h_post, EPWu, EPWl and SCW; a lateral view h_post, EPDu, EPDl and SCD. There are 22 levels, C3 to L5, since C2 has no upper end-plate row; T13 and L6 exist only as a phenotype's extra body.
- The phenotypes: typical, four_lumbar, six_lumbar, t13, lumbar_rib, no_rib_t12. Truth is the count from C2 (in t13 the inserted body is L1); the rib reading is reported beside it where it differs.
- Quoting rules the studies set (archive/docs/EXACT_IDENTIFICATION_RESULTS.md section 0, archive/CHECKPOINT_2026-10-26.md, archive/README.md): a guarantee holds within groups chosen by the measurements, never by the truth, so per-level, per-landmark and near-border rates are measurements; quote the worst case over the between-measure correlation r with its Imbens-Manski interval, never r = 0 alone; give per-phenotype rates, never a pooled number alone, and rank by the worst phenotype; a class under about twenty cases reads "no failures seen"; a six_lumbar or t13 rate from a typical fit is given twice, as recorded and with the unnameable body excluded.

## 1. The exact Bayes rule (SchwingCheck)

Records: archive/records/exact-2026-10-26/exact.json and supplement.json; text archive/docs/EXACT_IDENTIFICATION_RESULTS.md; summary bordercall README.md. The owner's asks of 2026-10-26 10:43 and 11:26 UTC: any formula, function or network over 1 to n consecutive bodies that names a vertebra unambiguously, then weaker statements testable nearly certainly ("below L1" = at or below L2; "below T4" = at or below T5).

Method. The rule enumerates the 23 - n places of an n-body field and scores each with the generator's own likelihood: per measure normal given size, a diagonal plus rank-one covariance (Sherman-Morrison, no inversion), the seven measures independent given size, size integrated out by 161-node Gauss-Hermite quadrature, a uniform prior over places. Nothing is fitted. Scored on 2,000 typical columns (seed 97) at every place and every width from 1 to 22, in both planes, AP, lateral and all 127 measure subsets; challengers fitted on 5,000 columns (seed 11). exact.json took 78 minutes on four cores.

- The field named as a whole, solution C, both planes, typical / worst phenotype (always t13) / typical within one: 1 body 37.0 / 32.7 / 70.2; 3 bodies 57.5 / 49.2 / 85.7; 5 bodies 70.2 / 60.5 / 92.0; 7 bodies 77.4 / 68.8 / 96.1; 9 bodies 83.5 / 72.6 / 98.7. One body from its own seven measures is right 37 percent, eight times chance; each added pair of bodies buys 20, then 13, then 7, then 6 points; once there are four bodies on either side, the field, not the target, names the body (hiding the target from a nine-body field costs 1.6 points).
- Neighbours alone, both planes, typical / worst / lowest within one: the body below only 39.3 / 34.5 / 71.7; the body above only 36.9 / 33.3 / 67.7; both flanking bodies 52.2 / 45.8 / 81.5 (within one 84.0 typical; Wilson 50.7 to 53.8); two on either side 67.1 / 57.7 / 90.0.
- No scalar formula of the strong kind: a monotone index exists in both planes but names only 28.6 percent; five to eight discriminant directions carry the information (direction 1 carries 0.80 of the separation). The published means decoded with nothing fitted come within 2.5 points of C at nine bodies in both planes (81.0 against 83.5). A nonlinear classifier adds nothing once the target is visible (within 1.2 points) and 3.5 to 4.6 points on the neighbour-only problem. C saturates by 1,000 fitted columns: more synthetic columns will not move the rates; a different generator would.
- The information is 3-D at every width: both planes beat AP by 5.9 to 6.8 points at one body, 9.0 to 9.7 at five and 8.1 to 8.7 at nine, and lateral by 11.7 to 15.6, with signs constant over r from 0 to 0.9 (9 to 29 SE). AP is the better single plane by four to eight points. The route to the third axis is specified, not built: read the out-of-plane measures from a preoperative CT by local registration over the visible bodies, used as a measurement device and never as a localiser; it needs registration error well under a millimetre (the depth steps between thoracic levels have a median of 1.2 and 1.3 mm), which is unmeasured.
- By level (flank, both planes): C7 is named best (0.81), T8 worst (0.26); T5 to T9 are the floor (0.26 to 0.39), where adjacent classes are 0.4 to 0.8 within-class SD apart; L1 is low in every regime (0.34) for no cause the record gives.

Scored against the rib reading t13 names 51.7 percent on flank while lumbar_rib and no_rib_t12 fall to 47.5 and 43.9: the model names shape, and which convention is right is not a shape question, so a namer must be told its convention. six_lumbar shows no shift once its unnameable last body is set aside. four_lumbar sits 2.3 to 5.4 points below typical for no mechanism found.

The handoff's 0.40 ms is the junction predictor on a Windows workstation three to four times slower. The clinical budget goes to segmentation and registration, not naming.

A fit on typical columns never says a name a typical column does not give the arm, so the last body of every six_lumbar and t13 column is wrong by construction (200 windows a cell). ## 5. The white paper, the decks, the panoramas and chapter 36

Sources: archive/docs/SCHWINGCHECK_LEVELCHECK.md (the ASCII copy at revision 189 of the owner's Claude Doc https://claude.ai/artifact/4VwMe9CKMX87s4jiEJBpRE, "SchwingCheck: an explainable Bayesian check on vertebral labels, three pathways to the operating room, and a randomized simulation study", Gregory Schwing, 26 October 2026); archive/records/whitepaper-2026-10-26/; archive/HANDOFF.md steps 10 and 11; SchwingNet #416 and its branch's docs/checkpoints/2026-10-27-cns-junction/. Every SchwingCheck number in the paper is synthetic. The paper states the method in full and is as sensitive as the NeurIPS deck.

- The owner's central sentence (2026-10-26 21:28 UTC): no function of the same measurements, formula or network, names fields correctly more often than this rule, because it is the Bayes rule for these fields and this prior over places, up to the 0.5 mm floor and the quadrature; its error is the floor for any network. Framed against the upstream labeller whose CT labels LevelCheck transfers, not against the registration.
- LevelCheck has no classifier: it registers a labelled preoperative CT to the radiograph and copies its labels, so its error is about the labeller's plus the registration's, and its published counts bound only the registration. SchwingCheck checks the labeller.
- Three pathways: LevelCheck as prior art only; SchwingCheck3D where a CT exists (check the CT's labels before surgery, carry them by a pose fitted to matched centroids, no DRR loop); SchwingCheck2D with no CT, the recommended default (AP and lateral SchwingNet panoramas, one FleXray mask a body, SchwingCheck names the run or checks FleXray's labels, a name shown only at 0.99).
- The limit, stated by the paper: with a correctly labelled CT and a registration that converges over four or more levels, LevelCheck's published accuracy exceeds SchwingCheck's forced-choice accuracy from up to nine bodies; SchwingCheck's case is checking the labels, calibrated refusal and working without a CT.
- Error likelihood (wrong on forced choice / off-by-one label missed at 0.01 / correct label flagged at 0.01): 52.7 / 87.6 / 0.65 percent from one body, 26.9 / 62.6 / 0.50 from three, 14.4 / 42.2 / 0.32 from five, 3.9 / 15.9 / 0.09 from nine. So nine bodies flag 84 percent of off-by-one labels while questioning one correct answer in 1,000; but only 71 to 78 percent for fields centred T6 to T10, against 99.3 at C7.
- A registration right 99 percent of the time that SchwingCheck contradicts at 0.01 is right two times in three (assuming errors land on an adjacent level, a share to be measured).
- Latency: a three-body field takes a median 13.6 ms (at most 17.4 over 50 calls) on the four-CPU container, excluding reading the measures off an image; LevelCheck takes 7 to 26 s (Lo 2015), a median 44 s (De Silva 2016), 17 to 72 s in the operating room (Manbachi 2018).
- Six provable statements: the Bayes floor; a name at threshold t is right at least t for any condition computed from the measurements; region and border claims inherit it; under a wrong table only a total-variation bound (99 percent kept only for delta at most 0.0013 from three bodies, while a tenth-of-a-spread shift in each measure gives delta 0.18); a real prior is one multiplication; answer when the posterior is at least 1 - c_e / c_w.
- One or two C-arm views with no CT: 96.1 percent of nine-body fields with both, 89.1 AP alone, 83.6 lateral alone; oblique views are not modelled and nothing yet reads the measures off radiographs.
- The panoramas, the owner's preliminary data (revision 189): at two bodies of overlap SchwingNet's panoramas were complete on 120 of 120 generated pans, counted exactly on 618 of 618 walks over 79 CT renders, 95 of 95 on 16 lumbar films and 110 of 110 on 24 films from C2; 99.66 percent of walks over all 801 renders. Pans were cut from renders and films (no parallax or motion) and scored with the annotation's boxes. Chapter 34's sets (SchwingNet docs/textbook/34-the-optimal-overlap.md): 120 of 120 only under the last-move rule; 6,234 of 6,255 walks on the 801 renders; nobody has swept a real column. Quote as a record, not a final value.
- Automatic labellers fail on transitional spines: TotalSegmentator has no L6 class and shifts every label below L2 one level caudal on lumbarized spines (an L6 label in 0 of 1,152 CTs; Schwing et al., accepted at NeurIPS 2026, confidential). VERIDAH's 0.82 percent of subjects not labelled perfectly (365 real CTs) sits above SchwingCheck's 0.31 percent forced-choice error from fifteen bodies, but they measure different things; comparability is a hypothesis.
- Patents: US 11,284,846 (Johns Hopkins and Siemens, granted 2022, listed to expire 2034) claims segment, line integrals, pose, DRR, gradient-information similarity, iterate, overlay; US 10,262,424 and US 10,368,956 the msLevelCheck and MR versions. The route around is a centroid point-to-projection pose with no DRR, similarity or overlay. A reading of claims, not an FTO opinion.
- The reader study (revision 189, specified only): 40 readers by 48 cases in four strata (standard with CT, narrow three-body field, transitional anatomy, no CT), each case randomised within reader and stratum to registration alone, SchwingCheck alone or both; truth the count from C2 on public de-identified full-spine CTs (VerSe first); primary contrast both minus registration, noninferiority within 5 points then superiority; power 0.98 noninferiority and 0.91 to 0.92 superiority at 40 by 48 under the owner's assumptions, calibrated critical values 2.31 and 2.05; exempt under 45 CFR 46.104(d)(3)(i)(A) if no key is held; pre-register on OSF. ## 8. The DRR comparison

Sources: SchwingNet docs/research/drr-domain-gap/README.md, docs/memory/drr-domain-gap.md; the owner's ask of 2026-10-25 (plot FleXray's DRRs beside every held X-ray corpus and test their separation) and of 22:20 UTC (render FleXray's recipe through this project's blocks and FleXray's own code, the replication test).
