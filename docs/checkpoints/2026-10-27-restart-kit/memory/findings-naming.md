Full numbers with their records: FINDINGS.md sections 1 to 5, beside this folder. Every number here is synthetic (columns drawn from Panjabi's tables); none comes from a patient or a real mask.

- Exact Bayes rule (bordercall README.md; archive/docs/EXACT_IDENTIFICATION_RESULTS.md; record archive/records/exact-2026-10-26/exact.json). Seven SPINEPS measures, 22 levels C3 to L5, uniform prior over the 23 - n places, nothing fitted. Names exactly 47.3 / 73.1 / 85.7 / 96.1 / 99.7 percent of fields from 1 / 3 / 5 / 9 / 15 bodies; AP alone 36.5 / 58.2 / 73.4 / 89.1 / 98.2, lateral alone 30.1 / 53.4 / 68.3 / 83.6 / 96.4. Names given at posterior 0.99 are right 99.70 to 99.98 percent from 2 to 15 bodies (17.1 percent of three-body fields answered, 71.8 of nine-body). No challenger beat it: at three bodies a network 71.6, a quadratic 72.2, the closed form 71.9 against 73.1; the linear classifier 58.2.
- Its weak point is the table, twelve spines. Re-estimated from twelve columns: exact 40.1 / 60.2 / 72.5 / 86.4 / 97.0 percent (1 / 3 / 5 / 9 / 15 bodies), names at 0.99 right only about 95 to 96 percent up to nine bodies; the honest rule (SDs widened by sqrt(13/12), 2 mm error assumed) is right 99.30 / 99.18 / 98.91 / 99.59 percent at 3 / 5 / 9 / 15 bodies but answers 5.5 percent of three-body fields. At 3 mm of measurement error no rule keeps its certificate.
- Region and border claims from one body: the side of C7/T1 is said for 56 percent of nearby bodies at 99.84 percent; T12/L1 for 7 percent at 97.9 percent. Anomalous columns, scored by the count from C2: T13 names at 0.99 right 96.2 / 91.2 / 90.6 percent from 3 / 5 / 9 bodies; six lumbar 92.9 from nine; four lumbar 99.86.
- Neighbour study (archive/docs/NEIGHBOUR_IDENTITY_RESULTS.md): the field named as a whole, both planes, typical columns, 37.0 / 57.5 / 70.2 / 77.4 / 83.5 percent exact from 1 / 3 / 5 / 7 / 9 bodies; from the two flanking bodies alone 52.2 exact, 84.0 within one. t13 is the worst phenotype in every cell, a confident one-level cranial shift below the inserted body that abstention does not find. Both planes beat AP by 6 to 10 points at every width and lateral by 12 to 16: the information is 3-D. No scalar formula names well; 0.018 ms a window. Open: the edge-names artefact (typical fits never say a longer column's last name).
- Level identifiability (archive/docs/LEVEL_IDENTIFIABILITY_RESULTS.md): Kunkel 2010 Fig 2A's premise fails; T12 is not the most isolated level. Best named C7 (distance 2.20), T1 and T2 (1.66); T12's one-body recall is the lowest of 22 (16 percent; 77 at nine bodies). Levers: field width (the largest for every level), both planes, the patient's size, Panjabi's pedicle rows (L5 0.87 to 2.23, L4 0.75 to 1.85), abstention.
- Junction predictor (CNS slides; archive/records/panjabi-2026-10-26/): three measures name C7/T1 85.2 percent exactly and 97.4 within one in every class; T12/L1 59.4 to 68.2 percent; a lumbosacral twin keeps the call in 498 and 496 of 500. Against the rib reading the transitional body scores 5.6 to 20.8 percent: shape cannot choose the convention.
- Panoramas, the owner's preliminary data (white paper revision 189): at two bodies of overlap 120 of 120 pans complete, 618 of 618 walks on 79 renders, 95 of 95 on 16 lumbar films, 110 of 110 on 24 films from C2. Quote as a record, not a final value, with chapter 34's sets.
