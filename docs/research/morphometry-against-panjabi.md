# The engine's body and canal chords against Panjabi, and what was wrong with them

Source: the owner's local Claude Code session of 31 October 2026, on his words that evening: plot Panjabi's values beside Benzel and the gold tiers, look for errors in the measuring code behind the gaps, fix them by a published method, and "i want all the spine within 1 mm of panjabi"; then, of the canal, "why am I not doing a more robust approach like the radiology patent on canal finding?". Aggregates only; the rows stay on the grid. Panjabi's means and standard errors are bordercall schwingcheck/data/panjabi_long.csv.

## How it was checked

A calibration harness ran the engine (bordercall at 3c43361, the code that measured the gold tier rows) on 89 real studies, 48 ENHANCE.PET, 21 VerSe and 20 TotalSegmentator, and read each candidate definition beside the engine's own on the same vertebrae; renders of the body's slabs and of the CT in each vertebra's frame showed what each reading takes in. Then the chosen readings ran over every full spine, one CT a person (grid job 40906993). The harness, its aggregator and the profile are in spine-program CNS/not_all_spines/generator/ (grid/ and figs_v17_6min/).

## What was wrong

- It was not the labels. ENHANCE.PET's, VerSe's (hand-corrected), TotalSegmentator's and gold tier 4's masks gave the same numbers within about a millimetre, and so did slices of up to 1.25, up to 2.5 and over 2.5 mm (profile job 40906866).
- The end-plate width took in whatever the label fuses to the plate's edge. In the neck the uncinate processes rise from the upper plate's posterolateral edge and the costal roots of the transverse processes join the body's side; in the thorax the pedicles' bases and the rib heads' facets stand at the posterolateral corners; at L5 the transverse processes' roots do. scanimport's end-plate slab took the extent of all of it, and read EPWu 4 to 6 mm over Panjabi at C3 to C6 and at T10 to L3, 11 at L5 and 14 at T1, while its depth read within about a millimetre.
- The neck's body was not a body. anatomy.clip_to_foramina found the foramen transversarium closed in 1 to 11 per cent of cervical vertebrae; otherwise it cut the body at the canal's side walls and a millimetre beyond, so the body's width was the canal's plus two millimetres, and where it found the foramina its cut took in the costal roots (27 to 35 mm).
- The canal was read on the label's hole. The ring closes in one to three slabs in the neck and at L5, so the pedicles were found on both sides in about 1 per cent of neck vertebrae and the interpedicular distances fell back to the hole's width through its middle; and the label's boundary narrows the hole. The neck's canal read 19 to 21 mm wide where Panjabi's SCW is 23 to 26.

## The fixes (bordercall morphometry/plates.py and morphometry/ct_canal.py)

- Each end plate on its own layer and its front half (plate_w, plate_d and their lower twins): the slab reaches no more than half a voxel layer past the plate's plane, and the width is the widest row over the front half of the depth, each row the run of cells through its middle. Every appendage above stands on the back half or above the plate.
- The canal on the CT (canal_w_ct, canal_ap_ct), as the published canal finders do it (Radnostics US 8,891,848; Yao 2006): the canal's soft tissue flood-filled from a seed 5 mm behind the body below 200 HU, the dense-bone floor of the published heuristic pipelines, in planes 0.5 mm apart through the body, kept only where bone encloses it; the width the widest row through the seed's column (Panjabi's SCW), the depth the midline run (SCD), each the median over the enclosed planes. It needs no ring in the label, and the label's boundary does not enter.

SPINEPS is trained on MRI; on CT its corpus is not the bone's outline, so segmenting again with it would not give the bodies.

## The body's edges on the CT, and who the population is

- The end plate's edges moved to the half-maximum between the bone's peak and the tissue outside it on the CT (the relative 50 per cent edge rule), row by row over the plate's front half, did not bring the widths closer (89 studies, job 40909784): the neck's upper plate fell from +4 to +6 mm to +2.5 to +4, while T7 to T12 rose to +3 to +5; the depths moved by under a millimetre. Dropping the cells that rise past the plate (the uncinate processes) changed the neck by 0.3 mm. Neither is in the engine.
- The upper plate's width and depth split by age and sex over the 1,008 autoPET and TotalSegmentator adults the covariates join dates and sexes (job 40909969): people under 50 sit 0.1 to 3.4 mm under Panjabi from T4 to L5, people of 65 and over 1.3 to 5.6 mm over; women 0.3 to 3.1 under (T10 +1.1), men 2.1 to 6.5 over. The thoracolumbar gap that remains is who the population is, older and more often male than a dozen cadavers, not the reading; matching the population to Panjabi's own age and sex mix, which his papers give, is the published way to set them side by side (direct standardisation). Done the same night (job 40912660; spine-program CNS/not_all_spines/generator/grid/standardise.py): each of Panjabi's twelve specimens (bordercall schwingcheck/data/panjabi_specimens.csv: 8 men, 4 women, 19 to 59 years) matched to our adults of its sex within 5 years of its age, 18 to 156 a specimen, the standardised mean the mean of the twelve. Within 1 mm of Panjabi after it: the CT canal's width at 14 of 17 levels, the upper plate's depth at 11 of 16, the lower plate's width at 10 of 17 (still 1.7 to 3.1 over at T2, T7, T10 and L1 to L4), the neck's canal depth at none (1.6 to 3.9 under). Matching on height as well or instead did no better. The CNS slide v19 (spine-program #255) draws these means.
- Other populations, the same night (job 40915304; spine-program CNS/not_all_spines/generator/grid/reweight.py, the CNS slide v20, #256):
  - Panjabi's 8 men and 4 women, each matched apart. Our men read 6.4 mm wider than our women at L3's lower plate (52.6 against 46.2 mm), 4.8 mm deeper at the upper plate, and 1.8 mm wider in the CT canal.
  - Tan 2004's ten Chinese Singaporean cadavers (56 to 77 years, 159 to 172 cm, 50 to 70 kg, sex not reported). The 73 autoPET adults inside all three ranges (16 men) read the lumbar plates 3 to 4 mm over Tan's means.
    - Tan's own canal is 20 per cent narrower and 32 per cent shallower than Panjabi's, so the canal comparison says more about Tan's measurement than about the population.
    - The same 73 adults read the CT canal's lumbar depth 2 to 3.5 mm deeper than the Panjabi-matched mean (20.7 to 21.9 mm against 18.1 to 19.5). Older, osteoporotic bone near the 200 HU floor may let the flood fill escape, so check this before quoting any number from it.
  - Alonge 2021's 120 Nigerian adults on CT (54 men, 66 women, 18 to 65 years, mean 40.2), reweighted by sex and age. The lumbar canal depth reads about 2 mm deeper than Alonge's APD. Alonge's width is an interpedicular distance at the widest pedicle cut, so it is not Panjabi's SCW.
  - African American spines: none of the 39 papers measures these chords. African American spines appear only in Yu 2015 (Hamann-Todd skeletons, pedicles) and Shin 2024 (disc heights).
- Every decade of each sex, unweighted, 20s to 80s (job 40928273; spine-program CNS/not_all_spines/generator/grid/bands.py, the CNS slide v21): 17 to 148 adults a line. At L3 the lower plate widens from 48.0 to 54.3 mm in men and 43.5 to 50.2 mm in women, and the upper plate deepens from 33.1 to 38.5 and 30.4 to 35.4 mm. The CT canal's depth reads 16.4 to 23.5 mm in men and 16.2 to 21.1 mm in women over the same decades. That runs opposite to what age does to a canal, so the flood fill leaks in older bone, as the Tan-matched check above suspected. The canal depth is not to be quoted by age until the reading is fixed, and the slide leaves it out of the decades.
- The CNS closing film (spine-program CNS/HANDOFF.md, v21): a prone lateral pan from the sacrum over one ENHANCE.PET CT (0036), made real by FLUX.2 klein-9B, with the reported level prototype on every frame. Frames were registered to one another by phase correlation and the boxes linked into one column. Counting up from the L5 the model named, then taking the model's own per-frame names above the shoulders, named 17 bodies, all as the CT labels them (L5 to T5, C2 to C5). The detector drew nothing from T4 to C6, under the shoulders. On an earlier CT with a hip replacement (0006), the same linking named 20 bodies, all right. In v22 (1 November) the frames were made again from the same CT with the shoulders taken out of the way: the shoulder girdle and arm bones set to soft tissue, a slab about the spine from T6 up cut after the body is found (170 mm at T6, 110 at T3, 80 from T1 up), and the bodies from C5 to T12 weighted up as a swimmer's view lifts them. The count then ran unbroken from L5 to C3, 22 bodies, all as the CT labels them; the detector boxed no C2. Two earlier settings show how fragile the junction is: lifting only C7 to T5 counted to T2 (19 named, all right, T1 to C5 unseen), and narrowing the shoulders without lifting the mid thoracic broke the count at T10, where the heart and the diaphragm's dome lie over the bodies.
- The CNS slide (spine-program #254, v18) draws the width at the lower end plate from C3, beside Panjabi's EPWl, since the upper plate's width takes in the uncinate processes in the neck, and the depth at the upper plate, beside his EPDu, since the lower plate's depth takes in the anterior lip; means alone.

## What 1 mm against Panjabi can and cannot mean

- Panjabi's means come from 12 cadavers. Their 95 per cent margins (1.96 SEM) are 0.7 to 2.6 mm on these chords, so a population mean within 1 mm of his is a finer bar than his own means hold.
- The published series disagree with him by more than 1 mm. On the bounds card (bordercall research_notes/synthetic-validation/records/bounds/bounds.csv) a dry-bone series puts the neck's canal at 19 to 21 mm wide and 10 to 11 deep, and the living CT series put the lumbar canal 15 to 17 mm deep where Panjabi has 17.5 to 19.7, and the lumbar end plate 28 to 31 deep where he has 34 to 35.
- What is left, and why: the neck's canal depth reads 2 to 4 mm under Panjabi on the CT as on the label, at every source; CT series of the neck read it 13 to 15 mm, so this is his cadavers against a CT population, not the code. The upper plate's width in the neck still takes in the uncinate processes, which stand on the plate's own layer in a whole-vertebra label; separating them needs a label of the body (SPINEPS's subregions, which the census holds for gold tier 4) or an edge model on the CT. T10 to T12 still read 2 to 4 mm wide on both plates, where the rib heads articulate on the body itself.
- So the slide draws what is measured by the definitions above and says where it is not yet; no number is calibrated to Panjabi.
