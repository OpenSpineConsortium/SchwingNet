# The FleXray audit beside the record: the instance question, the required test's two readings, the settings to compare and what the owner is asked

The thread "Plan the FleXray audit for #391", 2026-10-25. Label: confidential-method, as chapters 10 and 34 are: this note names the counting line and its numbers, stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere. Its public half, the threads for #391 with their dependencies and counts and what FleXray is from its code, is symphony-template's docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md; this note holds what that one may not: the record's numbers, the method the required test measures, the settings it compares, and the questions #391 marks to confirm, in the record's own vocabulary so the owner can answer them in a line each.

## 1. What FleXray did on the held frames, and what that shows

The public benchmark's one finished job (Slurm 40781570, commit fc027a6, docs/checkpoints/2026-10-25-public-benchmark/HANDOFF.md) ran FleXray zero-shot on C-ArmLSpine's 617 lateral and 631 AP frames with 1,627 truth boxes. Under the quarter-coverage rule every box counted as detected, and over detected bodies the flagship member named the level right on 99.14 percent (1,613 of 1,627; Wilson 98.56 to 99.49), the ensemble on 99.63 percent (1,621 of 1,627; 99.20 to 99.83), and the weakest member, flux000, on 98.22 percent. The reported stack names a level from one frame on 96.4 percent of lateral frames and 43.7 percent of AP frames (docs/textbook/10, from STATUS.md). Three things the number does not show, each a step of the audit:

- The split by view is unread. The job's JSON on the grid, flexray-carmlspine.json, holds the lateral and AP rows; the job log on symphony-template's branch grid keeps only the overall rows. If FleXray holds near 99 percent on the AP frames, where the reported stack has 43.7, that alone answers part of item 10. It is read with the stress test (item 9), no new job. The approved DeepFluoro rerun scores the lumbar spine as the union of FleXray's five lumbar channels in the benchmark's own scorer, which it must: the fork's evaluator never computes that union and its training remap sends DeepFluoro's stored lumbar_spine label to background, so the paper's 0.916 is not a number the fork reproduces by itself.
- The scoring gave FleXray the boxes. Each truth box took the level whose mask covered most of it, so the number is level naming given a body, the second stage of the reported stack's task, not detection; whether FleXray misses bodies or draws extra ones on these frames was not scored. The stress test scores FleXray's own masks against the boxes both ways.
- FleXray cannot name a transitional body. Its label space has one channel per vertebra from C1 to L5 and the sacrum and none for a sixth lumbar or a thirteenth thoracic body; the CT sets that label vertebra_l6 drop it to background before training, so those bodies were learnt as not a vertebra. C-ArmLSpine's transitional cases (case42, case121, case136, chapter 14) are in the 1,627 boxes or not; the per-case rows on the grid say which, and item 9 scores them apart. A model that names by class is a model that answers wrong, with confidence, on the spines the counting prior exists for.

So the owner's clarification has a two-part answer. FleXray names individual vertebral levels, by class and from one frame, and on normal lumbar anatomy it did so on the held frames about as well as the reported stack's own count does with the annotation's boxes (99.17 to 100 percent, chapter 10). It does not segment instances, order them or know the variants. That is the gap the record's counting line fills and the one item 11 writes the avenues for.

## 2. Why they could and the record could not, as a question to measure, not yet an answer

Item 10 writes the comparison; this section sets its frame from what is read so far, each line to be graded in that thread.

- Supervision. FleXray's per-vertebra labels are all projected from CT: MOOSE 3.0 and five smaller CT sets rendered on the fly (58.5 percent of batches) and FluXray, 138,063 edited renders of 1,597 MOOSE CTs at 90 poses each (37.5 percent); real radiographs of hands, feet, forearms and humeri are the remaining 4 percent, one percent each, and none of them shows a spine. The record's detector was trained on real films: the film-trained detector found 97.6 to 99.2 percent of bodies on real frames (the grant's 99.14 is unsettled), the render-trained one 71.67 percent, and counts with the render-trained one's boxes were exact on 30.06 percent of 79 held-out spines (chapter 10). FleXray reached 99 percent level naming on real fluoroscopy with no real spine frame in training and real film of any kind in one batch in twenty-five. So the difference is not real data in the batch. The candidates are the generative realism edit (FLUX.2) on three eighths of its training, which the project's cancelled FluoroGen arm (SDXL, chapter 13) set out to make and lost the vertebrae to; the scale, 1,597 CTs at 90 poses plus six CT sets rendered fresh every step for a million iterations; the per-label attenuation randomisation; and the dense per-pixel supervision of a segmenter against the record's boxes. Item 10 grades each.
- Rendering. nanodrr ray casting over a full orbit (alpha in plus or minus 180 degrees, tilt in plus or minus 30) at a random labelled isocenter, framing 128 to 451 mm of anatomy, a lognormal attenuation factor per label half the time that mostly makes bone denser, the image the line integral of attenuation with its polarity flipped at random, and no noise model, scatter or intensifier physics. The record's calibrated renderer and the strong randomisation block (chapter 13, docs/research/strong-domain-randomisation.md) cover the same physics; what the record measured about renders alone against real frames is set beside FleXray's result in item 10 with the grades chapter 13 gives.
- Architecture and schedule. One UNet at 256 pixels, 61 sigmoid channels, routed losses per dataset, 2,000 epochs of 500 iterations, EMA, an ensemble of five. The reported stack is a 1,024-pixel detector plus a 96-pixel localiser with a 27-level posterior over two to nine bodies. FleXray spends its capacity on one dense answer at low resolution; the record spends it on boxes at high resolution and a small sequence model.
- Field and scaling. FleXray's training frames 128 to 451 mm of anatomy at the isocenter, 0.5 to 1.76 mm a pixel at 256 pixels, so a nine-inch intensifier's field of about 14 cm sits at the small end of what it trained on, which fits its 99 percent on C-ArmLSpine's frames; the stress test scores it by field width. Its reader scales a 16-bit file by the dtype's full range where its training scaled each image by its own, so a 12-bit fluoroscopy frame stored in 16 bits arrives dim; the stress test scores each frame both ways.

## 3. The required test, flexray+optimal_window_width_count_from_anchor: two readings

The brief names the test with a window width and item 2 with an offset. The record uses "window" in three senses, and the owner is asked which the test means. The plan assumes reading A.

Reading A, the sweep's geometry (chapters 10 and 34). The window is the exposure field: on a nine-inch intensifier about 14 cm at the spine, four lumbar bodies in view; a 12-inch field and a collimated 100 mm field are the other widths the record measured. The offset is the commanded step between exposures, one field minus the overlap. The optimal setting is chapter 34's: two bodies of overlap, a move of about 7 cm on a nine-inch field, where chain completion peaked in all three recorded sets (88.3, 96.7, 97.5 and 82.5 percent at half, one, two and four bodies on the canonical 18 September page). Under this reading the test runs the counting line with FleXray's masks in place of the detector's boxes and the localiser's names: cut each panorama into overlapping windows of the field width at the step the setting commands, run FleXray on each window, link the windows from the anchor through the restricted correlation (the band of plus or minus 0.6 of a body about the commanded step, chapter 10), count, and score each body's count against truth and FleXray's own level name against truth. Version 1 has no such linker: src/schwingnet/chain.py counts within one frame and the sweep step joins boxes by overlap, so the linker the evidence used lives only in spine-program's simulator, sweep_map.py (chapter 34). Item 13 ports it into SchwingNet first, by pull request with spine-program attached, which needs no grid job.

Reading B, the intensity window. tools/train_blocks.py carries FluoroSAM's random windowing, a viewer's window a WINDOW_WIDTH share of the range wide (0.2 to 1.0), as an augmentation block. Under this reading the optimal window width is the display window at which FleXray's masks are best on the held frames, a preprocessing knob of FleXray inference, and the non-optimal ones are narrower or wider windows. The record never calls a display window optimal, and the brief pairs the phrase with counting from the anchor and with offsets, so this reading is thought unlikely; it is cheap to add as a one-axis sweep inside the same job if the owner wants both.

Reading C, the linking search window. The band of plus or minus 0.6 of a body about the commanded step is also called a window (chapters 10 and 34), and the counting evidence varied its half-width: at 1.2 and 2.1 bodies counting stayed at 98.9 to 99.6 percent on the 801 renders, with 21 wrong walks against 52 and 67, so chapter 34 finds that it is not what makes two bodies work. Under this reading the optimal window is 0.6 of a body and the non-optimal ones wider. The record calls only the overlap optimal, so reading C is less likely than A; it costs nothing extra in the scoring job, which can link each setting at more than one band.

## 4. The settings to compare, under reading A

The record already says which non-optimal offsets are informative, so the strategic choice of i to k is proposed from it, for the owner to confirm or change:

| Setting | Overlap, bodies | Why it is chosen | What the record says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| optimal | 2 | chapter 34's finding | chain complete 97.5 percent, 3.0 exposures, two-vertebra naming 98.3 percent |
| nonoptimal_i | 1 | the one-body alias: one shared body can lock a frame a whole level off | 96.7 percent, 2.6 exposures, 97.5 percent |
| nonoptimal_j | 0.5 | a body falls between frames when the overlap is under the 0.6-body in-frame rule | 88.3 percent, 2.5 exposures, 92.1 percent |
| nonoptimal_k | 4 | long blocks of shared bodies and more than twice the exposures | 82.5 percent, 6.9 exposures, 89.8 percent |
| optional | 3 | the C2 arms used three; 834 of 834 exact from C2 at three, 832 at two | counting exactness, not completion |

Field widths, if the owner wants them as a second axis: collimated 100 mm, nine inch and twelve inch, which at one body of overlap gave 3.8, 2.6 and 2.0 exposures and naming at 97.1, 97.5 and 97.1 percent (the superseded summary; chapter 34).

The jobs: one GPU job runs FleXray (the flagship and the ensemble) on every window of every setting of every panorama, since inference at 256 pixels is cheap and one job keeps the settings comparable; one CPU job links, counts and scores, each figure with its Wilson interval, as tools/bench_public.py does. The owner's count is asked as 1 GPU and 1 CPU; if the owner would rather count one GPU job per setting, it is 1 plus the number of settings (five with the optional row).

Scoring, as chapter 10 scores: a walk is exact when the mark lands on the body truly k from the anchor, among walks that reached the target; chain completion per spine, every body once and none missed; and a third column the record has never had, FleXray's own level name per body, so that the test shows what counting adds to a model that already names.

## 5. The panoramas the test runs on, and what is not held

The record is explicit: no real stepped sweep is held (chapter 34). The talk's recorded sweep, C-ArmLSpine case134, and case39 are cone-beam projection sweeps of 301 frames turning from +122 to -52 degrees, a rotation about the patient, so they test FleXray on video (item 9) and not an overlap along the spine. The counting evidence was scored on generated pans from CT renders by spine-program's simulator sweep_map.py (CTSpinoPelvic1K, 120 held-out spines; VerSe, 76 and 12 spines) and on real radiographs within one frame. So the test's panoramas are:

- Rendered whole spines, the same route as the evidence: the simulator's frames from VerSe and CTSpinoPelvic1K renders, or whole-spine renders by the project's calibrated renderer, or, once item 7 has it, by fxr-render from the same CTs, which puts FleXray on renders of the kind it trained on and on the project's.
- Real whole-spine films: AASCE's 609 AP films, labelled by corners from T1 to L5 with C2 out of frame, so they test a count from the sacrum only; the 236 standing lateral films of IEEE DataPort (data/sources/ieee_dataport_lateral_236.json), the one held real film with C2 and the sacrum both in view, which carries no level truth; BUU-LSPINE's 3,600 AP and 3,603 lateral lumbar films reaching the sacrum, a short panorama; and or_ready/wholespine's 12,950 frames, built from other sets, not acquired (docs/research/held-fluoroscopy-by-region.md). Which of them hold truth boxes, and where their files are, is open item 8 of docs/checkpoints/2026-10-25-dataset-accounting/HANDOFF.md, the one CPU job the plan sends first. The rendered sources are VerSe (374 series of 355 subjects, the whole column), TotalSegmentator's 142 whole-spine subjects and CTSpinoPelvic1K's 802 CTs from the sacrum to about T10.
- The transitional spines among them, scored apart: chapter 14's three C-ArmLSpine cases (case42, case121, case136; zero verified real-frame transitional test persons against 35 needed), CTSpinoPelvic1K's 194 transitional persons by the demo plan's count, chapter 34's arm C for anomalous sweeps (18 L6, 9 four-lumbar, 6 VerSe T13), and the rare-class renders of item 8 once they exist.

A full spine of 700 to 900 mm padded to a square and resized to 256 pixels gives about 3 to 3.5 mm a pixel, a lumbar body ten pixels tall; a nine-inch window of 140 mm gives about 0.55 mm a pixel, four bodies filling the frame. FleXray's training framed anatomy at 0.5 to 1.76 mm a pixel, so the whole panorama is about twice as coarse as its coarsest, while a nine-inch window sits at its finest and a 12-inch field inside its range. Its network is fully convolutional and its public preprocessing class takes another canvas size, so a window can also be run at its own pixels rather than resized to 256. The stress test scores whole, windowed at 256 and windowed at native size, so the test's windowing is not confounded with FleXray's scale.

## 6. The rare-class route and the licence line

fxr-render takes a packed CT set with a protocol that names the labels, needs no weights and is MIT. The held CTs with labelled anomalies are CTSpinoPelvic1K's (18 L6 by release label, 9 four-lumbar by the readers, 33 Castellvi-graded; about 4 percent transitional in the owner's audit) and VerSe's (masks C1 to L6 with T13 and a two-reader variant table; 6 T13, the L6 count open), with CTSpine1K's 24 lumbarisations and 13 sacralisations (chapters 7 and 14, docs/research/xr-from-3d/). L6 and T13 have a few hundred and a few dozen training patients in the record, almost all simulated from CT (chapter 14). Packed with vertebra_l6 and vertebra_t13 as stored labels under the fork's extended protocol (item 12), they render to labelled rare-class X-rays that are the project's own, beside FluoroGen's synth_rare renders (12,929 with 1,763 L6 and 86 T13, exact truth, licence unknown and so out of the dataset selector) and the calibrated renderer's. fxr-render takes no dataset spec, so the packed set must store protocol names; the packer does not resample, so each CT is brought to 512 by 512 in plane first. Two facts of the training code bear on item 14 and the avenues: a dropped label is a hard negative for every vertebra channel in both loss terms, so the released weights were pushed to call a sixth lumbar body background, and adding two channels while keeping the 61 trained ones needs a row-wise head copy the fork does not have, since --replace-head discards the whole head. The code does take real frames with masks for only some levels, through its partial-label route, which ignores the channels an image does not annotate: the record's real frames with level boxes, drawn as coarse masks, can supervise FleXray without a CT. The realism step is the project's: FluXray's FLUX.2 edit is not published, and FluXray's images are CC BY-NC 4.0. data/licences.json keeps FleXray's weights at non-commercial; the renders made with its MIT code from the project's CTs take the CTs' own class, which is what lets item 8 proceed before item 15.
