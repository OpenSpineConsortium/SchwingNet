# The FleXray audit beside the record: the instance question, the required test's three readings, the settings to compare and what the owner is asked

Label: confidential-method, as chapters 10 and 34 are: this note names the counting line and its numbers, stays in this private repository and is copied nowhere. Its public half, the threads for #391 with their dependencies and counts and what FleXray is from its code, is symphony-template's docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md; this note holds what that one may not: the record's numbers, the method the required test measures, the settings it compares, and the questions #391 marks to confirm, in the record's own vocabulary so the owner can answer them in a line each.

## 1. What FleXray did on the held frames, and what that shows

The public benchmark's one finished job (Slurm 40781570, commit fc027a6, docs/checkpoints/2026-10-25-public-benchmark/HANDOFF.md) ran FleXray on the C-ArmLSpine bank realxray/carmlspine (grid/bench.sbatch; the set holds 299 spot frames, data/sources/carmlspine.json) and scored the 1,627 truth boxes that have a FleXray channel, the sacrum's among them. It is zero-shot by the fork's configs, which name no spine fluoroscopy set (the public plan, section 1), but not yet by file: the configs do not list MOOSE 3.0's sites, so item 6's census settles whether any C-ArmLSpine volume reached training inside MOOSE or FluXray. Under the quarter-coverage rule every box counted as detected, and over detected bodies the flagship member named the level right on 99.14 percent (1,613 of 1,627; Wilson 98.56 to 99.49), the ensemble on 99.63 percent (1,621 of 1,627; 99.20 to 99.83), and the weakest member, flux000, on 98.22 percent. The reported stack names zero-shot, from one frame, 595 of 617 detected lateral bodies (96.4 percent, 88.0 with the 59 undetected counted wrong) and 276 of 631 AP bodies (43.7 percent) (chapter 10 and docs/research/demo-plan.md, from STATUS.md A2.1, A2.2 and A2.6). Those are the bodies its own detector found, not FleXray's 1,627 boxes, and FleXray was given every box, so the two are not read the same way. Three things the number does not show, each a step of the audit:

- The split by view is unread. The job's JSON on the grid, flexray-carmlspine.json, holds a tally per view group its frames' meta names (ap, lateral, oblique, or other where a frame names none) and no per-case rows; the job log on symphony-template's branch grid keeps only the overall rows. If FleXray holds near 99 percent on the AP bodies, where the reported stack has 43.7, that alone answers part of item 10. The paper's own angle result makes the split worth more than a view label: on DeepFluoro its lumbar spine falls from 0.840 Dice within 15 degrees of frontal to 0.751 beyond 25 degrees, so the stress test scores each held frame by its angle, where its pose is recorded or solved, as well as by view. It is read with the stress test (item 9), no new job. The approved DeepFluoro rerun scores the lumbar spine as the union of FleXray's five lumbar channels in the benchmark's own scorer, which it must: the fork's evaluator never computes that union and its training remap sends DeepFluoro's stored lumbar_spine label to background, so the paper's DeepFluoro Dice (0.916 as the scorer quotes the project page, 0.915 in docs/research/arcade-lab-x-ray-learning-against-schwingnet.md's reading of the paper) is not a number the fork reproduces by itself.
- The scoring gave FleXray the boxes. Each truth box took the level whose mask covered most of it, so the number is level naming given a body, the second stage of the reported stack's task, not detection; whether FleXray misses bodies or draws extra ones on these frames was not scored. The stress test scores FleXray's own masks against the boxes both ways.
- FleXray cannot name a transitional body. Its label space has one channel per vertebra from C1 to L5 and the sacrum and none for a sixth lumbar or a thirteenth thoracic body; the CT sets that label vertebra_l6 drop it to background before training, so a sixth lumbar body was learnt as not a vertebra; no training spec stores a T13 vertebra (the dropped rib_left_13 and rib_right_13 are ribs), so how one was labelled is not found in the fork. C-ArmLSpine's transitional stratum is case42, case121 and case136, the three L6 persons, with case139 and case44 (chapter 14; the demo plan). The scorer counts an L6 truth box apart, under no_channel, so no L6 body is in the 1,627, and the JSON keeps no per-case rows, so item 9 keeps per-box rows to score these cases apart. A model that names by class has no answer for the bodies the counting prior exists for; training pushed a sixth lumbar body to background, which predicts a missed body rather than a confident wrong name, and whether it also misnames the neighbours is measured in item 9.

So the owner's clarification has a two-part answer. FleXray names individual vertebral levels, by class and from one frame. Given the truth boxes, its flagship named 99.14 percent of C-ArmLSpine's boxes, against the reported stack's zero-shot 96.4 percent of detected lateral bodies and 43.7 percent of AP, and the count from the detected sacrum, which names 97.0 percent of anchored bodies with no n given (chapter 10). The counting line's 99.17 to 100 percent is per walk on simulated pans with the annotation's boxes and is not comparable. It does not segment instances, order them or know the variants. That is the gap the record's counting line fills and the one item 11 writes the avenues for.

## 2. Why they could and the record could not, as a question to measure, not yet an answer

Item 10 writes the comparison; this section sets its frame from what is read so far, each line to be graded in that thread.

- Supervision. FleXray's per-vertebra labels are all projected from CT: lumbar levels from MOOSE 3.0 and the two sets that share its label table, HANSeg and ShoulderCT (47.5 percent of batches together), cervical and upper thoracic also from RSNAFrac, while PedsCT and ElbowCT carry none; and from FluXray, 138,063 edited renders kept of the 143,730 that 90 poses of 1,597 MOOSE CTs give, about 4 percent filtered out by the paper (37.5 percent of batches in the flagship, none in flux000); real radiographs of hands, feet, forearms and humeri are the remaining 4 percent, one percent each, and none of them shows a spine. The record's film-trained detector found 97.6, 99.14 or 99.2 percent of bodies on held-out real film, three readings of three misses in 243 that the record has not settled; the render-trained detector found 71.67 percent, and with its boxes counting fell from 100 percent of walks to 30.06 percent on 79 held-out spines (chapter 10). FleXray reached 99 percent level naming on real fluoroscopy, given the boxes, with no real spine frame in training and real film of any kind in one batch in twenty-five. On lateral naming the record's encoder had already transferred from renders, 96.4 percent zero-shot with the device seen only in renders (chapters 12 and 13), so lateral naming is not what FleXray did that the record could not. Two things are open: AP naming, where the record has 43.7 percent and FleXray's split is unread, and detection, which FleXray's score was given and the record's renders-only detectors failed. The five members are a ready-made ablation of the FluXray share, all one recipe but for that proportion: flux000, trained with no FluXray at all, still named 98.22 percent against the flagship's 99.14, so on these frames the realism edit and FluXray's 1,597 CTs at 90 poses account for at most about 0.9 points, and the members' rows in flexray-carmlspine.json are read before item 10 grades it. So the candidates come in this order: the CT stream, six CT sets rendered fresh every step for a million iterations; the per-label attenuation randomisation; the dense per-pixel supervision of a segmenter against the record's boxes; and, a minor one on these frames, the generative realism edit (FLUX.2 [klein] 9B by the paper, at five orbit angles and no tilt), which the project's cancelled FluoroGen arm (SDXL, chapter 13) set out to make and lost the vertebrae to. Item 10 grades each.
- Rendering. nanodrr ray casting over a full orbit (alpha in plus or minus 180 degrees, tilt in plus or minus 30), at a random labelled isocenter for MOOSE and the volume's centre for the other CT sets, four of which also roll up to plus or minus 45 degrees; a lognormal attenuation factor, above 1 on about three draws in four, on every supervised structure half the time; the image the line integral of attenuation; then image-space augmentation with Gaussian noise of 0.01, contrast, gamma, plasma brightness, an elastic warp, inversion and a one-axis zero-padded crop, but no physical noise, scatter or intensifier model. The record's calibrated renderer carries more of the physics than FleXray's: polyenergetic, multi-material attenuation, nine fitted device terms (receptor blur, halo, vignette, heel and scatter among them) and a fitted photon budget (chapter 13), beside the strong randomisation block (docs/research/strong-domain-randomisation.md); what the record measured about renders alone against real frames is set beside FleXray's result in item 10 with the grades chapter 13 gives.
- Architecture and schedule. One UNet at 256 pixels, 61 sigmoid channels, routed losses per dataset, 2,000 epochs of 500 iterations, EMA, an ensemble of five. The reported stack is a 1,024-pixel YOLO11m detector plus a ResNet-18 level encoder reading each body at 96 pixels and a BiGRU head over two to nine bodies, with a 27-level posterior. FleXray spends its capacity on one dense answer at low resolution; the record spends it on boxes at high resolution and a small sequence model.
- Field and scaling. FleXray's training frames, at the isocenter, about 0.33 to 2.8 mm a pixel at 256 pixels (85 to 720 mm) across its CT profiles and the in-plane rescale, MOOSE's cone-beam draws at 128 to 451 mm; each render comes from one axial crop of 256 slices, so how much column it holds is bounded by 256 times the slice spacing. A nine-inch intensifier's field of about 14 cm lies inside that range; C-ArmLSpine's field width is not in the record, and the stress test scores FleXray by field width. Its reader scales a PNG or TIFF integer file by the dtype's range and a DICOM by its own range; whether a 12-bit frame stored in 16 bits then arrives dim depends on the bundle's declared normalisation, which the fork does not carry: if it declares the training's per-sample percentile_minmax, the two scalings agree on an unpadded frame and differ only by the padding offset on a non-square one. The stress test reads the released preprocessing.json first and scores each frame both ways.

## 3. The required test, flexray+optimal_window_width_count_from_anchor: three readings

The brief names the test with a window width and item 2 with an offset. The record uses "window" in three senses. The owner confirmed reading A on 2026-10-25 at 20:49 UTC: the window is the exposure field and the offset the commanded step, in vertebral bodies; readings B and C are kept below for the record.

Reading A, the sweep's geometry (chapters 10 and 34). The window is the exposure field: on a nine-inch intensifier about 14 cm at the spine, four lumbar bodies in view; a 12-inch field and a collimated 100 mm field are the other widths the record measured. The offset is the commanded step between exposures, one field minus the overlap. The optimal setting is chapter 34's: two bodies of overlap, a move of about 7 cm on a nine-inch field, where chain completion peaked in all three recorded sets: 88.3, 96.7, 97.5 and 82.5 percent at half, one, two and four bodies on the canonical 18 September page, and 81.7, 95.8, 91.7, 97.5, 96.7, 90.8 and 82.5 at half, one, one and a half, two, two and a half, three and four in sweep_v3, the one run the sweep note counts, whose two bodies reached 120 of 120 under the rule that the last move is a full move or none (chapter 34). Under this reading the test runs the counting line with FleXray's masks in place of the detector's boxes and the level encoder's names: cut each panorama into overlapping windows of the field width at the step the setting commands, run FleXray on each window, link the windows from the anchor through the restricted correlation (the band of plus or minus 0.6 of a body about the commanded step, chapter 10), count, and score each body's count against truth and FleXray's own level name against truth. Version 1 has no such linker: src/schwingnet/chain.py counts within one frame, and src/schwingnet/sweep.py joins boxes by intersection over union. The linker the evidence used is spine-program's sweep_map.py, copied there from spinesurg-ct-nnunet (chapter 34); tools/overlap_figures.py copies its geometry and pairing rule but not the correlation. Item 13 ports the linker into SchwingNet first, by pull request with spine-program attached, which needs no grid job.

Reading B, the intensity window. tools/train_blocks.py carries FluoroSAM's random windowing as one transform of the augment block strong (augment=strong): a viewer's window a WINDOW_WIDTH share of the range wide, 0.2 to 1.0, a range the module chose since FluoroSAM's paper gives none. Under this reading the optimal window width is the display window at which FleXray's masks are best on the held frames, a preprocessing knob of FleXray inference, and the non-optimal ones are narrower or wider windows. The record never calls a display window optimal, and the brief pairs the phrase with counting from the anchor and with offsets, so this reading is thought unlikely; it is cheap to add as a one-axis sweep inside the same job if the owner wants both.

Reading C, the linking search window. The band of plus or minus 0.6 of a body about the commanded step is also called a window (chapters 10 and 34), and the counting evidence varied its half-width: widening the half-width to 1.2 and 2.1 bodies raised the wrong walks on the 801 renders from 21 (99.66 percent at 0.6) to 52 and 67, about 99.2 and 98.9 percent if the reached walks stayed near 6,255 (chapter 34 gives the range as 98.9 to 99.6), so chapter 34 finds that the band is not what makes two bodies work. Under this reading the optimal window is 0.6 of a body and the non-optimal ones wider. The record calls only the overlap optimal, so reading C is less likely than A; it costs nothing extra in the scoring job, which can link each setting at more than one band.

## 4. The settings to compare, under reading A

The owner set the rule on 2026-10-25 at 20:49 UTC: the non-optimal settings are the minima of accuracy to the left and to the right of the maxima, each rounded to the nearest half vertebral body. The rule is fixed here, before any run, so the settings it picks cannot move after the data are seen; the values are read from the test's own curve. Accuracy is chapter 34's level exactness at the target, the count's own measure. The GPU job therefore runs every overlap on a grid of a quarter body from 0.5 to 4 bodies, the pre-registration's range, 15 settings in all. The CPU job finds the maximum of accuracy, which is flexray+offset_optimal, and every other local maximum; takes the overlap of lowest accuracy between each maximum and the next maximum, or the end of the range, on either side; and rounds each to the nearest half body. Those are nonoptimal_i to nonoptimal_k. Where accuracy keeps falling to the end of the range, the minimum on that side is the range's end, 0.5 or 4 bodies, and the report says so rather than calling it a turning point. Chapter 34's other conditions stand: every spine or film swept at each overlap, so the comparison is paired; the nine-inch field (140 mm at the spine) as the main condition and the twelve-inch (190 mm) as a secondary; the step jittered by up to 0.3 of a body, plus one condition with a step error of standard deviation 0.3 of a body; and the rule that the last move is a full move or none.

The record's curves say what the rule is likely to pick. sweep_v3's chain completion has maxima at one and two bodies and minima at half, one and a half and four, so if FleXray's accuracy has the same shape, i to k are 0.5, 1.5 and 4 bodies:

Outcomes and scoring, as chapter 34 fixes them: chain completion per spine, every body between the anchor and the last frame's top once, none missed or duplicated; exactness at the target, the mark on the body truly k from the anchor among walks that reached it, the anchor counted 0; the decline rate, the share of walks the system refuses, which no artifact has measured yet; and exposures per walk. Each is reported with a cluster bootstrap interval by spine (2,000 resamples), since a spine gives about eight walks. One column the record has never had is added: FleXray's own level name per body, so that the test shows what counting adds to a model that already names. Chapter 34's power analysis sets what the held panoramas can show: two bodies against four needs about 50 spines, completion at two above 95 percent about 384, and two against one about 813, more than any held arm, so that comparison is estimated with its interval and given no pass line.

The jobs: one GPU job runs FleXray (the flagship and the ensemble) on every window of every setting of every panorama, since inference at 256 pixels is cheap and one job keeps the settings comparable; one CPU job links, counts and scores. ## 5. The panoramas the test runs on, and what is not held

The record is explicit: no real stepped sweep is held (chapter 34). The talk's recorded sweep, C-ArmLSpine case134 (301 usable frames of 302, with poses and projected boxes), and case39 (its frames not yet told apart from the 696 files of its CBCT folder, with level names but no poses or boxes, docs/research/latency-and-sweep/README.md) are cone-beam sweeps turning from +122 to -52 degrees, a rotation about the patient, so they test FleXray on video (item 9), case39 unscored until its poses exist, and not an overlap along the spine. The counting evidence was scored by spine-program's simulator sweep_map.py on pans cut from CT renders (counting on CTSpinoPelvic1K's 79 and 801, TotalSegmentator's 522 and 204 and VerSe's 76 from C2; chain completion on 120 CTSpinoPelvic1K renders; naming on the sweep on 12 VerSe) and from real radiographs cut into fields (16 lumbar films; 24, 262 and 264 films from C2). Only the counts on real C-arm frames are within one frame (chapter 10). So the test's panoramas are:

- Rendered whole spines, the same route as the evidence: the simulator's frames from VerSe, TotalSegmentator and CTSpinoPelvic1K renders, or whole-spine renders by the project's calibrated renderer, or, once item 7 has it, by fxr-render from the same CTs, which puts FleXray on renders of the kind it trained on and on the project's.
- Real whole-spine films: AASCE's 609 labelled AP films (608 usable), corners on T1 to L5 by position with C2 out of frame and no sacrum box, so they test chain completion and the ordinal from the first annotated body, not the level (chapter 34); the 236 standing lateral films of IEEE DataPort (data/sources/ieee_dataport_lateral_236.json), the one held real film with C2 and the sacrum both in view, which carries no level truth; and BUU-LSPINE's 3,600 AP and 3,603 lateral lumbar films reaching the sacrum, a short panorama. Derived rather than real: or_ready/wholespine's 12,950 frames, built from the held CTs and films, not acquired (docs/research/held-fluoroscopy-by-region.md). Which of them hold truth boxes, and where their files are, is open item 8 of docs/checkpoints/2026-10-25-dataset-accounting/HANDOFF.md, the one CPU job the plan sends first. The rendered sources are VerSe (374 series of 355 subjects, ten never downloaded, mostly regional: the record finds one mask in 120 spanning C2 to the lumbar spine, and the whole-spine arms used 76 and 12 of its spines), TotalSegmentator's 142 whole-spine subjects (156 with C2 and the sacrum on the 21 September board, the gap unsettled, chapter 13) and CTSpinoPelvic1K's 802 CTs from the sacrum to about T10.
- The transitional spines among them, scored apart: chapter 14's three C-ArmLSpine cases (case42, case121, case136; zero verified real-frame transitional test persons against 35 needed), the held-out persons of the demo plan's transitional render arm (194 transitional persons across all sources by its count, frozen G3 split; the record leaves 194, about 190, 202 and 306 unsettled, chapter 7), beside CTSpinoPelvic1K's own 33 Castellvi-graded, 18 L6 and 9 four-lumbar, chapter 34's arm C for anomalous sweeps (18 L6, 9 four-lumbar, 6 VerSe T13), and the rare-class renders of item 8 once they exist.

A full spine of 700 to 900 mm padded to a square and resized to 256 pixels gives about 2.7 to 3.5 mm a pixel, a lumbar body seven to eleven pixels tall; a nine-inch window gives about 0.55 mm a pixel, four bodies filling the frame, only if it is cut square at 140 mm, since the scorer pads to the longer side (tools/bench_public.py), so a window cut full width from a wider film comes out coarser. FleXray trained at about 0.33 to 2.8 mm a pixel across its CT profiles and the in-plane rescale, so the panorama's scale is at or a little beyond its coarsest and a nine-inch window well inside its range; what no training render holds is a whole column, since each comes from one 256-slice crop. Its network is fully convolutional and its public preprocessing class takes another canvas size, so a window can also be run at its own pixels rather than resized to 256. The stress test scores whole, windowed at 256 and windowed at native size, so the test's windowing is not confounded with FleXray's scale.

## 6. The rare-class route and the licence line

fxr-render takes a packed CT set with a protocol that names the labels, needs no weights and is MIT. The held CTs with labelled anomalies are CTSpinoPelvic1K's (18 L6 by release label, 9 four-lumbar by the readers, 33 Castellvi-graded, 4 percent of 802 in the owner's audit, docs/research/flepp-and-killeen-critique.md, a graded count against 71 to 142 expected and not a prevalence, chapter 7) and VerSe's (masks C1 to L6 with T13 and a two-reader variant table; 6 T13, the L6 count open), with CTSpine1K's 24 lumbarisations and 13 sacralisations, whose 782 colonography volumes are CTSpinoPelvic1K persons, so the two sets are deduplicated by person before packing (chapters 7 and 14, docs/research/xr-from-3d/). L6 and T13 have a few hundred and a few dozen training patients in the record, almost all simulated from CT (chapter 14). Packed with vertebra_l6 and vertebra_t13 as stored labels, under a protocol and model label list that both name them (in the fork, item 12, or under protocol.config_root; a label in the protocol but not in the model list renders as background), they render to labelled rare-class X-rays made with MIT code, free of FleXray's weights' licence but under each CT set's own class: CTSpinoPelvic1K's and CTSpine1K's non-commercial, CTSpinoPelvic1K's labels share-alike too, VerSe's share-alike (data/licences.json). They sit beside FluoroGen's synth_rare renders (12,929 by their record, with 1,763 L6 and 86 T13; the record calls the truth exact, but the FluoroCap contradictions rule them the cancelled SDXL array's output, their file count 12,896 against 12,929 and their labels to check, chapter 13; licence class unknown, so no recipe takes them) and the calibrated renderer's. fxr-render reads the pack's stored_labels, which a dense CT pack always records, and falls back to the dataset spec only when they are missing, so the packed set must store protocol names; the packer does not resample, and a crop's in-plane size must equal the volume's own, so CTs packed together are brought to one in-plane size first, by padding or cropping (512 by 512 to match the released packs). Two facts of the training code bear on item 14 and the avenues: a dropped label is a hard negative for every vertebra channel in the cross-entropy and for the levels present in the image in the Dice term, so the released weights were pushed to call a sixth lumbar body background, and adding two channels while keeping the 61 trained ones needs a row-wise head copy the fork does not have, since --replace-head discards the whole head. The code does take real frames with masks for only some levels, through its partial-label route, which ignores the channels an image does not annotate: the record's real frames with level boxes, drawn as coarse masks, can supervise FleXray without a CT. The realism step is the project's: FluXray's FLUX.2 edit is not in the code, and FluXray's images carry two non-commercial terms, CC BY-NC 4.0 and the FLUX Non-Commercial License v2.1 of the klein 9B model that made them, while klein 4B is Apache 2.0. data/licences.json has no entry yet for FleXray's weights or for FluXray; the one statement is the fetch line of grid/run-platform-fetch.sh, a benchmark row only and never in a SchwingNet network, and item 6 adds both to the licence file as non-commercial. The renders made with FleXray's MIT code take the class of the CTs they are made from, never the weights', which is what lets item 8 proceed before item 15; a rare-class set for anything the filing touches needs anomaly CTs under a permissive class, which the held ones are not.

Item 11's avenues from the textbook, beside the three the public plan takes from FleXray's own code: a counting prior over FleXray's channels, the R + C = 22 check where C2 and the sacrum share one chain; the ordering of levels, which nothing in its head enforces; and the counting line of section 3 run on its masks in place of the detector's boxes, which is the required test itself. Each states its experiment and its count; none starts.
