## 5. The owner's direction against what exists

His prompts of 19:08 UTC, part by part (verbatim in docs/checkpoints/2026-10-28-final/memory/owner-direction-2026-10-28.md). The machine-written answers he pasted beside them are not measurements: their figures (under 1.2 mm, under 20 ms) are invented, and the earlier work they name is unread.

1. A Bayesian generator on synthetic per-level data, kept Bayesian, no invented features.
   - Exists: the exact rule and its PyMC rewrite (bordercall schwingcheck, PRs 7 and 9), 32 usable Panjabi measures with each kind's own error (PR 15), the build-up study (PR 18), and the chain of levels for missed bodies, L6 and T13 (draft PR 20). No challenger, the 64-unit network included, has beaten the rule on typical columns.
   - Missing: a table from real people. The rule's means and spreads are 12 cadavers, which breaks its own 0.99 promise; the cadaver note (PR 19) says about 50 to 68 normal CTs fix that, and no pooling of the literature can. The estimator that builds the table from CTs (TODO 5) went to the model account and has not started. An older border classifier's 95% on CTSpinoPelvic1K is set aside by the owner's word of 26 October.
   - Conflicts: none with his rule of no invented features; the model handoff already rules out derived features and any network before anomalies are handled. A learned measurer is the first network the plan would add.
2. A classifier from a 3D instance segmentation to named levels.
   - Exists: scanimport reads six CT label schemes, raw CT without a mask and DICOM covariates (bordercall PRs 8 and 13). Given measures, the rule is already this classifier, and the chain handles L6, T13 and missed bodies.
   - Missing: the step between them. Nothing turns a mask into the 32 measures; only the seven SPINEPS measures are registered, and scanimport has imported no real scan.
   - Conflicts: the rule names C3 to L5 only, not C1, C2 or the sacrum. On a whole CT that meets his truth rule, the count from C2 (with the ribs for an L6 or T13) already names every level, so morphometry earns its keep on partial fields: silver CTs, fluoroscopy windows and anomalous spines.
3. A network that learns the measurements too hard to program.
   - Exists: the definition of every measure in Panjabi's four papers (101 measure objects, one per measurement and side), held to its page; nine programmed chords in scanimport, tested on 13 phantoms; a report on measuring vertebrae without masks (bordercall reports/); the reread of all 43 papers for how each measure is taken and its pitfalls (PR 21).
   - Missing: a split of the 32 into those a few lines of geometry can take from a mask and those that need learning. The measures that carry the accuracy (facets, interfacet distances, end-plate areas, pedicles on C3 to L5) are the ones with no measurer today.
   - Conflicts: Panjabi measured 12 fresh cadaver spines with a 3-D digitiser, not CT. On CTSpinoPelvic1K canal depth, canal width and end-plate width came within 1 mm of Panjabi's, but posterior body height ran 5.5 to 7.5 mm above it, so a measurer that matches CT anatomy needs that offset modelled before the table is used.
4. "I have 2000 instances of spines with real-world training labels".
   - Exists: no held set is exactly 2,000. The nearest are TotalSegmentator v3 (1,935 persons), ENHANCE.PET (1,557 whole studies of 865 to 1,478 persons), VerSe (355 persons), and the census of 21,622 SPINEPS CTs.
   - Conflicts: under his truth rule only part of each counts (TotalSegmentator 142 to 156 whole from C2 to the sacrum, VerSe 76 holding C2), and his own accepted paper finds TotalSegmentator's labels below L2 shifted one level caudal on lumbarised spines, and an L6 label in none of 1,152 CTs. These labels are level names on masks, not measurements: no CT anywhere carries a chord, area or angle drawn by a person (person-placed corners exist only on radiographs: AASCE, CSXA, NHANES II, BUU). A network trained only through the naming likelihood is free to learn whatever features name levels best, which is the invented feature he wants to avoid, unless each output is tied to its measure's geometry. Licences: of ENHANCE.PET, autoPET's 979 studies are CC BY-NC and Leipzig's 379 and Careggi's 199 are CC BY; TotalSegmentator is CC BY; VerSe is CC BY-SA or CC BY, unsettled; each is per site.
   - Needs the owner: which set, whether any of its spines carry measurements rather than names, and the licence class the auto-anatomist is trained under.
5. See what the auto-anatomist thinks each measurement is, on the 3D mask.
   - Exists only as a plan: bordercall TODO 7.8 (a detector's landmark uncertainty as latent measures), not started. TODO 7.4 runs the other way, generating meshes to render as DRRs. No code draws a measurement on a mask.
   - Missing: this favours measures taken between named landmarks (points, chords and planes that can be drawn on the mesh) over a free regressor; a shape model with fixed vertices is one form of that. The rule sets an error budget: at 2 mm of real error its 0.99 answers stay right 99.8% of the time if it is told the error and 96.2% if it assumes 1 mm; at 3 mm they fall to 96.85% and 82.6% (bordercall archive/docs/EXACT_IDENTIFICATION_RESULTS.md). So each learned measure's error has to be measured against people, and the rule told what it is.
6. Low latency at inference; training cost does not matter.
   - Exists: the rule takes 0.31 to 9.68 ms for a 3-body field. NUTS is unusable at inference (166 to 712 times as long for a field alone, 113 to 300 times in a batch, and once silently wrong; schwingcheck/README.md). The chain's exact sum takes about 0.06 s a field at 3 bodies, 0.20 to 0.22 s at 5 and 2.6 s at 9 with all 32 measures. SchwingNet's budget is 97 ms a frame.
   - Missing: a latency number by SchwingNet's own rule (ONNX parity, a p95, one frame on a named device). None of the chain's figures is one, being batch means with setup inside, but at 5 bodies they are already about twice the budget. Also missing: the measurer's own latency, and a fast chain; amortised inference (TODO 7.6) is planned, not started. Every training run is a grid job, which needs his word and count.
   - Conflicts: a Viterbi pass needs each body's measures independent given its level. The rule's shared patient offset breaks that, which is why the chain enumerates paths; Viterbi means dropping the offset (a different model, and the 0.99 promise with it) or sampling it.
7. A segmentation ladder from perfect annotation down to HU thresholding.
   - Exists: his tiers are an annotation ladder already (gold and silver 1 to 4). scanimport's mask-free mode (PR 13) is an HU-threshold rung on CT, phantom-tested only and naming no body, and the DRR study renders from the CT alone (#431). Watershed is cited, not built; Multi-Otsu is neither. In 2D no rung exists without corners or a trained model's masks.
   - Conflicts: the middle rungs are segmenters (SPINEPS run again, TotalSegmentator, MOOSE). His rules say SPINEPS masks are reused and never re-run, the segmenter block takes only spineps and moose, and MOOSE only replicates FleXray. The ladder needs his word on which segmenters may run as rungs, and a grid count for each.
8. eDRR then Flux realism as the projection operator into fluoroscopy.
   - Exists: FleXray's DRRs; FluXray's 138,063 zero-shot FLUX.2 klein-9B renders, held; the Flux realism programme coded, with 5 fetches and 4 GPU jobs approved on 26 October and unsent; the DRR domain-gap study to measure the operator.
   - Conflicts: he assumes a perfect operator. The first DRR run tells six of the ten synthetic groups from real fluoroscopy and film in every space; FluXray and the Flux-edit pair are not yet measured (the approved rerun). FleXray has no L6 or T13 channel, so anomalous spines lose their labels in FleXray's label space, not in the physics. At 256 px a FluXray pixel is 1.25 to 2.21 mm, too coarse for end-plate angles to a degree. FleXray's weights and FluXray are CC BY-NC 4.0, and the realism programme's own note says no FluXray image enters a SchwingNet network; klein-9B's outputs are non-commercial while klein-4B is Apache 2.0, and the 9B licence click is still his.
9. The earlier work the pasted answers named: Smyth 1999 is cited in a bordercall report; Campbell and Petrella 2016 is in spine-program's render-device notes, unchecked as the paper meant; Kirschner and Ouyang's DISSM appear nowhere. None has been read for this.

## 7. What is known

Synthetic, the naming rule on columns drawn from Panjabi's table (bordercall's generator draws from the same table the rule assumes; none of these comes from a patient):

- The seven SPINEPS measures named exactly: 47.3 / 73.1 / 85.7 / 96.1% at 1 / 3 / 5 / 9 bodies.
- Answers given at posterior 0.99: right 99.7% or more at every width (99.78% at 3 bodies, 15 wrong of 6,848).
- All 32 usable measures (the build-up study): 98.3% exact at 3 bodies; from 25 measures every 9-body field is named.
- A table from 12 people, as Panjabi's is: answers at 0.99 right only 95.75% (seven measures) and 98.05% (32); the promise breaks. From 50 or more people, 99.36% or more; about 50 people come within 2 to 4 points of 2,000.
