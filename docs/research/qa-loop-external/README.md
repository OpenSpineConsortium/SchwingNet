# The QA loop with external real arms: where it stands

Source: the local session of 1 November 2026, on the owner's asks of that evening (real fluoroscopy in the validation of the 2D landmarks, DeepFluoro first then COBRA2026, and the sacral landmarks for the count's anchor). Confidential with the rest of this repository: the landmark direction stays private.

The design is PREREG.md beside this file: the QA loop of docs/research/qa-loop/ with two further sets of real X-rays read by its real naming and presence gates, never by the selection, each source's own result and the sacral anchor's presence recorded beside the pooled gates. It runs as the launched flow qaloop_ext (flows/qaloop_ext.py), sent by the grid word `qaloop qa_ext_smoke` or `qaloop qa_ext`.

- The pairs: tools/realx_pairs.py builds them from the realxray exports already on the grid (DeepFluoro's 2026-09-21 export, COBRA's Elekta export v3), into the data root's realx/deepfluoro and realx/cobra, each with a manifest of every person and the reason for every person not read. On 1 November: DeepFluoro 3 specimens and 142 frames, one specimen held out for test; COBRA 66 cases and 579 frames, 27 held out for test; frames more than 15 degrees from the anteroposterior or lateral view left out (48 and 1,005).
- The checks before any run: on the grid the projected landmarks of each level fell inside the export's own box for that level on 82 per cent of DeepFluoro's in-frame points and 86 per cent of COBRA's (each export drew its boxes from the same CT through the same pose; the rest are the transverse and spinous tips past a body box), and contact sheets of both sets were read by eye. The first sacral points took the top of S1 from the alae where they rise above the endplate; the top is now taken within 15 mm of the midline.
- Why: the base loop's real arm is 13 C-ArmLSpine validation persons, 6 frames for presence and 8 runs a view for real naming, too few for either bar's lower bound to pass however good the landmarks are.
- Where it stands: the first smoke (chain 40939308) failed in round 0 on a flat outline of the four sacral points in a lateral view, fixed in #549 by widening it with the lowest body. The smoke sent again (chain 40939352, an A30 on reqp, 13.5 minutes) holds, verdict 0. After 25 steps a round, so as a smoke and not as a result: the real gate runs were 16 from C-ArmLSpine, 169 from COBRA and 1 from DeepFluoro, whose frames show the lumbar column only at their top edge. Real naming, unanchored, named C-ArmLSpine's anteroposterior runs all right and its lateral three of four, but COBRA's only 0.23 and 0.41. Presence: 3 of 6 C-ArmLSpine frames, 163 of 330 COBRA's and 31 of 49 DeepFluoro's at or above the 0.6 percentile. The sacral anchor: 2 of 6, 134 of 529 and 69 of 127. So the 13 C-ArmLSpine validation persons flattered the naming, and on the larger arm a count with no anchor rarely names two or three lumbar bodies right; the anchor, which would settle the count, shows on about a quarter of COBRA's kV frames and half of DeepFluoro's C-arm frames. The full loop (chain 40937444's sibling, qa_ext chain 40939478) was sent the same night, six links on reqp's A30s.
- DeepFluoro's own annotations against the arm (the owner, 1 November: "the fluoro X-rays for deep fluoro comes with annotations and I think they should be used somehow"). The release ships, for every frame, fourteen pelvic landmarks a person placed in the specimen's CT (the femoral head centres, the greater sciatic notches, the obturator foramina's inferior and medial points, the pubic symphysis's top and bottom, the anterior superior iliac spines) projected through the ground-truth pose, and a 2-D label image the authors drew from their own 3-D labels (hemipelves, the lumbar vertebrae as one class, the upper sacrum where no other bone overlies it, the femurs). The arm used them only through the export's September check of its sacrum box; tools/deepfluoro_shipped.py now reads them for the arm's own frames (never the held-out specimen) and `tools/realx_pairs.py annotations` sets them against the arm three ways. Stated here before the check ran, with its bars: (1) the pose chain in the loop's 512 pixels, the person's 3-D landmarks through each pair's P against the shipped 2-D ones, median at most 1 px and 90th percentile at most 2 px, the export's reading of the coordinates the clear winner; a failure voids DeepFluoro's gate numbers. (2) The arm's lumbar landmarks within 8 px (the presence radius) of a shipped vertebra or upper-sacrum pixel, at least 0.9 of those in frame, reported by level and view beside the share near any bone, since the shipped image draws one bone where two overlie; the sacral promontory and the top of S1 the same, at least 0.9 each, the alae and the distance from the top of S1 to the upper sacrum reported. (3) The presence measure on the landmarks a person placed: the loop's own presence gate (0.6 percentile, share's lower bound 0.6) on the thirteen outline points, the femoral head centres, which lie inside the bone where an edge measure has nothing to find, as the control that is not an edge, and the arm's eighteen landmarks and anchor on the same frames beside them. If the person's outline points pass, the measure can see on these frames what a person marks, and an arm landmark below them is less visible than that; if they fail, the presence gate's DeepFluoro readings say nothing about whether a landmark is real. This check steers nothing in the running loop and changes no file the loop reads.
- What the check found (grid job 40939686, 1 November; the release file's sha256 a59e8c09..., the arm's three specimens and 142 frames, the held-out specimen never opened; all the arm's DeepFluoro frames are anteroposterior, none within 15 degrees of the lateral). (1) Holds: the person's 3-D landmarks through the pairs' P land on the shipped 2-D ones at a median of 0.001 px and at most 0.002 px over 1,542 points; the next reading of the coordinates is 447 px off at its 90th percentile. This proves the arm's pixel handling (canvas, scale, the 180-degree turn), not the registration itself, since the release made its 2-D landmarks through the same pose. (2) Holds: 96.3 per cent of the arm's in-frame lumbar landmarks sit within 8 px of the authors' vertebra or upper-sacrum label (L4 96.8, L5 95.2), 99.6 per cent near some bone; the promontory and the top of S1 sit within 8 px of the vertebra or upper-sacrum label on every frame. But that bar cannot place S1 finely: in these anteroposterior frames L5 overlies the top of S1, and the shipped image draws one bone a pixel, so the top of S1 lies inside the vertebra label, a median 57 px from the visible upper sacrum, and the alae lie under the hemipelves. Two of the three specimens have no L5 landmark read (the oracle read none of its eighteen). (3) Fails, as stated beforehand: the thirteen outline points a person placed reach the 0.6 percentile on 79 of 142 frames, the share's lower bound 0.474 below the loop's 0.6, so the presence gate's bar is above what landmarks a person marked on real C-arm frames reach, and the gate's DeepFluoro readings say nothing about whether a landmark is real. The measure does carry the signal: the femoral head centres, the control that is not an edge, reach it on 4 of 142 (median 0.23 against 0.62). By point: the obturator foramen's medial point 0.84 (75 per cent of frames at the bar), the sciatic notch and the iliac spine 0.68, the pubic symphysis 0.55 to 0.64. The arm's own: the canal's posterior wall (0.87, 87 per cent), the canal's left and right walls (0.79), the upper endplate's posterior end (0.87) and the spinous tip (0.84) score with the person's best; the transverse process tips (0.10 to 0.13), the lower endplate's anterior end (0.25) and the upper endplate's left end (0.35) score far below. The sacral anchor reaches the bar on 69 of 127 frames (median 0.65), level with the person's outline points. Looked at after the result, so exploratory: on the 49 frames where lumbar bodies show, with both drawn against the same null inside the bodies, the person's outline points reach the bar on 21 and the arm's eighteen on 20 (medians 0.58 and 0.56).
- What follows from it, for the next design and never for the running loop: a presence bar set above what a person's landmarks reach cannot tell a real structure from an artefact, so the next PREREG should judge presence against the person's landmarks on the same frames (an arm landmark at least as present as the person's, by a stated margin) rather than at a fixed percentile. And an adversarial reading of the sacral points against the oracle's L5 in 3-D (63 COBRA cases with L5 read) shows the top slab drawn toward the back of S1: the top's centre sits a median 10 mm behind the centre of L5's lower endplate and the promontory only 4 mm in front of it, behind it in 20 of the 63, where the anterior corner of S1 is wanted; the S1 top's height is right (a median 5 mm below L5's lower endplate). The points lie on S1's top but the promontory is not reliably the promontory; a definition taken from the oracle's L5 (the sacrum's surface facing L5's lower endplate, its front end facing L5's anterior corner) is the change to pre-register once the running chains end.
- COBRA2026's Varian part (centres F and G), which the Elekta exporter refuses, is built as data beside this arm: docs/research/cobra-varian-arm.md, realx/cobra_varian, 38 persons and 275 frames, its own check reading as the Elekta arm's does (84 and 64 per cent inside the boxes against 86 and 69). No loop reads it; it joins one only under a new PREREG, and PREREG.md here is unchanged.
