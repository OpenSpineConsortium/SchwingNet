# Strong domain randomisation: what SyntheX and FluoroSAM randomise and what the strong block takes

Source: the thread "Build plug-and-play training experiment framework", 2026-10-24, the owner's question of 22:29 UTC ("what part are they missing?") about the design note's trial arm 2, which named "the part of SyntheX's and FluoroSAM's randomisation the blocks have". ## What the two papers randomise

SyntheX trains on images rendered from CT and tests on real X-rays, and randomises at two levels.

- **The simulation:** the viewing geometry per task (for the hip, translation of 50 mm left and right and 20 mm up and down; for the robotic tool, a source-to-isocentre distance of 600 to 900 mm; for COVID-19, rotations of 5 degrees about each axis and a distance of 350 to 650 mm, with a shear of the CT), and the physics: its realistic renderer, DeepDRR, models the full spectrum, signal-dependent and readout noise and detector saturation, against a naive mono-energetic one and a heuristic thresholding.
- **The image, regular set**, applied at every iteration: Gaussian noise of spread 0.005 to 0.1 of the intensity range, gamma 0.7 to 1.3, and a random square crop of 90 percent.
- **The image, strong set**, of which "up to two" are concatenated per iteration: inversion (max minus x); impulse, pepper or salt noise on 10 percent of pixels; a random affine warp with translation, rotation, shear and scale; linear, log or sigmoid contrast; Gaussian blur of spread 3 and average blur of kernel 2 to 7; boxes corrupted with large noise; dropout of 1 to 10 percent of pixels or of one rectangle of 2 to 5 percent of the image; sharpening, and embossing, which "added the sharpened version rather than blending it"; average, max, min or median pooling, where "all of the pooling kernel sizes were between 2 x 2 and 4 x 4"; a multiply that "either changed brightness or multiplied x element wise with 50-150% of the original value"; and a random piecewise affine distortion.

FluoroSAM follows SyntheX ("following [7, 12], we apply strong domain randomization") and names coarse dropout, inversion, blurring, Gaussian contrast adjustment, random windowing and CLAHE histogram equalisation. Its simulation samples random and near-standard C-arm views, the fully random ones within 60 degrees of the anterior direction, and its network reads three channels, the second and third windowed by a K-means clustering of the pixel values.

## SyntheX's code beside its Methods

SyntheX's code holds its strong set in the dataset files of its three tasks (hip_imaging/src/dataset.py and scaledup_dataset.py, covid/src/dataset.py, surgical_tool_detection/src/scaledup_dataset.py), the same in all four, written with imgaug. It settles how the multiply reads: an imgaug OneOf of Multiply((0.5, 1.5)), commented "Change brightness of images (50-150% of original value)", which imgaug draws once per image, and MultiplyElementwise((0.5, 1.5)), which it draws per pixel and, by default, shares across an image's channels. So SyntheX's change of brightness is a multiply of the whole image by 50 to 150 percent, and its element-wise multiply a factor of the same range at every pixel.

Elsewhere the code departs from the Methods' words. Its pooling kernel is written [2, 4], which imgaug 0.4.0 reads as a choice between 2 and 4, so it never pools at 3, where the Methods say between 2 x 2 and 4 x 4. Its average blur takes kernels of 4 to 6 against the Methods' 2 to 7; its dropout drops 10 to 15 percent of the pixels, or the same share in coarse blocks, its mask drawn at 10 to 15 percent of the image's size, against 1 to 10 percent of the pixels or a rectangle of 2 to 5 percent of the image; its distortion is an elastic transformation (alpha 0.5 to 3.5, sigma 0.25), not a piecewise affine one; and its box corruption is written but never called. Where the paper gives no range, the code's sharpen (alpha 0.5 to 1, lightness 1 to 1.5), emboss (alpha 0.5 to 1, strength 1 to 2), linear contrast (0.5 to 2, beside a gamma contrast of 0.5 to 2) and sigmoid cutoff (0.4 to 0.6) differ from the imgaug documentation's ranges the strong block takes, and its affine, a turn of up to 5 degrees and a shear of up to 2, is milder than the block's own 15 and 10. And it applies more than two at once: on half the images it inverts, adds pepper or salt noise, changes the contrast and applies the affine, each at even odds, and then one or two of six groups (blur, dropout, sharpen or emboss, pooling, multiply, elastic), each again at even odds.

## What the blocks had, and what they lacked

The four augment blocks before this change (tools/train_blocks.py) are box-jitter (a shift of up to 6 percent and a resize of up to 8 percent), intensity (gamma 0.7 to 1.4, linear contrast 0.8 to 1.2, brightness 0.08), blur (a Gaussian under one pixel) and noise (spread growing with the square root of the signal, up to 0.04). They match SyntheX's regular set in kind and stay deliberately mild, "each a picture the C-arm could give". Of the strong set they had nothing: no inversion, impulse noise, rotation or shear, log or sigmoid contrast, heavy blur, box corruption, dropout, sharpening, embossing, pooling, multiply or piecewise warp, and none of FluoroSAM's Gaussian contrast, random windowing or CLAHE.

The simulation side does not carry over: it shapes images rendered from CT, and SchwingNet's level stage trains on real frames.

## The strong block

The fifth augment block, `strong`, takes the image-level strong set of both papers. Per crop it draws how many of the transforms to apply, none, one or two, and which, every draw from the run's own generator on the CPU so a run reproduces on any device. The two geometric ones, the affine and the piecewise warp, move a crop's level map with it. SyntheX's transform names match the augmenters of the imgaug library, which its code uses though the paper does not name it; where SyntheX names a transform but gives no range, the block takes a range from imgaug's augmenter of that name, the ranges imgaug's documentation shows, which are not all the library's defaults and were not checked against the library itself, and tools/train_blocks.py says which range is the paper's and which is imgaug's. Where the paper gives a range the block keeps to its words, the source the trials cite, and not to the code's departures above: its pooling draws a kernel of 2, 3 or 4 per crop, and its multiply, read with the code's help, multiplies a crop either by one factor or pixel by pixel, both from 50 to 150 percent. Whether the trials should follow the code in place of the Methods is open.

## The caveat the trials carry

Both papers use the strong set to carry training on simulated images to real ones. SchwingNet's trials train and test on real C-arm frames, so the strong block there is a test of robustness, not a reproduction of either paper's setting, and trial arm 2 cites the papers for the transforms, not for an expected gain.
