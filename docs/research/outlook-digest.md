## What Outlook offers

- One-touch buttons inside an email are Outlook actionable messages: an Adaptive Card in a `<script type="application/adaptivecard+json">` in the HTML body's head, whose Action.Http buttons make a GET or POST to an HTTPS endpoint from inside Outlook, with no browser. Action.Submit and Input.Time are not rendered; Input.Text, Action.ShowCard and Action.ToggleVisibility are (adaptive-card.md).
- Where it renders: Outlook on the web for Microsoft 365, the Windows Microsoft 365 apps on every channel, and, with a star, Outlook on Mac, iOS and Android, which take Adaptive Cards of version 1.0 and below only. Outlook on the web in a phone's browser shows no card, nor does Exchange on-premises. An Exchange administrator can turn actionable messages off for the whole organization (Set-OrganizationConfig), and a card does not follow dark mode (index.md). Community reports of late 2025 say the iOS app sometimes shows the card but its buttons do nothing; that is unconfirmed and is the first thing to try on the owner's phone.
- Who may send one: a message to oneself, From and To the same mailbox, renders with no registration and no originator. A message to anyone else needs a provider registered at the Actionable Email Developer Dashboard (https://aka.ms/ActionableMessagesPortal) and its id as the card's originator, or the card is stripped. The scopes are Test Users (named people in the registrant's own organization, self-service and approved automatically), Organization (approved by that organization's Exchange or global administrator) and Global (approved by Microsoft) (email-dev-dashboard.md, get-started.md).
- Proving the sender: an email from a server with DKIM and SPF, or, recommended because it works through any relay, a signed card: the card as a JWS signed with an RSA key whose public half is registered with the provider, carrying originator, sender and iat (security-requirements.md).
- Proving the person: every Action.Http call carries a JWT signed by Microsoft (keys at https://substrate.office.com/sts/common/.well-known/openid-configuration) whose iss is https://substrate.office.com/sts/, whose aud is the endpoint's host, and whose sub and sender name the person who pressed and the mailbox that sent; the endpoint checks all four before it acts. Setting the Authorization header to "" in the action moves the token to Action-Authorization. The endpoint answers 200, or a 4xx with CARD-ACTION-STATUS saying why (never naming the person or the time), and may answer with a new card and CARD-UPDATE-IN-BODY: true to replace the old one (adaptive-card.md). The legacy (EAT) token ended on 2026-06-08.
- Typed text: an Action.Http body or URL takes `{{id.value}}` of an Input.Text, substituted as typed, so a quote in it can break a JSON body.

## The design

Two ways to send, both drawing the same card (tools/outlook_digest.py):

- A, self-sent (the owner's choice, 2026-10-20 15:08 UTC: "I want to use as much Wayne state based onedrive/microsoft 365 native support ecosystem as possible"). The digest goes from the owner's mailbox to itself through Graph, under the sealed refresh token the Folders tab already keeps, with Mail.Send added. No registration and no signing. Costs a second Wayne State consent; and since the reply lands in the owner's own mailbox, a plain reply reaches Claude only with Mail.Read too, so in this route only the card's text box talks to Claude.
- B, from Symphony's own address. The digest goes from an address of Symphony's, such as digest@openspineconsortium.com, as a signed card, under a provider the owner registers at the dashboard with Test Users scope and the owner's own Wayne State address, which Microsoft approves at once with no administrator. Wayne State's consent is untouched. A reply goes to Symphony's address, not the owner's mailbox, so reading it needs no permission of the owner's at all. For other people later, each organization's administrator approves an Organization registration, or Microsoft a Global one.

In B the moving parts are the sign-in service the control panel already has, a Cloudflare Worker, with three new routes and no store:

1. Compose (code, no Claude). A scheduled or hand-started workflow reads the repository's issues with GITHUB_TOKEN and runs `outlook_digest.py compose`: what was done since the last digest (issues closed as completed, pull requests merged) and what waits on the owner (issues labelled decision, and proposed projects, epic and proposed, from drive_organize.py). It posts the message to the Worker's send route, proving it is that repository's workflow with GitHub's OIDC token (id-token: write), so no secret is shared.
2. Send. The Worker signs the card with the provider's private key, its one new secret, and sends it to the one address the repository's settings name. Cloudflare's Email Routing sends only to destination addresses verified once by their owner, which fits a digest to oneself; whether openspineconsortium.com's mail is on Cloudflare is not yet known.
3. Act. A button posts {d, n, c, t} (digest, issue, choice, text) to the Worker's action route. It checks Microsoft's JWT (iss, aud, signature, sub equal to the address the digest went to, sender equal to Symphony's), then sends the answer to the repository as a repository_dispatch through the App's installation token, and answers 200 with CARD-ACTION-STATUS "Recorded". A workflow then runs `outlook_digest.py action` and makes the operations it prints. Inbound, Email Routing hands a reply to an Email Worker that does the same with `outlook_digest.py reply`, checking that the reply's From is the digest's recipient and that it passed DMARC.

What an answer does, as `plan` writes it: Accept on a proposed project runs drive_organize.py accept, the yes that already exists; Decline closes the proposal as not planned, which the organizer reads as no; a choice on a decision comments it on the issue with a marker and takes the label decision off, so a second press from an old digest is refused (a proposed project stays a decision until accept takes proposed off, and accept is safe to run twice); text beside a choice rides in the comment; text on its own, or anything a reply says that is not a line such as "12 accept", opens an issue labelled from-email, which no worker takes until the owner, or a rule the owner sets, queues it, since a prompt that started Claude by itself would be Claude on a schedule.

The next step. When the coordinator integrates a finished epic, and when the drive-organize skill's projects are accepted or the owner says the drive holds none, Claude opens one issue "Next step: ...", labelled decision, a day or two of work, with Accept, Decline and perhaps a smaller option; the digest carries it. Where Claude runs for a from-email prompt is open: the repository's own Claude token in Actions, or the WSU grid's runner chain (docs/memory/the-grid-runs-a-job-through-beethoven.md), which the owner mentioned and which needs no new route, since the dispatch only opens an issue.

## Built in this pull request, sending nothing

- tools/outlook_digest.py: compose writes card.json, digest.html and digest.eml to a folder; action and reply print the operations an answer asks for. The card is version 1.0 with Action.Http and Action.ShowCard only, four decisions at most (more are answered by reply), expectedActors set to the recipient, no "<" inside its script; option labels hold no quote, backslash or brace, so they go into a button's JSON as they are, and the typed text goes last so a quote in it is still read. Replies cut the quoted digest at Outlook's From/Sent block, its underscore line, "Original Message" and "On ... wrote:", read HTML-only replies too, drop the lines mail apps add ("Get Outlook for iOS", "Sent from my iPhone"), take digits in ASCII, at most nine, refuse a second answer to one issue in the same reply and decide nothing from a reply that names no digest. A button's body is read by position, never as JSON, so text typed into the box can never change the issue or the option. Known limits: titles lose Markdown's brackets, stars, underscores and backticks in the card; a line of the person's own that reads like a mail app's quote header ("On Friday Ann wrote:") cuts what follows it; whether Outlook escapes the typed text in a body is to be seen on the first real card, and the reader keeps it as it arrives.
- tests/test_outlook_digest.py: every button's body is read back and planned, a stale digest and another actor are refused, the three quote styles and an HTML reply read the same.
- The coordinator's integrate prompt and the drive-organize skill propose the next step as a decision issue.
