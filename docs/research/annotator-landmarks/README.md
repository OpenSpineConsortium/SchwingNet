# Annotator landmarks: the points readers mark on spine and pelvis X-rays, and which of them this project holds

Source: the owner's local Claude Code session of 1 November 2026, on his words that night: "the bare minimum my landmark net should also be able to place landmarks annotators have found useful such as the ones deepfluoro placed, the corners a ton of xray groups have placed, etc; it should somehow find where those points correspond to the 3d ct scans and propagate them through and use them if possible for bayesian measurements, and even better if they correlate with known anatomical papers like panjabi", and then, of Radiopaedia, "include that as a source for data; either paired ct-xray or just xray". Every citation below was checked that night on the web (the journal's page, PubMed Central or the dataset's own page) or in a file the grid holds, and says where; every count was read on the grid that night, by listing the files or by the read-only CPU job 40940203 (scripts in the job's folder /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/qadiag/annotator-registry, its log annreg_40940203.out under hpcworker's logs), which printed structure and counts only. No film, no pixel and no person's identifier leaves the grid in this note. Confidential with the rest of this repository. The same registry, one entry per scheme, is [schemes.json](schemes.json).

## Why a registry, and the one distinction that matters

The landmark network names its points after the morphometry engine's constructions in the CT, projected through the pose, never after a corner fitted to the film (the owner's rule of 29 October). A point a reader marked on a film is therefore useful in two ways only: as the definition the projected construction must agree with on real films, and as an observation the Bayes rule can weigh beside the engine's own measure. Both need, for each point a reader marks, the 3-D construction it corresponds to. This note lists the schemes readers use, what each point is in the authors' words, the view it is read in, the datasets that carry it and whether the grid holds them, and the point's counterpart in 3-D: one of the 18 points of tools/qaloop_gates.py LANDMARKS, a construction of the vendored engine (grid/bordercall/morphometry, pinned by grid/bordercall/VENDORED), or a new construction.

Every point is one of two kinds, and the kind decides whether a fixed 3-D point can stand for it.

- A fixed anatomical point is one place on the bone, the same whatever the view: a femoral head's centre, the middle of the S1 plate, the top of the pubic symphysis, any point DeepFluoro's annotators placed in the CT. One 3-D point projected through the pose is its counterpart in every view.
- A silhouette point is where the bone's outline turns in the view the reader read: the lateral end of an end-plate on an anteroposterior film, the anterior corner of a body on a lateral one. The outline is made by the rays that graze the bone's surface, so as the C-arm turns about the column the corner slides along the plate's rim. A fixed 3-D point stands for it exactly only in the view it was chosen for, and less well as the view turns, the more so the rounder the rim. On a true lateral the corners at the midline are near fixed points, since the anterior and posterior ends of a plate are the midline's ends; on a true anteroposterior film the plate's lateral ends are the ends of its widest chord. The exact counterpart of a silhouette point is the outline of the projected mask, taken where the reader takes it; the engine draws no outline, so every silhouette scheme below needs that one new construction to be read exactly, and until then a fixed point read within a stated angle of the view.

The 18 LANDMARKS as the oracle places them (grid/autoanatomist/autoanatomist/oracle.py, body_traces): the four plate chords (endplate, endplate_lower, endplate_depth, endplate_lower_depth) end on the surface of the body's top or bottom slab, the uppermost or lowest 12 per cent of the body's central height and 1 to 3 mm thick (scanimport/geometry.py, ENDPLATE_SHARE and ENDPLATE_MM), at the slab's median height, so each lies about 0.5 to 1.5 mm inside the plate; the lateral ends (right, left) at the slab's median depth, the depth ends (posterior, anterior) on the slab's middle across. They are fixed points in the vertebra's frame chosen for the two views: right and left for the anteroposterior reader's corners, posterior and anterior for the lateral reader's. h_post's ends are the posterior fifth's median column height about its middle; the canal's four points lie on the canal's walls at its median slab; tp_span's ends are the vertebra's extent across; spinous_length runs from the canal's posterior wall to the spinous tip.

The engine constructions named below: body.plate_rims (the anterior and posterior bands on the midline 2.5 mm inside each plate's corners), body.rim_plane (a plate's plane from rim to rim, as a reader draws the plate's line from corner to corner), body.heights (the anterior, middle and posterior heights read in those bands), column.plate_centre (a point on the plate at the middle of the body's central part), column.plate_normal and column.coronal_cobb (the plates' normals and the coronal Cobb angle between them), column.wall_corner (where the posterior wall meets the upper or lower plate on the midline: the posterosuperior and posteroinferior corners), column.slip (the slip of one body on the next), pelvis.s1_plate (the S1 plate's centre and normal), pelvis.socket_centre (the centre of a sphere fitted to the acetabulum's surface on the hip bone's mask), pelvis.incidence (pelvic incidence, sacral slope and pelvic tilt from the S1 plate and the two socket centres), pelvis.crest_top and pelvis.crests (the iliac crests' tops and the intercristal level), and the pedicle module's isthmus (the narrowest section of each pedicle and its axis).

## The schemes

### DeepFluoro's fourteen pelvic landmarks (Grupp 2020)

Grupp RB, Unberath M, Gao C, et al. Automatic annotation of hip anatomy in fluoroscopy for robust and efficient 2D/3D registration. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg 2020;15(5):759-769, doi:10.1007/s11548-020-02162-7 (read on PubMed Central, PMC7263976). In the authors' words, fourteen landmarks, all bilateral: the centres of the femoral head (FH), the greater sciatic notches (GSN), the inferior obturator foramen (IOF), the medial obturator foramen (MOF), the superior pubis symphysis (SPS), the inferior pubis symphysis (IPS) and the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS). They were "manually annotated in 3D" in each specimen's CT, resampled to 1 mm, and each frame's 2-D landmarks are those points projected through the frame's registered pose, a point projecting off the detector counting as not visible; the 2-D labels were inspected by hand. Six segmented structures go with them: the two hemipelves, the two femurs, the vertebrae and the upper sacrum.

- View: any C-arm pose; the frames the QA loop's arm reads all lie within 15 degrees of the anteroposterior view, none of the lateral (docs/research/qa-loop-external/README.md).
- Kind: fixed, every one, since a person placed each in 3-D; their 2-D images are outline features (the notch, the foramen's rim, the spine's tip) but the release defines them by the 3-D point.
- Held: the release file /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/deepfluoro_h5/raw/deepfluoro/unpacked_full/ipcai_2020_full_res_data.h5 (HDF5; per specimen a group holding `vol-landmarks/<name>`, a 3-vector in the volume's world frame, LPS, and `projections/<frame>/gt-landmarks/<name>`, a 2-vector in pixels, with gt-seg and the pose), and per-specimen working copies under /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/realxray_work/deepfluoro/. Job 40940203 read the five specimens the arm may read (17-1882, 17-1905, 18-0725, 18-2799, 18-2800; the held-out 18-1109 was not opened): 14 3-D landmarks each, the same fourteen names, 70 3-D points, and 262 frames listing 3,668 2-D entries, every frame listing all fourteen whether or not the point falls on the detector. The record counts 366 frames over six specimens (data/sources/deepfluoro.json).
- Counterparts: FH, pelvis.socket_centre on the hip bone (the acetabulum's sphere, concentric with the head in a normal hip; a sphere fitted to the femoral head's own mask, ts_femur here, would be the direct one). GSN, IOF, MOF, SPS, IPS and ASIS: new constructions on the hip bone's mask (the notch's deepest point on the ilium's posterior border; the obturator foramen's lowest and most medial rim points; the symphysis's upper and lower ends at the midline; the most anterior point of the anterior superior spine). pelvis.crest_top is the crest's highest point, not ASIS. DeepFluoro is the one held set where a person's point is in the CT itself, so it tests a 3-D construction against a person directly, before any projection.

### Four corners per vertebra on anteroposterior whole-spine films: AASCE and BoostNet (Wu 2017)

Wu H, Bailey C, Rasoulinejad P, Li S. Automatic landmark estimation for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis assessment using BoostNet. MICCAI 2017, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 10435, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-66182-7_15; the AASCE 2019 challenge (aasce19.github.io) uses the same data, "spinal anterior-posterior x-ray images", released as SpineWeb dataset 16. The landmarks are the four corners of each of 17 vertebral bodies, twelve thoracic and five lumbar, 68 points a film, as the later papers that use the set describe it; the release itself confirms the count.

- View: anteroposterior.
- Format, read from the release: one MATLAB file per film whose one variable p2 is a 68 by 2 array of pixel coordinates (uint16), the vertebrae from the top down and each vertebra's four points in the order upper left, upper right, lower left, lower right as the film is seen (job 40940203 read the first film of each part: the seventeen fours' mean heights run down the film, and the first four points relative to their mean lie up and left, up and right, down and left, down and right); landmarks.csv holds the same 68 points a row as 136 numbers scaled to the image's size, and angles.csv three Cobb angles a film.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/thoracic_xr/mirror_zlinzju_ke30_u7_AASCE2019/unpacked/ke30_u7_AASCE2019-master/boostnet_labeldata, 481 training and 128 test films with their 481 and 128 files (job 40940203), so 609 films and 41,412 points; the challenge's 98 test films carry no landmarks (data/sources/aasce.json). The older request-form copy /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/aasce2019_boostnet/raw/aasce holds the 481 training films and 99 entries in its test folder.
- Kind: silhouette, all four.
- Counterparts: upper left and right, the qaloop endplate.right and endplate.left (which is which depends on the film's facing, as an anteroposterior film shows the patient's right on the viewer's left); lower, endplate_lower.right and endplate_lower.left.

### Four corners per vertebra on generated anteroposterior films: Spinal-AI2024 (Shao 2025)

Shao Z, Yuan Y, Ma L, et al. CurvNet: latent contour representation and iterative data engine for curvature angle estimation. Pattern Recognition 2025, doi:10.1016/j.patcog.2025.112546 (arXiv 2411.12604; the release's README). The set is 20,000 scoliosis X-rays generated by the paper's latent diffusion data engine from a private dataset, so no patient was imaged and no reader marked these points.

- View: anteroposterior.
- Format: COCO JSON, one annotation per vertebra whose segmentation is one quadrilateral of four points (eight numbers) and whose category is "text" (the release borrowed a text-detection format; each annotation carries a random transcription string), and two text files of three Cobb angles per image.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/spinal_ai2024/raw/spinal_ai2024/repo, 16,000 training and 4,000 test images at 512 by 512 with 273,450 and 68,815 quadrilaterals (grep and job 40940203), 342,265 vertebrae and 1,369,060 points; every segmentation is eight numbers, and most images carry 16 to 18 vertebrae (18 images carry one).
- Kind and counterparts: as AASCE. Being generated, it can train a corner reader's shape prior but not test one.

### End-plate lines, anteroposterior and lateral: BUU-LSPINE (Klinwichit 2023)

Klinwichit P, Yookwan W, Limchareon S, Chinnasarn K, Jang JS, Onuean A. BUU-LSPINE: a Thai open lumbar spine dataset for spondylolisthesis detection. Appl Sci 2023;13(15):8646, doi:10.3390/app13158646; the dataset's page (services.informatics.buu.ac.th/spine) says each image is annotated with "the upper and lower edges" of the lumbar vertebrae, as lines whose points the CSV files hold, with the diagnoses marked on the lower edge lines; the abstract reports a corner-point extraction experiment. The full text was refused to the fetch (403), so the column layout below is the release's own.

- View: both, an anteroposterior and a lateral film for each of 3,600 people.
- Format, read from the release: one CSV per film, one row per end-plate line, x1, y1, x2, y2 and a code: on an anteroposterior film ten rows, the upper and lower plates of L1 to L5; on a lateral eleven, the same and the top of S1. The line's two ends are the plate's two corners. The code is 0 on most lines; the release documents no meaning for the others.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/buu_lspine_3600/raw/buu_full/BUU-LSPINEv1: AP 3,600 films and 3,600 CSV, every one of ten lines, 36,000 lines and 72,000 points; LA 3,604 films and 3,606 CSV, every one of eleven lines, 39,666 lines and 79,332 points. The codes counted over the lines: AP 0 (35,870), 1 (61), 2 (69); LA 0 (38,862), 3 (465), 4 (194), 20 (15), 21 (130). Codes 1 to 4 may be the four disorders the page lists (left and right laterolisthesis on the anteroposterior film, antero- and retrolisthesis on the lateral); 20 and 21 lie beside the release sheet's 15 six-lumbar and 129 four-lumbar spines that spine-program's literature state records (02_transitional_naming_truth, topic1_bibliography.md row 12), so they may flag transitional spines; neither reading is confirmed. The 2,000-pair release (/rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/buu_lspine_2000/raw/BUU-LSPINE, AP and LA) is the same films, every name in the 3,600 release (spine-program's DATA_MANIFEST.md), with other codes in column 5 (posture: side bending and rotation on the anteroposterior film, flexion and extension on the lateral, as the page says): 2,000 AP CSV of ten lines and 2,000 LA CSV of eleven (job 40940203), 42,000 lines and 84,000 points that repeat the v1 geometry (the first file listed holds the same coordinates as its v1 file), with codes AP 0, 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 and LA 0, 3, 4, 9, 10, 20, 21.
- Kind: silhouette on the anteroposterior film; on the lateral, near fixed midline points in a true lateral.
- Counterparts: anteroposterior, endplate.right and endplate.left, endplate_lower.right and endplate_lower.left; lateral, endplate_depth.posterior and endplate_depth.anterior, endplate_lower_depth.posterior and endplate_lower_depth.anterior, the posterior ends also column.wall_corner, and the line itself body.rim_plane; the top of S1's line, pelvis.s1_plate for its middle and slope and a new construction for its two ends (the S1 plate's anterior end, the promontory, and its posterior end, the posterosuperior corner of S1). spine-program's converter reads the laterals as facing left, so a lateral line's first point is its anterior end.

### Four corners per vertebra on lateral lumbar films: Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 (Reyes 2025)

Reyes K. Spondylolisthesis vertebral landmark. Mendeley Data, version 1, 20 June 2025, doi:10.17632/5jdfdgp762.1, CC BY 4.0 (the page): 716 sagittal lumbar films, 208 from a Honduran set and 508 from BUU-LSPINE, with a box and four corner keypoints for each vertebra from L3 to S1, in JSON for PyTorch's Keypoint R-CNN; the page does not state the keypoints' order.

- View: lateral.
- Format, read from the release: 484 films labelled in JSON (boxes, keypoints four a vertebra as x, y and a visibility flag, and labels) and 214 in text files of the YOLO pose layout (seventeen numbers a vertebra: the class, the box and four keypoints with their flags), 698 labelled films in train and val folders, as the page's 698, and 16 unlabelled test films (job 40940203).
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/mendeley_5jdfdgp762/raw/mendeley_716/extracted/Dataset (714 films). The JSON files hold 13,000 keypoints over 3 to 11 vertebrae a film, most 6 to 8, and the text files 1,350 vertebrae, 5,400 keypoints, so 18,400 points; more vertebrae than L3 to S1 are marked on most films, against the page. About 500 of its people are BUU-LSPINE's (data/sources/mendeley_5jdfdgp762.json), so it adds a second reading of those films, not new people.
- Kind and counterparts: the lateral corners, as BUU-LSPINE's lateral.

### Corners and the C2 centre on lateral cervical films: CSXA (Ran 2024)

Ran Y, Qin W, Qin C, et al. A high-quality dataset featuring classified and annotated cervical spine X-ray atlas. Sci Data 2024;11:625, doi:10.1038/s41597-024-03383-0 (read on PubMed Central, PMC11176335; the grid holds the PDF beside the data). In the authors' words, 23 keypoints a film: "the four corner points of the third to seventh cervical vertebral body (C3 to C7)", where "the corner points are the four intersections which formed by the upper and lower endplates" with the body's anterior and posterior edges, and "the central point and the two corner points of lower endplate of C2"; the upper plate of C2 and C1 were not marked. Four spine surgeons annotated, cross-checked three times; 4,963 films from Dongzhimen Hospital. The C2 centre is there for the cervical sagittal measures the paper derives (its algorithm turns the keypoints into its quantitative parameters).

- View: lateral (the paper says only cervical spine X-ray; the record data/sources/csxa.json says lateral, and the points, each plate's front and back corners, are a lateral reader's).
- Format, read from the release: labelme JSON (version 5.2.1) with the image embedded, one shape per point labelled `C<n> top left`, `C<n> top right`, `C<n> bottom left`, `C<n> bottom right`, "C2 bottom left", "C2 bottom right" and "C2 centroid", where left and right are the image's sides (anterior or posterior by the film's facing).
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/csxa_cervical_xray_atlas/raw/csxa (images, annotations/datasets-JSON, the paper and the authors' code): 4,963 JSON files, 4,962 of 23 points and one of 24 (a second C3 top right), 114,150 points, every label present on every film (job 40940203). Most are shapes of type point; 2,131 are one-point polygons.
- Kind: the corners near fixed midline points in a true lateral; the C2 centre fixed.
- Counterparts: endplate_depth.anterior and endplate_depth.posterior, endplate_lower_depth.anterior and endplate_lower_depth.posterior, the posterior corners also column.wall_corner; C2's lower plate the same at C2; the C2 centre a new construction, the centroid of the body anatomy.build cuts (the engine computes the body but names no centroid point). C2's and C7's lower plates are the two plates of the C2 to C7 angle, which the engine reads as lordosis_C2_C7 (column.regional).

### Corner tracks on lateral cervical fluoroscopy: Bournemouth quantitative fluoroscopy (Branney, Breen, Breen 2024)

Branney J, Breen AC, Breen A. Cervical spine kinematics database from fluoroscopic imaging. Bournemouth University data repository, record 385, doi:10.18746/bmth.data.00000385 (the record's page): kinematic measures from fluoroscopic sequences of the neck in controlled flexion and extension on a motion frame, vertebral body positions, angles, translation and disc height by the methods of Frobin and colleagues, with each participant's age, sex and dose. The deposit holds the tracks and no pixels; the sequences are asked for (data/sources/bournemouth_cervical_qf.json).

- View: lateral, fluoroscopic, every frame of 252 sequences of 63 people.
- Format: one MATLAB file whose one structure, Database, has 126 image events by two columns (job 40940203: fields ImageEvent, Sex, Age, PatientOrControl, Templates, MachineAngle, Dose and orientation, the two-column fields 126 by 2), 252 sequences as the record says; the first sequence's Templates is a 9 by 4 cell array, read here as nine levels (C0 to T1 is nine) by four corners, whose cells hold the tracks (their contents were not read). The machine angle runs frame by frame (318 values in the first).
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/realxray/bournemouth_qf/raw/Cervical_Database.mat (24 MB) with its README.pdf.
- Kind and counterparts: the lateral corners as CSXA's, followed through motion; without the pixels they test only a shape prior.

### Landmarks and reference bodies on NHANES II laterals (Mo, Papiez, Chen; VertXNet 2024)

Chen Y, Mo Y, Readie A, Ligozio G, Mandal I, Jabbar F, Coroller T, Papiez BW. VertXNet: an ensemble method for vertebral body segmentation and identification from cervical and lumbar spinal X-rays. Sci Rep 2024;14:3341, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-49923-3 (read on PubMed Central, PMC10858234), with its data on Zenodo, record 10223910, "NHANES II Spinal X-ray with Extra Labels For Reference Vertebral Bodies (C2, S1)", version 3 of 30 November 2023, CC BY 4.0 (the record's page). The paper says NHANES II "provided landmark annotation for vertebral bodies in 544 images", of which "only C3-C7 and L1-L5 were provided with complete landmark coordinates to generate masks", and that two radiologists added the reference bodies C2 and S1 where visible, 445 of the 544; for its own other dataset radiologists "assigned 8 landmarks (same as the annotation process for NHANES II)" a body. Two other readings of the same films exist and are not held: the National Library of Medicine's nine morphometric points on 250 vertebra contours (Xu and colleagues, 2003, a Lister Hill Center publication), and Hipp JA, Grieco TF, Newman P, Reitman CA, Definition of normal vertebral morphometry using NHANES-II radiographs, JBMR Plus 2022;6(10):e10677, doi:10.1002/jbm4.10677, whose four corners per body, L1 to S1 and C2 to C7, on 7,364 lumbar and 9,662 cervical films, were placed by a series of neural networks, not by people.

- View: lateral, cervical and lumbar.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/nhanes2_zenodo_landmarks/raw/nhanes_annotated/NHANESII_Annotations_VertebralOsetophytes.zip, the Zenodo file (1,017,496,285 bytes), holding 543 pickle files, 275 cervical and 268 lumbar; the 17,073 films themselves are under /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/nhanes2_cdc_films/raw/nhanes_full (docs/research/data-manifest/MANIFEST.md). Job 40940203 read one cervical and one lumbar file through an unpickler that admits only numpy's arrays: each is a dictionary of a sample id, the film's own pixels (uint8, 1755 by 1462 and 2487 by 2048) and a dictionary of binary masks the film's size, one per named body and one of all of them (cervical C2, C2_lower and C3 to C7; lumbar T12, L1 to L5 and S1), some of them empty (that cervical file's C7 and C2_lower). So the two files read hold the masks the landmarks were drawn into, with each body's level, and no landmark coordinates, and every one of the 543 names ends in masks_4.pkl; the eight points a body the paper speaks of were not found on the grid.
- Kind: the masks' outlines are silhouettes, the body's outline as the reader drew it.
- Counterparts: each mask's level, the level name the engine reads; its outline, the outline of the projected body's mask, new. A corner taken back from a mask would be a corner fitted in 2-D, which the rule of 29 October keeps out of training, so the masks serve as a check of the projected outline and of the names, not as corners.

### Six-point quantitative morphometry and Genant's semiquantitative grade (Black 1991, McCloskey 1993, Genant 1993; SOF and MrOS)

Black DM, Cummings SR, Stone K, Hudes E, Palermo L, Steiger P. A new approach to defining normal vertebral dimensions. J Bone Miner Res 1991;6(8):883-892 (the normal means and standard deviations of vertebral height ratios from 2,992 women of the Study of Osteoporotic Fractures, as its abstract says). McCloskey EV, Spector TD, Eyres KS, et al. Assessment of vertebral deformity: validation of a new method with high specificity. Osteoporos Int 1993;3(3):138-147 (the citation as the papers citing it give it; the full text was not read), whose six points a vertebra the European Vertebral Osteoporosis Study placed. Genant HK, Wu CY, van Kuijk C, Nevitt MC. Vertebral fracture assessment using a semiquantitative technique. J Bone Miner Res 1993;8(9):1137-1148, PMID 8237484. The Study of Osteoporotic Fractures' morphometry form says "six points were placed on each vertebral body to define 3 vertebral heights: Ha (anterior height), Hm (mid height), and Hp (posterior height)", L4 up to T4 in the main cohort, and that the normal means and standard deviations come from Black's method (SOF visit forms V3, V7 and V8, Morphometry General, at the NIA Aging Research Biobank). The six points are the four corners and the middle of the upper and the lower plate. Genant's grade is read by eye: from 0 to 3 by the loss of anterior, middle or posterior height, about 20 to 25, 25 to 40 and over 40 per cent, with the body's shape (wedge, biconcave, crush). MrOS read the same six points with semi-automated software, an operator placing a point near each body's centre and correcting the outline, which records 6-point and 95-point morphometry (PMC4152008).

- View: lateral thoracic and lumbar films.
- Held: no. SOF (about 9,700 women) and MrOS (5,994 men) are asked for; their registrations are closed and the films are moving to the NIA Aging Research Biobank (data/sources/sof_lateral_spine.json, mros_lateral_spine.json).
- Kind: the four corners as the lateral corners above; the plate middles fixed (a plate's centre does not slide with the view as its ends do).
- Counterparts: the anterior corners endplate_depth.anterior and endplate_lower_depth.anterior; the posterior corners column.wall_corner (top and bottom), close to h_post.superior and h_post.inferior; the plate middles column.plate_centre (top and bottom). The engine's body.heights reads Ha, Hm and Hp (h_ant, h_mid, h_post_inset) in bands 2.5 mm inside the corners, not at them, so its heights are a reader's six-point heights taken a little inward; the ratios Genant and Black read are what the Bayes rule would weigh.

### The spinopelvic points (Legaye 1998) and the sagittal vertical axis

Legaye J, Duval-Beaupere G, Hecquet J, Marty C. Pelvic incidence: a fundamental pelvic parameter for three-dimensional regulation of spinal sagittal curves. Eur Spine J 1998;7(2):99-103, PMID 9629932 (its abstract; the definition as the later papers of the same group give it): pelvic incidence is the angle between the line perpendicular to the S1 plate at its midpoint and the line from that midpoint to the femoral heads' axis, the middle of the line joining the two heads' centres; sacral slope is the S1 plate's angle to the horizontal, pelvic tilt the angle to the vertical of the line from the hip axis to the plate's midpoint, and incidence is their sum. The reader marks the S1 plate's two ends (its midpoint follows) and the two femoral heads' centres. The sagittal vertical axis is the horizontal distance from the plumb line dropped from the centre of the C7 body to the posterosuperior corner of S1 on a standing lateral, the global modifier of the SRS-Schwab classification (Schwab F, Ungar B, Blondel B, et al. Scoliosis Research Society-Schwab adult spinal deformity classification: a validation study. Spine 2012;37(12):1077-1082, doi:10.1097/BRS.0b013e31823e15e2), whose goal is under 4 cm.

- View: lateral, standing for the published normals; a supine CT gives the supine values only (the engine names them sacral_slope_supine and pelvic_tilt_supine; incidence does not change with posture).
- Held: no film set on the grid carries these points as a reader marked them; the full-length lateral films with a reader's spinopelvic points that the literature uses (EOS sets among them) are not held. BUU-LSPINE's lateral line on the top of S1 gives the S1 plate's two ends on its 3,604 lumbar laterals, without the femoral heads.
- Kind: the S1 plate's ends near fixed midline points in a true lateral; the plate's midpoint, the femoral head centres and the C7 centre fixed.
- Counterparts: the S1 plate's middle and slope, pelvis.s1_plate; its two ends, new (the plate's anterior end, the promontory, and its posterior end, which is also the sagittal vertical axis's corner); the femoral heads, pelvis.socket_centre; the angles, pelvis.incidence; the C7 centre, the centroid of anatomy.build's C7 body, new as a point. tools/qaloop_samples.py's sacral_points is not yet the promontory: its promontory sat a median 4 mm in front of L5's lower plate centre and behind it in 20 of 63 COBRA cases (docs/research/qa-loop-external/README.md), where the anterior end of S1 is wanted.

### The Cobb end-plate lines (Cobb 1948)

Cobb JR. Outline for the study of scoliosis. American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons Instructional Course Lectures, vol. 5. Ann Arbor: J. W. Edwards; 1948:261-275 (the citation as the secondary sources give it; the text was not read). The angle is read between a line along the superior plate of the upper end vertebra and one along the inferior plate of the lower end vertebra, the end vertebrae being the most tilted at the curve's ends.

- View: anteroposterior for scoliosis; the same lines on a lateral give the regional lordosis and kyphosis.
- Held: AASCE's angles.csv (609 films, three angles each) and Spinal-AI2024's two text files (20,000 images) carry the angles; every corner set above carries the lines, a plate's line running through its two corners.
- Kind: the lines' ends are silhouette points; the line's direction on an anteroposterior film is the plate's coronal tilt, which a small turn of the view changes little.
- Counterparts: the line, body.rim_plane and column.plate_normal; the angle, column.coronal_cobb (coronal) and column.regional (sagittal: lordosis_L1_S1, kyphosis_T4_T12 and lordosis_C2_C7 between fixed plates); its ends, the endplate and endplate_lower ends of the qaloop LANDMARKS.

### Rotation from the pedicles: Nash and Moe (1969), Perdriolle (1985)

Nash CL, Moe JH. A study of vertebral rotation. J Bone Joint Surg Am 1969;51-A:223-229 (its record and the papers citing it; the text was not read): rotation graded from the position of the convex-side pedicle's shadow within the body's width on an anteroposterior film, as the pedicle moves toward the middle. Perdriolle R, Vidal J. Thoracic idiopathic scoliosis curve evolution and prognosis. Spine 1985;10:785-791 (the papers citing it give the volume as 10 or as 9; the text was not read): the torsiometer, a template laid over the body's lateral margins that reads the angle from the convex pedicle's position. The points are the pedicle oval's centre and the body's two lateral margins at the pedicle's height.

- View: anteroposterior.
- Held: no held set marks the pedicles.
- Kind: silhouette, all: the pedicle oval is the end-on outline of its cortical tube, and the body's margins are its outline's sides.
- Counterparts: new constructions. The engine's pedicle module finds each pedicle's isthmus and axis; the oval's centre in a near-anteroposterior view is where that axis projects, and the body's margins at that height are the body's outline at the pedicle's level. Neither is exported as a point.

### The slip of L5 on S1 (Meyerding 1932)

Meyerding HW. Spondyloptosis. Surg Gynecol Obstet 1932;54:371-377 (the citation as Radiopaedia gives it; the text was not read): the slip is the distance from the line of S1's posterior wall to L5's posteroinferior corner, as a share of the S1 plate's depth, graded I to IV by quarters and V beyond.

- View: lateral.
- Held: BUU-LSPINE's lateral lines and the Mendeley set's corners carry L5's lower plate and the top of S1 on lumbar laterals, so the slip can be read from them; the release's own diagnosis codes are on the lower lines.
- Kind: L5's posteroinferior corner near fixed in a true lateral; the S1 wall line silhouette.
- Counterparts: L5's corner, column.wall_corner (L5, lower), or h_post.inferior; the slip between two lumbar bodies, column.slip; S1's posterior wall and plate depth, new (S1 is no vertebra in the engine's reading: pelvis.s1_plate gives its plate's centre and normal only).

## How a reader's point meets the CT

Of the held sets only DeepFluoro has a reader's point in a CT; every other is a film with no CT of the same person. So a reader's point reaches the CT the other way round: the engine builds the construction this registry names in a CT, the pose projects it, and the network learns to place that projected point; on a held film, the reader's point is what the network's point is checked against, by the same name, against the readers' own agreement where a set or its paper records one (none of the held corner sets ships two readers' points). A fixed point can be checked on any view; a silhouette point only near the view the reader read, until the outline construction exists. DeepFluoro alone checks a construction against a person before any projection: its femoral head centres against pelvis.socket_centre in the same CT, its other twelve against the constructions still to be built.

For the Bayes rule the readers' points give measures, and the useful ones need no scale: most of these films carry no pixel spacing (CSXA's paper gives each image a pixel equivalent; AASCE, BUU-LSPINE and the Mendeley set give none in their files), but an angle (Cobb, pelvic incidence, a plate's wedge) and a ratio (Genant's and Black's height ratios, Meyerding's slip share) read the same at any magnification. Where a pair of a reader's points is one of Panjabi's chords (the lateral corners give the end-plate depths EPDu and EPDl, the anteroposterior corners the widths EPWu and EPWl, the posterior corners the posterior height VBHp), the chord can be set beside Panjabi and beside the engine's own reading on the gold tier (docs/research/morphometry-against-panjabi.md) once the film's scale is known.
