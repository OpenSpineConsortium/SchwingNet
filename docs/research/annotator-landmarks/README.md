## Why a registry, and the one distinction that matters

The landmark network names its points after the morphometry engine's constructions in the CT, projected through the pose, never after a corner fitted to the film (the owner's rule of 29 October). A point a reader marked on a film is therefore useful in two ways only: as the definition the projected construction must agree with on real films, and as an observation the Bayes rule can weigh beside the engine's own measure. Both need, for each point a reader marks, the 3-D construction it corresponds to. This note lists the schemes readers use, what each point is in the authors' words, the view it is read in, the datasets that carry it and whether the grid holds them, and the point's counterpart in 3-D: one of the 18 points of tools/qaloop_gates.py LANDMARKS, a construction of the vendored engine (grid/bordercall/morphometry, pinned by grid/bordercall/VENDORED), or a new construction.

Every point is one of two kinds, and the kind decides whether a fixed 3-D point can stand for it.

- A fixed anatomical point is one place on the bone, the same whatever the view: a femoral head's centre, the middle of the S1 plate, the top of the pubic symphysis, any point DeepFluoro's annotators placed in the CT. One 3-D point projected through the pose is its counterpart in every view.
- A silhouette point is where the bone's outline turns in the view the reader read: the lateral end of an end-plate on an anteroposterior film, the anterior corner of a body on a lateral one. The outline is made by the rays that graze the bone's surface, so as the C-arm turns about the column the corner slides along the plate's rim. A fixed 3-D point stands for it exactly only in the view it was chosen for, and less well as the view turns, the more so the rounder the rim. On a true lateral the anterior corners are near the plate's front ends on the midline, the convex front's furthest points. The posterior corners are less simple: the reader marks where the plate's line meets the posterior wall's line at the plate's level, and in the lumbar spine the body's posterior face is concave across and notched at the midline (the engine's own column.wall_corner docstring records a basivertebral notch 5 mm wide, 6 tall and 4 deep at mid-height), so the outline's posterior corner may lie behind the midline's; the engine's midline corner (column.wall_corner) is the counterpart taken here, and how far the outline's lies from it is unmeasured. On a true anteroposterior film the plate's lateral ends are the ends of its widest chord. The exact counterpart of a silhouette point is the outline of the projected mask, taken where the reader takes it; the engine draws no outline, so every silhouette scheme below needs that one new construction to be read exactly, and until then a fixed point read within a stated angle of the view.

The 18 LANDMARKS as the oracle places them (grid/autoanatomist/autoanatomist/oracle.py, body_traces). The plate chords' slabs are the uppermost or lowest 12 per cent of the body's central height, 1 to 3 mm thick (scanimport/geometry.py, ENDPLATE_SHARE and ENDPLATE_MM), taken over the body's central part, and every chord's ends sit at the slab's median height. endplate and endplate_lower end on the slab's surface across, right and left, at the slab's median depth: the anteroposterior reader's corners. endplate_depth and endplate_lower_depth run on the slab's middle across from the slab's anterior surface to a posterior end that is not read on the slab: it is the canal's anterior wall on the midline, the median over the slabs in the middle half of the body's height (scanimport/geometry.py canal_chords), carried to the slab's height, and the body is cut at that line (anatomy.py: "the largest connected part in front of the canal's anterior wall"), so nothing of the plate behind it is read. h_post's two ends lie at the mean depth and across of the body's posterior fifth (POSTERIOR_SHARE 0.20) within the middle half across, half the posterior column's height above and below its middle: inside the body, not at its corners. The canal's four points lie on the canal's walls at its median slab; tp_span's ends are the vertebra's extent across; spinous_length runs from the canal's posterior wall to the spinous tip.

So along the depth, the line a lateral film shows, the qaloop depth chords' ends lie a median 0.3 mm or less from the engine's corners in the thoracic spine and at the lumbar anterior ends, and the lumbar posterior ends a median 1.0 to 1.2 mm in front of the corner (a quarter of the upper and a third of the lower more than 2 mm), while along the plate's normal they lie 3 to 4 mm inside the body, since they sit at the central slab's median height and the corner at the plate's rim. In the neck the two disagree across as well (the anterior ends a median 8.9 and 16.8 mm apart across), which says one of the two readings of the cervical body takes in more than the body (docs/research/morphometry-against-panjabi.md found scanimport's cervical plate taking in the uncinate processes and the costal roots); which one was not looked at. h_post's ends are not corners: a median 3.4 mm in front of the posterior corner, 4.5 to 4.7 mm at the lumbar plates, whose depth between the corners is a median 34.9 mm (thoracic 28.7, cervical 18.3). The DeepFluoro specimens give only 20 lumbar plates and are counted in the "all" rows.

The engine constructions named below: body.plate_rims (the anterior and posterior bands on the midline 2.5 mm inside each plate's corners), body.rim_plane (a plate's plane whose slope front to back runs from rim to rim, through the plate's surface in those two bands, as a lateral reader draws the plate's line from corner to corner, and whose slope across is that of a plane through the extremes of the plate's central half, not the line through its two lateral corners), body.heights (the anterior, middle and posterior heights read in those bands), column.plate_centre (a point on the plate at the middle of the body's central part), column.plate_normal and column.coronal_cobb (the plates' normals, their slope across taken from rim_plane's central plane, and the coronal Cobb angle between them), column.wall_corner (where the posterior wall, read on all of the vertebra's voxels in front of the canal's middle within WALL_END_MM, 4 mm, of the plate, meets the plate on the midline: the posterosuperior and posteroinferior corners), column.slip (the slip of one body on the next, between those corners), pelvis.s1_plate (the S1 plate's plane fitted to the sacrum's top under the lowest lumbar body, within S1_FOOT of that body's width across and between that body's posterior wall and front in depth, and its centre, the mean of the plate's points on that plane, so that the centre follows the lowest lumbar body), pelvis.socket_centre (the centre of a sphere fitted to the acetabulum's surface on the hip bone's mask), pelvis.incidence (pelvic incidence, sacral slope and pelvic tilt from the S1 plate and the two socket centres), pelvis.crest_top and pelvis.crests (the iliac crests' tops and the intercristal level), and the pedicle module's isthmus (the narrowest section of each pedicle and its axis).

## The schemes

### DeepFluoro's fourteen pelvic landmarks (Grupp 2020)

Grupp RB, Unberath M, Gao C, et al. Automatic annotation of hip anatomy in fluoroscopy for robust and efficient 2D/3D registration. Int J Comput Assist Radiol Surg 2020;15(5):759-769, doi:10.1007/s11548-020-02162-7 (read on PubMed Central, PMC7263976). In the authors' words, fourteen landmarks, all bilateral: the centres of the femoral head (FH), the greater sciatic notches (GSN), the inferior obturator foramen (IOF), the medial obturator foramen (MOF), the superior pubis symphysis (SPS), the inferior pubis symphysis (IPS) and the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS). They were "manually annotated in 3D" in each specimen's CT, resampled to 1 mm, and each frame's 2-D landmarks are those points projected through the frame's registered pose, a point projecting off the detector counting as not visible; the 2-D labels were inspected by hand. The release's segmentation goes with them: vol_seg, seven labels (1 and 2 the hemipelves, 3 the lumbar vertebrae as one class, 4 the upper sacrum, 5 and 6 the femurs, 7 the lower sacrum; tools/deepfluoro_shipped.py), all seven in each of the five readable specimens' files (job 40940445).

- View: any C-arm pose; the frames the QA loop's arm keeps lie within 15 degrees of the anteroposterior view, the arm leaving out 48 frames of its three specimens more than 15 degrees from both the anteroposterior and the lateral (docs/research/qa-loop-external/README.md), so the release as a whole is not shown to be near anteroposterior.
- Kind: fixed, every one, since a person placed each in 3-D; their 2-D images are outline features (the notch, the foramen's rim, the spine's tip) but the release defines them by the 3-D point.
- Held: the release file /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/deepfluoro_h5/raw/deepfluoro/unpacked_full/ipcai_2020_full_res_data.h5 (HDF5; per specimen a group holding `vol-landmarks/<name>`, a 3-vector in the volume's world frame, LPS, and `projections/<frame>/gt-landmarks/<name>`, a 2-vector in pixels, with gt-seg and the pose), and per-specimen working copies under /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/realxray_work/deepfluoro/. The five specimens the arm may read (17-1882, 17-1905, 18-0725, 18-2799, 18-2800; the held-out 18-1109 was not opened) hold 14 3-D landmarks each, the same fourteen names, 70 3-D points, and 262 frames of 1,536 by 1,536 pixels listing 3,668 2-D entries, every frame listing all fourteen whether or not the point falls on the detector (job 40940203): 2,786 of the entries fall on the detector and 882 off it, which the paper counts as not visible (job 40940445). Read as LPS in ct.nii.gz's world, each specimen's left and right femoral head centres fall in vol_seg's labels 5 and 6; read as RAS they fall outside the bone (label 0) or outside the volume (job 40940445). The record counts 366 frames over all six specimens (data/sources/deepfluoro.json).
- Counterparts: FH, pelvis.socket_centre on the hip bone (the acetabulum's sphere, concentric with the head in a normal hip; a sphere fitted to the femoral head's own mask, ts_femur here, would be the direct one). GSN, IOF, MOF, SPS, IPS and ASIS: new constructions on the hip bone's mask (the notch's deepest point on the ilium's posterior border; the obturator foramen's lowest and most medial rim points; the symphysis's upper and lower ends at the midline; the most anterior point of the anterior superior spine). pelvis.crest_top is the crest's highest point, not ASIS. DeepFluoro is the one held set where a person's point is in the CT itself, so it tests a 3-D construction against a person directly, before any projection.

### Four corners per vertebra on anteroposterior whole-spine films: AASCE and BoostNet (Wu 2017)

Wu H, Bailey C, Rasoulinejad P, Li S. Automatic landmark estimation for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis assessment using BoostNet. In: Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Intervention, MICCAI 2017, Part I, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 10433, pp. 127-135, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-66182-7_15 (Crossref's record of the DOI gives the pages and Part I; the volume number is the one later papers and the paper's own header give); the AASCE 2019 challenge (aasce19.github.io) uses the same data, "spinal anterior-posterior x-ray images", released as SpineWeb dataset 16. The landmarks are the four corners of each of 17 vertebral bodies, twelve thoracic and five lumbar, 68 points a film, as the later papers that use the set describe it; the release itself confirms the count.

- View: anteroposterior.
- Format, read from the release: one MATLAB file per film whose one variable p2 is a 68 by 2 array of pixel coordinates (uint16); landmarks.csv holds the same 68 points a row as 136 numbers scaled to the image's size, and angles.csv three Cobb angles a film. On most films the vertebrae run from the top down and each vertebra's four points come in the order upper left, upper right, lower left, lower right as the film is seen, but not on all: of the 609 films, 28 (24 training, 4 test) list one vertebra above the one before it on the film, and sorting the fours by height puts 27 of them right; and 11 vertebrae (6 training, 5 test) have their corners out of that order, 9 with the upper and lower pairs swapped and 2 with left and right; 36 films carry one fault or the other (jobs 40940445 and 40940609, every .mat file; data/sources/aasce.json records the same artefact). So code reads a corner's level and its place by its position on the film, never by its index.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/thoracic_xr/mirror_zlinzju_ke30_u7_AASCE2019/unpacked/ke30_u7_AASCE2019-master/boostnet_labeldata, 481 training and 128 test films with their 481 and 128 files (job 40940203), so 609 films and 41,412 points; the challenge's 98 test films carry no landmarks (data/sources/aasce.json). The older request-form copy /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/aasce2019_boostnet/raw/aasce holds the 481 training films and 99 entries in its test folder.
- Kind: silhouette, all four.
- Counterparts: the upper corners, the qaloop endplate.right and endplate.left, the lower, endplate_lower.right and endplate_lower.left; but which side is the patient's right cannot be read from the release, which records no laterality, and a scoliosis film may be shown as seen from the front or from behind (no single convention holds), so the registry names these corners by the film's sides, the viewer's left and right, and sets them to the patient's only where a film's facing is known.

### Four corners per vertebra on generated anteroposterior films: Spinal-AI2024 (Shao 2025)

Shao Z, Yuan Y, Ma L, et al. CurvNet: latent contour representation and iterative data engine for curvature angle estimation. Pattern Recognition 2025, doi:10.1016/j.patcog.2025.112546 (arXiv 2411.12604; the release's README). The set is 20,000 scoliosis X-rays generated by the paper's latent diffusion data engine from a private dataset, so no patient was imaged and no reader marked these points.

- View: anteroposterior.
- Format: COCO JSON, one annotation per vertebra whose segmentation is one quadrilateral of four points (eight numbers) and whose category is "text" (the release borrowed a text-detection format; each annotation carries a random transcription string), and two text files of three Cobb angles per image.
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/spinal_ai2024/raw/spinal_ai2024/repo, 16,000 training and 4,000 test images at 512 by 512 (job 40940445) with 273,450 and 68,815 quadrilaterals (grep and job 40940203), 342,265 vertebrae and 1,369,060 points; every segmentation is eight numbers, and most images carry 16 to 18 vertebrae (18 images carry one).
- Kind and counterparts: as AASCE, the sides the image's. Being generated, it can train a corner reader's shape prior but not test one.

### End-plate lines, anteroposterior and lateral: BUU-LSPINE (Klinwichit 2023)

Klinwichit P, Yookwan W, Limchareon S, Chinnasarn K, Jang JS, Onuean A. BUU-LSPINE: a Thai open lumbar spine dataset for spondylolisthesis detection. Appl Sci 2023;13(15):8646, doi:10.3390/app13158646; the dataset's page (services.informatics.buu.ac.th/spine, read again on 2 November) says each image is labelled with "the upper and lower edges" of the lumbar vertebrae, as lines whose points the CSV files hold, names four disorders (anterolisthesis, retrolisthesis, left and right laterolisthesis), and says "Spine disorder diagnoses are marked in the lower edge lines"; of the 2,000-pair release it says each anteroposterior image "is diagnosed with two disorders: Side Bending and Rotation" and each lateral "with one disorder: Flexion/Extension". The page also says each image's name encodes the person's id, sex and age, so no name leaves the grid. The paper's full text was refused to the fetch (403), so the column layout below is the release's own.

- View: both, an anteroposterior and a lateral film for each of 3,600 people.
- Format, read from the release: one CSV per film, one row per end-plate line, x1, y1, x2, y2 and a code: on an anteroposterior film ten rows, the upper and lower plates of L1 to L5; on a lateral eleven, the same and the top of S1. The line's two ends are the plate's two corners. The code is 0 on most lines; the release documents no meaning for the others.
- Held, v1: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/buu_lspine_3600/raw/buu_full/BUU-LSPINEv1. AP: 3,600 films and 3,600 CSV, every one of ten lines, 36,000 lines and 72,000 points. LA: 3,604 films and 3,606 CSV of eleven lines each, of which 3,603 pair by name (3 CSV have no film and 1 film has no CSV, the names compared with case folded; jobs 40940445 and 40940609), so 39,633 lines and 79,266 points on held films, and 66 more on three films not held. The codes counted over the lines: AP 0 (35,870), 1 (61), 2 (69); LA 0 (38,862), 3 (465), 4 (194), 20 (15), 21 (130). Codes 1 to 4 may be the four disorders the page lists (left and right laterolisthesis on the anteroposterior film, antero- and retrolisthesis on the lateral); 20 and 21 lie beside the release sheet's 15 six-lumbar and 129 four-lumbar spines that spine-program's literature state records (02_transitional_naming_truth, topic1_bibliography.md row 12), so they may flag transitional spines; neither reading is confirmed.
- Held, the 2,000-pair release: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/buu_lspine_2000/raw/BUU-LSPINE, 2,000 AP CSV of ten lines and 2,000 LA CSV of eleven (job 40940203), 42,000 lines and 84,000 points, on the same films (every name is in v1, and spine-program's 01_carm_level_detection/docs/MANIFEST_DATA_MODELS_RESULTS.md finds all 4,000 JPEGs byte-identical to v1's). Its codes are AP 0, 1, 2 and 5 to 8 and LA 0, 3, 4, 9, 10, 20 and 21, the new ones (5 to 8, 9 and 10) on 245, 276, 724 and 586 AP lines and 816 and 69 LA lines (job 40940203), none a multiple of a film's ten or eleven lines, so they mark single lines and not whole films, as the page's diagnoses marked on the lower edge lines would; that they are the page's side bending, rotation and flexion/extension, and which code is which, the release does not say, and the films being v1's, no code is a posture of the film; column 5 differs from v1's on 1,316 files (610 AP over 1,859 of their 20,000 lines, 706 LA over 1,043 of 22,000; job 40940609). Its geometry is v1's on 3,923 of the 4,000 files and not on 77 (22 AP, 55 LA; job 40940445): on 2 lateral files every line is v1's drawn the other way round, its ends swapped; on the other 75 lines sit elsewhere, on 68 of them every line and on 7 one or two lines, by 1 to 38 px. Where every line moved, a file's lines moved on average a median 225 px (AP) and 223 px (LA), and its farthest end departs from that average move by a median 92 and 183 px, so the lines were not shifted together (jobs 40940609, 40940650, 40940690). Whether they were redrawn or are another film's, and which release is right on those films, was not checked against the films. So the 2,000-pair release is no duplicate to drop: its diagnosis codes and those 77 files' lines are its own.
- Kind: silhouette on the anteroposterior film; on the lateral, the corners as the section above says (the anterior near the midline's front ends, the posterior the midline corner as a stand-in).
- Counterparts: anteroposterior, endplate.right and endplate.left, endplate_lower.right and endplate_lower.left, by the film's sides as AASCE's; lateral, the anterior ends endplate_depth.anterior and endplate_lower_depth.anterior, the posterior column.wall_corner (top and bottom), whose nearest LANDMARKS endplate_depth.posterior and endplate_lower_depth.posterior lie as the table above says, and the line's slope front to back, body.rim_plane's; the top of S1's line, pelvis.s1_plate for its slope and a new construction for its two ends (the S1 plate's anterior end, the promontory, and its posterior end, the posterosuperior corner of S1) and for its middle. spine-program's converter reads the laterals as facing left, so a lateral line's first point is its anterior end.

### Four corners per vertebra on lateral lumbar films: Mendeley 5jdfdgp762 (Reyes 2025)

Reyes K. Spondylolisthesis vertebral landmark. - View: lateral.
- Format, read from the release: 484 films labelled in JSON (boxes, keypoints four a vertebra as x, y and a visibility flag, and labels) and 214 in text files of the YOLO pose layout (seventeen numbers a vertebra: the class, the box and four keypoints with their flags), both beside each other in Train/Keypointrcnn_data/labels/train (339 JSON and 155 text) and labels/val (145 and 59), 698 labelled films as the page's 698; the Test folder holds the 16 unlabelled films (jobs 40940203 and 40940445). The 501 films matched below are 640 by 640 exports. The keypoints' order, measured against BUU-LSPINE's lines on the BUU films below: the JSON files list a vertebra's lower plate's two corners and then its upper plate's (1,429 of 1,429 vertebrae matched), the text files the upper plate's first corner, the lower plate's two and the upper plate's second (1,030 of 1,037), each plate's corners in the order of BUU's own line ends (job 40940634).
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/mendeley_5jdfdgp762/raw/mendeley_716/extracted/Dataset (714 films). The JSON files hold 13,000 keypoints over 3 to 11 vertebrae a film, most 6 to 8, and the text files 1,350 vertebrae, 5,400 keypoints, so 18,400 points; more vertebrae than L3 to S1 are marked on most films, against the page.
- A second placement of BUU-LSPINE's corners: 501 of the films carry a BUU-LSPINE lateral film's name (data/sources/mendeley_5jdfdgp762.json: about 500 of its people are BUU's), so they add no new person but a second placement on the same films. On the 2,466 the corners lie a median 9.3 BUU pixels from BUU's line ends (quartiles 5.5 and 14.7, 90th centile 22.3), 3.0 pixels of the export, a median 4 per cent of the plate's length (90th centile 9.4); a point carried over from BUU's file through the resize would lie within about one export pixel, and 8 per cent lie within 3 BUU pixels; and the gap is no film's shift, since each film's mean offset is a median 4.2 pixels and taking it out leaves the median at 9.3 (jobs 40940634 and 40940650). So the corners were placed again, not copied from BUU's lines; whether blind to them, the record does not say.

### Corners and the C2 centre on lateral cervical films: CSXA (Ran 2024)

Ran Y, Qin W, Qin C, et al. A high-quality dataset featuring classified and annotated cervical spine X-ray atlas. Sci Data 2024;11:625, doi:10.1038/s41597-024-03383-0 (read on PubMed Central, PMC11176335; the grid holds the PDF beside the data). In the authors' words, 23 keypoints a film: "the four corner points of the third to seventh cervical vertebral body (C3 to C7)", where "the corner points are the four intersections which formed by the upper and lower endplates" with the body's anterior and posterior edges, and "the central point and the two corner points of lower endplate of C2"; the upper plate of C2 and C1 were not marked. Four spine surgeons annotated, cross-checked three times; 4,963 films from Dongzhimen Hospital. The C2 centre is there for the cervical sagittal measures the paper derives (its algorithm turns the keypoints into its quantitative parameters).

- View: lateral (the paper says only cervical spine X-ray; the record data/sources/csxa.json says lateral, and the points, each plate's front and back corners, are a lateral reader's).
- Format, read from the release: labelme JSON (version 5.2.1) with the image embedded, one shape per point labelled `C<n> top left`, `C<n> top right`, `C<n> bottom left`, `C<n> bottom right`, "C2 bottom left", "C2 bottom right" and "C2 centroid", where left and right are the image's sides (anterior or posterior by the film's facing).
- Held: /rs/rs_grp_oschome/go2432/data/csxa_cervical_xray_atlas/raw/csxa (images, annotations/datasets-JSON, the paper and the authors' code): 4,963 JSON files, 4,962 of 23 labelled shapes and one of 24 (a second C3 top right), 114,150 labelled shapes, every label present on every film (job 40940203). Of the shapes, 112,019 are of type point, 2,087 one-point polygons and 44 polygons of two to four points (job 40940445), the 44 on 38 films and each a double click, its points within 1.2 pixels of each other (median 0.8; job 40940609), so any of a shape's points, or their mean, is the landmark to within about a pixel, and 114,150 is also the count of landmarks.
- Kind: the corners as BUU-LSPINE's lateral; the C2 centre fixed.

### Corner templates tracked on lateral cervical fluoroscopy: Bournemouth quantitative fluoroscopy (Branney, Breen, Breen 2024)

### Six-point quantitative morphometry and Genant's semiquantitative grade (Black 1991, McCloskey 1993, Genant 1993; SOF and MrOS)

Black DM, Cummings SR, Stone K, Hudes E, Palermo L, Steiger P. A new approach to defining normal vertebral dimensions. J Bone Miner Res 1991;6(8):883-892 (the normal means and standard deviations of vertebral height ratios from 2,992 women of the Study of Osteoporotic Fractures, as its abstract says). McCloskey EV, Spector TD, Eyres KS, Fern ED, O'Rourke N, Vasikaran S, Kanis JA. The assessment of vertebral deformity: a method for use in population studies and clinical trials. Osteoporos Int 1993;3(3):138-147 (PubMed 8481590; the full text was not read). Genant HK, Wu CY, van Kuijk C, Nevitt MC. Vertebral fracture assessment using a semiquantitative technique. J Bone Miner Res 1993;8(9):1137-1148, PMID 8237484. The Study of Osteoporotic Fractures' morphometry form says "six points were placed on each vertebral body to define 3 vertebral heights: Ha (anterior height), Hm (mid height), and Hp (posterior height)", L4 up to T4 in the main cohort, and that the normal means and standard deviations come from Black's method (SOF visit forms V3, V7 and V8, Morphometry General, at the NIA Aging Research Biobank). The six points are the four corners and the middle of the upper and the lower plate. Genant's grade is read by eye: from 0 to 3 by the loss of anterior, middle or posterior height, about 20 to 25, 25 to 40 and over 40 per cent, with the body's shape (wedge, biconcave, crush). MrOS read the same six points with semi-automated software, an operator placing a point near each body's centre and correcting the outline, which records 6-point and 95-point morphometry (PMC4152008).

- View: lateral thoracic and lumbar films.
- Held: no. SOF (about 9,700 women) and MrOS (5,994 men) are asked for; their registrations are closed and the films are moving to the NIA Aging Research Biobank (data/sources/sof_lateral_spine.json, mros_lateral_spine.json).
- Kind: the four corners as the lateral corners above; the plate middles fixed (a plate's centre does not slide with the view as its ends do).
- Counterparts: the anterior corners endplate_depth.anterior and endplate_lower_depth.anterior; the posterior corners column.wall_corner (top and bottom), not h_post's ends, which lie a median 3.4 mm in front of them (the table above); the plate middles column.plate_centre (top and bottom). The engine's body.heights reads Ha, Hm and Hp (h_ant, h_mid, h_post_inset) in bands 2.5 mm inside the corners, not at them, so its heights are a reader's six-point heights taken a little inward; the ratios Genant and Black read are what the Bayes rule would weigh.

### The spinopelvic points (Legaye 1998) and the sagittal vertical axis

Legaye J, Duval-Beaupere G, Hecquet J, Marty C. Pelvic incidence: a fundamental pelvic parameter for three-dimensional regulation of spinal sagittal curves. Eur Spine J 1998;7(2):99-103, PMID 9629932 (its abstract; the definition as the later papers of the same group give it): pelvic incidence is the angle between the line perpendicular to the S1 plate at its midpoint and the line from that midpoint to the femoral heads' axis, the middle of the line joining the two heads' centres; sacral slope is the S1 plate's angle to the horizontal, pelvic tilt the angle to the vertical of the line from the hip axis to the plate's midpoint, and incidence is their sum. The reader marks the S1 plate's two ends (its midpoint follows) and the two femoral heads' centres. The sagittal vertical axis is the horizontal distance from the plumb line dropped from the centre of the C7 body to the posterosuperior corner of S1 on a standing lateral, the global modifier of the SRS-Schwab classification (Schwab F, Ungar B, Blondel B, et al. Scoliosis Research Society-Schwab adult spinal deformity classification: a validation study. Spine 2012;37(12):1077-1082, doi:10.1097/BRS.0b013e31823e15e2), whose goal is under 4 cm.

- View: lateral, standing for the published normals; a supine CT gives the supine values only (the engine names them sacral_slope_supine and pelvic_tilt_supine; incidence does not change with posture).
- Held: no film set on the grid carries these points as a reader marked them; the full-length lateral films with a reader's spinopelvic points that the literature uses (EOS sets among them) are not held. BUU-LSPINE's lateral line on the top of S1 gives the S1 plate's two ends on its 3,603 held lumbar laterals, without the femoral heads.
- Kind: the S1 plate's ends as the lateral corners above; the plate's midpoint, the femoral head centres and the C7 centre fixed.
- Counterparts: the S1 plate's slope, pelvis.s1_plate's normal; its two ends, new (the plate's anterior end, the promontory, and its posterior end, which is also the sagittal vertical axis's corner); its midpoint, the middle of those two ends, new: pelvis.s1_plate's centre is the mean of the sacral top under the lowest lumbar body's footprint, so it moves with L5 and in a slip is not the middle the reader marks; the femoral heads, pelvis.socket_centre; the angles, pelvis.incidence, whose centre is s1_plate's; the C7 centre, the centroid of anatomy.build's C7 body, new as a point. tools/qaloop_samples.py's sacral_points is not yet the promontory: its promontory sat a median 4 mm in front of L5's lower plate centre and behind it in 20 of 63 COBRA cases (docs/research/qa-loop-external/README.md), where the anterior end of S1 is wanted.

### The Cobb end-plate lines (Cobb 1948)

Cobb JR. Outline for the study of scoliosis. American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons Instructional Course Lectures, vol. 5. Ann Arbor: J. W. Edwards; 1948:261-275 (as cited; the text was not read). The angle is read between a line along the superior plate of the upper end vertebra and one along the inferior plate of the lower end vertebra, the end vertebrae being the most tilted at the curve's ends.

- View: anteroposterior for scoliosis; the same lines on a lateral give the regional lordosis and kyphosis.
- Held: AASCE's angles.csv (609 films, three angles each) and Spinal-AI2024's two text files (20,000 images) carry the angles; every corner set above carries the lines, a plate's line running through its two corners.
- Kind: the lines' ends are silhouette points; the line's direction on an anteroposterior film is the plate's coronal tilt, which a small turn of the view changes little.
- Counterparts: on an anteroposterior film the line through the plate's two lateral corners, new as a line, its ends the qaloop endplate (or endplate_lower) right and left; the engine's column.plate_normal and column.coronal_cobb take their slope across from body.rim_plane's central plane, the plate's central half, not from its corners, so they read the plate's central tilt and differ from the reader's line where the plate's edges stand off its middle. On a lateral, the line from corner to corner is body.rim_plane's slope front to back, and the angles column.regional (lordosis_L1_S1, kyphosis_T4_T12 and lordosis_C2_C7 between fixed plates).

## How a reader's point meets the CT

Of the held sets only DeepFluoro has a reader's point in a CT; every other is a film with no CT of the same person. So a reader's point reaches the CT the other way round: the engine builds the construction this registry names in a CT, the pose projects it, and the network learns to place that projected point; on a held film, the reader's point is what the network's point is checked against, by the same name, against the readers' own agreement where a set records one. One held pair does: on 501 of BUU-LSPINE's lateral films, BUU's line ends and the Mendeley set's corners are two placements of the same corners, a median 9.3 BUU pixels (4 per cent of the plate's length) apart, so a network's corner that lies as close to either is as close as the two placements are to each other; that is a floor of the readers' disagreement if the second placement was made in sight of BUU's lines. No other held corner set ships a second reading. A fixed point can be checked on any view; a silhouette point only near the view the reader read, until the outline construction exists. DeepFluoro alone checks a construction against a person before any projection: its femoral head centres against pelvis.socket_centre in the same CT, its other twelve against the constructions still to be built.

For the Bayes rule the readers' points give measures, and the useful ones need no scale: most of these films carry no pixel spacing (CSXA's paper gives each image a pixel equivalent; AASCE, BUU-LSPINE and the Mendeley set give none in their files), but an angle (Cobb, pelvic incidence, a plate's wedge) and a ratio (Genant's and Black's height ratios, Meyerding's slip share) read the same at any magnification. Where a pair of a reader's points is one of Panjabi's chords (the lateral corners give the end-plate depths EPDu and EPDl, the anteroposterior corners the widths EPWu and EPWl, the posterior corners the posterior height VBHp), the chord can be set beside Panjabi and beside the engine's own reading on the gold tier (docs/research/morphometry-against-panjabi.md) once the film's scale is known. The reader's chord runs between the corners, the engine's column.wall_corner and its front-end mirror; the qaloop depth chords end a median of about a millimetre or less from those corners along the depth in the thoracic and lumbar spine (the table above), and h_post's ends are no corners, so VBHp is read between column.wall_corner's two corners, not along h_post.
