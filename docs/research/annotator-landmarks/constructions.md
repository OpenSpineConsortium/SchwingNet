# The annotators' landmarks as the CT engine's constructions, checked against the points a person placed

Source: the owner's local Claude Code session of the night of 1 November 2026, on his words that night: "the bare minimum my landmark net should also be able to place landmarks annotators have found useful such as the ones deepfluoro placed, the corners a ton of xray groups have placed, etc; it should somehow find where those points correspond to the 3d ct scans and propagate them through and use them if possible for bayesian measurements, and even better if they correlate with known anatomical papers like panjabi". Revised the same night after two adversarial reviews of pull request #554 (the section "The review, and the second check"). Confidential with the rest of this repository.

## What was built

tools/annotator_landmarks.py places, on a labelled CT, the points readers mark on films, each as a named 3-D point in world millimetres in LPS (x to the patient's left, y posterior, z superior, the frame of DeepFluoro's vol-landmarks and of the QA loop's pairs), each from one construction of bordercall's morphometry engine (the pinned copy under grid/bordercall/, 9c85de1), by the owner's rule of 29 October that corners and landmarks come from the CT engine's constructions, projected, and never from a 2-D fit. The tool's CONSTRUCTIONS table says each in words; in short, as the tool stands now (the points read on a shape were first read otherwise, as the sections after the runs say):

| construction | what it is | QA loop landmark nearest it (a different construction; see below) |
|---|---|---|
| anterior_superior, anterior_inferior | each end plate's front end on the midline: column.wall_corner's construction mirrored to the front, in the plate's own frame | endplate_depth.anterior, endplate_lower_depth.anterior |
| posterior_superior, posterior_inferior | each end plate's back end on the midline, where the posterior wall meets it (column.wall_corner, the corners the slip is read between) | endplate_depth.posterior, endplate_lower_depth.posterior (and near h_post's two ends) |
| centre_superior, centre_inferior | each end plate's centre on its plane (column.plate_centre): the middle points of quantitative six-point morphometry, as Oei et al. (2013) review it; Genant's 1993 method grades by eye and places no point | none |
| superior_right, superior_left, inferior_right, inferior_left | each end plate's lateral ends on the bone: the ends of the widest row of the plate's own layer across its front half, as bordercall's plates.py reads the plate's width (commit 69cae89, repeated in the tool since the pinned copy predates it) | endplate.right/left, endplate_lower.right/left |
| hip_centre | the acetabulum's centre (pelvis.socket_centre: a vote of the hip bone's surface normals over radii of 18 to 32 mm, refined by a sphere) | none |
| femoral_head | the femoral head's centre, read again on the femur: the same vote with the normals turned inward over the femur's proximal 100 mm, refined by a sphere through the head | none |
| crest_top | the iliac crest's highest point along the scanner's long axis (pelvis.crest_top) | none |
| s1_centre, s1_anterior, s1_posterior | the S1 end plate under L5 (pelvis.s1_plate), on a sacrum that holds S1, its front end on the midline, the sacral promontory, and its back end at the sacral canal: the plate's layer walked from its centre forward and backward until a gap of more than 2 mm; flagged where the plate lies more than 20 mm below L5's lower plate or 10 mm beside the midline (s1_against) | none (the QA loop's sacral anchor is tools/qaloop_samples.py's, which this does not change) |
| asis | the anterior superior iliac spine: where the anterior pelvic plane (the plane resting on both ilia above the hip axis and on the pubis in front, turned until every point lies behind it) touches each ilium | none |
| symphysis_superior, symphysis_inferior | each pubis's face at the symphysis (its bone within 6 mm of the other pubis beyond their narrowest gap); its upper end along the joint's own long axis, its lowest point along the scanner's long axis (DIRECTIONS) | none |
| obturator_inferior, obturator_medial | the obturator foramen as the largest hole in the silhouette of the hip bone below the acetabulum over a search of views about the anterior pelvic plane's normal; its outline in 3-D, the middle of the bone round the hole's edge in the view that shows it largest; the outline's extreme toward the ischium (back and down with the plane upright, the direction DeepFluoro's annotators' points lie in) and toward the midline (DIRECTIONS) | none |
| obturator_inferior_curvature, obturator_medial_curvature | the source's definition of IOF and MOF, the foramen's inferior and medial points of maximal curvature: the corners of the hole's edge in that view (each a point turning most within 15 mm of arc, and more than the loop's mean turning), the lowest and the most medial with the anterior pelvic plane upright, and the bone there (loop_corners) | none |
| sciatic_notch | the greater sciatic notch's deepest point: the hip bone seen along the hip axis, the point of its posterior concavity furthest inside its convex hull, and the bone there | none |

Three kinds of point, written on each point as its kind (KIND in the tool): "bone", a point fixed on the bone wherever the CT was taken; "pose", crest_top and symphysis_inferior, extremes along the scanner's long axis, which on another CT move on the bone with the pelvis's tilt on the table (6 to 31 degrees against the anterior pelvic plane on these specimens); and "corner", the vertebral points, fixed on the bone, which stand for the corner a film reader marks on the body's silhouette. That silhouette corner moves with the beam and the vertebra's rotation (the lumbar posterior wall is concave at the midline, so a lateral film's posterior line runs along the posterolateral margins, not the midline wall corner), and nothing here checks the two against each other: DeepFluoro's person placed no vertebral point.

The QA loop's points of the names in the third column are not these. Its oracle (grid/autoanatomist/autoanatomist/oracle.py body_traces) puts a plate's lateral ends at the whole slab's x-extremes, at the slab's median depth and height, and a plate's front end on the whole slab's front surface; bordercall's own plates.py (69cae89) says that slab's upper width reads 4 to 6 mm over Panjabi's at C3 to C6 and T10 to L3, 11 mm at L5. The first version of this note said the two sets "join one target list"; they do not until the offset between the two constructions is known. The second check measures it (below), and each point's JSON now carries the name as qaloop_near, not as its own.

Where a definition was ambiguous, the reading chosen and why:

- Grupp et al. (2020, IJCARS 15:759-769, "Automatic annotation of hip anatomy in fluoroscopy for robust and efficient 2D/3D registration", arXiv 1911.07042) name the fourteen points, say they were "manually digitized" in the CT and give no spread between annotators (read on PMC7263976, 1 November 2026: its heatmaps' spread was "subjectively chosen to approximate the variance found in manual landmark annotation", with no number). For the landmarks it cites its reference 7: "These landmarks were previously identified as being useful for obtaining initial registration estimates of the pelvis". That is Grupp et al., "Pose estimation of periacetabular osteotomy fragments with intraoperative X-ray navigation" (IEEE Trans Biomed Eng 67:441-452, 2020; arXiv 1903.09339, read in its text that night), which does define some of them: "Other landmarks identified for registration purposes were the medial and inferior points of maximal curvature on the obturator foramen, the greater sciatic notch, and inferior symphysis", and "The point of maximal curvature on the medial portion of the ipsilateral obturator foramen was chosen as the initialization landmark"; its appendix lists ASIS, AIIS, FH, SPS, IPS, MOF, IOF and GSN, annotated in 3D Slicer (its Fig. S-1, a picture not read here). The first version of this note missed that paper and said no definition existed, so it took a textbook reading of each name: the ASIS as the iliac spine the anterior pelvic plane rests on (Lewinnek's plane, which a navigation system registers by palpating the same spines); the notch's apex as its deepest point; the foramen's inferior and medial points as its rim's extremes downward and toward the midline; the symphysis's points as each pubis's own end of the joint, since the release gives a left and a right point for each. The foramen's points by the source's definition, its corners, are now placed beside the extremes (obturator_*_curvature).
- Before the first run, the foramen's extremes were taken in one view, the one the search found largest, because the rim is a band of bone 10 to 20 mm thick along a view and its 3-D lowest voxel is wherever the band's edge happens to dip; the point was the band's mean at the extreme, the middle of the bone a person clicks on. The run showed that view's "down" is not the annotators' (below); the band's mean is kept, as the foramen's outline in 3-D. The first search ran 40 degrees from the plane's normal each way, and the view it found largest lay on that edge on every side of every specimen (yaw at 40 degrees in 20 of 20), so the view along the foramen was never reached and the outline depended on where the search stopped; it now runs from 70 degrees below to 40 above in pitch and 80 degrees each way in yaw, and a view still on its edge is recorded.
- The femoral head's centre is read twice: once as the acetabulum's centre, which the engine already reads and which a congruent hip makes the head's centre too, the joint space lying between two spheres about one centre, and once on the femur's own ball where a femur mask exists; the check shows how far the two lie apart on these specimens.

## The first check, and its bars

DeepFluoro's release holds, for each of its six cadaveric specimens, fourteen points a person placed in the CT (vol-landmarks: the femoral head centres FH, the greater sciatic notches GSN, the obturator foramen's inferior and medial points IOF and MOF, the pubic symphysis's superior and inferior points SPS and IPS, and the anterior superior iliac spines ASIS, each left and right), in the volume's world frame, which is ct.nii.gz's world in LPS. `tools/annotator_landmarks.py persons` reads them, and nothing else of the file, for the five specimens 17-1882, 17-1905, 18-0725, 18-2799 and 18-2800; 18-1109, the QA loop's held-out test specimen, is never read. `check` builds every construction twice per specimen, on TotalSegmentator's masks (ts/, ts_femur/ and, since the review, ts_s1/) and on the dataset authors' own 3-D labels (vol_seg.nii.gz: hemipelves, the lumbar vertebrae as one class split into its connected pieces and each named by the TotalSegmentator level that holds 70 per cent of it, the sacrum, the femurs), and reports per landmark the 3-D distance between each construction and the person's point (median, maximum and n), the distance between the two sources' constructions, and how far each person's point lies from the authors' bone labels, which shows whether the two frames are one.

The bars, as committed in 18110ff4. This note first said they were written before any run; that is not so. A smoke job of `check` on 17-1882 alone (job 40940246, 03:33:23 to 03:34:22 UTC on 2 November) ran and finished before 18110ff4 was committed (03:35:02 UTC) and pushed (GitHub's record of the branch's creation, 03:35:31 UTC), and its table for 17-1882 (IPS 21.4, IOF 10.8, ASIS 8.5 mm among them) was printed before the bars were pushed. The code did not change between the two jobs (smoke/17-1882.json and frozen/17-1882.json are byte-identical), but one specimen in five had been seen when the bars were stated.

- A construction agrees with the person where its median distance over the specimens is at most 5 mm. Grupp et al. give no spread between annotators to set this against (above), so the bar is this check's own figure. The first version justified it as "about the size of the error their own network made on the detector plane over these specimens (5.0 mm)"; that figure (5.0 +/- 5.2 mm, their section 3.2) is a 2-D error in the detector plane, pooled over the leave-one-specimen-out test images of all six specimens, the held-out 18-1109 among them, and the C-arm's magnification makes a detector-plane error larger than the same error at the bone. It is a scale for the bar, not the size of a 3-D error on these five. Every landmark is reported whatever it shows.
- The frame is one where every person's point other than the femoral head centres lies within 5 mm of the authors' bone labels (the head centres lie inside the femur, at no distance); if not, no distance below means anything.
- The femoral head centre agrees on both readings, the acetabulum's and the ball's, or the difference between the two is reported as what it is.
- Nothing here is checked against a person for the vertebral and sacral points: DeepFluoro's person placed none. Those are reported as the agreement between the two sources (TotalSegmentator against the authors' labels) and, for the promontory, as its place against L5's lower plate, the check the QA loop's sacral anchor failed (docs/research/qa-loop-external/README.md): the promontory should stand in front of L5's lower plate centre by about half the plate's depth, not 4 mm.

## The review, and the second check

Two adversarial reviewers read pull request #554 on the night of 1 November 2026 and found fourteen problems; the pull request body lists each with what was done. Those that change the tool:

- read_totalseg joins TotalSegmentator's vertebrae_S1 (from ts/ or ts_s1/) to the sacrum, as scanimport does, and notes when it is missing.
- The S1 plate is read under L5 (or L6 where the labels hold one) alone, and only where the engine read that vertebra and its chords are a body's (lowest_lumbar); otherwise no plate, with the reason.
- Every vertebra's chords between its named points (the two plates' widths and depths, the posterior height, and the centre height held to the posterior height's band, since Panjabi's lumbar tables give no mid-body height) are set against Panjabi's mean at its level in the pinned engine's reference table (grid/bordercall/bordercall/reference/panjabi_full_spine.csv), in spreads of sem times the square root of n (plausibility). The vertebra's own reading by the engine (its Panjabi chords) is written beside them, so a broken frame can be told from a broken construction.
- The QA loop oracle's construction of each vertebral name is read on the same body in the same frame (slab_points), and the distance from each of this tool's corners to it is reported (qaloop_offsets_mm).
- The foramen's view search runs from 70 degrees below to 40 above and 80 degrees each way, and records a view still on its edge; the foramen's corners by curvature are placed beside its extremes (loop_corners).
- Each point carries its kind (bone, pose or corner) and its QA loop name as qaloop_near.

The bars of the second check, written and pushed before it runs; this time no job of any kind runs on the grid before the commit holding them is pushed:

- The foramen's points of maximal curvature, the source's definition of IOF and MOF, agree with the person where their median over the ten points (both sides pooled) is at most 5 mm on both labelings, the first check's bar. The definition is the source's, but the choice of which corner is "inferior" (the lowest with the anterior pelvic plane upright) and which "medial" (the nearest the midline), the arc lengths (8 mm for the turning, 15 mm for a corner's reach) and the view search were set by someone who had seen where the person's points lie on these five specimens; so a pass is in sample for those choices, though nothing in it was fitted to the points.
