# scanimport: from a scan to a column the rule can name

Confidential with the rest of this repository: nothing here goes to a public place before the provisional is filed.

The owner's ask of 27 October 2026, 09:44 UTC, item 6 of that queue: "get 2D/3D scan to bordercall-importable data pipeline done so i can start importing empirical data". This package is that pipeline. It takes a CT's label volume, a CT with no labels at all (the mask-free mode), or a radiograph's corner annotation or FleXray's masks, measures Panjabi's seven chords on every body, records what the labels say about the study (whether it was counted from C2, its phenotype, its evidence tier), and names it with the archive's exact rule, extended so that a real study's missing measures, gaps and uncertain scale are handled by the same model rather than dropped.

No real scan has been imported yet. Every scan this would read is on the grid, and nothing goes to the grid without the owner's word and count. Everything below was checked on synthetic spines with known chords and on Panjabi's own synthetic columns.

## What it reads

- A CT label volume in NIfTI, in any of six label schemes: VerSe, SPINEPS's instance masks (the same alphabet), CTSpinoPelvic1K v11, MOOSE's stored ids, ENHANCE.PET 1.6k's vertebra group, and TotalSegmentator, either as its folder of one mask per structure or as a multilabel volume with its own class map. SPINEPS writes vertebra X's end-plate as 200 + X and the disc below it as 100 + X; the end-plates are joined back to their vertebrae and the discs left out, and the study records how many voxels of each (`labels_merged`). SPINEPS's subregion volume is read beside the instance volume where there is one. Every raw value is resolved in [schemes.py](schemes.py), and each scheme says what it cannot express. TotalSegmentator and ENHANCE.PET have no T13, and TotalSegmentator no L6, so a column read from them is never called typical on the strength of their absence. ENHANCE.PET's hips and sacrum (26 to 28) would read as a VerSe sacrum, coccyx and T13, and a VerSe read that holds all three is named as a problem.
- A CT with no labels: a NIfTI file, a folder of one DICOM series, or one DICOM file holding an Enhanced CT Image. The chords come from the intensities alone, by published training-free rules ([The mask-free mode](#the-mask-free-mode-a-ct-with-no-labels)). The bodies are found but not named, so such a column is never countable and is named by the rule from its chords.
- One radiograph or fluoroscopy frame, as one DICOM file (CR, DX, XA, RF, a CT localizer, or a secondary capture, recognised by its exact SOP Class, .7 and .7.1 to .7.4) or a PNG, JPEG, TIFF or BMP, with its corners in schwingnet-spine-xr/1, FleXray's masks, or both ([One image](#one-image-corners-flexrays-masks-and-the-headers-scale)).
- A radiograph annotated in SchwingNet's X-ray format, schwingnet-spine-xr/1: four corners per body, with the level named. An AP gives the two end-plate widths. A lateral gives the two end-plate depths and the posterior height, once the image says which side is anterior. A body may carry an optional `chords` object with two points for any measure the corners do not give and the view shows: the canal's width on an AP and its depth on a lateral, and likewise the transverse span on an AP and the spinous length on a lateral, which are kept in the record only. This is the one addition to version 1 the importer reads. An AP and a lateral of one study merge into one column.

## What it writes

A directory of imports holds:

- `columns/<study>.json`, the whole record;
- `bodies.csv`, one row per body and measure that has a value or a reason, with how the measure was taken and why it was not, where it was not; a body measured not at all (C1, C2 and the sacrum, a mask-free body bounded by one disc or left out for metal) has a row per measure carrying the body's reason;
- `fields/<study>.csv`, the study as one field in the format `python -m schwingcheck name` reads, headed by the scale its values carry (`# scale_known`, `# scale_log_mean`, `# scale_log_sd`). Only `python -m scanimport name` reads those lines, so a field it names from its file is named as its column would be; schwingcheck reads the same file but skips every `#` line and takes a known scale at its generator's spread, so a field in pixels, or one whose magnification is less certain, is not named by it as its column would be;
- `studies.csv`, the facts and each study's own tier;
- `tiers.csv`, one row per person at the best tier their studies reach, alone and as a CT and X-ray pair;
- `MANIFEST.json`, every import into the directory, with its command, the sha256 of every input file that changes it (for ingest-ct the label volume, or TotalSegmentator's folder, with SPINEPS's subregion volume and the label map where given; for ingest-xr each annotation; for ingest-raw-ct the CT, file or folder, and a NIfTI's dcm2niix sidecar; for ingest-image the image, its corners, FleXray's arrays and the label_names.json that named their channels, given or found beside them), the commit of the code, and the versions of Python and of each library the run imported (numpy with its BLAS, scipy, nibabel, pydicom, Pillow). A folder's digest is the sha256 of one line per file under it, its relative path and its sha256, the lines sorted, under the folder's name with its count of files, and the manifest states that rule (`digests`);
- `RUNS.json`, in a directory that `tiers`, `compare` or `score` wrote without importing, every such run with its command, commit and versions.

The imports hold per-body measurements under the source's own case ids. They stay on the grid with the data, and only aggregates come back to git.

## The commands

```sh
python -m scanimport ingest-ct --labels seg.nii.gz --scheme verse --study s0001 --source verse \
                               --annotation human --out imports/verse
python -m scanimport ingest-ct --labels inst.nii.gz --subregion sub.nii.gz --scheme spineps \
                               --study s0001 --source verse --annotation machine --made-by "SPINEPS" --out imports/spineps
python -m scanimport ingest-xr --xr ap.json --xr lateral.json --study f0001 --source aasce \
                               --annotation human --out imports/aasce
python -m scanimport ingest-raw-ct --volume ct_dicom_folder --study s0002 --source enhance --out imports/raw
python -m scanimport ingest-image --image ap.dcm --corners ap.json --flexray ap_probabilities.npy \
                                  --study f0002 --source x --annotation human --out imports/x
python -m scanimport check     imports/verse
python -m scanimport tiers     imports/verse imports/aasce --out imports/tiers
python -m scanimport compare   imports/verse --out imports/compare
python -m scanimport name      imports/verse/fields/s0001.csv
python -m scanimport score     imports/verse --n 1 3 5 9 15 --out imports/scores
```

`--annotation` (human or machine) is required on every import of labels or corners: ingest-ct, ingest-xr, and ingest-image whenever `--corners` is given, where it is checked against the annotation's reader id before FleXray names anything. An import from FleXray's body masks alone is a machine's by construction, and a CT read from its intensities carries no annotation ("none"). It is the caller's statement of who drew the labels, never inferred, and a machine's labels never raise a tier. `compare.csv` opens with a line saying which bodies it was taken from: those of studies counted from C2 by default, or every named body with `--all`.

It needs nothing the archive does not already need for label volumes and annotations: numpy, scipy and nibabel, from [archive/requirements.txt](../archive/requirements.txt). Reading DICOM files and pictures takes pydicom and Pillow as well, pinned in [requirements-raw.txt](requirements-raw.txt). It was tested with numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1, nibabel 5.4.2, pydicom 3.0.2 and Pillow 13.0.0 on Python 3.13.

## How the chords are taken on a CT

[geometry.py](geometry.py) gives every vertebra its own frame. The z axis starts along the column, through the neighbours' centroids, and is then refined to the direction along which the body is thinnest, so that a neighbour's turn or slip does not tilt it. The y axis runs from the canal to the body, and is found from which side beyond the canal walls holds more of the vertebra, so a prone patient is read the same way as a supine one. Every chord is taken in that frame from the voxels' world positions, through the volume's affine, so nothing is resampled. The canal is the hole the arch closes round in a slab across the column, and the body is what lies in front of it. A whole-vertebra label needs no subregions and no threshold. Where SPINEPS's subregions exist, they are read directly.

A surface lies between the last voxel centre inside the bone and the first outside it. How far beyond the outermost centre it lies depends on how the grid meets the chord, and `edge_gap` reads that from the centres themselves.

Each end-plate is read on a slab under a plane resting on the body's top or bottom. On thick slices oblique to a plate, the voxel centres under it form a staircase whose treads fall short of the plate by up to one slice; the plane rests on the steps' edges rather than through their middle, and is kept level where its tilt cannot be told from the slices. It rests on the central part of the body, so uncinate processes and osteophytes at the rim neither lift it nor tilt the axis.

The synthetic spines in [tests/phantom.py](tests/phantom.py) have known chords: a 44 by 34 mm body 27 mm high, a 22 by 16 mm canal, a cervical-sized variant, and variants with uncinate processes, an anterior osteophyte, or a middle body bent against its neighbours. Here is the worst error over the seven chords on three bodies, with the chord it falls on and the test's bound for that chord (the larger of 0.75 mm and one voxel's shadow on the chord's axis):

[tests/test_geometry.py](tests/test_geometry.py) holds every chord of these thirteen spines to its bound. On slices 2.5 mm and thicker the posterior height carries most of a slice's uncertainty, as it must: the slices sample it once every 2.5 to 5 mm. A vertebra cut by the field of view loses the chords it is cut through, and each lost chord carries its reason.

One case is known to fall far outside the bound, and nothing in the record flags it: a body bent against the line through its neighbours by less than the slices can resolve, as a wedge or a slip at a junction can be on thick slices. The body's own axis cannot be told from the slices' quantization there, so it is measured in its neighbours' frame and its end-plate slabs cut its tilted plates obliquely. On the phantom with 0.7 by 0.7 by 3 mm voxels, the middle body bent 10 degrees sideways (the column at the phantoms' default 18 degree tilt) reads its upper and lower end-plate widths 16.5 and 15.8 mm short, a 44 mm body read as about 28 mm wide; bent 10 degrees forward (the column along the scanner's axis) it reads its end-plate depths 7.5 and 2.3 mm short. The bounds there are about 1.5 mm, and neither body carries a flag or a problem. [tests/test_geometry.py](tests/test_geometry.py) measures both (`KnownFailure`), so these numbers fail there first if they change. The angle between the kept axis and the body's thinnest direction cannot flag the case: it is 10.5 degrees on the body bent sideways and up to 14 degrees on the straight phantoms with 5 mm slices, each inside the band its slices cannot resolve. On 1 mm slices an 8 degree sideways bend stays within the bounds: 0.46 mm with whole-vertebra labels and 0.63 mm with SPINEPS subregions (the table's last row).

What the phantoms cannot show is whether these chords are Panjabi's on real bone. A whole-vertebra label includes the cortex, a segmenter can swell a body by a voxel, and Panjabi's calipers sat on landmarks no mask names. `compare` sets each imported chord beside Panjabi's mean and SD, level by level. It reports the shift in millimetres and in Panjabi's SDs, and the spread of the ratio down the column. A small spread is a scale, which the rule absorbs. A large one is a change of shape, which it does not. That comparison is the first thing to read on real imports, before any score.

## The mask-free mode: a CT with no labels

The owner's ask of 27 October 2026, 13:45 UTC: "permit importing just the raw volume (either nifti or dicom) and calculating the measurements from just the intensities ... identify the bone by HU thresholding ... MAKE SURE ALL YOUR CODE FOR THIS METHOD IS BASED ON PRIOR ART AND IS ENTIRELY TRANSPARENT, REPRODUCIBLE, TESTED, ENCAPSULATED". The prior art was read first, and is [reports/Vertebra measures without masks.md](../reports/Vertebra%20measures%20without%20masks.md), with its notes in [research_notes/Vertebra measures without masks/](../research_notes/Vertebra%20measures%20without%20masks/). Every step of [intensity.py](intensity.py) is built from published training-free rules, named in its docstring where they are used; where a step adapts a rule or a constant has no source, the docstring or the constant's comment says so:

1. The body is everything above -500 HU, half way from air to water.
2. The canal is the longest chain, slice to slice, of holes inside the body in the 200 HU bone mask (the lowest level Radnostics' disc weight counts) whose voxels are mostly -50 to 50 HU, no smaller than 12 mm2 and no larger than 600 mm2 (the largest canal of Panjabi's means as an ellipse of its width and depth, L5's 27.1 by 19.7 mm, 419 mm2, as an axial slice up to 45 degrees off cuts it, rounded up), keeping, of two such holes one behind the other, the posterior one. The share of canal-density voxels adapts Radnostics' per-pixel vote along z (US 8,891,848 B2) to a share per hole. Keeping the posterior hole stands in for Radnostics' seeding from the spinous tips and its shape tests, which are left out, and is this module's own rule. The longest chain across slices, with how far it may step and skip, is this module's design: Yao et al. 2006 extract the canal with a graph search, but only their abstract was read, so their search is cited for the idea and not for this chain. A chain whose holes have a median area above 419 mm2 is said in the study's problems.
3. Cross-sections perpendicular to the canal's smoothed centreline are weighed by their dense bone, and the discs are the weight's dips (Radnostics' disc rule). Profiles along a curved reformation that follows the cord are Yao et al. 2006's; Radnostics' own profile follows a curve through the expected body centres, and taking the axis from the canal's centreline is the research note's inference, not a published step. The research note flags the patent's text as garbled at the weight's equation, and the weight is used as the note reads it. The weight is averaged over 3 mm, and a dip and the spikes either side of it are measured above the moving minimum over Radnostics' 40 mm window, an adaptation, so a weight every section shares, such as an aorta full of contrast, moves no disc; how deep a dip must be is this module's choice. A dip's rise must also be at least half the column's typical rise from a disc to a body (the median of the moving maximum less the moving minimum, this module's choice), so a shallow dip with no deeper one within 20 mm, such as a sacral segment's rudimentary disc or a ripple along a fused block, is no disc; where most of the weight is sacrum or fused bone, the typical rise is their ripples' and this guard does not hold. Radnostics' gap analysis is not implemented.
4. A vertebra is the bone between two discs; the bodies at either end, bounded by one disc, are kept unmeasured.
5. Its edge is half way between its own soft tissue and its cortical rim's blurred peak, never below 130 HU, Westerhoff's threshold for bone and vessels together, which keeps soft tissue out but not contrast. Half way is the 50 per cent relative threshold Treece, Gee and Poole compared in TR634 (Hangartner 2007 found 49 per cent on the one scanner he tested), and taking the rim's blurred peak as the upper level makes it the full width at half maximum form, which TR634 found less accurate in cortical thickness than a simple threshold; step 7 corrects it. The edge is placed on a grid no coarser than half a millimetre for voxels up to 2 mm and capped at four sub-voxels beyond (0.75 mm across 3 mm slices); holes inside the rim are filled, so an osteoporotic core is not lost.
6. The pseudo-mask goes through the same chords as a whole-vertebra label (geometry.measure_whole).
7. The half-maximum edge lies outward of a cortex thinner than the blur, so each chord's ends are moved to the surface Treece's blurred-step model places ([edges.py](edges.py); cortical bone mapping, Treece et al. 2010), fitted with one cortical density and one blur per kind of surface per vertebra. Cortical bone mapping estimates its density from the scan; this module assumes 1,200 HU instead, its own choice, and an estimate over the whole scan was not tried. The blur is chosen by a trimmed sum over a majority of the profiles, so a minority of bad profiles is left out or outvoted (a majority is not), on a fixed grid with the two other tissue levels solved in closed form where the patent fits by Levenberg-Marquardt, and each chord's end moves by the median shift of its kind of surface where cortical bone mapping keeps a surface point by point; both are this module's choices. `--half-maximum-only` skips it.

No trained model, no random number and no iteration to convergence enters, so the same volume gives the same chords, bit for bit, on the same machine and numpy, scipy and BLAS build; the last bits may differ across CPUs and can rarely tip a tie. A DICOM series is read through its Rescale Slope and Intercept, its positions and orientation, and slices sorted along their normal. A series whose Rescale Type is anything but HU (MGML, Z_EFF, HU_MOD or another term of PS3.3 C.11.1.1.2) is refused, and so is a multi-energy series not stated in HU (PS3.3 C.8.2); a slice with no Rescale Type is HU, since the standard requires one wherever the values are not. A missing slice, mixed series or an uneven step is refused, and the header's sex, age, size, weight, kVp, kernel and scanner become the study's covariates; an Enhanced CT Image's kVp, kernel and slice thickness are read from its functional groups, where it keeps them. A Patient's Size above 3, probably centimetres where the standard says metres, and an age of 000Y, which the research note's inference treats as missing (the reason, that an age under a year is written in days, weeks or months, is this module's), are recorded as left out, with why. A NIfTI file gives what its dcm2niix sidecar gives, if there is one, held to the same rules. A NIfTI whose qform_code and sform_code are both 0 has no orientation and is refused, and a trailing dimension of length 1 is dropped. A volume whose lowest percentile is far above air is said to be possibly uncalibrated.

Error, in millimetres (chord minus truth), on one body of three on each phantom of [tests/phantom.py](tests/phantom.py), in the order EPWu, EPWl, EPDu, EPDl, VBHp, SCW, SCD. `python -m scanimport.tests.bench_mask_free` writes this table:

The half-maximum edge is accurate on a sharp scan and runs long under clinical blur, by a millimetre and more on the widths and depths and short by as much on the canal. The fit removes most of that where the cortex is thin and dense, as the thin dense phantom's is (within 0.25 mm there; that a vertebra's end plates and canal walls are often under 1 mm thick is the research note's inference, untested in any source it read), and over-corrects by up to about 1 mm, the widths, depths and heights short and the canal long, where the cortex is less dense than the 1,200 HU it assumes. Fitting the density per kind of surface as well (`edges.fit_density`) is not used: `python -m scanimport.tests.bench_mask_free --density "thin dense" default` gives a worst chord of 0.74 mm against 0.24 mm on the thin dense phantom (it chose 600 HU for the walls and the end-plates and 1,800 HU for the canal) and 0.57 mm against 0.67 mm on the default one ([edges.py](edges.py), HOW IT IS FITTED). Across slices thicker than 2 mm the heights are uncertain by up to a slice, the fit does not help them, and the study says so in its problems.

What it cannot do:

- It names no body. Every body is "?", the column is not countable and its annotation is "none", so it reaches tier 4 alone; in a pair, 2, or 1 when the X-ray carries a person's annotation ([tiers.py](tiers.py)).
- A rib head or a contrast vessel touching the body at clinical resolution joins it. A vertebra whose region holds metal (2,500 HU or more) is not measured and the reason is given; metal within 60 mm of the canal is also a problem of the study, and metal farther away is not.
- The transverse span and spinous length are not taken, since the plane between two discs cuts the neighbours' arches.
- No training-free CT chord measure in the sources read has been validated in millimetres against manual or caliper measurement. This one is checked on phantoms only, and on real scans `compare` of a mask-free import beside a labelled one of the same scan is the measure to read first.

## One image: corners, FleXray's masks and the header's scale

[film.py](film.py) reads one image with what was drawn on it. A film is never measured from its intensities alone. Training-free designs that read the intensities of a radiograph or a lateral DXA scan do exist: three expired Siemens patents find the spine's axis, its boundaries and end-plate lines with no training; Lunar's DXA patent places six morphometry points per vertebra, with no operator input or training described; and Benjelloun et al. 2011 find the corners between two clicks before a trained shape model takes over. But none of them publishes an accuracy of its own: the patents give none, and Benjelloun's corners only start the trained model, whose result is the one measured. The training-free methods that do publish numbers only locate the spine or, with seeds, give Cobb angles, so no training-free method, seeded or not, reports a corner or end-plate point error in millimetres, and every millimetre-level corner result in the research read comes from a model trained on annotated images ([radiograph_classical_methods.md](../research_notes/Vertebra%20measures%20without%20masks/radiograph_classical_methods.md), sections 3 and 4). A chord from a method whose error nobody has measured would carry that error into the rule, so an image with neither corners nor masks of the bodies is refused.

- The scale comes from the DICOM header, in the order the standard's attributes allow ([readers.py](readers.py), `projection_scale`): a fiducial calibration, then a geometric correction, then a Pixel Spacing smaller than Imager Pixel Spacing with no calibration type (corrected for magnification or calibrated against an object, by a method not stated, PS3.3 10.7.1.1), then Imager Pixel Spacing over the Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor where it is above 1, over SID/SOD where the source-to-patient distance is the shorter, or over the default magnification, and last a Pixel Spacing of unknown provenance, each with a stated spread. Two parts of the report's rows depart from them. Row D6 reads the source-to-object distance by module (to the table for DX, the field centre for CR, the isocentre for XA) and can take an object depth, M = SID/(SOD - depth); neither is built, so every module's SID/SOD is used as stated with one spread. Row D8 says only that a Pixel Spacing of unknown provenance carries the widest uncertainty; treating it as the detector's pixel and dividing it by the default magnification is readers.py's choice, so a spacing already corrected would read about 21 per cent short, and its spread of 0.12 puts magnification 1 about two spreads below the default. The default is 1.26 with a log spread of 0.10, midway between the means Ravi and Rampersaud measured for vertebral bodies on lateral digital films against CT or MRI, 1.21 cervical and 1.31 lumbar (ranges 1.06 to 1.57 and 1.09 to 1.63; the report's row D7); the midpoint and the spread are readers.py's choices, and an AP film takes the same default, since the report found no AP figure. On scanned film, Nominal Scanned Pixel Spacing stands in for Imager Pixel Spacing wherever the latter is named (PS3.3 10.7.1.1, C.8.6.2), and a secondary capture with neither it nor Pixel Spacing has no scale of its own. An Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor not above 1, or an SID not longer than the SOD, is passed over and recorded as a problem, and so is a Pixel Spacing larger than Imager Pixel Spacing with no calibration type, since a correction for magnification only makes the pixel smaller. A fiducial or geometric Pixel Spacing larger than the detector's pixel, which would put the patient at a magnification below 1, is used but recorded as a contradiction. Pixels that are not square (row and column spacing more than 1 per cent apart) are refused, a CT localizer's among them, since every chord is scaled by one length per pixel; scaling each corner's x and y by its own spacing (the report's row M6) is not built. `--pixel-mm` replaces only the header's pixel length; the header's magnification, stated or implied by its corrected Pixel Spacing, still divides it, else the default does. A picture file carries no scale, so its scale is the caller's `--pixel-mm`, else the annotation's pixel_mm, else unknown, and never the file's print resolution; the reader's word that the scale is unknown is kept only when no scale is found at all. A JPEG's EXIF orientation is applied before anything is measured. The importer reads an image's size from its header (a DICOM file's Rows and Columns, a picture's size after its EXIF turn) and decodes no pixel, since nothing it measures reads an intensity; so a palette image or a compressed transfer syntax with no decoder installed is still imported. Where `readers.read_projection` is asked for the pixels, a colour image is made grey by Pillow's documented convert("L") weights (no research note covers them), and a DICOM palette image is refused.
- An annotation must be of this image: where it states the image's sha256 (in either case), width or height, a mismatch is refused. `--view` and `--modality`, where given, must match what it states and fill in what it leaves out (absent, null or blank), so an image the caller calls a DRR is never tiered as a real X-ray. An annotation that states no modality (absent, null or blank) is read as film, and a stated one is kept without the spaces around it. An annotation's pixel_mm that is not a positive length (zero, negative or not a number) is refused, on ingest-xr and ingest-image alike. Who drew the corners is checked before FleXray names anything. Where the image has a scale of its own and the annotation also states pixel_mm, the image's scale is used and the two are compared at the detector, against the caller's `--pixel-mm` where given, else the header's Imager Pixel Spacing, its Nominal Scanned Pixel Spacing on scanned film, or a Pixel Spacing of unknown provenance: a disagreement of more than one per cent is recorded as a problem. So is a header that gives its pixel only at the patient (a Pixel Spacing calibrated against a fiducial or for geometry, with neither of those spacings beside it), against which the annotation's value cannot be compared. The study records the pixel as the annotation states it (`pixel_mm_stated`) beside the pixel its chords were scaled by, at the detector and at the patient (`pixel_mm_at_detector`, `pixel_mm_at_patient`).
- FleXray's vertebra channels are read here as whole vertebrae, arch and processes with the body, so they name bodies and never measure them. That reading is this module's: the research notes give the channels' names and the protocol, not what the masks held. A body of the annotation takes the level whose mask covers most of its quadrilateral, if that covers at least a quarter of it. A disagreement with the annotation's own name is recorded as a problem, and the annotation's name is kept. A body the annotation left as "?" takes FleXray's name only where that keeps to the reader's names. FleXray names nothing once it contradicts any name the reader gave: the MOOSE dataset spec drops L6 to background (the research note infers the same of T13), so on a transitional column its count can be a body off. It never repeats a reader's name, and never breaks the image's top-down order against the reader's names or its own. Each body left "?" says why: FleXray's call was refused, or it made none because a corner is missing or hidden, because no level's mask covers a quarter of it (with the largest share), or because the arrays hold no vertebra. Once a model has named levels, the study's annotation is "machine", whatever drew the corners. The channels are named by a label_names.json, given or found beside the arrays, else FleXray's own 61, and that file is hashed. A vertebra's channel must be spelled as FleXray spells it (vertebra_l1, sacrum), and arrays with no such channel are refused.
- Masks of the bodies alone (`--mask-content body`) give each body's corners as the largest-area quadrilateral on its outline (Franchini et al. 2014), the exact maximum over the outline's hull vertices, ordered as the format orders corners by the head's direction from the neighbours. On a test image of 1,000 by 600 pixels, three bodies of 100 by 70 pixels turned 10 degrees came back with every corner within one canvas pixel (3.9 image pixels) of the drawn rectangle's corner, and both end-plate widths within one canvas pixel in millimetres.

## How the rule names an import

[bridge.py](bridge.py) is the archive's exact rule ([archive/bordercall/exact.py](../archive/bordercall/exact.py)), imported from where it is and not copied, with three marginals of the same model:

- A missing measure is integrated out. The covariance of the entries present is the subset of a diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix, which is again diagonal plus rank one, so Sherman-Morrison runs over what is present.
- Bodies with gaps between them are compared each with its own level, through offsets taken from the names.
- The scale is integrated with the study's own spread. A CT carries the generator's 0.07. A film carries that plus the spread of its magnification, which radiograph.py records (default m0 1.26 with a log spread of 0.10, a stated choice that the image's own geometry replaces). A film with no pixel size is named under a flat prior on its scale, with the measurement error carried by the scale, so it gives the same answer in any unit.

On complete fields of consecutive bodies, its log evidence equals the archive's (exact.evidence_lean on the same trapezoid nodes) to 1e-10. With values missing or gaps, it equals the dense normal marginal written out with a Cholesky factor to 1e-9, and an unknown scale gives the same posterior whatever the unit, to 1e-9 ([tests/test_bridge.py](tests/test_bridge.py)).

`score` runs the rule on every window of each width in every study whose labels were counted from C2, against those labels. Studies whose phenotype is not typical are scored in a group of their own, since the rule is the typical column's. `name` names one field file and prints the posterior, the mapping quality and the 99 per cent set.
